Strangers and Freaks have returned in GTA 6. These aren't missable missions in the sense that you lose them directly because of how time passes in GTA 6 day to day, which the game flags with an hourglass icon on the mission marker. There are also missions, such as Kenton's treasure dives in chapter 1, that can become locked for a period of time as you progress through the story, but these will unlock later. Instead, you need to find these "Stranger and Freak" NPCs in GTA 6 within their relevant chapters, or else--at least in some cases--you'll miss the chance to kick off their questlines. Thankfully, most of them begin with texts or calls you won't miss if you're diligently glued to your phone like a modern American is expected to be. It'll take you at least around 45-50 hours to get through the story if you're solely focusing on it. However, if you're also partaking in some side content, then you'll want to know when the "point of no return" is during the GTA 6 story. This is essentially when the game locks you out of anything aside from reaching the end credits and finishing the story. So, if you want to finish up any loose ends in Leonida, you need to know when the point of no return occurs. Warning: there are spoilers for the GTA 6 main story below. What leads up to the point of no return in GTA 6? Fortunately, Rockstar Games makes it fairly clear as to when the point of no return is in GTA 6. However, the decision to continue on with the story until the end is somewhat thrust upon you, so it's still worth knowing what leads up to this moment. During Chapter 5, Jason and Lucia have two primary goals: get out of Vice City and make enough money so they can support Tommy in a new life. To accomplish both of those goals, the protagonists work with Valentina, their money launderer and political go-between, to secure a deal for their coveted hard drive. The deal could be worth a lot of money, but Jason and Lucia also need to shore up their immediate finances so they have something to live off once they escape Leonida and start over with Tommy. In the background of all this, the pair's lawyer, Abe, is working with the U.S. government to secure a pardon for them, which expunges their record of all illegal acts they've committed. To push the pardon through, though, you need to fork over a whopping $150,000 to Abe's law firm as a service fee. When does the point of no return happen in GTA 6? After you BuckMe that tidy sum to the law firm, you'll receive a new story mission on the map marked with Abe's initials. If you go to the mission's starting point, Jason and Lucia will stand in front of the law firm's entrance and ponder whether they're truly ready to sign the pardon and start their new life. It's at this point that you receive a black and white quick-time event that asks whether you're ready to move on to the endgame or stay and continue tying up loose ends. This is the point of no return in GTA 6; if you decide you want to see the ending through, then choose to sign the pardon. However, if you still want to make extra money or finish up any side missions, choose to stay and not sign the pardon yet. It's important to note that going past the point of no return in GTA 6 doesn't lock you out of your save. Even after seeing the ending and rolling credits, the game allows you to go back just before the point of no return and continue on with the game. You can try to get a different ending, complete side content, and do everything else that was available before the point of no return originally. Still, whenever you decide to sign the pardon at Abe's law firm, you've gone past the point of no return in the story, even if you've already seen the ending to GTA 6. So, if you haven't come close to reaching that point yet, don't sweat it, as you still have a while to go. Even if you are close, though, the game doesn't force you to continue with the story until you're ready. Capcom has been on a truly generational run in recent years, but even their legendary lineup of games was bound to have a few stumbling points. Exoprimal is a prime example – the dinosaur-themed hero shooter was not a terrible game by any means, but it requires online play and hasn't managed to maintain much of a player base. Today, Capcom announced plans to sunset the game, rendering it unplayable by September of next year."Thank you so much for your continued use of Exoprimal," an announcement on the Exoprimal site reads. "We regret to announce that service for Exoprimal will be discontinued as of 8:00 PM (PDT) on Wednesday, September 29, 2027." The announcement goes on to specify that the game will "no longer be playable" after that date – Exoprimal is an online-only game which requires an internet connection, so once the servers are offline, that's it. Between now and September 29, Capcom will bring Exoprimal down in stages. As of yesterday, players can no longer purchase a Survival Pass Premium Tier for seasons 1-4. On March 30, Exoprimal and all its DLC will be de-listed from stores, but software owners can still play it and/or re-download it. Finally, the game will fully shut down by September 29.Any online-only game has a finite lifespan, but it's still sad to see them go. Many gamers are frustrated in recent years because it feels like they don't really own anything, whether it's a subscription-based product or a digital download that can be discontinued at a moment's notice. It's the primary cause of outrage behind PlayStation's decision to stop producing physical discs in 2028.As for Exoprimal, our reviewer Marcus Stewart largely enjoyed his time with it, writing, "Exoprimal’s creative subversion of expectations impressed me in more ways than one, and its approach to telling a robust narrative within a multiplayer framework is an example I hope other titles study. I just hope it’s enough to keep the game from going extinct."
