Gears Of War Should Have Killed Off Marcus Fenix Years AgoNEW
Gears of War is arguably the most masculine property in video games. A franchise founded on the idea of muscular, refrigerator-sized dudes tearing apart stinky underground monsters with badass chainsa...
Gears of War is arguably the most masculine property in video games. A franchise founded on the idea of muscular, refrigerator-sized dudes tearing apart stinky underground monsters with badass chainsaws until they’re covered in bloody viscera was always destined to attract such a reputation, but this only makes the moments when it subverts our expectations all the more impactful. Instances of quiet within its post-apocalyptic world, or conversations shared between its main cast when everything seems lost, always hit the hardest.  Once you strip away the chainsaw bayonets, you’re left with a profound narrative of grief as Marcus Fenix tries to save a humanity that is already flirting with extinction. While all of his muscles and bravado might make the man seem infallible, Fenix is frequently tasked with the impossible--to save a human race that is constantly under the threat of annihilation. He’s doomed to fail, to eventually fall alongside his comrades until a new generation emerges to continue the fight. This makes perfect narrative sense, and would also give the iconic hero some much needed rest. So, what would happen if The Coalition actually went through with it and killed him? Ever since I first laid eyes on Marcus Fenix as he explored the darkened streets of a fallen city to the melancholic beauty of "Mad World," I have viewed him as a tragic character. He’s fighting an impossible war after being freed from unjustified imprisonment, and spends the entire original trilogy watching close friends succumb to their personal demons or fall to an enemy that shows no signs of slowing. Making the player feel doomed from the outset can be an incredibly powerful storytelling tool, so it’s no surprise that E-Day focuses so heavily on the world falling apart as a younger roster of heroes is forced to take action.  When you turn up to save the day amidst the rubble of Kalona, it’s often about picking up the pieces while walking over fresh corpses of thousands, where finding even two survivors is a reason to celebrate given the circumstances. Not once does Fenix entertain leaving innocent people behind, and you truly feel for him when retreat becomes the only option. Unfortunately, once the excellent opening act concludes, the campaign settles into a predictable routine that can’t help feeling middle-of-the-road. This question has been on my mind for over a decade now, since the character arc of Fenix came to a natural conclusion in Gears of War 3 after the Locust are defeated and humanity is guaranteed a future. Our flawed hero might be stricken with grief by the sacrifices of Dom and his other comrades, but he remains surrounded by people who love and care about him in ways that can now be treasured in a world where survival is guaranteed. But thanks to this need to keep popular franchises alive and avoid disappointing legions of fans, Marcus Fenix has remained integral to Gears ever since.  While the details are cloudy all these years later, there was a time when Gears of War was potentially going to leave the planet of Sera behind in pursuit of pastures new. Speaking to Game Informer as part of a cover story back in 2016, creative director Chuck Osieja claimed that The Coalition was originally thinking about taking Gears to a fresh planet with an all-new cast of characters who were only loosely connected to what came before. So long as Gears focuses on the machinations of conflict with satisfying combat and a worthwhile story, I can see it taking place anywhere, but without the stewardship of Epic Games it settled for the most predictable destination imaginable. Marcus Fenix has a son, the Locust comes back, and humanity once again finds itself on the backfoot. Turns out the original trilogy was for naught.  Gears of War 4 centered on a new trio of likeable characters--JD Fenix, Kait Diaz, and Del Walker--who were all raised in a world without the Locust threat, but still living beneath the thumb of an outdated militaristic society that refuses to leave old habits behind. It’s a more saturated and freeing world, but the very same darkness awaits beneath the surface. As for Marcus Fenix, he has secluded himself in the wilderness after the death of his wife and the trauma of perpetual conflict, resentful of a son who decided to join a military he has actively come to despise. The war may be over, but Marcus has little interest in returning to a world that refuses to learn from its mistakes.  This new trilogy would eventually shift focus away from the Fenix family and instead mostly  on Kait and her strange connection to the new Locust threat, with Gears 5 offering a harsh and sudden player choice in the final act as you’re asked to save the life of either JD or Del. Whoever you don’t choose is murdered before your eyes, but since the trilogy was ditched, at least for now, for a prequel in E-Day, we never get to witness the repercussions. Even Marcus seems pretty unfazed by the death of his only son, when this could have been a killer moment for decades of anger, grief, and resentment to culminate in something meaningful. The Top 10 Best Horror Games To Play This HalloweenNEW

Humans are drawn to horror in all forms, real life and fiction. Perhaps it’s the desire to get as close to death as possible without actually dying; perhaps it’s the morality of life and knowing death comes for all of us; perhaps all our heads are just a little miswired. Whatever the reason, we flock to movies where supernatural entities and puppets on tricycles upend otherwise lovely lives, TV shows where murderous cults kill and stump local detectives, and books where the words on the page seem to follow you to bed. Games, however, go a step further, letting you enter the horror and control it (or become its victim), and you could argue that, as a result, they’re the most immersive form of fiction. We at Game Informer love a healthy dose of horror from time to time, and we often review games like this. Some terrify us. Others… don’t. And the best ones stick with us for years and years after the credits. Below is a list of 10 of the best horror games we’ve played recently. This isn’t an all-time list, so don’t fret if your personal favorite isn’t here. Instead, this is a collection of 10 horror games ordered by release date that we think are great for spooky season and beyond. Let us know in the comments what you would add to the list! Village In The Shade PlayStation 5, PlayStation 4, Switch, PC The cozy corner of the horror genre seems to have exploded overnight, and now tons of new slice-of-life horror games are in various stages of development. That said, the team behind newcomer The Village in the Shade is no stranger to the form; they made the Yomawari series, a cute-and-terrifying collection of creepy virtual tales. The Village in the Shade takes things back to the homestead, giving you (a child who emerged from a hole in the ground and was immediately given the responsibility of running an entire farm by yourself) the freedom to plant, sow, reap, ranch, and more. Just follow the important rules: don't go out at night, don't share secrets, and don't leave the village among others. You wouldn't want to break the rules, right? You could, theoretically, go about your idyllic rural living. But as you unravel the threads of what this village is, who you are, and why you're here, it's hard not to get drawn into the strange, mysterious, delightfully disturbing truths hiding in its shadows. Just don't forget to water your turnips before you go sneaking out again. Tenebris Somnia PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch, PC Fans of survival horror and old-school FMV adventure games will find plenty to love (and fear) in Tenebris Somnia. When a young woman visits her ex-boyfriend’s apartment and discovers he's trapped in a hellish dream world, it kicks off a well-executed survival-horror trek of inventive puzzles and resource management to overcome dangerous monsters. High-production, live-action cutscenes help tell the story of a deteriorating relationship and the toll an indie horror film took on its creators, and they play an important role in gameplay. Tenebris Somnia may not look like much due to its classic pixelated presentation, but its scares and story captivate. – Our Review Resident Evil Requiem PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 2, PC Resident Evil Requiem is not just one of the best Resident Evil games, but it’s also one of the best horror games ever made. It features a unique dual-protagonist structure. Grace Ashcroft is timid, new to the whole “shooting zombies in the head while dealing with Umbrella viruses” thing, and is generally just trying to do her job. Leon S. Kennedy, however, is a veteran of all that and more. Requiem splits its runtime between the two characters, letting you experience both sides of Capcom’s marquee survival-horror style. Grace’s sections leave your hands clammy and your blood pressure high as you desperately try to survive. Leon’s sections leave your hands shaking with adrenaline and your blood pumping with excitement as he mows down zombies (sometimes literally, courtesy of those pesky chainsaws). The result is a Resident Evil game that blends two typical RE experiences – terrifying and tough or exhilarating over-the-top action – into one cohesive package. It’s perfect for someone who wants to feel scared and then soon after receive the catharsis of being the one in control, like being the tragic victim who dies in Act One of the horror movie and the last one standing who lives to tell the tale. – Our Review Reanimal PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 2, PC Little Nightmares developer Tarsier Studios has a knack for delivering creepy side-scrolling adventures, but Reanimal eschews any whimsy to become its scariest game yet. While mechanically it is similar to Little Nightmares and still puts small children in unimaginable peril, Reanimal is capital-H horror thanks largely to its terrifying threats and disturbing animal-themed imagery (this probably isn’t the game for animal lovers; some messed-up stuff happens to these critters). Escaping the game’s island setting is thrilling and chilling whether you’re playing alone or with a friend in co-op. Months after release, we’re still thinking about Reanimal’s memorable final sequence, which is worth the price of admission on its own. – Our Review Silent Hill 2 (2024 Remake) PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC 2001’s Silent Hill 2 is considered one of the best horror video games of all time for good reason. At release, it was a graphical powerhouse, using the PlayStation 2's horsepower to create a setting that begged to be explored, even though every bone in your body said you shouldn’t. The remake from developer Bloober Team took on a big task in modernizing the game without losing the original’s soul, and it did a great job. Among all the recent Silent Hill releases (Silent Hill f, Silent Hill: Townfall), the remake is our favorite. The story of James Sunderland and his troubling relationship with his departed wife is complex, emotional, and – important for the purposes of this list – unsettling and scary. – Our Review Alan Wake 2 PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC Alan Wake 2 is an excellent pick for some spooky madness, in the vein of David Lynch’s seminal Twin Peaks mystery-surrealist series. Don’t worry too much about the “2” part of the game’s title. You just need to know that the titular Alan Wake character is a not-great writer who acts like he’s really great, his wife is missing, and he got trapped in the ambiguous Dark Place. He’s only one half of the Alan Wake 2 equation, with his segments set in a dream-like version of New York City. Playing as him feels the most Twin Peaks-y. The other half is Saga, a police officer who arrives in the Pacific Northwest town of Bright Falls to investigate a murder that might be linked to a cult. You can expect more traditional survival-horror gameplay from her. But this is still a Remedy Entertainment game, and without spoiling anything, it goes places. And along the way, you can expect plenty of scares. A group of people in the city of Honjo find themselves caught in a spectral battle royale, using powerful objects and conditions to curse one another, with the allure of a prize at the end. Its magic isn't just in the flowchart timeline, where you can hop between different perspectives and protagonists to see how events play out from every angle, but also in the gorgeous stereoscopic environments and narrative puzzles. As the Replika unit Elster, you delve deep into a remote complex filled with horrors and monsters, searching for something, or someone, waiting down below. While love for classic horror games and other hallmarks of genre fiction will certainly draw you in, the stylish presentation and devastatingly human core will keep you forging ahead to the finale. Once you've breached the surface of Signalis' retro-future super-structures, you won't be able to turn back. Immortality PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC, iOS, Android In horror movies, audiences often groan at a protagonist for putting themselves in seemingly grave danger just to learn more about a cursed amulet or bloodthirsty killer. But in Immortality, you are the protagonist, and despite a lingering sense that you shouldn't be digging into the archives of these abandoned films, each eerie new scene makes you want to dive deeper. It's a game about Marisa Marcel, an actress who almost starred in three movies in the late 20th century, but has since disappeared. None of the movies were ever released due to mysterious circumstances, but you have access to the archived, unedited live-action footage, including behind-the-scenes conversations. Immortality is one of a kind and an easy recommendation for horror fans who want to try something a little different.
