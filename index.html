GTA 6’s Best Song Is A Nelly Furtado Cover From AzerbaijanNEW
One of the mainstay features in Rockstar's Grand Theft Auto series is radio stations that can be listened to while driving in cars or, as in GTA 6, on the go if you acquire headphones. As a music-disc...
One of the mainstay features in Rockstar's Grand Theft Auto series is radio stations that can be listened to while driving in cars or, as in GTA 6, on the go if you acquire headphones. As a music-discovery vehicle, GTA: Vice City introduced me to many '80s classics across a variety of genres. With GTA 6 taking players to Leonida, in which Vice City is located, it's apt that the game features music that many probably would never hear anywhere else. One of the radio stations you'll find in the game is Kaleidoscope FM. It features covers of very-well-known songs that are performed by artists from around the world. It's a fantastic hook for a radio station and delivers some of the catchiest and most-unexpected covers from artists that have a pretty low profile outside of their home countries. The first song I heard on the station, and the one that remains my absolute favorite, is a cover of Nelly Furtado's "Say It Right" from Azerbaijani artists Alim Qasimov and Farghana Qasimova. The duo are known for performing Mugham, a type of music that blends poetry, melody, and improvisation. I had never heard of them before playing GTA 6, but the music instantly transported me to my youth listening to Qawwali, a type of music that is rooted in Islamic sufism.  Initially, my exposure to Qawwali, a type of music that is over 600 years old, was limited to my own home, but it has since gained a worldwide following thanks to Nusrat Fateh Ali Khan, who is known as the King of Kings of Qawwali. These days it's pretty common to hear songs that either sample or are inspired by Qawwali, but, even so, hearing Mugham in GTA 6 was a huge surprise. I didn't expect to be playing Rockstar's latest opus and be reminded of Qawwali. https://www.youtube.com/watch?is=GsirJ2mQmdOdZt1a&v=6iA9fogYpLA I already love "Say It Right"--I think it's one of Furtado and producers Timbaland and Danja's best--but this cover draws out the melancholic soul that underpins the original, giving it a more emotional weight. Much of that can be attributed to the religious mysticism that both Mugham and Qawwali evoke.  What I appreciate about this cover is how brilliantly it allows the unfamiliar to grasp what makes Mugham and Qawwali so beautiful by presenting it through a song that many people already have some familiarity with. Both types of music are performed by an ensemble, but the improvisational nature means that others are encouraged to contribute. The use of the kamancheh, tar, and daf, along with the vocals covering Furtado's chorus and the khananda's additions, come together to make the cover feel magical.  There are other songs that will no doubt catch you by surprise on Kaleidoscope FM--another favourite is a South Asian cover of "Careless Whisper." Naturally, I was intrigued by how this station came to be and, according to Ivan Pavlovich, Rockstar's vice president of music, it stems from an obsession that studio co-founder Sam Houser has. “Historically, we have always loved to champion a quirky, concept-driven station," Pavlovich told GameSpot. "Sam loves foreign-language covers of his favorite songs and thought this could be that station for GTA VI. Tracking down rights holders proved particularly tricky in certain countries." The biggest surprise, however, is the radio station's host: Academy Award winner Jeremy Irons. "Jeremy Irons has the perfect voice as the travel-weathered host telling tall tales of his adventures from around the world," Pavlovich added. "Jeremy loved the script, and nailed the wry humor of his character in one session. Hopefully, this station makes fans smile as much as we did when we were making it." Reviewed on: PlayStation 5 Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S Publisher: Rockstar Games Developer: Rockstar Games Release: November 19, 2026 Many superlatives will be heard in the discussion around Grand Theft Auto VI – biggest world, most activities, best graphics, deepest narrative – but its real triumph is the way all those features and others work in service of a cohesive, believable immersion in a criminal fantasy that is hard to grasp until you’ve played it for yourself.Grand Theft Auto VI – Video Review: Rockstar’s latest returns players to a dramatically expanded version of the Vice City seen in a prior game, now largely unrecognizable after real and in-game years have passed. Leonida is a joy to explore, filled with a wealth of activities, both mundane and illicit. The game’s design makes many ordinary tasks as compelling as the illegal ones. Go fishing off the boat, brew coffee to wake up, work out at the gym, scuba dive for long-lost treasure, dust crops, motocross in the mud, go dancing at the club. As you move between those activities, the world feels alive with minor events, from traffic accidents to shady individuals a little too interested in your walk to the ATM. GTAVI delights in life's minutiae, with animations, voice lines, and background characters that keep things fresh even dozens of hours into the story. The level of detail everywhere is astounding and, at times, overwhelming. Getting sidetracked is easy.The focus on detail carries into the visuals. Lucia’s hair blows in the wind, and she absently pushes it back behind her ear. A drug den is littered with paraphernalia and empty containers. A shifty contact quirks a smile – your only hint that things are about to go south. Sweat glistens on your character’s skin after a hard workout, their breathing and heart rate noticeably heightened. Clouds roll together in the sky, creating realistic weather simulations. The commitment to an authentic simulated world is encompassing, thoughtful, and intentional, a testament to hand-crafted and meticulously painstaking development work, paying off in an absorbing play-space that is hard to step away from.While the day-to-day simulation is fascinating, it also adds pacing and counterpoint to the explosive march of major missions, narrative threads, and set-piece encounters that drive the game forward. Many of the best moments leverage an expert sense of musical accompaniment, weaving a slick original score alongside one of the most impressive and wide-ranging licensed soundtracks ever heard in a video game – often to surprisingly emotional effect. Ranged combat is punchy and enjoyable; smart use of cover and time-slowing focus makes most battles manageable. On-foot navigation is in a similar boat – simple to pick up and uninterested in needless complexity or challenge; the focus is on choosing smart moments for stealth, knowing when to run and when to fight, and selecting the precise instant for a burst of sudden action. Driving is fast and intense, made more appealing by a huge variety of functionally distinct vehicles to enjoy. If there’s a fault to the core and optional story missions, it’s that many feel a bit on rails regarding where to go and what to do at each step. At the same time, that directed approach keeps the pace relentless, and the action and outcomes thrilling. Over many hours, the game morphs and throws new wrenches into the mix at key junctures, teasing and then playing out criminal experiences from petty theft to group heists to illicit spec ops, and punctuates those distinct vibes with memorable and singular moments.Like its predecessors, GTAVI simultaneously lampoons and celebrates life in modern America, taking aim at targets like social media, wellness lifestyles, rap and music culture, manosphere content, and dozens more. It’s a bitter message delivered in honey-wrapped, exhilarating gameplay, but it shines through.Of all its significant feats, the relationship between Lucia and Jason elevates the game to new heights, lending this installment a heart, tension, and emotional core missing from prior entries. Like the best crime fiction, you’re left surprised by how much you cheer for two people who regularly commit terrible acts. The New, Non-GTA 6 Games You Should Be Playing Right NowNEW
While Grand Theft Auto 6 is set to dominate what GameSpot covers for the next month (at least), it doesn't mean there aren't plenty of other games out there that those of us are playing at the same ti...
While Grand Theft Auto 6 is set to dominate what GameSpot covers for the next month (at least), it doesn't mean there aren't plenty of other games out there that those of us are playing at the same time (or in the cases of those of us who aren't big GTA fans, what we're playing instead). Whether it's a new demo for a game we're eagerly anticipating, an indie title that's bravely facing off against Rockstar's juggernaut of a title, or just something that made us smile, we want to take a chance to highlight those games too. Below, you'll find a list of smaller or lesser-known games that we're playing alongside or instead of GTA 6, as well as links to our coverage of those titles. As we get into the thick of Grand Theft Auto 6 season, we'll keep coming back here to add additional impression pieces we want to highlight. In This Article Memorable DemosAt Fate's EndDraftulaExterminautsMy Arms Are Longer NowNirvana NoirParanormal Activity: ThresholdNew ReleasesAustralia Did ItDeadlockHungerTanuki: Pon's SummerThank You Bus DriverQueen's DomainVillage in the Shade Memorable Demos A fantastic demo can reinforce your decision to go ahead and wishlist or preorder a game you already have your sights on, but the best-of-the-best are those that surprise. Sometimes you have no idea what a game is going to be like or whether you'd even like it, and then you try out the free demo at the last in-person E3 you ever attended and suddenly the game becomes your entire personality for the next six years. Over the past month or so, we've played demos in both camps. None of these games are out yet, but they're all scheduled to launch either later this year or sometime next year. If you've got a spare hour or two, these snippets are an excellent way to spend your time. At Fate's End My most anticipated game of 2026 was At Fate's End. I say "was" because it was delayed to 2027. Thankfully, a demo for the game was released alongside the announcement of the delay, allowing me to at least experience a morsel of what's to come. Spoiler: It's fantastic. This is the next game from Spiritfarer developer Thunder Lotus, which sees you embody the role of a warrior princess who has to duel her hurting siblings with sharp swords and even sharper words--it's the exact emotional devastation I crave. And both the art and music direction are nothing to sneeze at. This is a pretty game to both look at and listen to. Draftula So you took GameSpot editor Steve Watt's wonderfully written Castlevania: Belmont's Curse review to heart, bought the game, and enjoyed fighting Dracula as yet another member of the Belmont family. But now what? Well, you could go help rebuild Dracula's castle in preparation for the next invasive adventurer. Writer Dave Irwin enjoyed the demo for Draftula, an upcoming roguelite that sees you needing to build Dracula's misshapen castle over and over, higher and higher, in order to waylay any would-be heroes. Exterminauts Okay, technically not a demo, but a beta test that you can join right now is demo-ish. GameSpot editor Mark Delaney compares Exterminauts to Helldivers 2, although this three-person player-versus-environment shooter stars regular schmucks obsessed with do-it-yourself projects, not patriotic starship troopers. The game otherwise plays very similarly to Helldivers 2, so the beta feels more like a means of testing to see whether the narrative flavor is to your liking. If you already like Helldivers 2, you'll probably like the gameplay of this shooter too. My Arms Are Longer Now GameSpot managing editor Chris Pereira and I often muse in our weekly one-on-one meetings that we rarely have time to destress via gaming, since the portal by which we primarily play games is also the machine we sit at all day for work. So any weird game that gets Chris to relax makes me happy and is more than deserving of a shout-out. My Arms Are Longer Now is a bizarre game that's strangely compelling--between the ridiculous humor of controlling an arm that stretches belief and the silly hijinks that you're able to get up to, this will likely remain the funniest demo in this roundup.  Nirvana Noir Genesis Noir is one of the most memorable games of 2021. Its use of abstract art to frame a compelling noir detective story within the explosion of the big bang created an experience that was quite novel. The next game, Nirvana Noir, aims to tell an even weirder story. Writer Khee Hoon Chan likes how the vibrant color of Nirvana Noir makes for an eye-catching sequel, one in which a detective hops between parallel realities (one in which the Big Bang happened, and one in which it didn't) to unravel a conspiracy centered around the final moment of Genesis Noir. The game looks gorgeous, and the demo already indicates that this game's puzzles and mysteries are going to be just as spellbinding as the first game. Paranormal Activity: Threshold Mark puts it perfectly: The scariest game of the year is a demo for a game that isn’t even coming out. Like Silent Hills before it (and Devotion seemingly until it surprisingly did come back), Threshold is a fantastically spooky good time that's just never going to release in full. But at least we still have this great demo to experience a tiny glimpse of what was going to probably be a strong first-person horror game. It's really no big surprise that this demo is good--Threshold was being developed by DarkStone Digital, the same studio behind the extremely popular (and oftentimes way too scary for me) The Mortuary Assistant.  New Releases There aren't just new demos to play right now. Some studios are bravely staring down the Goliath that is Grand Theft Auto 6, the sling that is their much smaller title in hand. Releasing in the weeks leading up to GTA 6 or in the weeks post-GTA 6's launch is a bold choice, but those of us who aren't huge into sandbox games are thankful that they're here. Too much of just one thing for a long time isn't always fun. These games fall into their own niche--you're not going to find any games listed here that feel like GTA 6 but smaller. We imagine most of those games were delayed to 2027 or beyond in order to not be overshadowed by Rockstar's action-adventure. Australia Did It A fear of being spoiled on GTA 6's story is pushing Chris to try and focus on Rockstar's action-adventure game, but a very different game has been slowly clawing its way into his heart. Between its fun unit-combining mechanics and hilarious conveyor belt of doom, Australia Did It has been pulling Chris away from GTA 6. Part turn-based tactics game, part tower-defense game, part survivorslike, Australia Did It seems tailor-made for GameSpot's resident tactics aficionado, but don't let Chris have all the fun. Be sure to check out this game yourself! Deadlock Deadlock is the latest in a now dwindling field of hero shooters, but unlike the swing-and-a-miss mishaps of Concord and Highguard, Deadlock seems to have the juice to stick around. The game's player numbers saw a huge surge in September with the City Never Sleeps update, and it's basically been doing just fine ever since despite technically still being in early development. Fingers crossed that continues to be the case. Deadlock has some of my favorite lore of any shooter ever, which is only amplified by the impressive world design of its maps and phenomenal character art direction. Writer Ana Diaz said that Deadlock is the game that convinced her we should all be role-playing in competitive games, and after seeing so many people doing just that in Deadlock and posting those conversations on YouTube, I'm inclined to agree! Hunger Unlike most of these games that are releasing just before GTA 6, Hunger is scheduled to launch just after Rockstar's game comes out. Set to be released in Steam Early Access on December 3, Hunger is an extraction role-playing game in which you navigate a plague-stricken Napoleonic Europe (so weird that we got two first-person zombie-adjacent Napoleon-centered games in 2026). If you're anything like me, you see the words "extraction shooter" and want to log off, but hold on! After playing Hunger for eight hours, writer Cameron Koch points to the extraction RPG's promise of permanent progress as its most compelling feature. You're doing everything else you would in other extraction games--looting, encountering other players, trying to get away--but there's a bit of roguelite-inspired gameplay at work here too, ensuring you can retain permanent upgrades not only from run-to-run, but season to season.  Tanuki: Pon's Summer A bike-riding, mail-delivering raccoon dares to challenge GTA 6, and we should all support him. Mark played this game in the week leading up to GTA 6's launch and spoke with game director Liam Edwards as to how the team decided that releasing their third-person adventure game between The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake and GTA 6 was the right move. Blending together BMX tricks and Japan-inspired locales, Pon's Summer sees the titular tanuki racing through four cities to deliver the mail as quickly (and as cool) as he can.  Thank You Bus Driver Friendslop is all the rage these days but, as the name implies, you do need friends to play with in order to enjoy them. Or I guess you could cheat like Mark and just have children. But not all of us are so lucky to be blessed with the circumstances to create people to play games with. That's what writer Jade King enjoys most about Double Fine's Thank You Bus Driver--this wacky first-person driving adventure game feels like playing friendslop, but it's single-player. The game sees you playing as the titular bus driver and utilizing 1930s animation logic to safely get your passengers to their destination--stretching your arms to absurd lengths to smack troublemakers in the back of the bus, for example, or utilizing magical trinkets to transform how the bus handles. Queen's Domain Quite a few of us at GameSpot are obsessed with Queen's Domain--it's quickly gone from a "I'll try to play this game at some point" to "I should play this before GameSpot's Best Of deliberations." GameSpot senior video producer Jean-Luc Seipke first put the game on our radar with their argument that Queen's Domain is gonna be a cult classic. Really hard to argue that I don't need to check out a game with a statement like that. But the pressure to check this game out only grew when Jade added that Queen's Domain has the most terrifying mimic ever, and that the whole game just has some of the best enemy creature design of any action-adventure game this year. You should join me in trying out this game this November. Village in the Shade First released in Japan on July 30, Village in the Shade released in North America and Europe more recently on October 30. Featuring a hand-painted art style and cute-looking characters, Village in the Shade looks like another in a long line of relaxing farming sims. But it's not. Instead, this game is another in a long line of "looks like a cozy game but is 100% not." Your farmer is immediately inundated with strange rules: don't talk to strangers and don't go out after 11 PM, among other similarly strange limitations. When writer Heidi Kemps checked out the game, she noted that it doesn't take long for Village in the Shade's otherworldly, encroaching horror to take hold, and you can't help but want to unravel the mounting mystery of the town.

The Blood Of Dawnwalker Just Got A Free 1.2 Update, Adding New Game PlusNEW
The Blood of Dawnwalker is one of the year's biggest, most ambitious games, and the developers over at Rebel Wolves have done a good job cleaning up bugs and performance issues in the weeks s...
The Blood of Dawnwalker is one of the year's biggest, most ambitious games, and the developers over at Rebel Wolves have done a good job cleaning up bugs and performance issues in the weeks since launch. Today, that trend continued; the new 1.2 update is out now on PS5, Xbox Series X/S, and PC, and it adds New Game Plus. The new mode is uniquely ideal for a game like Dawnwalker. The narrative is very player-driven, whether it has to do with your literal dialogue options, your ability to manage your vampiric hunger, or the order you complete quests, and all of these elements can cause your experience to vary wildly. It makes sense, then, that players would want to jump back in to try things again and see what might happen if the game is played differently, and New Game Plus allows them to do that while keeping combat fresh.According to the update notes on the Dawnwalker website, "NG+ unlocks after you complete the game’s Epilogue and lets you experience the story again while keeping your progress – mainly your character level, skills, and inventory." You don't gain access to these skills until you complete the prologue, however, which marks the adventure's proper start.The new mode will also rebalance the world's level scaling. In the base game, certain areas are much harder than others, but in New Game Plus, all enemies will rise in power to match your level, meaning you can explore the world in whatever order you want. On that note, the level cap has also been raised to 100, and protagonist Coen can collect new, more powerful versions of weapons marked with an "NG+" label.That's basically everything in this update. Rebel Wolves also added some slight tweaks to combat in the base game and adjusted some geometry in the open world, but the main focus here was New Game Plus. This is in contrast to the 1.1 update, which added a much wider suite of bug fixes and made the game run more smoothly.
