The cinematic trailer showed Rufus Shinra giving an address following recent world events, and the world has become overrun with Weapons. Those kaiju-like beasts were a part of the original Final Fantasy 7, including as optional bosses, but they seem to have a much bigger role in the story here. In dialogue the cast talks about them being guardians of the planet, and humans are seen as a harmful invasive species. The trailer showed off Ultima Weapon and Emerald Weapon in particular, but there may be more. The trailer also showed off a good bit of the full cast, an aerial battle in Cid's Highwind, and ground battles against familiar Final Fantasy foes like Typhon (aka Mr. Chupon, for you old-school fans). The final chapter in the Final Fantasy Remake trilogy--but maybe not the last game in this series--Final Fantasy 7 Revelation is building on the groundwork laid by its predecessors with new content and some fine-tuning of its open-world gameplay. One of the new features is the FITS system that allows you to equip Cloud and the gang with new roles in combat, and if you want to stick to the golden path without having to worry about stopping to take part in a game of cards, minigames will be more optional in the threequel. For more on the game, you can also check out dozens of screenshots showing off the world of Midgar, why the planetary weapons were game director Naoki Hamaguchi's favorite thing to remake, and who's voicing Sephiroth this time.

This year's Gamescom Opening Night Live ceremony just came to an end, and the livestreamed showcase had no shortage of highly anticipated announcements and trailers. From a closer look at the upcoming...
This year's Gamescom Opening Night Live ceremony just came to an end, and the livestreamed showcase had no shortage of highly anticipated announcements and trailers. From a closer look at the upcoming Witcher 3 DLC and the announcement of two PUBG spin-offs, to new glimpses of Control: Resonant, Silent Hill: Townfall, and Final Fantasy 7 Revelation, ONL seemed to have something for everyone. There was even a live performance of a track from the Mewgenics soundtrack. But between demands for a new Path of Exile 2 trailer (which finally materialized 90 minutes into the showcase) and random declarations of "GTA 6!!!," one message kept popping up in the showcase's live Twitch chat: #RIPDOLLY. Just moments into the live broadcast, country singer and pop culture icon Dolly Parton's death was announced via an Instagram post shared by her nephew and head of security, Bryan Seaver. https://www.instagram.com/p/DceNXgPJyZ9/ Parton passed away at 80, after decades of smash country hits and admirable charitable giving. Aside from appearing in several casino games, Parton had very little to do with gaming, her biggest contributions being her 2020 collaboration with Word With Friends and the inclusion of her songs "9 to 5" and "Odd Jobs" in 2009's Pop'n Music for the Nintendo Wii. (Though she did appear in her own pinball machine in 1979.) But despite her lack of deep connections to the world of gaming, homages to Parton continued to flood the chat even as Gamescom's opening showcase entered its second hour. And in his announcement of her death, Parton's nephew Bryan Seaver explained things perfectly. "My family and I have shared a lifetime of beautiful memories with Dolly, but so have you," he said. "She has always been on your television, your record player, your CD and on your phone playlist, becoming an extended part of your family whether you liked it or not. Our family and the team she has entrusted with her legacy will work diligently to ensure her spirit will live with us forever. Dolly was a farmer, just like her daddy; he farmed the soil, [while] she farmed songs and happiness." Perhaps that's the essence of it. Parton connected with everyone--not just country music lovers or fans of her many films. Like games themselves, Parton was a fond part of many of our childhoods. Her charisma was so strong that her memory managed to steal the show at a gaming showcase held half a world away from her home in Tennessee, where her family says she passed peacefully.  Image credit: Getty Images / Ian Gavan
