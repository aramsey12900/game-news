It can sometimes be easy to confuse a ghost event with a ghost hunt because, during a ghost event, the spectre can appear in full or partial form, chase you, sing, turn the lights red, or shatter them, interfere with electronic equipment, and even trigger the heartbeat sound. While these signs may indicate a ghost hunt has been triggered (and can be frightening), that’s not always the case. To understand whether you’re actually dealing with a ghost hunt or an event, you need to know the signs of a real hunt. Here are the key indicators a ghost hunt is taking place: All exit doors will close and lock, making an audible locking sound. Lights won’t turn on and off, but will flicker or dim if the ghost is close. The ghost will appear in its full model after a two-to-five-second grace period during which it’s invisible and cannot hunt; this starts after the exit doors close and gives you time to prepare. Ghost speed will vary, depending on its unique behaviors. Ghost footsteps will be audible. The ghost will make the same vocal sound every time it hunts during the same contract. A heartbeat sound can be heard when within 10 meters of the ghost and when it achieves line-of-sight (LOS) with you (also applies to events). Electronic equipment will flicker within 10 meters of the ghost, or 15m for a Raiju, if on the same floor as it (also applies to events). Van’s activity monitor will display level 10 (if available) during a hunt, but can also show this reading outside of hunts. Apart from The Twins, ghosts will always initiate a hunt from their current location. The length of a hunt depends on the difficulty level and map size you’re playing on, with larger maps and higher difficulty levels producing longer hunts. On average, normal hunts last between 15 and 60 seconds. However, if the ‘Kills extend hunts’ setting is enabled, hunts can last up to 45 seconds longer. Once a hunt is finished, the exit doors open and the ghost cannot hunt again for 25 seconds (or 20 seconds if a Demon). However, this isn’t the only type of hunt in Phasmophobia. Cursed hunts are special hunts that can only be initiated by using (or more likely misusing) cursed objects. These hunts can be triggered at any Sanity level, ignore hunt cooldowns, and only have a one-second grace period. In addition, cursed hunts last 20 seconds longer than normal hunts, with any hunts that follow (cursed or normal) also lasting 20 seconds longer than usual. How to prevent a ghost hunt The best way to survive a hunt is to prevent the ghost from triggering one in the first place. You can do this a couple of ways, but be warned: you can’t hold off a hunt forever. The first thing to note is that a hunt cannot be triggered when the set-up phase is active. The set-up phase is a period (between two and five minutes) during which the ghost cannot hunt and is active on Amateur and Intermediate difficulties, allowing newer players some breathing room. Your set-up time begins when you open the van door; players can track how much time they have left using the van’s countdown timer. Once it hits zero, you’re fair game for the ghost--well, almost. Keep your Sanity level high Keep an eye on your team's average Sanity level. This brings us to the first and most important method for preventing a ghost hunt: keeping your Sanity level high. Each ghost has a Sanity threshold at which it can hunt; when your team’s average Sanity drops below that level, the ghost can trigger hunts (though the Banshee initiates hunts based on the targeted player’s individual level). The standard threshold for most ghosts is 70% Sanity, but some ghosts’ unique abilities or behaviors can impact this. For example, the Demon can use its unique ability to hunt at any Sanity level, while the Obambo’s threshold is determined by the ‘state’ it is in. So, to help prevent a ghost from triggering a hunt, you and your team need to try to keep your average Sanity level high. One of the best ways to do this is to turn on main lights or use fire sources, such as the Firelight, to prevent Sanity drain. However, if your Sanity is seriously dipping, make sure the most affected members of your team use some Sanity Medication to restore their levels. Just remember, a cursed hunt can be triggered regardless of Sanity level. Crucifix Make sure to place your Crucifix so it covers as much of the room as possible. You can also use equipment to prevent hunts. By placing a Crucifix, you can prevent the ghost from triggering a hunt within its active range (determined by its Tier). Instead of hunting, the ghost burns the Crucifix and is unable to try to hunt again for 20 seconds. However, the Crucifix has limited uses and once it’s completely burned up, offers no protection. And while a Tier 3 Crucifix can prevent a cursed hunt, it requires the item to be unburned. Incense Don't forget your Igniter. Incense can also be used to prevent a normal hunt, but not a cursed one. If you light an incense within three to five meters of the ghost, depending on the tier, it prevents the ghost from initiating a hunt for 90 seconds (180 seconds for a Spirit and 60 seconds for a Demon). However, Incense is a consumable item, and you may need it to escape the ghost, so be strategic about when you decide to use it. How to escape or survive a ghost hunt in Phasmophobia If you’re unable to prevent a hunt, you need to know how to escape or survive it. There are a few options here, but not all will always be available, so it’s worth getting to grips with each method, as they may help you out in a pinch. Hide Make sure to turn off your electronic equipment. When possible, hiding is usually the safest and most reliable method to escape a ghost hunt, as it stops the ghost from getting you within its line of sight. Each map has official hiding spots that you should familiarize yourself with as soon as you start a contract; these include lockers, closets, small tents, and behind boxes. When playing on lower difficulty levels, more of these spots are available, but on higher difficulty levels, fewer (if any) will be available. When a hunt begins, find a nearby spot, get into it, and (if applicable) crouch or close the doors. Now, here’s the important bit: turn off all your electronic equipment, including headgear, as the ghost can detect electronic items--this is true for any hiding spots. I cannot count how many times a ghost has found me because I forgot to turn off my headlight. Also, if the ghost happens to open the doors to your hiding spot as it passes by, close them again, or better yet, hold the door closed. But what about when there are no official hiding spots? In this case, you need to find a makeshift hiding spot that covers you to eye level (crouched counts) and prevents the ghost from getting you in its line of sight. Hiding behind furniture, like the fridge in Edgefield or the dining table in Tanglewood, is usually a good option, but avoid hiding behind smaller items like lamps and bedside tables. If you struggle to find a hiding spot, you can always quietly hide in a smaller room, far from the ghost (if possible), with the door closed. However, the ghost may still find you. Note that the only ghost you should not hide against is the Deogen, which will always find you. So, if you hide properly and the ghost miraculously locates you, it may be a Deogen. Regardless of the ghost you face, it can hear you, so make sure to stay quiet (in-game and on voice chat) once a hunt begins. Run or loop Large in-map objects like this can be useful for looping. If you have no hiding spot near you during a hunt and the ghost is getting close, you may have to leg it. Ideally, your aim should be to get into a hiding spot, but if this isn’t possible, you need to be a bit more strategic. Once the ghost starts hunting, turn off your electronic equipment and try to decipher where it is to avoid running into it. To get to your spot, use your sprint ability in short two-second bursts to avoid the five-second cooldown that comes with using sprint for its full three seconds. If the ghost is within 10m of you, you’ll hear the heartbeat indicator. You’ll also get this indicator if it spots you and its line of sight is clear, which can also result in a speed boost (depending on the ghost). So, if the ghost spots you, don’t run in a straight line; instead, turn corners and move behind objects where possible to break its line of sight, while utilizing the sprint trick. If you haven’t got a hiding spot to get into, you just need to run down the hunt timer. Another great way to do this is to learn to loop. Looping is a useful Phasmophobia trick that sees you tightly circling a large in-map object, like a car or piece of furniture, or several rooms during a chase, staying within the ghost’s sight so it can follow you, but far enough away that it can’t reach and kill you, with the object consistently breaking the ghost’s line of sight and therefore interrupting its speed boost. It’s a risky technique that requires practice and isn’t always effective, especially against particularly fast ghosts like the Revenant.   Incense Incense can be a lifesaver when you need extra valuable seconds to escape. Incense doesn’t just prevent a ghost from hunting; it can also blind a ghost, slowing, stopping, or redirecting it, depending on the tier you use. As such, it’s a useful piece of equipment when the ghost catches up to you and you need a few extra seconds to escape--just make sure you take an Igniter with you. The most effective method to blind a ghost using Incense is to wait until the hunting spectre is within the Incense’s required radius (check this in your equipment manager menu before the contract), face the ghost, and then light it when the ghost is within range. While the ghost is blinded, you are briefly invulnerable, so quickly run through it (utilizing the sprint technique from before) and in the opposite direction from where the ghost was heading. This takes some practice, so don’t worry if you don’t get it right on your first, second, or even third try. Monkey Paw The Monkey Paw is a risky option. During a hunt, the investigation area’s exit doors are all locked. However, by using the Monkey Paw, you can make a wish that opens them, allowing you to escape, though this comes with consequences. If a Monkey Paw spawns for your contract, you can use it to make the wish, “I wish to leave,” during a hunt. This unlocks all locked exit doors, but you become slower for five seconds, and your vision becomes obscured. So, maybe stick to hiding if you can.

The presentation broke down classes and non-combat activities and revealed the game’s true structure.As previously detailed, Fortune’s Weave centers on four playable protagonists who enter the Heroic Games, a gladiator-style arena tournament, to have their wish granted: Cai, Dietrich, Theodora, and Leda. You can switch between playing each one at any time, with progress for each one saved, allowing you to experience each character’s narrative in parallel. This plays into the game’s main theme of multiple fortunes intertwining. However, the twist is that this era is actually set in the past. Fortune’s Weave’s true plot is set five years in the future, where the world has been destroyed by the returning demon god Balor. The progenitor god Sothis has summoned the one soul capable of stopping him, a nameless hero clad in white who is the game’s true protagonist. To avoid facing Balor alone, this hero travels to the past to influence the fates of the four champions in order to assemble them in the future to save the world.  Arena combat involves choosing a team of units and placing them on the field in traditional Fire Emblem fashion. Units can wield swords, gauntlets, spears, axes, bows, and magic, each with different properties and even forms (such as a gun(!)). Unleash Combat Arts deal greater damage at the expense of weapon durability, while Blaze Arts unleash bespoke special moves at the expense of HP. For example, Kai can blast a fireball that deals burn damage, Dietrich can instantly teleport to new squares, and Leda can buff surrounding allies and summon a monster to the field. After each move, you can rewind to any previous point in battle to redo any mistakes.The game’s classes are divided between Beginner, Specialty, and Advanced. Here’s a breakdown of each one, as detailed by Nintendo. Beginner – There are five Beginner Classes, each dedicated to playing a foundational role in combat. They include gladiators who excel in handling weapons, diviners who wield magic, ornius riders with increased movement range and more.Specialty – There are over 10 Specialty Classes, each with specific abilities. Armored knights offer ironclad defense, wing soldiers can leap obstacles and cover long distances, charioteers can strike multiple enemies in a straight line, and more.Advanced – These classes are masters of their skills. Cataphracts have the mobility of horses and are heavily armored; guardians are skilled with white magic and spears; and elephant riders fight astride their giant mounts, to name a few. Between matches, Free Time occurs. You can prepare for future battles by training in the arena or by watching rivals battle to gather intel on them. The shopping arcade offers new weapons and enhancements. At Temple Row, you can volunteer at certain temples to temporarily gain stat buffs in battle, such as reduced damage. Taking certification exams lets units learn a new class, provided they have prerequisite skills. You can also take on quests from townsfolk, which must be finished within a deadline. Certain citizens can be recruited to your cause, though some require gifts or a meal to convince them to join. Inns let you lodge, eat, and pass time to gain resources automatically. Bonded characters converse in cinematics, raising their bonds.Outside of Dagsion, you can explore a world map to visit towns, gather information to find treasures, and battle in random encounters. You can also find dungeons to get materials for weapons and other rewards. Interestingly, dungeon battles unfold as traditional turn-based battles instead of taking place on a grid. The presentation concluded with smaller updates. Nintendo Music will be updated to feature a selection of tracks from Fortune’s Weave, such as the main theme. The Nintendo Today app also has new Fire Emblem themes coming tomorrow, along with new information leading up to launch. A new Ask the Developer article will be published on September 15. Fire Emblem: Fortune’s Weave is coming to Switch 2 on September 17.

Beast of Reincarnation Review – Game Freak's Take On Action Combat Impresses, But Little Else In The Game DoesNEW
Reviewed on: PC ...
Reviewed on: PC Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC Publisher: Fictions Developer: Game Freak Release: August 4, 2026 Rating: Teen The protagonist of Beast of Reincarnation is a synthetic human being named Emma. She does not have a soul, but she thinks she wants one. It’s lamentably fitting, then, that Beast of Reincarnation feels soulless. Emma, perhaps appropriately, is stiff and lifeless, but so are her companions and just about every other character in this post-post-apocalyptic Japan. Even the world around them – a network of wide corridors made up of concrete reminders of what was, blanketed in familiar vines, shrubs, and greenery – is stiff. The explorable setting is unchanging and placed within gameplay uninterested in the mystery the setting otherwise evokes. Everything funnels you forward to the next fight, rushing you to advance in an effort to obscure the literal and metaphorical cracks in each zone. Fortunately, that next fight is the best part of Beast of Reincarnation, courtesy of a satisfying combat system filled with experimentation and tense fun. If only the rest of Game Freak’s venture into the action space matched it.  Emma, a mysterious old man named Brad, a young girl named Kagura, and your trusty wolf companion Koo travel throughout Japanese colonies in a Cleanse Walker mech. Emma, a purifier, is tasked with, well, purifying beasts overtaken by a blight en route to the Capitol, where she must defeat the Beast of Reincarnation to save mankind. These characters talk directly to Beast of Reincarnation’s transparent themes, taking a blunt approach and leaving little for the player to dissect or work through. The importance of friendship, family, having a soul, fighting to save people, and more are discussed ad nauseum in overly long cutscenes void of life. These characters are paper-thin, and the story, which features predictable arcs, lackluster villains, and plot beats about Koo you’ve seen in dozens of other dog-related stories, does little to inspire me as the controller of Emma. But her blade picks up the slack.  Game Freak blends Emma’s Sekiro- and Stellar Blade-style swordplay, which relies on parrying, dodging, and stagger bars, with Koo’s QTE elemental abilities that are reminiscent of last year’s Clair Obscur: Expedition 33 to create an excellent combat system. So excellent, in fact, that sitting through 10-plus-minute cutscenes after each milestone of progression is doubly excruciating. For a game that demonstrates such confidence in its combat, Game Freak is determined to pull you away from it with yet another exposition dump. While clearly pulling from the aforementioned inspirations, Beast of Reincarnation’s combat is easier. Bosses and more elite enemies still pose a great challenge, but generous HP recovery, powerful attacks that are easy to pull off, and large parry and dodge windows will help the less experienced feel capable and seasoned players feel untouchable. Initially, boss fights are tense arenas for learning and mastering the combat. However, they, too, become repetitive, with recurring bosses appearing far too much, especially for what are meant to be Beast of Reincarnation’s end-of-stage setpiece moments. Pay attention too closely, and you’ll find that enemies share many animations, removing the enigma of duels and bouts. It doesn’t help that Beast of Reincarnation takes a formulaic approach to each stage, either: The big boss of the area appears for a quick fight at the start, runs off when you’ve hurt it enough, and then waits in its lair while you fight through the stage until you reach it for a proper fight. But still, fighting a boss, whether a repeat or not, means engaging in the game’s combat, and doing so is congenial throughout.  Challenge aside, which comes in three difficulty settings, the combat is rewarding and highly reflexive to one’s strengths. I stuck to an elemental build where I’d begin each fight with Koo’s lightning Javelin equipped with a 2x status build-up mod, allowing my lightning-imbued katana to inflict the “Shock” effect in just seconds. That creates a window of paralysis that lets Emma’s various combo moves and finishers inflict heavy damage and stagger, if not outright kill. And that’s just one of the many builds I experimented with in Beast of Reincarnation. There’s room for ranged, stealth, fire, poison builds, and more. Through an expansive skill tree that continues to grow, even after the game’s final (and anticlimactic) fight, there’s no world where two players’ Emmas work the same. Generous attribute points used for raising attack, defense, and the usual standard RPG stats make the act of doing so feel inconsequential, and I rarely paid attention to my progression here. But the subsequent skill points I’d unlock, which let me power up specific abilities and moves in Emma and Koo’s kit, came in the dozens, and working through the associated skill tree was a gluttonous climb of power.  There’s a unique double jump in the form of a vine that Emma can use to push herself higher, and she can use those vines to whip to high-up perches and locations, but Game Freak is uninterested in how this might affect locomotion outside of battle. Utilizing these vines to get some air and slam down into enemies below with my Helm-Splitter ability is a treat – whipping up to a boring hallway littered with boring enemies to open a boring chest with boring rewards inside is not. You will open the same type of chest to receive the same types of resources countless times, and the “take all” button is your best friend because what these items do matters little. There are crops to grow from seedlings collected for Kagura to cook up stat-boosting meals, but these bonuses are negligible. There are skulls to collect to power up your Cleanse Walker, but I had maxed out this mech before the game’s halfway point. There are books (you can’t read) to collect, rations for a hunger system that matters little, and currency you will never struggle to accumulate. Seeking out these resources is as rote as the resources themselves.  The final act of Beast of Reincarnation, with its telegraphed revelations and color-by-numbers moments, left me feeling… nothing. But the ensuing New Game+ notification stopped me from uninstalling the game after the credits rolled. I don’t know if I’ll return to play more, but if I do, it won’t be because of Emma, but rather, what Emma’s sword can do.  Score: 6 About Game Informer's review system
