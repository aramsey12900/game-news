Co-founder and director Greg Foertsch, who spent 22 years at Firaxis, was joined by several other former Firaxis developers, as well as team members from other studios with diverse backgrounds, which Foertsch says brings new spins and perspectives to that core group's way of thinking. However, having that foundational group of Firaxis employees helped Bit Reactor hit the ground running, even though the studio was started during the COVID-19 remote work period."We were starting this up while people were remote, but we haven't just shipped one game together... a lot of us have shipped four or five games together, and have been together for decades, not just a year or two," Foertsch says. We have trust. All those things are so important to building a game that if you don't have them, they create hurdles. We were, out of the gate, able to sidestep some of the early startup stuff because of our experience together and, most importantly, our trust in each other." Bit Reactor was always going to make a turn-based tactics game for its debut title. However, it was the late Vince Zampella, former head of Respawn Entertainment, who pushed Bit Reactor to create a Star Wars game. "Vince ended up calling me and said, 'Hey, can you talk to me about what you want to make?' And I did, and I can't say enough about Vince having the vision and conviction to let us pursue this. Despite having a long-running animated series devoted to explaining the events of the Clone Wars, Foertsch knew it was the right time period to tell the story he wants to tell. That happened the vast majority of the time. However, it wasn't always cause for celebration. Star Wars Zero Company carries forward the common convention of permadeath, so while I was consistently recruiting new members of Zero Company, it wasn't always due to expansion. In fact, this may be telling on myself for some of the boneheaded moves I made, but by the time I reached the end of my demo, only Hawks and one other Zero Company member from the start of the story remained alive. My only major concern from my time with Star Wars Zero Company is the seeming lack of mission variety. While the maps are all thoughtfully crafted, the missions often boil down to eliminating all enemies or reaching a point of interest before evacuating your squad while more enemies spawn in. While those are tried-and-true tactics classics, I was more excited when missions with more variety popped up. I cherished each well-executed plan. I cursed my squadmates' missed shots despite the 90-percent chance of hitting. I didn't expect Star Wars Zero Company to scratch it with the intensity of a lightsaber. I came away from my time with Bit Reactor's leap into the Star Wars galaxy not only excited to see where the story goes and who might show up, but also feeling incredibly compelled to jump back in for more tactical missions. The gameplay loop, the tactical combat, and the impactful choice and risk/reward mechanics all amount to a formula for something potentially special. Add in the Star Wars dressing, and the nostalgia factor amps the anticipation up even more. I think we're starting to see a gamer population that's more open to, 'It's a good game, cool story, cool mechanics, it's something different.'"And with an IP like Star Wars, Zero Company may just have a noteworthy leg up over other titles in the tactics space, a feeling further amplified by the fact that it's crafted by several masters of the genre. However, we won't know for sure how far-reaching it is until Star Wars: Zero Company arrives on PS5, Xbox Series X/S, and PC on August 27.
