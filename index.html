Gaming has never been more expensive. We're here to investigate why, and help you play more while spending less. Welcome to GameSpot Cheap Week. Fewer and fewer games emphasize couch co-op these days, as we've moved to an increasingly online world that often assumes if you're playing video games with your friend, you're doing it in two different locations over the internet. That's all well and good, but it also means that in most cases, you and your friend both need to own a copy of a game in order to play it together. Yes, most of the time this is true, but a number of games have released over the last several years that support cooperative play without everyone needing to own a copy. This is usually accomplished via a digital item called a Friend's Pass, which lets someone without the game access it as long as they're playing with someone who does own the game.  Inflation and the cost of living are soaring, and it can be very difficult to justify a video game purchase just because your friend wants to play through it with you. As such, for Cheap Week, we've rounded up some of the best games you can enjoy with friends, even if only one of you owns a copy. These range from mandatory co-op experiences to games that are just a lot more fun with more people, and there's a very good chance you'll find something up your alley on this list. FBC: Firebreak It failed to attract much of an audience, but Remedy's Control spin-off FBC: Firebreak is still worth checking out for fans of the studio's brand of Lynchian weirdness, and you don't need to own multiple copies to play it with a friend. The game features a Friend's Pass that can be accessed across PS5, Xbox Series X|S, and PC. Because the game is so focused on cooperative action, this should make it much easier for you to find someone to play with (though it's meant for three players, so cross your fingers that someone else also bought the game).  See at Steam Lords of the Fallen Lords of the Fallen is a souls-like, meaning it's fully playable as a single-player experience, but if you want to experience it cooperatively with a friend, the game supports a Friend's Pass. Even better, it supports full cross-play with the Friend's Pass, so your co-op partner can download it on console while you're playing on PC, or vice versa. Lords of the Fallen is an especially good choice for a Friend's Pass, as souls-likes can be notoriously frustrating and another player can make that experience a little less stressful. Or you'll just both get upset.  See at Steam Midnight Murder Club Not only does Midnight Murder Club only cost $10 if you're purchasing it outright--it also supports one of the most generous passes of any game on this list. The Guest Pass gives up to five other players access to Midnight Murder Club alongside the person who owns the game, and since it's a six-player multiplayer party game, that makes it very easy to convince your friends to play with you. It also supports cross-play, so some players can be on PS5 while others play on PC.  See at Steam Reanimal  Reanimal may not seem like the type of game that would come with a Friend's Pass, but the horror-adventure allows you to bring along another player completely free to experience the entire campaign online. The feature works across PC, Xbox Series X|S, and PS5, with one player installing the full game while the other installs the Friend's Pass. The one exception is on Nintendo Switch 2, where it makes use of the system's GameShare feature, and this requires you to have a Nintendo Switch Online subscription, as well.  See at Steam A Way Out The first Hazelight game on this list, A Way Out is only playable in two-person co-op mode. As such, the studio smartly includes a Friend Pass so only one player needs to own the game. A Way Out's gameplay relies entirely on cooperation, with a split-screen perspective showing one player what the other one is doing at all times as they attempt to escape from prison by any means necessary. It's a departure from the more colorful, whimsical adventures the studio is known for, but it's no less effective, and it's a very creative use of the medium.  See at Steam It Takes Two One of the best games from Hazelight to date--it won the top prize at The Game Awards in 2021--It Takes Two sees a couple on the brink of divorce working together to solve puzzles and, hopefully, mend their relationship. As with A Way Out, it features a Friend's Pass, so only one person has to own the game. Also like A Way Out, the game is only playable in co-op and there are plenty of creative multiplayer-focused gameplay features that you'll discover during the adventure. It Takes Two was, as the name would suggest, clearly designed from the beginning for the co-op experience, and it works incredibly well.  See at Steam Split Fiction  You're never going to believe this, but for the follow-up to It Takes Two, Hazelight decided to make … a game with mandatory two-player co-op. Split Fiction's approach to the concept is very different than It Takes Two or A Way Out, however, splitting the action between fantasy and science-fiction based on the preferences of the two main characters. The first is that if you start the game in co-op, you have to finish it in co-op, and you cannot go from a solo game to a co-op game without restarting. The second is that cross-play is not supported, so make sure your friend has the platform you plan to buy the game for.  See at Amazon Ghost Recon Breakpoint It's not the best Ghost Recon game, that's for sure, but Ghost Recon Breakpoint is certainly more fun when you have a friend joining you for its open-world chaos. We do highly recommend playing Youngblood with another person, as well, because while the game does support single-player for its campaign, the action was clearly designed for two players working together.
