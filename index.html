Best Items To Get Early In Mortal Shell 2NEW
Are you thinking about the best items to get early in Mortal Shell 2? There are plenty of goodies to be found all over the ruined lands of Fallgrim. These include melee weapons, sidearms, and consumab...
Are you thinking about the best items to get early in Mortal Shell 2? There are plenty of goodies to be found all over the ruined lands of Fallgrim. These include melee weapons, sidearms, and consumables. Some, like Tarstones, also help you greatly as they add new effects to your equipped gear. Our guide has all the details that you need as you explore some nearby locales. Best early-game items in Mortal Shell 2 Before we get started, we do recommend a couple of articles that you can check out. The first is our beacons and maps guide, which gives you a lay of the land while you’re roaming around. The second is our Fainweald exploration guide, which is a step-by-step outline of the places that you need to check out upon arriving in the region. Marsh Peals Marsh Pearls are items that you should get early in Mortal Shell 2. You can obtain at least four from an NPC named Gorf just by handing over a single Weltcap. Just keep talking to Gorf and resetting the world via the nearby beacon, all while exhausting his dialogue responses. Each Marsh Pearl can be sold for 2,000 coins. That’s an easy 8,000 coins very early in the campaign. With all that gold, you can buy a bunch of goodies, as well as spend some on weapon and sidearm upgrades. The Marsh Pearls from Gorf net you quite a lot of gold. Leech effect Tarstones Tarstones with the leech status effect grant bonus health. It might not seem much until you realize that stacks persist even if your character perishes, as long as the weapon with that Tarstone isn’t equipped at that time. The first Tarstone that you want to get is the Parasitic Stone, which is in the Martyr’s Tomb dungeon. It’s just slightly south of the Great Arbiter of Flesh miniboss. The second Tarstone is called the Unwieldy Stone, which can be purchased from Merrick in Marrow Keep for 2,000 coins. The leech effect from Tarstones (left) continuously stacks with each enemy you slay (right). Troubadour’s Lute The Troubadour’s Lute, which comes from the One-Legged Wolf Tavern, is one of the best early-game items in Mortal Shell 2. That’s because, unlike other sidearms in the game, it can actually inflict the confusion status effect on enemies. If your target is unable to resist confusion, it will attack its old buddies for a few seconds. By extension, multiple hostiles that are confused would, naturally, just end up killing each other while you stare in disbelief. Note 1: The tavern where you find the Troubadour’s Lute is also where you can unlock the Gragu shell, provided that you’re able to complete Gragu’s task. Note 2: The Troubadour's Lute can be used to play various theme music. Get ready to cause some confusion with the Troubadour's Lute. Grisha Remnant The Grisha Remnant is another item that you need to get early in Mortal Shell 2. There are several of these found in the game world. In fact, by the time we concluded the campaign, we already had seven. As for the earliest one that you can get, well, it’s actually in the Flooded Village, the same place where you obtain the Forgotten Crossbow. There’s a Grisha that’s trapped in a pit. If you manage to kill it, you receive its remains as a prize. So, what exactly makes the Grisha Remnant a must-have item? It’s due to its passive effect, which grants a chance to summon a Grisha spirit. The description says this is an “on-hit” effect, but it’s more of an “on-swing” proc. That means there’s a pretty good chance that a Grisha pops up during tense battles, slamming and pouncing on your foes until they’re beaten to a pulp. Summon a Grisha to bulldoze your opponents. Justiciar’s Stone and Gloombound Stone Both the Justiciar’s Stone (gold boost) and Gloombound Stone (gloom boost) are integral Tarstones in the long run. This is primarily due to how much they increase your resource acquisition as you defeat enemies. The Jusiticar’s Stone is in eastern Fainweald, in a cavern just past the Broodshell Swarm encounter. The Gloombound Stone, meanwhile, is in the Stones of Remembrance graveyard just a short distance away. Amass more gold with the Justiciar's Stone. Axatana We consider the Axatana as one of the best weapons in Mortal Shell 2, which is why you should get it early. That said, it’s going to be a long trek: From the Outskirts of Mammon beacon, keep going south and jump across the massive chasm by way of the bone gates. At the opposite side, in the fortress known as Castigator’s Keep, continue going south while avoiding the enemies. These include a couple of annoying Ballista-Head creatures. At the courtyard with the Synaptic Vessel, start heading eastward to the snowy rock outcropping. Keep using the bone gates to jump across the gaps. Eventually, you’ll reach the Forgotten Tower. Shoot the bell underneath the bridge to fully repair the structure. Alternatively, use the other bone gate to make it across. Press the switch next to the metal door and defeat the Dagger Sester, then claim your reward. It's going to be a long trek (left) just to get the Axatana (right), but it's well worth the effort. The Axatana has extremely fast attack animations. Not only does it deal a lot of damage, but the speed itself helps immensely with debuff application. That does it for our guide on the best early-game items in Mortal Shell 2. Don’t forget to check our beginner’s guide if you need help while starting out. Oh, and we also have articles about all weapon locations and all shell locations if you’re excited to venture across the regions of Fainweald and Mammon.

Do you want to know where you should go first in Mortal Shell 2? The lands of Fallgrim, now broken up into multiple regions, remain vast. Setting out on a trek isn’t for the faint of heart given all the challenges along the way. Fortunately, our guide here will walk you through what you can expect within the first few hours of playing the campaign proper--i.e. after the prologue. In This Article Where to go first in FainwealdWidow’s Overlook and Glimpse from pre-cleansed beaconsRevered Gland and Gloombound FlameA quick trek to the south: The Martyr’s Tomb, region map, and Tiel shellHeading to the north: Part 1 - Gorf and gold-farmingHeading to the north: Part 2 - The Proxima shell and another region mapA few odds and ends: Ova, Tarstones, and unlocking fast travel Where to go first in Fainweald Let’s say you’ve already completed Mortal Shell 2’s prologue. That means you’ve checked out everything in Marrow Keep--which acts as your main hub--and you’ve just ridden the elevator to the top of the tower. The side that leads to the Ruins of Mammon region is blocked by corrupted growths, and you don’t want to go there yet anyway. Instead, you should look at the teleporter gate that’s cleared, one that’s overlooking the Fainweald region. That is where you need to go first in Mortal Shell 2. Note: We recommend checking our fast travel beacons and region maps guide so you have an idea about the beacons that you should tag and the maps that you should collect. A map showing some early-game points-of-interest. Widow’s Overlook and Glimpse from pre-cleansed beacons The only beacon that’s active in Fainweald is called Widow’s Overlook. Once you land there like a meteor, enter it to find a Glimpse in the inner chamber. The Glimpse resource is used via the Shellkeeper NPC in Marrow Keep. This improves your bonds with the shells that you unlock. Doing so allows you to select amazing perks, including boosts to active abilities and status effect immunity. The first pre-cleansed beacon that you reach (left) has a Glimpse, the resource needed to increase shell bonds (right). Revered Gland and Gloombound Flame From the Widow’s Overlook beacon, turn around to see a muddy area guarded by the Great Arbiter of Flesh miniboss. You can fight this fella if you’re up to the task, or you can just avoid him and quickly grab some loot: Gloombound Flame - Bring this to the top of the Marrow Keep tower and light the lantern. This lets you change the game’s difficulty. Revered Gland - Use it via a beacon to increase the number of Mether’s Pulse--i.e. healing flask--charges. The Great Arbiter of Flesh is guarding two important items. A quick trek to the south: The Martyr’s Tomb, region map, and Tiel shell From the Great Arbiter of Flesh’s area, keep following the path to the south to spot the following: Fainweald map - Use the air current to get to the top of the rock formation. Tiel shell - Found buried in a shallow grave. This ought to be the first shell that you unlock. You should then utilize Tiel's stealth-based abilities. As an aside, near the miniboss' area, there’s a ruin with a staircase that leads to a dungeon called Martyr’s Tomb. Defeat the enemies down below to claim the Obsidian Lathe and the Parasitic Stone. The former is used for Tarforge upgrades, and the latter is a Tarstone that grants the leech effect--i.e. a temporary increase to your HP--when you kill hostiles. Simply shoot the device above the metal grate to exit the room. Get Tiel immediately (left) and start checking out dungeons (right). Heading to the north: Part 1 - Gorf and gold-farming Now that you have the Tiel shell, where should you go next in Mortal Shell 2? Well, we recommend going on a quick trek to the north this time. Just past the Widow’s Overlook beacon, you’ll see a combat trial. Feel free to eliminate the enemies there to receive the Retribution Stone, which increases riposte damage. From the trial altar, swing to the right to stumble upon a giant frog NPC named Gorf--because, you know, that’s “frog” backward. Anyway, if you give 1x Weltcap to this fella, he’ll hand over a Marsh Pearl. It’s an item that can be sold to a vendor for 2,000x coin. Return to the beacon to reset the world and talk to Gorf again to receive yet another Marsh Pearl. Keep doing this until you have four of the items, which will then cause Gorf to give you a Poisoned Dagger as part of his quest. Note: By now, you should have enough coins to buy the Revered Gland and Synaptic Vessel items from Merrick in Marrow Keep. At this point, you’ll have 5x flask charges with a slight boost to the healed amount. Gorf's true love will cure him of all his ailments. Heading to the north: Part 2 - The Proxima shell and another region map You’re not done yet, though. Just past the trial altar, there’s a rope bridge at the left-hand side. This leads to the second map for the Fainweald region. Next, if you take the other rope bridge--i.e. the one closer to Gorf's location--you’ll come across the Blackridge Pass beacon. Just beyond that, there’s a clearing where Cultists and Treants are fighting. The sloping path to the left has a Spider-Leech enemy guarding a lone beacon. Run past it and enter the structure. Inside, you’ll be able to unlock the Proxima shell. Check the Blackridge Cliffs area (left) to find the Proxima shell (right). A few odds and ends: Ova, Tarstones, and unlocking fast travel So, you’ve got two shells, a couple of beacons and maps, and some upgrades already. Where should you go next in Mortal Shell 2? Now, at this stage, it would be a good idea to grab some additional loot and Ova--i.e. glowing eggs that are required for campaign progression. Blackridge Pass beacon - Cleanse the beacon’s dungeon to obtain 650x coin, 1x Glimpse, and 1x Ovum. Mushroom Village beacon - Cleanse the beacon’s dungeon to obtain the Emberseed Stone, 1x Glimpse, and 1x Ovum. Mushroom Village Gate beacon - Keep heading to the southeastern part of Fainweald to tag this beacon. Then, cleanse its dungeon to obtain the Volatile Fragment, 1x Glimpse, and 3x Ova. Cleansing the Mushroom Village Gate beacon grants you access to the Corrupted Gate major dungeon known as the Glutted Mire. You may also pick up some additional Glimpse near the gateway. Even better, since you’ve picked up at least 5x extra Ova, you can return to Marrow Keep’s ova chamber to siphon the eggs. This unlocks the Mether’s Breath power. With it, you’ll be able to remove corrupted growths in overworld areas. Likewise, you’ll be able to fast travel to the beacons that you’ve tagged. Collect and siphon enough Ova to unlock fast travel. That does it for our guide on where to go first in Mortal Shell 2. From here, you can go ahead and delve into more dungeons, complete the rest of Gorf's quest, check out the Flooded Village, or enter the aforementioned Corrupted Gate. Exoprimal was built using Capcom’s proprietary RE Engine, which is surprising considering the other Capcom games that have used the engine are quite different. The first game to publicly use it was Resident Evil 7: Biohazard, which caused many to dub the engine the Resident Evil engine, which is not what the ‘RE’ stands for. Instead, the acronym is confusingly shorthand for Reach for the Moon. In Japan, the Resident Evil series is called Biohazard, which is why Exoprimal technical director Kazuki Abe is surprised that anyone ever thought the engine was named after the survivor horror series . “It’s really just a matter of coincidence that Resident Evil games are also developed using this engine.” Exoprimal Since Resident Evil 7, RE Engine has been used to develop Resident Evil Village, the Resident Evil 2, 3, and 4 remakes, Devil May Cry 5, Ghosts’ n Goblins Resurrection, Monster Hunter Rise, Capcom Arcade 2nd Stadium, Street Fighter 6, and the multiplayer Resident Evil Re:Verse. It is also the engine used to develop Pragmata, Dragon’s Dogma II, and Onimusha: Way of the Sword. Ghosts 'n Goblins Resurrection Its versatility and flexibility are impressive, thanks to the RE Engine team being in-house at Capcom. It means that if a Capcom game needs the engine to do something specific for the game being made, the team can go directly to the source and have it added. For example, the Onimusha team needed a Dynamic Dismemberment System, so they put in a request to the internal engineers at Capcom. Rather than putting a game in an engine, the RE Engine can pivot and mold around the game based on needs. “If certain components for the game don’t exist in our engine already, the RE Engine team can work to implement those features to allow those things to become possible,” Abe says. Resident Evil Village The Exoprimal team was less confident about where the RE Engine logo came from, which features a creepy, slightly misshapen hand reaching for the sky, silhouetted in front of the moon. “The idea of the orientation logo with the hand reaching up is the idea that as an engine, the game that you envision, creating the dream that you have for game development, is just within reach,” Abe says. “And by reaching out with this engine, you can grab it, reach it, and make it happen.
