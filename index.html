The game, originally developed by Quintet and published by Enix (in Japan) and Nintendo (PAL), is an action RPG and the third entry in the developer’s unofficial “Soul Blazer trilogy”, following 1992’s Soul Blazer and 1993’s Illusion of Gaia. The story centers on a boy named Ark, and follows his generations-spanning quest to resurrect life on Earth after the planet’s decimation in an apocalyptic battle between divine forces. Terranigma is often acclaimed as a masterpiece for its storytelling, soundtrack, and fast-paced gameplay. Developer Clear River Games helms this modern release and states in a press release that the re-release features “a host of upgrades, tweaks and bonus features, designed to make the best, better still and provide an ideal point of entry for modern gamers.” The studio does not specify what these upgrades are, but has enlisted the help of Terranigma’s original artist, Kamui Fujiwara, to create new in-game designs and key art, and to offer feedback on the re-release’s production.“30 years ago, Terranigma was released on the SNES,” Fujiwara writes in a press release. “Now, at long last it will be available once again on console and PC, digital and physical. It may not be exactly the same as it was back then, but I'm sincerely delighted about it making its return to the current gaming environment. During the original's development, I was involved with designing not only the characters, but the world too, and even some of the game settings as well. I hope the themes of god and humanity, creation and destruction, and the atmosphere of the world itself resonate with today's players too. Not as a relic of the past, but as a living, breathing work. This moment has been long awaited and now it is finally here. I hope you will enjoy it with all your heart.”Terranigma is coming to PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 2, PlayStation 4, Xbox One, Switch, and PC in early 2027.

This isn't your granddaddy's smoke. Far from being static, this smoke interacts with its environment, moving and morphing as players run, shoot, and slide through it. Explosions will disperse the smoke, too. This opens up all kinds of tactical possibilities, both offensively and defensively. Attackers can use it to block sightlines while still making temporary kill holes by shooting through the smoke. It's a dynamic that works on defense as well. Throwing a smoke grenade and seeing where bullets are opening up holes in the smog gives you more information on where hostiles are coming from. Being able to disperse smoke with bullets and grenades also presents players with decisions to make in the middle of a firefight: Do you shoot through the smoke to try and clear sightlines but give away your position? Do you throw a grenade and try to disperse the smoke? Or do you wait patiently and try to take full advantage of the shroud the smoke provides? These moment-to-moment gameplay questions are present to a lesser extent in previous Call of Duty games, but this new volumetric smoke, and all the ways players can interact with it, is a small game changer. It's an addition I would love to see Infinity Ward double down on and design more of the game around. It makes Modern Warfare 4's objective modes in particular more engaging, with more choices when it comes to attacking enemy capture points or positions. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yaNr1hHAg2M It's tactical and clever in a way that most of Modern Warfare 4's multiplayer isn't, and strangely enough, feels at odds with the overall speed and pace of this year's Call of Duty. One match of the beta is all it takes to reveal players are jump firing and sliding around the battlefield like they're pilots from Titanfall. Infinity Ward's new volumetric smoke, for all its impressiveness, feels a little out of place as a result. In Modern Warfare 4, smoke, while visually impressive and extremely cool, feels at odds with the rest of Infinity Ward's fast-paced vision. As it stands, Modern Warfare 4's new volumetric smoke is one of the few aspects of this year's game that feels new to the series and stands out. There's still more beta, and the full game, to play, so it remains to be seen how useful or powerful Infinity Ward's new smoke tech will be post-launch. Nonetheless, it's a cool addition for a franchise that too often feels the same year after year, and one I hope Infinity Ward does more with in the future.

Blood Dungeon Review - Skilled Ugly ChaosNEW
Reviewed on: PC ...
Reviewed on: PC Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch, PC Publisher: Messhof Developer: Messhof Release: August 25, 2026 Rating: Mature Talking about Blood Dungeon is impossible without making at least some reference to Vampire Survivors. Blood Dungeon is clearly born from Vampire Survivors’ undeniable loop and sense of progression, but developer Messhof addresses arguably the primary shortcoming of the 2022 Bullet Heaven game (I think that’s what we’ve agreed to call these, right?): movement. Moving in Blood Dungeon is fun and it sets the game apart within the genre and demands enjoyable skill from the player, but some balance annoyances keep the game from soaring. Blood Dungeon is a functional platformer before you are even awarded the first upgrade to your starting weapon. The nine characters play similarly (though there are two that feel fairly different), and they leap with ease, can climb any wall or ceiling, clamber along fences, and even swim. If there weren’t sometimes hundreds of enemies on screen, I can confidently say that Blood Dungeon would probably work in the platformer genre.Despite the quality platforming, however, Blood Dungeon is more about powering up and staying alive. Like Vampire Survivors, you defeat enemies, collect the titular blood, level up your weapons, acquire new ones, and choose between a series of passive upgrades that appear randomly. If you’ve played a game in this genre, you know what to expect, and it does the style well. Some upgrades feel useless, but many more are easy selections when they appear in the roulette. A few, however, feel required. I don’t know that I ever beat a final boss without the basketball (which sends a flurry of orange balls bouncing around the environment) or the upgrade that doubles everything. I hit a point where I was rerolling frequently to get those two upgrades, and when I couldn’t, it often led to disappointing failure. I would have preferred the weapons to feel similarly powerful, even if they all played differently, which I recognize is a difficult balancing act.Blood Dungeon is also surprisingly challenging, which has its pros and cons. The genre is successful at rewarding players who make smart decisions more than their ability to dodge enemies. The power fantasy is staggering at the end of a great run in a Bullet Heaven game, but I never quite felt all-powerful here, even when I successfully defeated the three bosses of any given level (which is how you beat a level). That’s the con, but the pro is that Blood Dungeon is a much more active and intense game, and when your platforming skills are what save you more than randomly getting the basketball, that is rewarding. Bones awarded at the end of each run can be used for permanent upgrades, which is great; I eagerly invested in the elements of my characters I wanted to improve, but I hit a wall earlier than I expected. I ran out of upgrades to buy, and the requirements to access the few unlockable upgrades were unclear. This led me to amass a large bank of unspent bones while I hoped I'd triggered something to unlock the other purchasable upgrades, which felt random rather than rewarding.The final element of Blood Dungeon that sets it apart from comparable games is its art style, which I don’t love. The characters are purposefully ugly, and it all feels intentionally gross. The fart cloud, which casts a very useful periodic cloud of poison, for example, is an upgrade I would sometimes avoid just because I wasn’t eager to hear my character fart every few seconds. But all that said, it is undeniably unique and perfectly fits the game’s overall mood and tone. The style just isn’t going to work for everyone, and I happen to technically be an everyone.Blood Dungeon is a refreshing take on the genre Vampire Survivors popularized only four years ago, and it effectively establishes its own identity both with its art style and platformer-focused mentality. It is fun to play, whereas many of these games are ultimately fun to experience passively – a build to the point where you can put the controller down and watch chaos blaze across the screen. You can’t ever put the controller down in Blood Dungeon, and that’s why it’s so easy to spend your bones (if you haven’t already purchased everything) and jump right back in for another run.
