Far from describing a game with adventure in it (that would include a lot of games!), the adventure genre is loosely defined as one that emphasizes narrative and exploration over action, which may also rely on light puzzle mechanics. In other words, a game that doesn’t have enough simulation to be a sim, action to be an action title, puzzle-solving to be a puzzle game, and… you get the idea. Despite the nebulous classification, games in this genre are unambiguously incredible experiences, often exploring refreshing and fascinating concepts. Shooting for games that are rolling around in the zeitgeist right now, some of the genres’ towering giants are not listed here. However, for anyone looking to check out today’s great experiences, feel free to peruse our list of the best adventure games to play right now. 1000xResist PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch, PC When we say a game's writing is good, we're usually comparing it to other video games, but 1000xResist transcends even that categorization as an example of fantastic writing, full stop. It's set in a post-apocalyptic world where everyone is a clone of the same person – the Allmother, whom they have an almost religious reverence for. You play as Watcher, one tasked with communing with your fellow sisters to learn about the world. In doing so, you live through the past, observing the Allmother's teenage life, the events that led the world to fall apart, and the fractures within the surreal colony you live within. With complex, flawed characters, a rich, layered world, a plot that goes places you couldn't expect, and gorgeous, poetic dialogue, it's an easy game to recommend, and it's one that's raised the bar for the narrative games that follow. | Game Informer's 1000xResist Review And Roger Switch, PC And Roger is not a traditional adventure game, but it belongs in the genre thanks to the way the player interacts with the world and solves its puzzles. It recalls games like 2018’s Florence and even WarioWare, oddly, but without the rigid timers of Nintendo’s bizarre microgame collections. Over the span of three succinct chapters, And Roger tells the tumultuous tale of a girl scared and confused by the presence of a mysterious man in her home. Who is the person? Why does he seem to know her even though he’s a stranger? What happened to the girl’s father? The narrative is effective in that it’s an ordinary tale grounded in a tragically relatable core, but it is ultimately an affecting tale of love, patience, and forgiveness. | Game Informer’s And Roger Review Dispatch PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 2, Switch, PC Dispatch picks up the pieces of Telltale’s TV-series-like approach to video games and takes it a step further. The result is a game that’s basically a TV series with some light interaction, and it rules. It helps that the original conceit of Dispatch was an animated series, and that pedigree is on full display in the game, but dialogue choices, critical story-changing moments, and a fun superhero dispatching minigame make it an even better experience. Mecha Man suit user Robert Robertson is returned to a life of normalcy when his suit is trashed by villains and placed in control of a dispatch operation for this universe’s Suicide Squad equivalent. Across the game’s 6 to 10 hours, he begins to care for this group of misfits, and they care for him in turn, turning a charming workplace comedy into a high-stakes family drama in its final hours. With an all-star voice cast, which includes Aaron Paul, Laura Bailey, Jeffrey Wright, Matthew Mercer, Travis Willingham, Erin Yvette, and more, Dispatch feels like a Hollywood-style adventure game that understands why this is one of gaming’s oldest (and best) genres. | Game Informer’s Dispatch Review Emio – The Smiling Man: Famicom Detective Club Switch Famicom Detective Club's roots in the adventure genre run deep, back to 1988's The Missing Heir. These adventures had players solving mysteries through basic text menus, inputs, and choices: look around an area, decide what to look at, and who to talk to. Emio – The Smiling Man picks up right where those games left off with its 2024 revival, as you investigate a string of deaths tied to the mysterious "Smiling Man" legend. It's dark and tense, with moments of sheer tension and thrills you wouldn't always expect in a Nintendo game. But Emio's core, beneath the twisting mystery, is its town and setting. This is perfect for someone who wants a small-town tale with compelling characters and outstanding atmosphere. Indika PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch There’s nothing worse than being a nun who wants to be devout to her faith only for Satan to come along, personally whisper in your ear, and cause you to embark on a surreal odyssey with a prisoner that will have you running from bloodthirsty wolves, operating heavy machinery in winter, and looking at your past in new and revealing ways; at least if your name is Indika. Set in 19th-century Russia, Indika is an outcast who just wants to feel like a normal nun, but the aforementioned Satan invades her mind, and while he’s not as mischievous as other versions of the fallen angel, he’s a powerful storytelling device that serves as a catalyst for the questions the game serves up about morality, sin, faith, and doggedness. The surrounding third-person adventure game will have you solving environmental puzzles, engaging with 16-bit platformer flashbacks, and navigating through hellacious hallucinations as you search for a remedy to your devilish ailment.
