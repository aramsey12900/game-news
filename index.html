Across the presets, you can expect support for upscaling technology like Nvidia DLSS 4.5, AMD FSR 4, and Intel XeSS 3.0T, which will ease the burden on your PC. Fire Emblem: Fortune’s Weave – Which Flame Lord Should You Pick First?NEW
Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave is a sprawling tale composed of four different interconnected heroes, each with their own story campaign. These can be played in any order, and the choice might seem daunt...
Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave is a sprawling tale composed of four different interconnected heroes, each with their own story campaign. These can be played in any order, and the choice might seem daunting. Which hero is the best to start your journey with? The simple answer is that you can begin with any hero you choose, but if you want to make an informed choice about which one might work best for your tastes, we can help. After a brief prologue, you're taken to the Obelisk Chamber, where you'll see the obelisks to the four Flame Lords. Those are your passageways into their individual stories. What sets the Flame Lords apart are their special questlines, their Blaze Arts, and their surrounding cast. While you have a great deal of flexibility in recruiting new soldiers to your side and steering your party members in any direction, they are mostly built to excel at particular class types and that will define the type of parties you build with each.  "Kindhearted Boy from Ribeira Village: Cai" The Starter Option: Cai You can think of Cai as the starter character. His special quests grant renown a little more quickly than the others, and so he has an easier time recruiting party members. His Blaze Art is one of the most powerful, an area-of-effect attack that can hit multiple enemies at once, which is a rarity in Fortune's Weave. And his map is more restricted than any of the other Flame Lords, letting you get a feel for how map exploration works without it becoming too dauntingly large and complex.  Cai's story is similarly made to ease you into the world. Cai is just a kid, so he's not involved in the complex politics of Dagda and the surrounding regions. His goal in the Heroic Games is to free his father, who has been falsely imprisoned. So during his path you'll meet other characters and get a general sense of the sequence of events during the Heroic Games, but it's all very digestible.  Thus, both from a mechanical and story perspective, choose Cai if you want to ease into Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave.  "Elegant Battle-Ready Swordsman: Dietrich" For Combat Lovers: Dietrich Dietrich is a bit more complex, and aimed at getting in reps with the combat system. His special quest is all about finding formidable opponents to fight, which means you can gain lots of combat experience (and valuable experience points) while also getting renown for recruitment. His Blaze Art emphasizes movement and positioning, allowing him to close gaps quickly on an opponent. He can also learn a wide variety of Combat Arts, giving him a ton of combat versatility. And his map opens up fully to let him find faraway opponents to fight, so it will feel like an expansion if you played Cai's story first. He can also summon enemies to fight close to his current location, so you're never far from a battle with Dietrich. Dietrich's story is also relatively simple. His sword needs blood, so he needs strong opponents and monsters to fight to feed it. There are pieces of political intrigue in his story, as in all the Flame Lords stories, but his personal motivation is plainly just to fight the biggest, baddest people around. That makes Dietrich ideal for those who love Fire Emblem combat, or who want to get lots of combat reps in.  "Queen of the Saramis Kingdom: Theodora" Best For Vets: Theodora  Theodora is the most complex of the combat-focused characters. Her special quest involves scouring the world for supplies to send back home in order to gain renown, so you'll need to do a lot of map exploration and more careful time management to boost your numbers. She also has exclusive access to Gambits, which allows you to assign squadrons of fighters to specific types of units. You can also send recruits out on scouting missions for supplies. Her Blaze Art is straightforward by comparison--she just grabs a gigantic chunk of rock and chucks it across the battlefield, giving her unparalleled attack range. That's partly because, broadly speaking, Theodora and her companions lean the most toward tank-focus, making for slower movement across the battlefield.  Theodora's story is also among the most complex. As a queen of a neighboring nation, she has the most personal stake in the political machinations of Dagsion, and her story includes lots of proper nouns and discussions of past conflicts.  All of this means Theodora is best for Fire Emblem veterans, or those who just want to jump in with both feet. "Musician Out for Revenge: Leda" For Support Players: Leda Finally, Leda is a very different type of character, with an emphasis on support. Her Blaze Arts are a series of dances that grant passive benefits to allies, and her retinue of surrounding cast members are more slanted toward spell-slingers than regular combat units. Her special quest involves traveling the world to perform at different taverns, so you'll need to uncover a lot of the map to find your way to all of the locations. She can also get insider tips on secret spots of the map that only she can access.  Leda saw her father slaughtered in front of her and made it her life's mission to find the man who killed him and exact revenge. Her story is therefore extremely personal, and while she witnesses some of the political intrigue in and around Dagsion, she doesn't really care about it beyond how it impacts her quest for vengeance. She practically floats outside of the main events of the story because she's so single-minded to her goal. Leda is best if you want to play support and experience a more personal story. Eshmel, the Pale Raven Or, Just Jump Around So in broad terms, the characters are essentially ordered left-to-right to give you a smooth path through the game, if you take them in that order: Cai to ease you into the world and mechanics, Dietrich to get a lot of combat experience, Theodora to build on the others' combat and story complexity, and Leda to round it out with support and a more personal story. However, all of the paths start on the easier side and slowly ramp up, so you won't feel too overwhelmed taking on any of them first. You should pick whichever Flame Lord interests you the most. And you can always switch to a different Flame Lord at almost any point, so there's no reason you can't hop around or even play their chapters one at a time for each.

I got to experience WoW: Forever's new Skyborne elf starting zone with the control scheme during a hands-on demo at BlizzCon 2026. A prompt outlining the controls noted controller support was in an "alpha" state, but despite supposedly still being in early development, it worked surprisingly well. That's a first for Blizzard's MMORPG. Though Blizzard has added barebones support for controllers in recent years, an entirely mouse-and-keyboard-free setup relies on unofficial, player-made add-ons like ConsolePort to deliver a true console-like experience. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=uQuKE0KDgiM From what I played at BlizzCon, Blizzard adding a fully featured controller option could be a game-changer. Despite having played WoW on a mouse and keyboard for 22 years, I was shocked at how natural it felt. Cycling through friendly or enemy NPCs is done with the shoulder buttons. Interacting and jumping are done with the face buttons. Abilities and spells are bound to both the d-pad and the face buttons, with additional keybinds accessible by pressing one or both triggers. Each trigger provides access to eight keybinds, with another eight from holding down both triggers at the same time. Combined with the d-pad, you have 28 buttons at your fingertips.  That sounds like a lot of buttons, but in some scenarios, particularly in WoW Classic or WoW: Forever, it might not be enough. Certain classes, particularly spellcasters, are notorious for having access to an almost comical number of skills and abilities in WoW Classic. This is largely due to many spells having multiple ranks, with lower ranked versions doing less damage or healing but also costing less mana. That makes them still useful in certain situations, but definitely clutters up one's action bars. Outside of the actual basics of using combat abilities and talking to NPCs, WoW: Forever's controller support felt a little more cumbersome. On keyboard and mouse, opening your inventory is as easy as hitting the "b" key and then clicking what you want. On a controller, it requires bringing up a radial menu, sliding over to the inventory section, and then cycling through all the items in your bag until you reach the one you're looking for. Considering players can have dozens of items in their bags at once, trying to select what you want definitely feels slower and less straightforward than is ideal. https://twitter.com/JezCorden/status/2099219123922182558?ref_src=twsrc%5Etfw%7Ctwcamp%5Etweetembed%7Ctwterm%5E2099219123922182558%7Ctwgr%5E53ac5f169755e08f4ae3f2b1fe7853e8398273a2%7Ctwcon%5Es1_c10&ref_url=https%3A%2F%2Fwww.wowhead.com%2Fforever%2Fnews%2Fworld-of-warcraft-forever-will-have-full-controller-support-382873 Ultimately, full controller support for WoW: Forever might be more suited for some classes or particular playstyles over others. The modern "retail" version of WoW, currently in the middle of its most recent Midnight expansion, would arguably benefit from controller support even more than WoW Classic or Forever. Modern WoW generally has fewer buttons to press and less inventory management to do, and I could see this controller setup working wonders for those who want to run some delves on their couch or casually complete world quests. Blizzard's full plans for WoW controller support aren't currently clear. The demo labeled controller support as an alpha, and Blizzard announced during the show Forever will be fully playable with a controller come launch on November 4. It made no announcements regarding if the feature will eventually come to retail, though considering how much DNA is now being shared between the multiple versions of WoW, it seems safe to assume it will. GameSpot has reached out to Blizzard for comment and clarification about its plans for controller support, and will update this post if we hear back. The elephant in the room is whether Blizzard's controller initiative will pave the way for an eventual console version. Blizzard is, of course, currently owned by Microsoft, the owner of Xbox. Fans have speculated given WoW: Midnight's user-made add-on restrictions (something that will carry over into Forever) and its pruning of many class abilities last year that Blizzard was setting the table for a console version. It wouldn't be too shocking to see WoW at long last make its debut on Xbox, though Blizzard made clear last year that if it were making a console version, it would tell fans about it. Handheld PCs, including Xbox-branded ones, are also more popular than ever before, and WoW receiving full controller support would open the way for the MMO to be officially playable on those devices as well. Regardless of whether a console version eventually happens, having a fully functioning controller option still feels long overdue from a simple accessibility standpoint on PC. Seeing that Blizzard is finally hard at work delivering one is nothing but good news. From what I played, it's simple, intuitive, and perhaps even easier in some ways than playing on mouse and keyboard, though I'm sure there are scenarios, like inventory management, where it might leave something to be desired. Whatever Blizzard's full plans for controller support are, it can't come soon enough.
