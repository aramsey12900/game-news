The scale of characters, depth of locations, and overall scope of Link’s world is seemingly identical to the Nintendo 64 original. Monsters might be newly positioned and items could be gained in different ways, but it’s clear that the gameplay experience fans are intimately familiar with is being preserved. It’s just a shame that our Hero of Time doesn’t look like he belongs in this world at all thanks to his cumbersome design.  There is no denying that Young Link is cute, yet he also feels strangely overdesigned as a way to flaunt both the best and worst parts of this new artistic direction. This lack of expression worked in the Nintendo 64 original because of the technical limitations at the time, but future versions of Link from Wind Waker, Twilight Princess, and Breath of the Wild understood that even if our hero doesn’t speak, he still needs to act like a human being … or rather, a Hylian. But older Link looks great, and so do major supporting cast members like Zelda, Ganondorf, and Sheik; so it stands to reason that Link simply possesses way too much visual fidelity for his own good. The presence of voice acting doesn’t help either, as I’m already picturing Link staring dead-eyed as emotional scenes unfold around him.  The remake is only a couple of months away, and I’m sure we’ll receive a more detailed look at gameplay before November, but I hate that my first impression is dominated by the tragic fact that a relatable child of the forest who once propelled my childhood fantasies now feels like an unfortunate intruder.

We've been waiting for new details surrounding the upcoming movie based on the Legend of Zelda franchise, and during today's Legend of Zelda 40th anniversary Nintendo Direct, many were expecting a new trailer. Instead, we learned of the official title and received some first narrative and thematic hints from series creator Shigeru Miyamoto.The movie is simply called The Legend of Zelda, with no subtitle nor any qualifier that it is a movie, as has been seen in The Super Mario Bros. Movie or The Super Mario Galaxy Movie. According to Shigeru Miyamoto, the name not containing any subtitle will make sense once you see it. "This is an original film based on the many stories, ideas, and concepts of the series, built up through the games for 40 years," he said during the Nintendo Direct livestream. "Through the creative power and visual expertise of the talented production team, it's become an artistic work worthy of the Legend of Zelda series. Both the development team from Nintendo and I are also involved, of course." To this point, we only know a few castings, including Benjamin Evan Ainsworth as Link, Bo Bragason as Zelda, Uli Latufeku as Ganondorf, and Dichen Lachman as Impa, as well as other actors who aren't officially attached to characters. Outside of that, fans have gleaned small details and potential plot points through leaked set photos that have since been deleted. Though he didn't mention any characters in the movie, Miyamoto hinted that the Triforce will play a role in the film. "When we talk about The Legend of Zelda, there's the Triforce," he said. "What role do you think it will play in the film? We'll share more details at a later time, so please look forward to it!"Much like the two Mario animated movies from the last few years, we can likely expect a Nintendo Direct dedicated to the film's first trailer. The Legend of Zelda hits theaters on April 30, 2027.
