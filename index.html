Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 Review - Silver BulletNEW
Reviewed on: PC ...
Reviewed on: PC Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC Publisher: Activision Developer: Infinity Ward Release: October 23, 2026 Rating: Mature Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 arrives at a crossroads for the series, thanks in large part to the inconsistent quality of recent entries. With a shaky predecessor, Infinity Ward had a lot to repair and conclude, especially with 4 set to bookend the rebooted Modern Warfare series. Fortunately, backed up by a fun campaign, thrilling multiplayer, and loads of new modes and content, Modern Warfare 4 makes up for the past and then some. Modern Warfare 4 continues after the events of 2023’s Modern Warfare 3, with Captain Price hellbent on stopping Makarov and his higher-ups. The story features shifting perspectives, from a new group of ragtag Korean soldiers to familiar characters like Gaz. The character dynamics harken back to the original trilogy, especially in the boots-on-the-ground missions with the South Korean unit, led by Park (played by Beef actor Young Mazino).For a series that, at least recently, has been more spectacle than story, giving the stakes the time they deserve is nice. While it moves away from the stealthy and modernized elements, it makes sense for the matters at hand. The majority of Modern Warfare 4’s attempts at wrapping up the overarching plot of the rebooted series works, but it rushes through some character arcs and story beats in uneven fashion. Furthermore, much of the game’s cast, especially Mads Mikkelson’s Archer and Prisca Kim’s Ri Ju-Young, are underutilized, which is a shame since they both deliver great performances.  Despite the shortcomings of the campaign, Call of Duty's bread and butter, multiplayer, still delivers. While it doesn’t have the fluidity of Black Ops’ newer entries, the customization and design of weapons and maps make it worth digging into. From small, intimate shooting galleries with Mumbai and Nautical to wide free-for-alls like Coal and Reactor U92, the range helps maintain excitement, even if it gets repetitive over time. The variety of multiplayer modes keeps things fresh on the battlefield. Returning modes like standard Team Deathmatch and Kill Confirmed continue that enjoyable competitive flair that made them the stars that they are. However, new modes like Combat Outpost and Hijack fit right into the already great lineup. In particular, Combat Outpost upscales your typical 6v6 player count to 24v24, leading to more action and teamwork. Hanging onto a team’s tank, shooting enemies from afar, and taking back outposts is exhilarating, even if it can all go bad moments later by a well-placed RPG shot.Even after hours of gameplay, the variety of options to craft your perfect gun is impressive. Figuring out the best scope, ammo, stock, and muzzle, as well as the advantages and disadvantages of them, is a fun way of customizing your playstyle. Gunny, a mechanic that helps optimize your gun for certain styles, can help with decision paralysis, but I rarely used it. Not many FPSs go that far and Modern Warfare 4’s commitment to the feature makes it stand out. The newest mode, DMZ, impresses out of the gate. After a few flat attempts in the past, this is the definitive Call of Duty extraction shooter, whether you play solo, or in duos or trios. Immediately, you’re thrust into the wide open world of Hajin and left to fend for yourselves with what you have and what you can gather and extract. Fans of Arc Raiders and Marathon should feel right at home as the gameplay borrows from the typical extraction shooter experience. Intense, action-packed moments keep you coming back after every win or loss. And the excellent gunplay and level design elevate it into something worthwhile, no matter how you play.  Despite not encountering many live players during my time in Hajin in DMZ, it still proves challenging, even for seasoned pros. The world is not your friend, and taking risks can come at a high cost. A good team, as well as a thoughtful loadout and traits, are required to survive. The unpredictability of it all keeps you on your toes, making for an exciting addition to the already strong multiplayer suite.Regardless of the mode, Modern Warfare 4 is one of the best-looking Call of Duty titles to date. New York and Paris are lifelike, and it truly embodies the realistic, modern aesthetic the series has always aimed to achieve. However, I encountered a few technical hiccups, especially in the campaign. Slow framerate plagues some high-octane scenes and texture loading is frequent across the board. These distractions are far from game-breaking, but enough to be noticeable. While the plot execution is a bit shaky, engaging multiplayer options, including DMZ, aptly demonstrate that Infinity Ward can still deliver compelling military-FPS experiences. Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 is a strong return to form that serves as a solid sendoff for the rebooted subseries. Score: 8.5 About Game Informer's review system

Call Of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 Tips For MultiplayerNEW
We went hands-on with Modern Warfare 4 early, and while it will feel familiar to many Call of Duty veterans, Infinity Ward has updated some of the movement mechanics and features from recent years. In...
We went hands-on with Modern Warfare 4 early, and while it will feel familiar to many Call of Duty veterans, Infinity Ward has updated some of the movement mechanics and features from recent years. In this guide, we'll provide some of the best tips to help you score wins in multiplayer when Modern Warfare 4 launches on October 23. Practice the new movement Modern Warfare 4 overhauls the movement from the last two years of Treyarch's Black Ops games. This game strips away the fancy omnimovement, but Infinity Ward has reworked the mantling system, refined the sliding mechanic, and added a way to climb up pipes. The best way to get practice with Modern Warfare 4's movement system is to play the game's new mobility course, which is found in the multiplayer playlist menu. It includes a series of three difficulties, including tutorials for beginners, intermediate, and experts. There are rewards for completing the mobility course as well. Without omnimovement, double jumps into windows and other high places are no longer possible. However, you can climb up pipes to reach otherwise inaccessible areas. These specific pipes are copper in color and stand out fairly easily. These are found on standard multiplayer maps, Kill Block, and even Modern Warfare 4's Zodiac map for Warzone. Hipfire is your friend Infinity Ward removed bloom from guns this year, which is a mechanic that previously caused bullet spread when firing from the hip, meaning some bullets did not hit their target. Hipfiring is more accurate in Modern Warfare 4, so you don't always need to aim down sights with your gun. If someone is sliding at you from around a corner, or perhaps you just find yourself in a close-range engagement. Don't waste time aiming. Just hipfire your gun. Adjust your settings Modern Warfare 4 has plenty of customization and accessibility settings. These include options to help make the game's movement easier, refine your aiming, and help with issues like Tinnitus or motion sickness. Additionally, Modern Warfare 4 defaults to some cinematic-style visual settings that aren't great for multiplayer. Disabling the settings for motion blur and depth of field will greatly help with the game's visibility. If you're on console, we have a recommended settings guide for getting the most out of Modern Warfare 4 on PlayStation or Xbox consoles. Build custom loadouts MW4 Gunsmith feature The base guns in Call of Duty always feel better to use with a good set of attachments equipped. This year's Gunsmith will let you fully customize your guns, and it will recommend a few builds with the new Gunny bot system. This isn't an AI chat bot, but instead it cycles through preset builds from the developer. These weapon builds are decent, but I find it doesn't always recommend the absolute best attachments. You also don't have Gunny unlocked until you rank up a few levels. Try your Apex attachments Modern Warfare 4's adds a new weapon attachment type called Apex attachments. Every primary and secondary weapon in Modern Warfare 4 has one Apex attachment. These are special add-ons you can unlock for each gun, which will drastically change how the weapon is used. Apex attachments are similar to the weapon conversion kits found in the recent Call of Duty games, but in Modern Warfare 4 they don't cost one of your attachment slots. You can have a five-attachment build and still equip an Apex attachment, so there's no reason why you shouldn't test out some of these unique conversions. Some of these special attachments sound a little outlandish, with one adding the option to fire throwing knives or another that adds a mounted strobe light, but some of them will likely be powerful options in multiplayer. For example, the Oden's Apex attachment turns the assault rifle into a powerful and precise semi-auto sniper rifle. Apex attachments aren't unlocked until you've reached the maximum weapon level for a specific gun. Leveling up guns can feel slow in the beginning, but Call of Duty often offers double XP weekends, or you can always make use of any double weapon XP tokens you might have saved up. Navigating Kill Block Kill Block map with marked quadrants Kill Block is a Gunfight map designed as a unique modular battleground where the map's design reconfigures every match, creating hundreds of potential map combinations. It can be harder to give callouts or orientate yourself on a constantly changing map, but the outer edges of Kill Block are labeled as quadrants. Look for the grid's letters and numbers labelled along the outside wall for help with callouts. Communicate and have awareness Sound is an important element in Call of Duty's multiplayer You'll want to use a headset when possible to gain precious intel from the game's audio cues. This helps with increasing map awareness, which includes hearing enemy footsteps better and being able to pinpoint their positioning. Consider using a mic when you can. Multiplayer is a team-based game for almost all game modes, so having a headset with a mic is also recommended. This can feel especially important when you're playing objective-based modes, so you can help provide callouts or talk strategy.  However, we also know Call of Duty lobbies can be toxic. If you don't want to talk to others, Modern Warfare 4 does feature a decent ping system with the D-pad. This lets you ping enemies and important map locations to provide your team with some basic intel. The duty is better with friends Partying up with friends can increase your odds of winning. It's good to have reliable teammates watching your back and doing callouts with you, and hopefully they are willing to play the objective or provide intel. Try to party up with at least one or two friends to give yourself better odds of victory. If you're looking to jump into the story, make sure to check out our Modern Warfare 4 campaign guide to learn how long it will take to beat.

CoD: Modern Warfare 4 Has a Thrilling Campaign, Exciting DMZ, Strong MultiplayerNEW
In 2019, Infinity Ward rebooted Call of Duty's Modern Warfare series, delivering a gritty and polished campaign featuring Captain Price and the assembly of the iconic Task Force 141. Despite the stron...
In 2019, Infinity Ward rebooted Call of Duty's Modern Warfare series, delivering a gritty and polished campaign featuring Captain Price and the assembly of the iconic Task Force 141. Despite the strong start, the series' sequels stumbled a bit, with  Modern Warfare 3 in particular standing out as having a weak story and open-world missions that missed the mark. But with Modern Warfare 4, Infinity Ward returns the series' campaign to strength with a satisfying narrative and well-crafted missions for an epic conclusion to the rebooted series. Modern Warfare 4's single-player goes back to being a traditional and mostly linear experience that some recent entries have strayed from. There is only one open-world mission this year, and it is far more engaging than the previous attempts. Modern Warfare 4 doesn't introduce anything new or innovative, but it finds success with a consistently strong narrative and engaging gameplay built on nail-biting tension. Thankfully, you're never forced into any mission objectives that feel like silly chores or ill-timed distractions. Every mission in Modern Warfare 4 carries urgency and drives the plot forward. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=HM5tzrBuWFo That plot closes out the ongoing narrative surrounding Price and Task Force 141, but it also takes Call of Duty to Korea and introduces a fresh new cast of characters. The campaign intertwines two seemingly separate stories for an over-the-top, globetrotting affair across New York City, Paris, and Mumbai, in addition to several parts of Korea. This is the explosive Hollywood-style action you'd expect from a Call of Duty campaign at a quality level we haven't seen from the franchise in years--that includes the recent Black Ops entries. One half of the game places you in the boots of Private Park, a young South Korean soldier, who has never experienced real combat before. Private Park and his squad are forced to fight back to save their country when North Korea launches an invasion. The other half of the campaign follows the events of Modern Warfare 3 and the aftermath of John "Soap" MacTavish's death. In Modern Warfare 4, Captain Price is in a much darker place, going rogue to hunt down Makarov and also tie up any other loose ends with the villain's associates. This new outlaw version of Price ties back to one of the most-telling quotes of Modern Warfare 2019, where he lays out his ethos on where the lines in war are."You draw the line wherever you need it, Sergeant," Price says. "End of the day, someone has to make the enemy scared of the dark. We get dirty and the world stays clean. That's the mission." That line is seemingly in a place no one could have foreseen, as Price is furious and more willing than ever to get his hands dirty in pursuit of making everyone allied with Makarov scared of the dark. Being on the run leaves Price with very few allies, though. In a desperate attempt to reach the Archer (Mads Mikkelsen), who is another threat linked to Makarov, Price makes a deal with Valeria Garza and the cartel. Price hasn't turned into a complete monster, but the cartel don't mind blasting through civilians in order to achieve their mission. This team-up makes for chaotic gunfights through Paris and engaging banter between the characters. Price is constantly trying to look out for civilians and wants to minimize the collateral damage, but Valeria just comically taunts him, calling him Papi and old man, as she operates with less strict rules. The extent to which Price is a contrast to Valeria becomes a key part of the story. After a chase scene where they commit a particularly brutal act of violence, Valeria tells Price that "he's still a good guy," but she is a notorious cartel leader trying to get paid for delivering a weapon to the bad guy, so how much weight can her words really carry? Price's cartel team-up also leads him to realize this weapon delivery is dangerous enough to shift the balance of power, setting in motion a much greater conflict. To make matters worse, Price also has a target on his back as the remaining members of his Task Force 141 team, Simon "Ghost" Riley and Kyle "Gaz" Garrick, are sent to capture or kill him. This manhunt for Price leads to a standout mission called Gone Dark, where your POV switches to Gaz as he hunts Price inside of a dark hotel. At the start of the series, Captain Price served as a mentor and moral compass for Gaz, so there's a lot of emotional weight to the mission as Gaz, and the player, see just how far Price has fallen from a clearer third-party perspective. There are shocking moments and intense exchanges between the now fractured members of Task Force 141 that leverage the squad's long history effectively. There's an emotional pull throughout the mission, as Price begs Ghost and Gaz to back off, and a bigger gut punch when Price says he doesn't want to kill them, but implies he will. These moments really nail down how Price's moral compass has broken. The events of Gone Dark also set up a powerful moment in a later mission to bring the series full circle in a way I appreciated. The Korean invasion intertwines with Price's epic revenge tour, causing the new South Korean squad and US forces to team up. Although that may seem overwhelming, even with these shifting POVs and a non-chronological story, the narrative flows well from mission to mission, keeping things moving at a good clip through stealth missions, on-foot and vehicular chase sequences, slow-motion breach-and-clear segments, and even some satisfying action of manning a chopper gunner. There's also a Clean House-style mission with night-vision goggles, where you need to clear rooms with extreme trigger discipline to avoid shooting any civilians.  Modern Warfare 4 Although the missions are a mostly familiar affair, they remain effective Call of Duty staples and the  campaign as a whole had me on the edge of my seat throughout. It doesn't necessarily do anything better than some of Call of Duty's most notable other campaigns, but it's proof that, for me, the series doesn't need to reinvent the wheel to produce a thrilling single-player experience. In this instance, having a strong narrative and compelling characters carried me through the missions. Whether I was playing as Park or Price, I always felt the weight of my actions. The game's only open-world mission is called Stranded, and this is definitely one of Call of Duty's best attempts at a fully open-world mission. You play as Park, who is stranded behind enemy lines with his squad in a snowy and mountainous setting in Korea. Park needs to scavenge supplies to bring back to his squad, and there are three main objectives on the map. You can choose to complete these objectives in any order, but more notably, you can fast travel directly to each one and bypass the entire scavenging and exploration part of the mission. Doing this means you won't have the best guns and gear to complete your objectives, and it will mostly feel like you're navigating through a standard mission instead of going through a vast open-world experience you may not be interested in. If you choose to explore in Stranded, there are bags of supplies to scavenge all around the map, and there are sealed shipping containers with combination lock puzzles you can solve to secure even more loot. The supplies you bring back to your squad can be used to repair suppressed weapons, craft lethal equipment, or utilize a mortar strike to help get the objectives done with more firepower or stealth, depending on your preferred playstyle. While I'm relieved the entire campaign isn't built on open exploration, there was an allure to the optional scavenging for weapon upgrades in Stranded. By undertaking a little of it, I looted enough supplies to secure Park a suppressed sniper rifle that proved its worth later. But for the sake of time, I fast traveled to my objectives with little consequence, calling into question the value of even having the option. At the very least, the open section introduces a replayability factor, for those that want it. Modern Warfare 4 emphasizes boss-fight-style encounters where you only have your fists or a knife equipped and go toe-to-toe with an opponent. In these scenarios, of which there are a few, timing your blocks is critical, allowing you to parry and counter with hits of your own. Admittedly, these aren't overly complex fights, but they serve as a good way to mix things up and each one adds just the right amount of tension to the scenario and the campaign overall, especially when they tie into the competing ideologies that character dynamics are built on. Modern Warfare 4 Modern Warfare 4 also keeps the optional dialogue prompts introduced in Modern Warfare 2, which allow you to ask your fellow soldiers questions or make small talk. You can choose to say nothing, but the banter usually adds a small layer of depth to the supporting characters and sometimes lightens the mood after an intense mission. It's a small amount of agency in what is essentially a rollercoaster that is appreciated, even if it doesn't really amount to anything deeper. One mission places you in the boots of a high-ranked North Korean soldier who doesn't always agree with the orders he's given. This part of the story is a harrowing mission filled with public executions, but it does make use of the dialogue prompts as you walk around interrogating civilians, providing the opportunity to define the demeanour of the character a little more. The conversation prompts let you choose how to approach many situations. You can intimidate or try to calm people with the dialogue prompts you're given, but you can also just pull out your gun and threaten them with action instead of words. These small interactions don't impact the overall story and oftentimes your actions are fruitless, but it's good to have the option to choose empathy when dealing with civilians. Ultimately, this mission also does a good job of showing the ruthless nature of this North Korean threat, and how many soldiers will still blindly follow their orders. While I'm more emotionally invested in the Task Force 141 storyline, I still enjoyed all of the missions focused on the South Korean squad. Park is forced into the military through conscription, so being a soldier wasn't a choice or an act of valor. Initially, it seems like he doesn't want to be a soldier at all, but when war hits his doorstep, he still tries to push back his fear and make the best decisions he can to protect his people. Park also provides a nice moral balance to Price's side of the campaign, where nothing matters except his thirst for revenge. Despite the campaign otherwise being a blast, the final "boss fights" of the game are a major disappointment. Neither fight feels particularly challenging, and they feel too brief for the climactic moments, but the way the story concludes is satisfying all the same. Overall, Modern Warfare 4 wraps up the series with several shocking moments and ends on a high note. The pacing never falters despite the two interwoven storylines and alternating POVs, and the wide variety of missions and locations ensure that the story remains engaging. Modern Warfare 4 is another edge-of-the-seat thrill ride. What distinguishes its campaign from others is a feeling that the devs had more time to think through the experience to create something that can serve as a worthy conclusion for the Modern Warfare series. Because of that, Modern Warfare 4's campaign ranks as the best we've seen since Modern Warfare 2019. Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 - DMZ Mode Review Building on the success of the DMZ beta launched in 2022, Infinity Ward elevates the extraction mode with major upgrades for Modern Warfare 4, including a more dynamic map, an engaging social hub, in-depth story missions, and deeper progression. All of this allows for more replayability and new ways to experience the sandbox magic that can happen within a match of DMZ. In fact, most of Infinity Ward's Call of Duty innovation this year is packed within DMZ, and thanks to a satisfying gameplay loop, the extraction mode is the standout part of Modern Warfare 4. Matches of DMZ allow for up to 60 players to group up in trios. Each squad is tasked with surviving and extracting before a circle of irradiated gas floods the map, killing everyone in its path. This is PvPvE gameplay with a focus on exploration, combat, and a wide variety of missions. As with most extraction shooters, dying in DMZ means losing everything, making it a high-risk for high-reward game mode. DMZ's new Hajin map is a vast upgrade from the original Al Mazrah location. This is the largest map ever created for Call of Duty, outsizing Warzone's Verdansk and even Black Ops 7's massive Avalon map, allowing for deeper exploration and greater map diversity. Hajin is set in a fictional version of the demilitarized zone between North and South Korea, and the area provides a wide range of main and secondary points-of-interest. In the hours I spent playing DMZ, my squadmates and I always seemed to spawn in a new area. No match ever felt the same, and there was always so much more we wanted to explore before our time ran out. Al Mazrah's diversity of locations doesn't even come close to Hajin, which takes you through dense forests, as well as a selection of city-based locations, such as military compounds, underground tunnel systems, and even an irradiated nuclear power plant. Updated NPC behavior and dynamic weather make Hajin feel more alive than Al Mazrah. Rain or fog can roll in at any time but aren't guaranteed in every match. Weather changes don't affect the entire map and are randomly localized to specific areas, shifting how you experience Hajin from point-to-point. Fog can be beneficial for stealth as it hinders the view of everyone in the vicinity, including NPC enemies, but it also limits your ability to check your surroundings. Rain on the ground (or traversing through other water sources) can create audible sloshy footsteps, betraying your position. I'm always skeptical about dynamic weather in games, but Modern Warfare 4's graphics are stunning, and it was impressive watching the fog roll across the coast and move inland. And on at least one occasion, driving through dense fog allowed my squad to evade an entire convoy of enemies. Fog is more impactful than the rain, but both are a welcome touch to the world, as they can change how you approach a situation, and they add a bit of immersion and variety to matches. With Hajin, you'll see enemies moving through the world in a more organic way. At military bases, you might find soldiers doing push-ups outside in the yard, while patrol cars full of enemies drive around the map. Drones search the sky in various locations, suicide-bomber robo-dogs can chase you around, and a boss-level tank patrols the streets. The AI threats aren't smarter, but these behaviors and a wide range of enemy types provide a sense of immersion and make for engaging combat encounters. You also can't underestimate them entirely because of their lack of intelligence in combat, as they can easily overwhelm you with their numbers. In the higher-tier zones, the NPCs have heavier armor and some wield riot shields, so it takes firepower and strategy to take them down. Changes to progression make the experience much more rewarding. You can load into a match with the intent of looting cash and resources, hunting other players, engaging in story missions, or completing smaller side objectives. No matter how you choose to play, all of these actions count toward your DMZ player level, and you earn this XP even if you fail to successfully extract. You'll also earn player XP and weapon XP for multiplayer and Warzone, but you can only rank up your DMZ level by playing DMZ. Modern Warfare 4's DMZ has a bigger focus on mission prepping, and this is handled through the new FOB (Forward Operating Base) feature. The FOB is your personal home base between missions. This is a place you can walk around and interact with various mission-prep stations, meaning no one has to sit in boring menus or lobby screens while their teammates get their loadouts ready. You don't need to load in and out of the firing range feature like you do in multiplayer, as there's a shooting range inside the FOB, so you can walk up and use it for target practice. Teammates are added to the FOB of whoever is hosting the party, so there is a social element as well, but this base is also a crucial command center that evolves with you as you level up in DMZ. This adds another layer to DMZ's engaging gameplay loop, as you're constantly trying to level up and unlock helpful stations and upgrades within your FOB, including a vendor, bounty board, dog tag case, and more importantly, a 3D printer for crafting crucial gear. There are no Buy Stations on the map this year, meaning you can't purchase what you need during the match. This is a positive change that prevents everyone from getting their hands on powerful items right away. You'll have base weapons and gear to start, but you'll need to do some scavenging in your matches in order to get better equipped. This change puts more emphasis on looting and exploration, requiring you to search out guns and gear in-match, or at the very least, find the resources to craft what you need. Crafting is done through the FOB's 3D printer, using looted materials to print essential items like backpacks, armor, and parachutes. Once you've reached level 10, you'll unlock the Gunsmith FOB station. This lets you buy and customize base weapons, but you'll be limited to a small selection of guns until you've scavenged weapon manuals to unlock more. The base price for guns isn't pricey, but you'll need to pay to unlock attachment slots as well as each individual attachment you want to include. Slots also get more expensive, so you can end up spending a lot to fully customize a single gun that you risk losing every time you load into a match. You can choose to load into a match with a free random loadout consisting of the most basic gun and supplies, but ultimately, the Gunsmith change adds more weight to your decision to risk something expensive or play it safe with less-effective gear. Leveling operators in the beta was simplistic, but MW4 gives you three different trait trees you can invest in: Warfighter, Scout, and Survivalist. The longer an operator stays alive, the more powerful they become and the more upgrades they can acquire, but dying in Hajin means you've lost that specific operator inside the DMZ zone. This sounds devastating if you've invested time into leveling up an operator and upgrading their traits, but they're not permanently lost. You have the option to spend in-game money to send an evac team to rescue them from Hajin. This can get pricey if they are a high-level operator, but it's a relief to have the option to salvage something from your loss. There are often several layers of complexity to extraction modes, so they can feel overwhelming and confusing at first. DMZ's menus and mission deployment options are no different in that regard, especially with all the new bells and whistles baked into the mode. You are onboarded with a brief tutorial mission to teach you the ropes, but this mostly teaches you how to follow mission markers and heal yourself after a fight. Beyond that, DMZ offers various mission types to choose from, and it's initially difficult to differentiate what each type offers. There are currently only six main story missions available, but they are engaging multi-step story objectives. One mission sent us on a fun casino heist, where we had to locate and breach a vault, hijacking as many bags of cash as we could before irradiated gas spread through the vault. Another tasked my squad with locating a hostage in a hospital, who was handcuffed to a gurney, and we had to wheel them out to an exfil location while enemy forces made our exit difficult. I wish there were more than six missions, but they were a blast to play, and you can opt for different strategies to complete them.  The charm of DMZ remains the random and chaotic moments you get with the sandbox nature of the mode, and the living world of Hajin definitely elevates the unpredictability, allowing every playthrough to feel unique. The world is also filled with smaller missions and objectives, so there are still many ways to play and level up. DMZ addresses the elephant in the room, which is the growing divide between players in extraction shooters. Some players believe the games should always have a PvP focus while others want the extraction experience in a PvE environment. DMZ attempts to appeal to both sides with its approach to PvP. Killing players now activates a heat level, which is similar to the Wanted system in GTA, and you'll earn a marked bounty on your head. The more players you kill, the higher your Wanted bounty grows. Hajin has bounty board stations, where it shows all active players in the match who are marked as Wanted. This bounty board lets you either pay cash to reduce your own heat level, or you can buy intel to locate the other Wanted players. This feels like a great way to cater to the PvP-focused extraction players, allowing them to hunt each other down for large sums of money, while passive players can stay off the radar if they avoid killing other operators. Another major change this year is that DMZ is no longer a free-to-play mode tied to Warzone, and instead is packed into Modern Warfare 4 as part of its $70 price tag. While losing a free game is always a bummer, the amount of upgrades packed into DMZ do feel worthy of a premium buy-in. While it's not pay-to-win per se, DMZ does provide additional benefits to players who purchase the pricey Vault edition of Modern Warfare 4. This gives them access to an additional operator slot, two medium-size backpacks, two armor-plate carriers, and other beneficial but single-useitems. Vault edition owners getting these freebies isn't a major concern, as the specific items don't have a huge impact on whether or not you lose a fight against them. However, this gets tackier when the game stops being free-to-play, and there's always the worry it's a downhill slide toward further bonuses that do become pay-to-win. Hopefully this doesn't lead to players getting bigger advantages such as UAVs or high-rarity guns. In-game bonuses are best handed out as cosmetic items like operator and vehicle skins. Once you get past the confusing mission lingo, DMZ settles into a sandbox experience with all the thrills of a high-risk/high-reward extraction shooter. Despite the original DMZ being an engaging concept, it struggled with barebones features and weak missions, but you can see how it served as the building blocks for Modern Warfare 4's more robust and engaging gameplay loop. Much of the magic often comes from the chaos that builds as you fight or flee through dangerous areas of Hajin, and the new progression layers make it easy to get lost in the world of DMZ. Overall, Infinity Ward has elevated Call of Duty's extraction mode with engaging features and riveting gameplay, and I can't wait to jump into my next match on Hajin. Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 - Multiplayer Review After two years of Black Ops games, the removal of omnimovement creates an adjustment period for Modern Warfare 4's multiplayer. That system made it easy to sprint, jump, dive, and slide in any direction, but Modern Warfare 4's movement offers fewer options. Several times, I caught myself trying to perform a wall jump or attempt a backward dive to avoid an enemy's grenade. But once I overcame this omnimovement muscle memory, Modern Warfare 4's multiplayer felt a lot like Modern Warfare 3. Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 ditches the advanced omnimovement system without compromising the overall feel of high-speed movement. While multiplayer offers the familiar fast-paced action and strong gunplay you'd expect from a Call of Duty game, a more significant innovation lies in the fresh and ever-changing Kill Block map. The less agile, more grounded approach to movement sounds like it could be a step backward, but it isn't necessarily a bad thing. Modern Warfare 4's movement is still fast and responsive. Infinity Ward has implemented smoother mantling and sliding mechanics this year, including the ability to seamlessly transition from mantling over objects into a slide, and there's also a supine slide that lets you get under low-hanging obstacles. These new movement tweaks allow you to chain various actions together in a satisfyingly fluid way. So movement is still pretty speedy and fluid, but players who want to opt for a more methodical approach can still play slowly and hold their ground without being too overwhelmed by fast-moving and agile sweaty players--ditching omnimovement has removed the irritation of facing any "omnimovement kings," who hop, flop, and wall jump all over the map. Overall, this movement system feels like a great middle ground between the players who want the fast and twitchy approach of the new era and the old school players who prefer the more grounded feel. There is also the new ability to climb up pipes, which sounds silly, but this is Modern Warfare 4's way of making elevated areas just as accessible as they were with the wall jump in Black Ops 7, without undermining the grounded contemporary setting. You can't wall-tap into a window anymore, but you can climb a pipe to sneak up and reach an enemy on an upper level. Climbing pipes is a risk that leaves you vulnerable, but it's a quick shimmy up, so you're not in danger for long. Pipe climbing is a smart way to provide verticality in a Modern Warfare setting, which players have come to expect now. As for the maps, there's a solid variety of designs, ranging from small to medium-sized standard maps set under the bright lights of New York City, in a snowy region of Korea, and in a colorfully decorated village in Mumbai. There are also a few large-scale Ground War maps played on chunks of the DMZ mode's Hajin map, for bigger infantry battles and the addition of vehicular warfare. The diversity in battlefields is enough to cater to various playstyles and shouldn't get boring quickly. However, the real standout is Kill Block,  Infinity Ward's ambitious Gunfight map. Kill Block's size is equivalent to the popular Shoot House map from 2019's Modern Warfare, but the design consists of three modular pieces that can be swapped out and connected with other sections in different ways. Between each match and during rounds of Gunfight, in which two teams try to eliminate each other and players only get one life per round, the active three pieces decouple and a new configuration is set. When Infinity Ward first announced Kill Block, I was skeptical of how the studio would execute this living map with a vast amount of potential configurations, but the modular swaps between rounds feel seamless, and all of these movable parts fit surprisingly well together. Kill Block is a blast to play and it's easily one of the best innovations of this rebooted Modern Warfare series. Every new configuration keeps you on your toes, as you can't anticipate which three pieces will get stitched together. The map chunks also include iconic designs, so longtime Call of Duty fans get the treat of enjoying classic maps in new ways. For example, a piece of Shoot House might be combined with a section of Storage Town from Warzone's Verdansk and Highrise from the original Modern Warfare 2. Kill Block features matches of 10v10 and 6v6 Gunfight, but I hope this map is eventually integrated into standard game modes, too. The Gunsmith weapon customization feature returns in MW4 and it adds the new virtual Gunny assistant to help you build loadouts. This isn't some wonky AI feature that the name evokes, but  a set of prebuilt loadouts the developer has baked into the game to help suggest weapon builds based on playstyle. For example, if you like the M4 assault rifle, Gunny can suggest builds depending on whether you want the gun customized for short-range, to be balanced, or for long-range combat. This new Gunsmith assistant didn't always suggest attachments I liked or thought were the best overall builds, but I think Gunny will be great for casual players looking to jump into matches faster without needing to worry too much about crafting the perfect "meta" weapon build. Another positive change for weapon customization is the addition of Apex attachments. Every primary and secondary weapon (excluding launchers) has one unique add-on designed to drastically change how the weapon is used, similar to the weapon conversion kits found in the recent Call of Duty games. But in Modern Warfare 4, they don't cost an attachment slot, so you can have a five-attachment build and still convert your gun with its Apex attachment. This creates more of a reason to try something unconventional than there has been in past titles. The Oden's Apex attachment is one of my favorites, as it transforms the automatic assault rifle into a powerful single-fire sniper. This isn't ideal for every mode, but instead something I equip for more power and precision when playing on Modern Warfare 4's large-scale Ground War maps.  While Oden has one of the more practical Apex attachments, some are a bit wacky and will cater to very niche playstyles and interests. The Oris 8.6 marksman rifle's Razor attachment, for example, gives you the option to shoot throwing knives in close-range combat. Again, it's less than practical in many situations, but these are fun attachments that won't cost you anything to try. Weapon leveling is a component of Call of Duty that has been a huge slog in recent years. Guns always have dozens of attachments, and it then takes hours of usage to make any real progress leveling them up and accessing the attachments you want. One major improvement in Modern Warfare 4 is the reduced weapon-leveling grind. Guns have several universal attachments now and unlocking these for one gun means you don't have to suffer the grind for that same attachment with other weapons. This shortens the list of attachments and levels you need to progress through, making it easier to tailor your ideal gun.  Call of Duty multiplayer always features either a killstreak or scorestreak system to calculate how you earn the game's lethal or support streaks. Modern Warfare 4 is more of a killstreak-based system, but it does award some points for objective-based actions such as grabbing tags in Kill Confirmed or capturing a Domination point. However, this doesn't feel rewarding as a hybrid system, as the points feel miniscule for that team player who is capturing and defending objectives. Earning streaks in Modern Warfare 4 is too heavily reliant on your ability to chain together kills. I'm always someone who prefers scorestreaks, as it better  incentivizes you and your teammates to fight for the objectives and play for the win. Modern Warfare 2 best settled the debate of killstreaks versus scorestreaks by offering the option to choose the system you preferred, allowing you to toggle on killstreaks or scoresteaks at any time. Modern Warfare 4 unsuccessfully attempts to bridge the gap with the hybrid system, and I hope a toggle can be added to give player choice. Outside of playing Kill Block, much of multiplayer's excitement comes from Modern Warfare 4's new objective game modes. Call of Duty's staples are still available with Team Deathmatch, Domination, Hardpoint, Kill Confirmed, and Search and Destroy, but Infinity Ward cooked up new modes that breathe more heart-pounding action into multiplayer with Inflation and Collateral. Inflation is a more intense, cash-based spin on Kill Confirmed. Instead of collecting dog tags from fallen players, you're picking up cash. Any player can collect fallen cash from someone's death, and the team holding the most money at the end of the match wins. I really love the momentum swings you get during the late game stage of Inflation, as players who accumulate large sums of money can cause a significant shift if they die and drop a portion of their cash. With less than a minute left of your game, you can go from drastically losing to winning in an instant, and that's excitement you don't get in a match of Kill Confirmed. Collateral is a round-based mode which puts a twist on Call of Duty's iconic Search and Destroy, adding a cash system similar to Counter-Strike. Much like Search and Destroy, teams alternate between attacking and defending two bomb sites, but killstreaks, automatic health regeneration, and custom loadouts are all disabled. Instead, everyone starts with a pistol and earns in-game cash throughout the match to spend on strategic upgrades between rounds. You lose your loadout if you die, but all earned money is kept between rounds. This makes Collateral an adrenaline-fueled, high-stakes competitive mode where every dollar counts and every decision matters. Do you want to save your money to buy a high-tier weapon in a later round or do you want to be more of a team player and spend your cash on a UAV for your whole team? This extra layer of strategy is a refreshing deviation from the typical Search and Destroy game. The gunplay, killstreaks, and perks are all what you'd expect to get with a Call of Duty game, but Modern Warfare 4 is full of small but thoughtful adjustments to make this return to a grounded environment still feel exciting to play. The scaled- back movement never feels hindering, and the new objective game modes bring fresh thrills. However, Kill Block is easily the star of Modern Warfare 4's multiplayer, and is one of the most surprising innovations Call of Duty has had for a boots-on-the-ground game. As a fan of Gunfight, I could easily spend hours playing the dedicated Kill Block playlist, but I still hope this living map gets utilized in more ways outside of Gunfight modes. Overall, Modern Warfare 4 delivers a strong and engaging multiplayer experience to complement an epic campaign conclusion and captivating DMZ mode.
