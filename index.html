Now that Fields Of Mistria is officially out of Early Access, it's probably the best possible time to start playing. If you're new to the game, it's essentially a magical Stardew Valley with plenty of unique characters to meet, fun activities to complete, and lore to uncover alongside farming, fishing, and foraging. Not everything is explained in-game, though, and some time-saving hacks or money-making measures can be good to know when you're first starting out. We've compiled a few tips and tricks to get you started in Fields Of Mistria, but you can also boost your chances of social success by checking out our Fields Of Mistria gift guide. This guide will help you make the most of your time in Mistria. Starting Out Free energy Your Stamina bar will be fairly short when you start playing, and after knocking down a few trees and smashing a few rocks, you'll find yourself ready to hit the hay. Fortunately, even when money is tight, there are a number of places around Mistria where you can get free energy boosts to help you squeeze more into your day. Check out the drinking fountains--on the Eastern Road, The Narrows and The Beach--the free daily soup at the Sleeping Dragon Inn, and take advantage of Juniper's offer of three free baths at the Bathhouse.  Recipes Through The Mail When you're growing crops it can be tempting to stockpile, gift them to new friends, or experiment with cooking--all of which is an excellent use of your time, but you might miss out on one of Fields Of Mistria's easiest knowledge bases. By shipping one of every crop and edible foragable, you'll soon get a letter in the mail from someone in the town with a recipe attached featuring that item. The best order for new edibles is probably: first one to the Museum, second one in the shipping box, then the rest are yours to do with as you please. If you need a bit of help getting started with crops, check out our guide to crops in Fields Of Mistria. Shipping produce is a great way to get recipes in the early game. Label Chests It's something many players will spend several in-game years playing without, before realising and kicking themselves, but you can label chests in Fields Of Mistria without the need to craft or learn any recipes. In fact, every Chest comes with its own labelling facility. Just play around with the buttons at the top of the Chest interface, the first one will let you add a picture to categorize what's inside.  Time-Saving Hacks Lost And Found If you're constantly having to run back to the farm to empty your pockets, this tip might save you some precious time. Fields Of Mistria is a little more laid back than many of its contemporaries, and things don't tend to decay, whether it's quests, crops, or foraged goods. If you don't mind a bit of temporary litter, it's good to know that once picked, fished, or dug up, items will stay floating until you go and collect them. So you can run around picking everything even with full pockets, then go back the next day to pick them up--you might even get to pick up double if the item has respawned. Later on in the game you'll even unlock a "lost and found" box, which collects all your dropped items and deposits them in one place every Monday morning.  Storage is limited, so label your chests and leave things out to collect later. Keep one of each item for later Although as we said earlier that you should ship and then send to the Museum, if you're able to keep one of each item in a chest as well it'll save you a lot of time later on. Throughout the Mines you'll reach checkpoints where you'll need to collect a selection of items from the previous biome, quests will pop up on the board requiring items that may have belonged to the last season, and you might find yourself needing huge amounts of wood all of a sudden. So the more you can stockpile items, the better, if you don't want to rely on RNG when the time comes.  Combat Tools In The Mines Another secret hidden in plain sight in the mines, but did you know you can use your tools to make combat easier? Down in the Lava Caves, you can use your watering can to traverse the boiling lava, but you can also use it on lava-type enemies to cool them off and give you time to mount a better attack. The Summon Rain spell can also come in handy down here, as it will render all of the lava completely harmless, turning it into walkways instead.  When you're in the Crystal Caves, you can use your bug catching net to harvest Clod Bombs from the Clod Enemies (the ones that shoot silver at you). These can be thrown back at any enemy or saved up for a particularly difficult room. Don't try and gift them though, as pretty much everyone dislikes being handed a bomb.  Don't neglect your Mining and Combat skills. Caldarus In The Mines The Caldarus Shrine in the Mines gives you different perks to the one in your farm. To those who know, this might seem obvious, but you'd be surprised how many people miss this entirely and think the two shrines carry the same perks. The one in the Mines offers perks related specifically to Mining and Combat skills, so don't sleep on them when you're trying to muscle your way down to level 100. Settings Disable Perks On the subject of perks, you can disable any perks you feel are hampering your progress. If you're looking for specific fish, for example, you might want to disable some of the perks that expand the available selection of fish so you can narrow down your search. You won't need to use Essence to unlock them again, you can simply toggle them on and off once they've been unlocked. If you're looking for more tips that could help with fishing, check out our Fields Of Mistria fishing guide. Slowing down your game gives you more time to enjoy things like dates and festivals. Slow Things Down If you find your days are running away from you and you're not getting everything done, you're not alone. Fields Of Mistria is packed with things to do, items to collect, Mines to dive, people to win over, and quests to complete, so sometimes you need to buy yourself some extra time. In the settings, you can extend the day and slow down the speed of time passing--and can quickly shorten it again if you change your mind.  You can also turn off the oversleep penalty when you stay up past your bedtime. So if you're regularly burning the midnight oil, this might help you mitigate some of the next-day issues that come with forgetting to sleep on time.

If you want to progress the main story in Fields Of Mistria, you're going to need to embrace your yearning for the mines. Alongside being necessary to learning the secrets of the town, mining is a great way to make money, find crafting materials, fulfill requests, and gather gifts for your new Mistrian pals.  In this guide we've compiled everything you need to know about mining progression in Fields Of Mistria--including which tools to aim for, what you'll find in each biome, and how to keep yourself safe down below. For more information on what to do with your newfound relics, check out our Fields Of Mistria Gift Guide, and keep an eye on our guide to monsters and drops as well.  Mining Basics When you first enter the mines in Fields Of Mistria, you won't be able to go very deep. Climbing down the ladder gets you to the first floor, where you'll need to break rocks to find the next ladder down, and so on. Every 5 floors, you'll unlock an elevator that takes you between that floor, all the ones you've previously unlocked, and the top floor exit. This means once you've unlocked Floor 10, for example, you won't have to start at Floor 1 every time, you can skip right ahead to Floor 10 and go from there. Mine floors contain primarily rocks and enemies of various types, with items to be foraged, chests to be opened, as well as fish and bugs unique to those areas. The further you go, the more biomes you'll unlock--each with their own types of rocks, enemies, collectables, and forage.  To get the most out of the Mine, you'll need to come equipped, your bag packed efficiently, and with enough hours in the day. Time passes fast in Mistria, especially when you're losing yourself in combat. If your Health reaches 0, you'll faint and wake up at the Clinic, and if your Stamina reaches 0, you won't be able to fight or mine any longer. An efficient inventory for venturing into the Mines would include: a sword, pickaxe, net, fishing rod, and at least 2 meals with everything else left at home.  At the top of the Mine, near the entrance, is a Dragon Statue. Interacting with it allows you to trade Essence for Mining and Combat Skill perks, which can influence things like drops and critical strike chance. Trade Essence for Skills at the Dragon Statue. Combat Basics Combat in Fields Of Mistria is fairly simple, and if you have the right armor and gear, it shouldn't pose any problems, even at the highest levels. There is one weapon--the sword--which can be leveled up through using different materials to craft it, with each of these materials being available in the Mine. Armor is also acquired through similar means, although it is possible for both armor and weapons to drop as a combat reward.  If your Health hits 0 and you end up in the Clinic, you don't lose anything currently in your pockets. You just won't be able to carry on with your day, which can lose you precious hours. To avoid a faint, carry food with you, make use of the fountains scattered through the Mines, and use armor wisely. Each monster will require a slightly different method to fight it, which you can check in our guide to monsters in Fields Of Mistria.  Tips For Exploring The Mines If you see a whirlpool in the water, jump into it, it'll take you off somewhere new and previously inaccessible, like a secret room. These usually have something useful or valuable in them. Climbing up a ladder won't take you back to the floor before, it'll put you right back at the entrance to the Mines, so only use them to go up if you're sure you're done for the day. Climbing down a revealed ladder will always take you down to the next floor. If you can't see a ladder, you need to keep breaking rocks until one appears. If you find a Dragon Priestess Statue, you can offer it something from your pockets, and it will in turn reward you with either Health, Stamina, or Essence. Ahead of its release in October, Square Enix has dropped several new details about Final Fantasy Resonance, its HD-2D reimagining of the mobile game Final Fantasy: Brave Exvius. Based on the first season of Brave Exvius, here's a closer look at the game's world, characters, gameplay, vision systems, and completionist content. Empires strike back The Zoldaad Empire has a few defectors. Two of the regions that players can explore include the powerful military nation of Zoldaad and the city of mages known as Mysidia. Just like in Brave Exvius, Zoldaad appears to be on the march as the home of the fabled Fire Crystal, and has secretly been building up a fleet of airships and mass-producing magitek as it expands its borders into neighboring countries. Considered to be a legend, the advanced magical civilization of Mysidia is home to some of the strongest magic-wielders in the world and is said to also house the Ice Crystal. Characters Previous trailers have already shown us the main cast of Final Fantasy Resonance--Rain, Laswell, Fina, Jake, and Lid--and if you played the mobile game, you can also expect to see a few more familiar faces like the Mysidian sage Sakura. Opposing the heroes are the Sworn Six of Paladia, the Veritas. Each Veritas represents an element, and their roster includes the brutish Veritas of Flame, the honorable Veritas of Earth, and the volatile Veritas of Light. Gameplay Classic Final Fantasy gameplay meets retro-inspired visuals. At its core, Final Fantasy Resonance is an old-school turn-based RPG, full of hard-hitting Limit Break attacks and chances to strategically outmaneuver enemies. One of the big draws in the game are the Visions of legendary Final Fantasy heroes, who can fight alongside Rain and his companions when they're summoned. Players may use the magic and abilities of Visions in combat, and the stronger your bond with them grows, the more abilities you'll unlock for these legends. Staggering all enemies in one turn will even allow you to trigger a Resonance with a vision, resulting in them performing an exceptionally devastating attack--while your eyes get treated to a slick piece of CG animation. You can even make your Visions stronger, but there's a catch: You'll need magicite to grant them stronger abilities, but because the material is exceedingly rare, you'll have to choose which Vision you strengthen. Some of the Visions also include original characters that were first introduced in Brave Exvius: Amelia: A maid armed with Fire-aspected guns Camille: A mercenary who uses water and earth attacks Wilhelm: An imperial general who can act as a party's shield Victoria: A black mage who wields dark magic Ayaka: A white mage who can buff the party with support and healing magic As mentioned above, heroes from previous Final Fantasy games can also be recruited to your party: Firion (Final Fantasy) Onion Knight (Final Fantasy 3) Vaan (Final Fantasy 12) Lightning (Final Fantasy 13) Noctis (Final Fantasy 15) Clive Rosfield (Final Fantasy 16) Espers Don't mess with Espers. For some extra firepower, you can also summon Espers. These powerful beings will join your battle for three turns, and once the timer is up, they'll drop a devastating attack before they vanish. The cuddly Carbuncle will cast Reflect magic on your party, the sea serpent Leviathan will deal water damage to enemies with a Tsunami, the armor-clad Odin can dish out a non-elemental attack that might instakill some enemies, and the mighty dragon Bahamut unleashes his signature Megaflare assault on foes. Completionist content Ultima Weapon is going to kick butt...mostly yours. Anyone looking to 100% Final Fantasy Resonance will want to make certain their party is ready to fight. Square Enix is teasing a battle with the mysterious bladesman Gilgamesh, and if you're feeling really confident, you can even challenge Ultima Weapon. A boss battle that has cropped up in several Final Fantasy games over the decades, Ultima Weapon demands perfect preparation, or you can expect your challenge to be over in just a few short turns. Final Fantasy Resonance launches for PC and consoles on October 22.
