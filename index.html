Mount Chiliad is nice this time of year. This positive thought comes to mind constantly as I begin my ascent up the iconic mountain at dawn, peering in awe as the fog dissipates and the bright orange sun begins to peer over the horizon. I could have just taken the cable car to the summit, but I want to savour the experience this time around. I want to feel stones in my shoes as I clamber up awkward inclines, I want to bump into fellow hikers and pose for the occasional selfie, and I want to feel a sense of triumph as I gaze out at the Los Santos skyline one final time.  It’s a view that millions of people around the world have grown familiar with over the past 13 years, and a testament to the scale and sophistication of Rockstar’s masterpiece, which pushed the PS3 and Xbox 360 to their absolute limits. Even back then, it felt like a living world we’d never seen in the medium before, at least not to this level of depth, detail, and potential. I’d wager I’m not alone in revisiting Grand Theft Auto 5 days ahead of its successor’s arrival, as it seems like a necessary farewell to a virtual world that has defined a generation. I stepped into the shoes of Michael, Franklin, and Trevor for the first time as a teenager, and since then it’s been hard to ignore their impact on gaming culture.    Scratch that. With 235 million copies sold, avoiding the significance of GTA 5 is impossible. It’s a behemoth of untold proportions that has earned its stellar reputation even as parts of the package begin to show their age. Despite its immense popularity, each and every time I play through the campaign, it becomes my own. It's a journey that follows the same basic path yet offers up countless twists and turns brought forth by my own curiosity.  Grand Theft Auto V Los Santos isn’t going anywhere, but there is still something strangely bittersweet about how it will soon be succeeded by a newer model and no longer considered the benchmark of realistic game worlds. So, if you fancy getting nostalgic and sentimental for a little bit, please join me on one final road trip across Grand Theft Auto 5. But first, I need to get down from this damn mountain.  The Sights And Sounds Of Blaine County  After parachuting down Mount Chiliad and narrowly preventing my own demise against a sharp cliff face, I commandeer an old banger from a nearby parking lot and begin driving towards Grapeseed. Often seen as the county’s agricultural heartland, there is something oddly serene about how quiet and sparse this bountiful stretch feels. There are no robberies, drive-bys, or wanton murder to speak of--just people getting on with their daily lives.  The small-town American vibes continue as I head towards the coast, but not before making a quick pitstop at Gordo Island to admire the lighthouse sitting atop the little piece of land. I sorely wanted to wait for night to fall so I could admire this structure in all its glory, but with a strict goal in mind, I took a few pictures before leaving it behind. Besides, there are plenty of lush sights left to see as I enter Paleto Bay and across the winding roads leading into Raton Canyon. You could spend hours walking the streets, simply observing the NPC behaviour or dipping into alleyways, to see exactly how much detail Rockstar decided to put into the most unnecessary of places.  Grand Theft Auto V One thing I love about this world is how perfectly it mimics an old-fashioned American road trip. Every road you wind around hides an unexpected secret to discover or a familiar sight that has long been burned into memory. I haven’t driven these roads in several years, but there is something so warmly nostalgic about leaving the map markers behind and exploring of my own volition. It’s peaceful, so long as I don’t accidentally bump into another vehicle or get the cops called on me, but maintaining this illusion is arguably part of the joy. Little can beat watching routines unfold without interruption like you’re nothing more than another soul trying to make ends meet in this world.  Sandy Shores, Pacific Highway, And Beyond  At this point in the trip I decide to change things up, commandeering a motorcycle from a poor soul I had no choice but to bludgeon to death with a hammer. This small hiccup aside, the journey into Sandy Shores goes off without a hitch. Trevor’s stomping ground is packed with rusting trailer parks, sprawling deserts, and a military base that uses the poverty of this area to its twisted benefit. I mean, there is a location called Stab City, and if that doesn’t tell you something, I’m afraid nothing will. The Grand Senora Desert is eventually left behind as I start approaching the western coast and catch my first look at Grand Theft Auto 5’s take on the archetypical Californian paradise. Sapphire oceans as far as the eye can see, busy highways flanked by vast lines of imported trees, and properties so expensive you need to run a data center to afford them. Progress a bit further into the solo campaign or GTA Online and you can inhabit mansions galore, but for this pilgrimage I want to remain a fly on the wall who watches individual parts of Los Santos operate as I pass through them.  Grand Theft Auto V Whenever I visit a city like Los Angeles, Tokyo, or London in reality, I’m often immune to their vastness as an occupant. You’re so busy spending time with friends or attending events that the sheer size of a metropolis ceases to matter. But as I approach the city limits of Los Santos and the skyscrapers turn from distant shadows to towering monoliths, it all becomes clear--this is a city that millions of virtual inhabitants have called home for over a decade.  I drive inward towards Vinewood Hills first and foremost so I can take in the iconic sign and briefly stop at the Galileo Observatory to marvel at the panoramic view, but after that we’re hitting the town and riding the ferris wheel. Vinewood is a disgusting example of excess as illustrious venues like the Oriental Theater are contrast with run-down neighbourhoods where normal people are struggling to stay afloat, turning to crime and drugs out of sheer necessity. Grand Theft Auto 5’s biting satire is outdated for a bunch of reasons, but its vicious contrast of prestige and poverty couldn’t be more salient.  Goodbye Los Santos, Hello Vice City The sun is starting to set as I walk onto Del Perro Pier, accompanied by hordes of locals and tourists alike looking to indulge in some generic attractions to close out the day. During the day, it's hard to ignore the rotting wooden planks that hold up this monument or the poor homeless people sleeping on the pavement outside, but during the night it becomes a neon celebration of tacky excess. I’m British, so I love nothing more than wasting my money at the seaside on mediocre donuts and overpriced roller coasters, so off I went to finish up this trip in style.  After speedrunning all of the pier’s rides, I decide the perfect way to end the day is to take a long, leisurely walk across Vespucci Beach. The hustle and bustle of the city slows to a dull hum as the moon hangs overhead, while the sands are dotted with parties lighting fires and drinking the night away. Despite the crime and excess of Los Santos, there’s such beauty to be found on its streets and the wilderness that awaits beyond, all we need to do is seek it out.   Grand Theft Auto V GTA is renowned for excessive violence, biting satire, and vast open worlds to explore, but I can’t help but see Los Santos as a character all its own. An immaculately constructed setting that is so packed with lovingly presented detail that players are still unearthing new parts of it to this day. Over the years we have peeled back the layers and grown familiar with how each part of it operates, but somehow it still manages to pull us in. This brief yet memorable jaunt was a reminder of that as innovation and nostalgia collided to prove once again that nobody does it quite like Rockstar. Even when Grand Theft Auto 6 has showcased what the future of this series is destined to look like and the sun rises again in Los Santos, we’ll still be coming to it for the rest of our lives.
