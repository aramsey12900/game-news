Here Are The Mechanics and Dozens Of New Cards From Magic: The Gathering’s Star Trek SetNEW
After a year that featured alternate realities, ninja turtles, superheroes, and hobbits, the final Magic: The Gathering expansion of 2026 is going where no Universes Beyond set has gone before, as Sta...
After a year that featured alternate realities, ninja turtles, superheroes, and hobbits, the final Magic: The Gathering expansion of 2026 is going where no Universes Beyond set has gone before, as Star Trek is scheduled to launch in November. Today, Wizards of the Coast revealed the mechanics players can expect in the set, along with dozens of new cards from both the main set and the preconstructed Commander decks. We also got a glimpse at some of the set's special treatments, including the seven cards that will come signed by the people depicted on the artwork. Here's everything revealed during today's Magic: The Gathering Star Trek debut. The mechanics of MTG Star Trek Seven new mechanics--two of them found only in the Commander cards--highlight the set, along with a eighth mechanic returning from 2024's Edge of Eternities. OPen Communications First is Beam Me Up, which allows you to cast certain spells from your graveyard by returning a creature you control to its owner's hand and paying a separate cost. The example card provided is Open Communications, which normally costs a single blue mana, but you can cast it again from your graveyard by returning a creature to its owner's hand and paying two generic and one blue mana. Next is Face A Dilemma, which adds an additional wrinkle to a mechanic Magic fans are very familiar with. Basically, any time you have to choose a mode in a spell, you are "facing a dilemma," and there are cards in Star Trek that will give extra benefits simply because you made a choice. Seven of Nine, for example, lets you draw a card any time you have to make a choice on a modal spell. Star Trek also introduces new modal spells, like the enchantment Kobayashi Maru Scenario. Every turn after casting this card, you must choose one of the four effects listed on the card, and you can't choose the same one twice--which means if you can't remove the enchantment after four turns, it's game over. The next mechanic is Federation, which cares about the number of creature types among creatures you control that aren't the Borg (which makes sense, because the Federation and the Borg are like oil and water). With Captain Jonathan Archer, by paying one green mana and tapping Archer, you can give another creature +X/+X until end of turn. As Archer is a Human Officer, and he creates a Dog token, that's at minimum +3/+3 on the first activation. Starfleet Academy, meanwhile, allows you to pay five generic mana, tap it, and choose a color to add one mana of that color for every creature type among creatures you control. If you have a wide board of varied tokens, that could be a massive mana source. Strange New Worlds is the next new mechanic, which cares about the lands you control. The more lands with different names you have on the board, the more effective these cards will be. Deep Space Scan, for example, will let you draw a card for each differently named land on your side of the table. If you're playing a three-color deck, and you have an Island, a Mountain, and a Forest on the board, that's three cards coming to your hand. Captain Pike, Fullness of Time, meanwhile, has his power and toughness determined by the number of differently named lands you control. If you're the type of person who likes to play different non-basic lands in your Commander deck, casting Captain Pike late in the game could result in a massive creature on the spot. So far, we've focused on the good guy mechanics, now here's one for the baddies. Assimilate allows you to take any creature from a certain zone and add it to your battlefield, though it will become a Borg artifact creature instead of its other types. Borg Cube lets you exile any creature on the board when it enters, and then when it attacks for the first time, you can add that exiled creature to your board as a Borg. Then, for every subsequent attack, you can repeat the process. Borg Cube also shows off the major returning mechanic, Edge of Eternities' Spacecraft and Station mechanic. Multiple iconic spaceships from Star Trek's history appear on cards in the set, and they all have the Station mechanic, which allows a player to tap a creature in order to add charge counters to the Spacecraft equal to that creature's power. Once enough charge counters are added, the Spacecraft becomes an artifact creature and gains extra abilities. For example, when three charge counters are added to D-7 Battle Cruiser, it will create a 2/1 Klingon Warrior creature token at the beginning of combat on your turn. When eight or more counters are added to it, it becomes a 3/3 flying creature. Worf, Defiant Commander The final two mechanics are exclusive to the Commander format, and only found on cards in the preconstructed commander decks. The first is Battlefront, which grants additional perks based on how many opponents you attack during a combat phase. Worf, Defiant Commander is the face card of the Klingon Fury precon deck, and his Battlefront ability lets you exile a number of cards from the top of your library equal to the number of opponents you attacked. Until your next turn, you can play those cards as if they were in your hand. Now, this ability only cares about players you actively attack. Abilities like myriad, which add tokens to the board already attacking, will not count toward a Battlefront ability, so choose wisely. Jean-Luc Picard, Luminary Seek Out is the other Commander-only mechanic, and it gives an added perk whenever you draw your second card in a turn, even if the permanent with the ability wasn't on the field when you drew your first card. Jean-Luc Picard, Luminary is the example card, as he gives a +1/+1 counter to a creature and two charge counters to a Spacecraft every time you draw your second card in a turn. Sony isn't the only company introducing new upscaling technology to its flagship PlayStation 5, as Microsoft and AMD have announced that FSR 4.1 is coming to more devices--including Xbox Series X|S consoles--in the future. Similar to Sony's hardware-accelerated upscaling solutions for PS5 and PS5 Pro, AMD FidelityFX Super Resolution (FSR) 4.1 is an advanced, machine-learning-based temporal image upscaling and reconstruction technology that can render a game at a lower resolution and then upscale it to a higher resolution. While it's highly doubtful that Xbox Series X|S consoles will start offering PS5 Pro-like graphics (both consoles lack dedicated hardware for machine-learning algorithms), this should still give owners a noticeable bump in graphical quality and performance if implemented properly. "At Xbox, we're building a gaming platform designed to meet players where they are and make it easier for creators to reach them," Xbox chief technology officer Scott Van Vliet said in the announcement video. "AMD has been an important partner in that journey for more than two decades. Together, our teams have been pushing gaming forward, across Xbox and Windows, from the silicon at the heart of our consoles and handhelds to technologies like DirectX, Advanced Shader Delivery, and Auto SR that help developers get more from the hardware and deliver better experiences for our players. Today, we're excited to take another step forward together, with FSR 4.1 coming to Xbox." Van Vliet added that FSR 4.1 will come to Xbox Series X|S "soon," and that Microsoft and AMD are working to bring the tech to the next-gen Xbox console, Project Helix. https://youtu.be/fT6TIXnmv-s Beyond that, AMD didn't share any more details on FSR 4.1 for Xbox Series X|S. FSR 1 and FSR 2 relied primarily on spatial algorithms or basic temporal accumulation to upscale games, and questions remain about who will adopt the technology and add support. Just because the latest version of FSR is out doesn't mean a game will automatically use it. For example, Capcom built the console versions of Pragmata around FSR 1, scaling up from 720p on Xbox Series S and 1080p on Xbox Series X. FSR integration requires manual developer work and testing, so results may vary. We're already seeing signs of this on PS5 with Quick Spectral Super Resolution; for now, only Marvel's Wolverine and Ghost of Yotei will support the new AI upscaling upgrade on base PS5 consoles. Essentially, developers will have to actually patch their games to use it; AMD FSR 4.1 will not automatically upgrade every older game overnight. The announcement comes just weeks before the launch of Grand Theft Auto 6, and while Rockstar hasn't confirmed if its open-world game will utilize any upscaling technology like PSSR, QSSR, FSR 4.1, we could be looking at a current-gen arms race to find the best console to play the biggest game of the year on. Beyond Xbox hardware, AMD FSR 4.1 will also be supported in Radeon RX 6000-series graphics cards, Gorgon Halo and Strix Halo Ryzen 9 laptops, and mobile gaming PC handhelds like the Asus ROG Xbox Ally devices that use Z2 Extreme and Z2A chipsets.

Resonance wears the HD-2D aesthetic confidently; Square Enix has obviously listened to past criticisms of its beloved graphical style, and it paid off. Resonance is an HD-2D game, but its highly detailed character sprites and occasional jaw-dropping pixel-based "cinema" scene differentiate it from the Octopath Traveler series and the Dragon Quest HD-2D remakes.  Resonance's distinctions are its greatest strength, in fact. It's easy to assume at first blush that its story about a boy and his best friend is nothing but a table to prop retro Final Fantasy references upon. It's true that a key part of Resonance's gameplay involves summoning "Visions" into battle, said visions being projections of characters from other Final Fantasy games. But that's all they are: Projections. They teach transferable skills, à la Final Fantasy V's job system. They can offer characters permanent stat boosts as they level up, like Final Fantasy VI's espers. And when conditions are met, they can be summoned to perform a super attack via a CGI spectacle that will remind you Square Enix have been masters of game cinema direction since day one.Each Resonance character can equip a Vision that lends them their powers in a fight, and also grants passive upgrades and skills.
