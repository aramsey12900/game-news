"When you first see the pretty screenshots of the lighting and rendering and liquids and stuff, and you're like, 'Oh man, that's cool.' But once you're playing it, you really get that immersion that you felt when you first started playing World of Warcraft. And it does feel like, 'This is how I remember it.' Even though if you look at it side by side, it's not, but it feels like it." WoW: Forever's closed beta is currently live, with the full game set to launch November 4. The organizational structure of Xbox has long been complex and unwieldy, and that hasn't changed after the latest wave of changes and layoffs as part of Xbox's "reset." But Microsoft's latest reshuffling of its studios and workforce does prompt the question of what the "Xbox Game Studios" brand even is now, with apparently only a few studios left under that banner. The Xbox division (formerly named Microsoft Gaming) consists of Xbox Game Studios, Bethesda parent company ZeniMax Media, and Activision Blizzard, but after some rearranging, Xbox Game Studios appears to only consist of the following companies: The Coalition (Gears of War) inXile Entertainment (Wasteland, Clockwork Revolution) Playground Games (Forza Horizon, Fable) Not included is Mojang, the Minecraft studio that Microsoft has been leaning heavily on-- that studio now reports directly to Xbox CEO Asha Sharma. Graphics like this official Xbox Game Studios image from 2019 have aged poorly. Former studios under the Xbox Game Studios brand include Rare and World's Edge, the developer of Age of Empires, but both now fall under Activision Blizzard. And while it's unclear if Halo Studios as an organization is now formally an Activision Blizzard entity, it's official that Activision is indeed making the next Halo game. Meanwhile, Xbox studio Obsidian Entertainment has jumped to Bethesda, and Turn 10 has merged with Playground Games. ZeniMax now has the following studios: Bethesda Game Studios id Software Obsidian Entertainment MachineGames ZeniMax Online Studios Save for King (which also reports directly to Sharma), a few Call of Duty support studios, and whichever team is making the next Halo game, the major teams at Activision Blizzard consist of: Beenox Blizzard Entertainment High Moon Studios Infinity Ward Raven Software Sledgehammer Games Rare Treyarch World's Edge Technically, all of these development teams are still Xbox studios, and Xbox will continue to publish games from partners, like OD and Physint from Kojima Productions. But the state of "Xbox Game Studios" as its own publishing label is a curiosity. It's not outside the realm of possibility that Microsoft is doing away with the branding altogether. Since mid-2025, the Xbox website has lacked any dedicated webpages or references to Xbox Game Studios. Contrast that with PlayStation Studios, which has its own page and lists 17 studios under Sony's umbrella. At its largest, Xbox Game Studios consisted of at least a dozen studios, and now the publisher has been whittled down to just a handful after shutting down or divesting from most of them. The remaining three studios, The Coalition, inXile, and Playground, are all releasing a game within the next year (Gears of War: E-Day, Clockwork Revolution, and Fable, respectively). Under Sharma's leadership, Xbox has highlighted its plan to strengthen its brand, with exclusive content as a core part of that initiative. But as its internal publisher shrinks and becomes deemphasized, what even makes an Xbox game an "Xbox game" anymore? Chances are that this distinction will remain hazy until Xbox finishes its brutal reset.

Earlier this year, Xbox performed a massive wave of layoffs, eliminating 1,600 roles and sharing that more cuts would come soon. Today, those cuts have come: Xbox has announced it is laying off 268 roles across its first-party studios, XGS management, and central functions layer. Halo Studios in particular has been significantly impacted, and today's announcement states that Activision will take over development for the next Halo title.The news broke via an Xbox Wire post called "Continuing Our Reset," by Xbox executive vice president and chief content officer Matt Booty. It makes three major points. First, it lays out the layoffs and says the company is "roughly three-quarters of the way through previously announced restructuring."Next, he announces some internal studio restructuring. Activision will expand its remit to World's Edge (known for Age of Empires) and Rare (known for Sea of Thieves), and a new team will be created within Activision to develop the next Halo title. Meanwhile, a "small team at Halo Studios will continue supporting the Halo community and games in-market." This last part is huge news – the makers of Call of Duty are now also in charge of Halo. It's perhaps the clearest example of how massive studio mergers can ultimately stifle competition – the two series hailed as the pillars of the first-person shooter are now being made under the same roof.Other studios are also getting shuffled around. You can read Booty's breakdown verbatim below:Bethesda will expand its remit to include Obsidian, which will continue its current projects, including Grounded, as well as the new Fallout project made in collaboration with Bethesda Game Studios.King will expand its remit to include Microsoft Casual Games, uniting our two global leaders in casual game design and live operations.Playground and Turn 10 will come together as one studio focused on the Forza and Fable franchises. These two studios have been creative and franchise partners for over a decade, and today’s changes will formally move their work into one team.The final note details Xbox's studio divestitures, which it announced a few months back. Compulsion and Double Fine have officially spun off to run independently, and Undead Labs has a deal with a new publisher, but will still release State of Decay 3 on Game Pass when it launches next year. On the other hand, "two separate agreements for Ninja Theory fell through," and Xbox will begin consultations on a proposed closure. Arkane is in a similar position, though Booty says those consultations are expected to last "through the end of the year."The news is unfortunate across the board, but the cuts to the Halo team are especially impactful. Halo: Campaign Evolved was one of our most recent cover stories, and we enjoyed the final product at launch, but it seems to have done little to hold off Xbox's all-encompassing waves of cuts. Meanwhile, Halo's original creators at Bungie also laid off "most of" the Destiny 2 team, though Bungie also recently announced plans to bring back vaulted Destiny 2 content.
