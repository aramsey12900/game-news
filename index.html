Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 Review - Silver BulletNEW
Reviewed on: PC ...
Reviewed on: PC Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC Publisher: Activision Developer: Infinity Ward Release: October 23, 2026 Rating: Mature Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 arrives at a crossroads for the series, thanks in large part to the inconsistent quality of recent entries. With a shaky predecessor, Infinity Ward had a lot to repair and conclude, especially with 4 set to bookend the rebooted Modern Warfare series. Fortunately, backed up by a fun campaign, thrilling multiplayer, and loads of new modes and content, Modern Warfare 4 makes up for the past and then some. Modern Warfare 4 continues after the events of 2023’s Modern Warfare 3, with Captain Price hellbent on stopping Makarov and his higher-ups. The story features shifting perspectives, from a new group of ragtag Korean soldiers to familiar characters like Gaz. The character dynamics harken back to the original trilogy, especially in the boots-on-the-ground missions with the South Korean unit, led by Park (played by Beef actor Young Mazino).For a series that, at least recently, has been more spectacle than story, giving the stakes the time they deserve is nice. While it moves away from the stealthy and modernized elements, it makes sense for the matters at hand. The majority of Modern Warfare 4’s attempts at wrapping up the overarching plot of the rebooted series works, but it rushes through some character arcs and story beats in uneven fashion. Furthermore, much of the game’s cast, especially Mads Mikkelson’s Archer and Prisca Kim’s Ri Ju-Young, are underutilized, which is a shame since they both deliver great performances.  Despite the shortcomings of the campaign, Call of Duty's bread and butter, multiplayer, still delivers. While it doesn’t have the fluidity of Black Ops’ newer entries, the customization and design of weapons and maps make it worth digging into. From small, intimate shooting galleries with Mumbai and Nautical to wide free-for-alls like Coal and Reactor U92, the range helps maintain excitement, even if it gets repetitive over time. The variety of multiplayer modes keeps things fresh on the battlefield. Returning modes like standard Team Deathmatch and Kill Confirmed continue that enjoyable competitive flair that made them the stars that they are. However, new modes like Combat Outpost and Hijack fit right into the already great lineup. In particular, Combat Outpost upscales your typical 6v6 player count to 24v24, leading to more action and teamwork. Hanging onto a team’s tank, shooting enemies from afar, and taking back outposts is exhilarating, even if it can all go bad moments later by a well-placed RPG shot.Even after hours of gameplay, the variety of options to craft your perfect gun is impressive. Figuring out the best scope, ammo, stock, and muzzle, as well as the advantages and disadvantages of them, is a fun way of customizing your playstyle. Gunny, a mechanic that helps optimize your gun for certain styles, can help with decision paralysis, but I rarely used it. Not many FPSs go that far and Modern Warfare 4’s commitment to the feature makes it stand out. The newest mode, DMZ, impresses out of the gate. After a few flat attempts in the past, this is the definitive Call of Duty extraction shooter, whether you play solo, or in duos or trios. Immediately, you’re thrust into the wide open world of Hajin and left to fend for yourselves with what you have and what you can gather and extract. Fans of Arc Raiders and Marathon should feel right at home as the gameplay borrows from the typical extraction shooter experience. Intense, action-packed moments keep you coming back after every win or loss. And the excellent gunplay and level design elevate it into something worthwhile, no matter how you play.  Despite not encountering many live players during my time in Hajin in DMZ, it still proves challenging, even for seasoned pros. The world is not your friend, and taking risks can come at a high cost. A good team, as well as a thoughtful loadout and traits, are required to survive. The unpredictability of it all keeps you on your toes, making for an exciting addition to the already strong multiplayer suite.Regardless of the mode, Modern Warfare 4 is one of the best-looking Call of Duty titles to date. New York and Paris are lifelike, and it truly embodies the realistic, modern aesthetic the series has always aimed to achieve. However, I encountered a few technical hiccups, especially in the campaign. Slow framerate plagues some high-octane scenes and texture loading is frequent across the board. These distractions are far from game-breaking, but enough to be noticeable. While the plot execution is a bit shaky, engaging multiplayer options, including DMZ, aptly demonstrate that Infinity Ward can still deliver compelling military-FPS experiences. Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4 is a strong return to form that serves as a solid sendoff for the rebooted subseries. Score: 8.5 About Game Informer's review system

Tenebris Somnia Review – A Hair-Raising And Entertaining ThrowbackNEW
Reviewed on: PC ...
Reviewed on: PC Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch, PC Publisher: New Blood Interactive Developer: Andrés Borghi, Saibot Studios Release: October 16, 2026 Tenebris Somnia channels the old-school appeal of classic point-and-click adventures, survival horror, and full-motion-video (FMV) experiences, then smartly modernizes them to create a creepy, entertaining experience. Surviving the twisted threats of a demonic dream world is a tightly packed adventure that rewards you with what’s essentially an impressive indie horror film peppered throughout. It’s a nightmare you may not want to wake up from.The blend of live-action and in-game storytelling works well in Tenebris Somina’s adventure, which begins when protagonist Julia visits the apartment of her filmmaker ex-boyfriend, Ivan, after suffering recurring nightmares of him being massacred by monsters. What begins as a well-meaning check-in on his status spirals down an unsettling rabbit hole when Julia’s worst fears are realized and then some. The dialogue-free tale presents a scary but fascinating exploration of obsession, both in art and in romance, that constantly delivers surprising, and at times upsetting, revelations during its eight-hour runtime. While Tenebris Somnia sometimes leans a little too far into “tell, don’t show” territory when explaining the cool lore of its supernatural threats, I never felt lost or confused about the wild things that regularly unfold on screen. FMV experiences are nothing new, but Tenebris Somnia’s live-action scenes are a highlight. They’re well shot, sport impressively gruesome practical effects, and even present visual clues that make them important to gameplay. These scenes are additive, never taking too much time away from the in-game action, and they feel well placed to elicit the strongest emotional response. I also appreciate how the live scenes fit thematically, as much of Tenebris Somnia’s tale centers on a horror film shot by Ivan and Julia that significantly impacted their former relationship; at times, it’s like watching a movie about the movie. While I enjoy smaller moments, like opening a door to trigger a live-action jump scare, the filmmaking succeeds in selling Julia's emotions and the cast's reactions. Seeing real fear, concern, and determination in Julia’s eyes as she navigates a dreamworld made up of Ivan’s memories helped endear me to what would otherwise be a featureless pixel sprite.Gameplay may look unimpressive due to its old-school adventure game presentation, but Tenebris Somnia’s spin on familiar survival-horror mechanics hits the right notes. Taking place entirely within a dreamlike version of a four-story apartment building, you’ll traverse floors collecting and combining objects and deciphering clues to solve well-crafted puzzles. I’m impressed by how well the game’s puzzles make the most of its limited graphical presentation, including clever scenarios involving mirror/screen reflections I won’t spoil, and how consistently creative they are. One devious exercise even utilizes the game’s limited save points as an unexpected resource in a way that changes how you approach your adventure going forward. No matter how strange puzzles become, they are never opaque, requiring just enough out-of-the-box thinking to make me feel smart when I connect the dots. Battling grotesque monsters (which, naturally, look even creepier in live-action) also feels good, with melee and gunplay sporting deliberate animations reminiscent of classic adventure games without feeling mechanically clunky. Taking on multiple enemies by swinging a pipe or blowing them away with a shotgun feels better than it looks, and the most intense encounters end just as they approach the line of becoming overwhelming.Boss battles, perhaps most unexpectedly for a largely puzzle-focused adventure, are fun and distinct from each other, be it wave-based bouts against small gremlin-like foes, protecting a fragile effigy of yourself from a parade of demented house servants, or battling literal mirror images of a tentacled flesh monster the Resident Evil series would be proud to claim. Managing limited ammo and health strikes a good balance of feeling tense but not oppressive. Playing conservatively is rewarding, as few non-boss threats need to be engaged if you can evade them well enough. But when I did find myself down to my last bullet or on the verge of death, Tenebris Somnia lets you feel the pinch just long enough before dropping a box of bullets or a health vial at the right moment.Tenebris Somnia mines the enjoyable nostalgia of playing the horror games of yesteryear without feeling dated in the worst way. Weaving a solid indie horror film adds to the experience rather than feeling like a gimmick. Hajin is set in a fictional version of the demilitarized zone between North and South Korea, and the area provides a wide range of main and secondary points-of-interest. In the hours I spent playing DMZ, my squadmates and I always seemed to spawn in a new area. There is also the new ability to climb up pipes, which sounds silly, but this is Modern Warfare 4's way of making elevated areas just as accessible as they were with the wall jump in Black Ops 7, without undermining the grounded contemporary setting. You can't wall-tap into a window anymore, but you can climb a pipe to sneak up and reach an enemy on an upper level. Climbing pipes is a risk that leaves you vulnerable, but it's a quick shimmy up, so you're not in danger for long. Pipe climbing is a smart way to provide verticality in a Modern Warfare setting, which players have come to expect now. As for the maps, there's a solid variety of designs, ranging from small to medium-sized standard maps set under the bright lights of New York City, in a snowy region of Korea, and in a colorfully decorated village in Mumbai. There are also a few large-scale Ground War maps played on chunks of the DMZ mode's Hajin map, for bigger infantry battles and the addition of vehicular warfare. The diversity in battlefields is enough to cater to various playstyles and shouldn't get boring quickly. However, the real standout is Kill Block,  Infinity Ward's ambitious Gunfight map. Kill Block's size is equivalent to the popular Shoot House map from 2019's Modern Warfare, but the design consists of three modular pieces that can be swapped out and connected with other sections in different ways. Between each match and during rounds of Gunfight, in which two teams try to eliminate each other and players only get one life per round, the active three pieces decouple and a new configuration is set. When Infinity Ward first announced Kill Block, I was skeptical of how the studio would execute this living map with a vast amount of potential configurations, but the modular swaps between rounds feel seamless, and all of these movable parts fit surprisingly well together. Kill Block is a blast to play and it's easily one of the best innovations of this rebooted Modern Warfare series. Every new configuration keeps you on your toes, as you can't anticipate which three pieces will get stitched together. The map chunks also include iconic designs, so longtime Call of Duty fans get the treat of enjoying classic maps in new ways. For example, a piece of Shoot House might be combined with a section of Storage Town from Warzone's Verdansk and Highrise from the original Modern Warfare 2. Kill Block features matches of 10v10 and 6v6 Gunfight, but I hope this map is eventually integrated into standard game modes, too. The Gunsmith weapon customization feature returns in MW4 and it adds the new virtual Gunny assistant to help you build loadouts. This isn't some wonky AI feature that the name evokes, but  a set of prebuilt loadouts the developer has baked into the game to help suggest weapon builds based on playstyle. For example, if you like the M4 assault rifle, Gunny can suggest builds depending on whether you want the gun customized for short-range, to be balanced, or for long-range combat. This new Gunsmith assistant didn't always suggest attachments I liked or thought were the best overall builds, but I think Gunny will be great for casual players looking to jump into matches faster without needing to worry too much about crafting the perfect "meta" weapon build. Another positive change for weapon customization is the addition of Apex attachments. Every primary and secondary weapon (excluding launchers) has one unique add-on designed to drastically change how the weapon is used, similar to the weapon conversion kits found in the recent Call of Duty games. But in Modern Warfare 4, they don't cost an attachment slot, so you can have a five-attachment build and still convert your gun with its Apex attachment. This creates more of a reason to try something unconventional than there has been in past titles. The Oden's Apex attachment is one of my favorites, as it transforms the automatic assault rifle into a powerful single-fire sniper. This isn't ideal for every mode, but instead something I equip for more power and precision when playing on Modern Warfare 4's large-scale Ground War maps.  While Oden has one of the more practical Apex attachments, some are a bit wacky and will cater to very niche playstyles and interests. The Oris 8.6 marksman rifle's Razor attachment, for example, gives you the option to shoot throwing knives in close-range combat. Again, it's less than practical in many situations, but these are fun attachments that won't cost you anything to try. Weapon leveling is a component of Call of Duty that has been a huge slog in recent years. Guns always have dozens of attachments, and it then takes hours of usage to make any real progress leveling them up and accessing the attachments you want. One major improvement in Modern Warfare 4 is the reduced weapon-leveling grind. Guns have several universal attachments now and unlocking these for one gun means you don't have to suffer the grind for that same attachment with other weapons. This shortens the list of attachments and levels you need to progress through, making it easier to tailor your ideal gun.  Call of Duty multiplayer always features either a killstreak or scorestreak system to calculate how you earn the game's lethal or support streaks. Modern Warfare 4 is more of a killstreak-based system, but it does award some points for objective-based actions such as grabbing tags in Kill Confirmed or capturing a Domination point. However, this doesn't feel rewarding as a hybrid system, as the points feel miniscule for that team player who is capturing and defending objectives. Earning streaks in Modern Warfare 4 is too heavily reliant on your ability to chain together kills. I'm always someone who prefers scorestreaks, as it better  incentivizes you and your teammates to fight for the objectives and play for the win. Modern Warfare 2 best settled the debate of killstreaks versus scorestreaks by offering the option to choose the system you preferred, allowing you to toggle on killstreaks or scoresteaks at any time. Modern Warfare 4 unsuccessfully attempts to bridge the gap with the hybrid system, and I hope a toggle can be added to give player choice. Outside of playing Kill Block, much of multiplayer's excitement comes from Modern Warfare 4's new objective game modes. Call of Duty's staples are still available with Team Deathmatch, Domination, Hardpoint, Kill Confirmed, and Search and Destroy, but Infinity Ward cooked up new modes that breathe more heart-pounding action into multiplayer with Inflation and Collateral. Inflation is a more intense, cash-based spin on Kill Confirmed. Instead of collecting dog tags from fallen players, you're picking up cash. Any player can collect fallen cash from someone's death, and the team holding the most money at the end of the match wins. I really love the momentum swings you get during the late game stage of Inflation, as players who accumulate large sums of money can cause a significant shift if they die and drop a portion of their cash. With less than a minute left of your game, you can go from drastically losing to winning in an instant, and that's excitement you don't get in a match of Kill Confirmed. Collateral is a round-based mode which puts a twist on Call of Duty's iconic Search and Destroy, adding a cash system similar to Counter-Strike. Much like Search and Destroy, teams alternate between attacking and defending two bomb sites, but killstreaks, automatic health regeneration, and custom loadouts are all disabled. Instead, everyone starts with a pistol and earns in-game cash throughout the match to spend on strategic upgrades between rounds. You lose your loadout if you die, but all earned money is kept between rounds. This makes Collateral an adrenaline-fueled, high-stakes competitive mode where every dollar counts and every decision matters. Do you want to save your money to buy a high-tier weapon in a later round or do you want to be more of a team player and spend your cash on a UAV for your whole team? This extra layer of strategy is a refreshing deviation from the typical Search and Destroy game. The gunplay, killstreaks, and perks are all what you'd expect to get with a Call of Duty game, but Modern Warfare 4 is full of small but thoughtful adjustments to make this return to a grounded environment still feel exciting to play. The scaled- back movement never feels hindering, and the new objective game modes bring fresh thrills. However, Kill Block is easily the star of Modern Warfare 4's multiplayer, and is one of the most surprising innovations Call of Duty has had for a boots-on-the-ground game. As a fan of Gunfight, I could easily spend hours playing the dedicated Kill Block playlist, but I still hope this living map gets utilized in more ways outside of Gunfight modes. Overall, Modern Warfare 4 delivers a strong and engaging multiplayer experience to complement an epic campaign conclusion and captivating DMZ mode.
