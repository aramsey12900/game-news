Fortnitemares Is A Spooky Dream Come True At Universal’s Horror NightsNEW
While Halloween is drawing nearer, the clock is ticking before Epic Games reveals this year's Fortnitemares event, which will carry Fortnite through spooky season with haunting new skins, bosses, weap...
While Halloween is drawing nearer, the clock is ticking before Epic Games reveals this year's Fortnitemares event, which will carry Fortnite through spooky season with haunting new skins, bosses, weapons, and points of interest on the map. Until that drops, though, there is another way to get your Fortnitemares fix if you happen to be visiting Universal Studios in Orlando or Hollywood. This year's Halloween Horror Nights (HHN) event has continued the recent trend of gaming properties having a major presence. The past few years have seen The Last of Us, Fallout, and Five Nights at Freddy's brought to life, thanks in no small part to the popularity of the live-action adaptations of each. This year, Fortnite takes center stage with Fortnitemares scare zones based on the annual celebration of all things horror taking place on both coasts, with each park getting a unique take on the event. To adapt Fortnitemares, creative teams at both Universal parks worked with Epic Games to craft an experience that feels genuine to the experience of traversing a Fortnite island. Speaking to GameSpot, Universal Studios Orlando creative development show director Ramón Paradoa set the stage. Guests are dropped into the middle of Freaky Fields, a Fortnitemares location from the past two years (Chapter 5, Season 4 and Chapter 6, Season 4) during a battle royale round. "You've got pumpkins. You've got scarecrows. You've got cornfields. You've got barns. You've got a bunch of creepy jack-o'-lanterns with glowing faces. A lot of things that you expect and feel like Halloween comfort food," he explained. "But how do we take it further?" Fortnitemares at Halloween Horror Nights That's the process Paradoa refers to as "HHNifying" the property, going beyond adapting the aesthetics and finding a way to make it immersive. In Orlando, that starts as you enter the zone and are faced with a version of a character-select screen, with Skull Trooper Jonesy as the chosen skin. Your mission is to survive Freaky Fields to get to the Halloween Horror Nights POI on the other side, where you're greeted by the Battle Bus and the makings of a spooky party. (While normally you would start your match on the Battle Bus, having it present at the end of the scare zone makes sense, given the extraction endgame introduced last season.) Along the way, you'll cross paths with popular Fortnite skins and even some deeper-cut NPCs.   "What we landed on in partnership with Epic Games was bringing the Shadows to life," Paradoa said. "If you remember the Shadow characters that were kind of derivative of the Shadow Midas character from a few years ago, [we're] bringing those characters in as monsters that are traversing through this space, in addition to Husks. And I think people who go way back with Fortnite and Epic Games lore, no one loved those Husks." Shadows first arrived in the Fortnitemares 2020 event as a monster players would become after being eliminated in a match. Husks, on the other hand, come from Fortnite: Save the World and are still tormenting those playing the game's original mode. In the scare zone, both of the monsters--as well as the skinned "players"--are costumed characters moving freely throughout the zone, doing whatever they can to torment guests passing through. Well, most of them are, anyway. "Skeleterra is there to interact with the guests in a variety of different ways but also to perform a select number of emotes that she can perform while you take pictures and videos with her," Paradoa teases. Sadly, when visiting the zone ourselves, Skeleterra was nowhere to be found. Of course, that didn't stop guests from emoting for photos and videos in any of the several spots that looked practically built to be TikTok video backdrops, including one that featured a dramatically posed Curdle Scream Leader. We also missed out on seeing any of the non-Fortnite characters Paradoa confirms will pop up in the maze, though their inclusion makes sense. Previous Fortnitemares events have included skins of The Grabber (Black Phone), Michael Myers (Halloween), Billy the Puppet (Saw), and Jason Voorhees (Friday the 13th), all Horror Nights legends in their own right. It's not just walk-around characters and set dressing bringing Fortnitemares to life at HHN, though. According to Paradoa, this space includes the most extensive use of technology they've ever put to work in a scare zone, including an attempt at recreating the experience of being caught in the storm. "It is one of the coolest things I think we have ever done in Halloween Horror Nights before," he said. "[It's] so immersive in so many different ways, and when it happens, no matter where you are in the scare zone, you feel it. You feel the consequences of the storm rolling in, and the characters are also reacting and reacting to the storm rolling in as well." Visually, it is certainly the best aspect of the zone. LED lighting has been placed throughout the trees, along with massive purple floodlights and smoke machines, all timed to the circle closing in on you as you progress through the zone. Before you know it, you're bathed in light, with LED strips approximating the jolts of electricity surrounding you and the smoke emulating the smoke that rises from your character while they're losing health in the storm. It's a fun technical trick, though being on a loop that resets fairly often takes away from the experience a bit if you linger to check out Hollowhead battling Skull Trooper. Beyond the storm, the most impressive technical element of the Fortnitemares zone is that Universal created a short scene that uses projection mapping to simulate Wrath building structures to reach an enemy that looks a lot like Jack Gourdon. Once he reaches him, Gourdon's time is up as he gets blown to pieces. It might only be a quick moment, but it's a fun one. https://twitter.com/SpeculationMatt/status/2093174259849875841 What both the storm and the building scene left me wondering was how good an actual Fortnitemares haunted house at the event could be. While scare zones are outdoors, recreating the settings in a sound stage where you can completely control the level of lighting and direct the guests toward specific scares would be exciting. Sound would also play a bigger role in a house, as being indoors would allow the audio elements like the storm alarm or eliminations to boom across the various rooms. In the scare zone, those details are lost due to it being outdoors and stuffed with guests all making their way to the Battle Bus. Were there a way to combine a house and zone, the team could likely craft an even more memorable and fleshed out rendition of Fortnitemares. As it stands, though, Fortnitemares at Universal Studios Orlando was a blast for this longtime Fortnite fan. Seeing skins I play alongside regularly in the real world while venturing toward a Battle Bus was a surreal experience and one I would gladly do again. And with Fortnitemares bringing new elements to Fortnite each year, hopefully this isn't the last time Universal attempts to adapt it. Halloween Horror Nights is open now at Universal Studios Orlando, while Hollywood's Horror Nights begins September 3. The event runs through November 1 on both coasts and features haunted houses based on the movies Sinners, Hellraiser, and Evil Dead Burn, as well as the music of Ozzy Osbourne, the final season of Stranger Things, and more. From me, an Avatar fan, Turok: Origins should take that as a compliment. I just hope it has not quietly morphed into a live-service game upon release. I am looking for experiences, not obligations.

My personal experience with the Total War series has been frequent toe-dipping and experimenting, but never committing full-time. I love the chaos of a Warhammer 40,000 video game, though, whether that means directing my forces in Dawn of War or tearing through swarms of Tyranids in Space Marine. So, while I'm reluctant to speak to the changes for this series, I can absolutely confirm Total War: Warhammer 40,000 can sate a thirst for explosions and declarations of loyalty to the Emperor. Four factions are currently planned for the game, and they're the ones you likely expect: Orks, Aeldari, the billions of humans in the Imperium forces, and of course, Space Marines.Part one of my demo put me through a small skirmish, pitting my small Space Marine detachment against a raging warband of Orks. Gradually, the tutorial walked me through the basics: units can be deployed behind a line, and then once battles commence, I can order them around, pausing or speeding up whenever I wish. My Space Marines may have been outnumbered, but their powerful bolters mixed with a good position – on top of a hill and in cover – saw waves of Orks dashed against the proverbial rocks. With enough time – and bombing runs I could call in on a cooldown – it didn't take long for me to route my foes.In the second section, I zoomed out to the larger map and commanded forces around the planet I'd found myself on, working my way towards a nearby Ork army. Absolutely. Despite carefully planning a strategy to hold a small but defensible line and then pincer from opposing flanks after the enemy closed in, it all fell to ruins fast. Partly because my units weren't fast enough to swing around and close in while my core still held, but also because I didn't notice my right flank was blocked off from a faster route by a parked train.Afterwards, I confirmed with a Creative Assembly developer that I could have blown up the train and punched through to the side of my foes, but a simple "move" command sent my right-side units swinging the long way around instead. I chalk it up to user error, though it does highlight a level of destruction and ingenuity players might need to get used to having, in this far-flung future.Getting a sense of the breadth of Total War: Warhammer 40,000's deeper systems and scope in the time I had allotted was difficult. For other games, an hour slice might be enough of a toothpick to see if the cake's baked well, but for something as big as a Total War game, it felt like just a taste.I'd certainly like to see how the other factions and races fare against the Orks and Space Marines, and get deeper into the longer-term progression systems. What I've seen so far confirms to me that Creative Assembly isn't holding back on its first foray into the 40,000 universe.

Orbitals Review - Space For ImprovementNEW
Reviewed on: Switch 2 ...
Reviewed on: Switch 2 Platform: Publisher: Kepler Interactive Developer: Shapefarm Release: September 3, 2026 Rating: Everyone 10+ The first time I saw footage of Orbitals, the anime-themed co-op game from Shapefarm, it was love at first sight. But the more I got to know it, the more I realized good relationships are built on more than appearances. Orbitals is a stunningly beautiful sci-fi adventure with decent gameplay and a disappointing story. Despite my criticisms, Orbitals is kind of like a misbehaving puppy; any gripe I have is immediately softened by its charming appearance, and I left the experience more positive than negative. By far, Orbitals' greatest feature is its presentation. Cutscenes are animated in a gorgeous retro anime style, gameplay has a similarly pleasing 3D-rendered aesthetic, and the soundtrack (especially the title theme) is an electrifying throwback. Whimsy is off the charts, with fun banter, animal-like aliens to interact with in each level, and inspired cassette futurism environmental designs.Players control Maki and Omura, two young adults living in an isolated space station. Their home is in danger of being overrun by a deadly storm, and the only way to protect the community is to gather three reactor cassettes. The duo ventures out in their ship to explore abandoned and run-down space stations in hopes of finding the parts they need. The premise is decent enough to propel most of the game, which prioritizes gameplay over the overarching narrative.That focus shifts in the last half hour or so, however, dumping a ton of story content on the player all at once. The resulting finale is rushed and unsatisfying, and snuck up on me after Orbitals' mostly slow pace. Ultimately, the story concludes with moments that lacked enough setup to make the emotional payoff worthwhile. Because of this, I wish the game focused more specifically on Maki and Omura's connections to their community rather than a MacGuffin-motivated mission. While I conceptually understood the duo loved the people back home, those relationships were talked about more than I experienced them in-game, and I never cared about most characters the way the protagonists did. Maki and Omura, on the other hand, are (inter)stellar leads. While it's never properly established, they have a fun sibling dynamic, and clearly enjoy each other's company, speaking in sync, making puns, and singing silly songs. It's oddly refreshing after playing similar games where the player characters are constantly butting heads – I like the person I'm playing this game with, and it's nice to have our respective avatars feel the same way.Gameplay is co-op only, and almost always asymmetrical, save for some minigames and occasions when Maki and Omura don't have tools equipped. The bulk of the gameplay has each player wield a different piece of tech – usually some combination of a laser, grappling hook, and water cannon – and work together to solve puzzles. No two sections felt the same, and I was impressed by Orbitals' sheer puzzle variety, finding new ways to use each tool.Some felt unsatisfying and too simple; solutions were reduced to "player 1 uses a tool, then player 2 uses a tool, and vice versa until the level is complete," with little critical thinking or coordination required. That said, the whimsical vibes help a lot, elevating middling gameplay with fun concepts. Shooting platforms at a wall for my partner to jump on wasn't particularly exciting or novel, but when those platforms are bright blooming flower petals I'm blasting into a wall of soil, I don't mind as much. To travel between stations, you team up to pilot a home base spaceship that evolves over the course of the game with new critters and minigames. When you're ready to take off, one player sits in the pilot's chair and the other controls the guns. It's an engaging way to solve puzzles, with one of us moving obstacles and flipping switches and the other maneuvering, but I was regularly frustrated by the space combat. Dodging fast homing missiles was a consistently uncomfortable affair, and one late-game sequence was downright overwhelming. Orbitals doesn't quite crash and burn, but its execution leaves a bit to be desired. That said, even after a lukewarm ending, returning to the game to hear the title theme and see the smiling protagonists once more makes me undeniably sentimental. I wish I connected more to Orbitals' story and systems, but I can't deny its playful, cozy vibes. Score: 7 About Game Informer's review system
