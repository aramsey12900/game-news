I Stopped Playing Palworld For This Creature-Collector GameNEW

When I think of creature-collector games, I think of the Pokemon series and, in more recent years, Palworld. Pokemon's decades-long dominance faced plenty of imitators throughout the years, until Palworld's monster-collecting, base-building, and weapon-crafting hit broke through. Since then, every new creature-collector game has to answer the same question: What makes that worth playing instead? HoYoverse might finally have an answer. Enter Honkai: Nexus Anima. It goes without saying that Nexus Anima borrows ideas from the genre's biggest titles, but it feels more dedicated to combining the best parts of Pokemon and Palworld than replacing either of them, while still adding its own flair by way of interesting battle mechanics, beautiful worldcrafting, and a riveting narrative. I can't stop playing it. The Anima are adorable. In my formative years playing Pokemon, I knew the idea was to fill the Pokedex, but it was the feeling of connection with my Pokemon that made me fall in love with the series. Choosing my starter, taking them on my entire adventure, refusing to consider stat-adjusting moves, and instead going for an all-out assault on my opposition no matter the circumstances, are things I still love to do now. That's why, when I met a shy Inkifoal with a mysterious backstory in the early hours of Nexus Anima, that feeling began to reignite. While there are plenty of screens, stats, and paragraphs of information about Nexus Anima's, well, Anima, they don't exist just to fill my collection. Their adorable designs, expressive animations, and various personalities had me choosing my party based on the vibes, rather than their strengths. There are bonuses to considering your team synergy of course--bringing multiple Anima with the same Aspect will grant buffs to your attack in combat--but if you're purely interested in having the cutest, coolest, or most novel team combinations, that's not punished.  There's a huge variety of original creatures to find. HoYoverse has built its reputation through titles like Genshin Impact, Honkai: Star Rail, and Zenless Zone Zero, where it crafts characters with instantly recognizable designs and engaging personalities, and it's somehow managed to apply that philosophy to a creature-collecting game where the majority of the Anima don't have the ability to speak. Where other titles that tried to reach the lofty heights of Pokemon struggled was their inability to capture the magic of Pokemon's creatures. Temtem was considered similar to Pokemon, but an MMO, and while it proved that capturing Pokemon's battle systems was possible, emulating its sense of discovery was much harder. 2020's Nexomon had hundreds of creatures, but they all felt like variations on familiar ideas rather than memorable individuals. Even Palword, despite its massive success, focused on reinventing survival mechanics rather than the deep bond that defines Pokemon games--and even became the subject of a lawsuit from Nintendo for how similar some of its mechanics were.   Battles take place in a grid format. Where Pokemon's combat is relatively straightforward, Nexus Anima takes all the strengths of HoYoverse's action-RPG roots to create fast, fluid, and visually stunning battles that encourage you to consider your positioning and technique, all without becoming overwhelming. Battles take place on a hexagonal grid, and as you level up, you can take more Anima into battle with you. Anima have different Traits, Roles, and Damage Types, which affect their efficiency in combat, as well as unique Abilities to use to take down the opposition. Where Honkai: Nexus Anima struggles slightly is making it obvious when your Anima are using those abilities, and I'd like to see that indicated more clearly without clogging up the UI.  The environments are also absolutely breathtaking. Nothing immerses me more in a game than when I actually enjoy being in that game and soaking up the scenery. In Nexus Anima I found huge satisfaction in walking down a street filled with believable NPCs, a variety of stores, and some that you can even go in and meet the Anima working there, or complete challenges. It feels like a HoYoverse game in all of the best ways, and I keep finding myself pushing forward through the story to discover the next setting, wondering which angle the developer has opted for. Will it be an eerie corridor lined with ominous red blooming flowers, will it be a vast, open beach with the most beautiful skybox I've seen in a game in a long time, or will it be a water-filled arena complete with lilypads, mist, and hanging banners?  Arena design aesthetics alter from one area to the next. Honkai: Nexus Anima isn't trying to be Pokemon, or Palworld, or any of their mimics. It feels premium from the outset, offering a highly satisfying combat cycle, engaging narrative, and creature designs that make me want to find and befriend them all. The dedicated HoYoverse playerbase has infinite scope to build teams, discover new Anima, experiment with different combinations, and tinker with the synergy, all while being rewarded by the game's progression systems.  Whether Nexus Anima can retain players after the initial buzz remains to be seen, but for me HoYoverse has achieved something I wasn't expecting: It made me excited about a new creature-collecting game. While the Pokemon Winds and Waves releases are still on my radar, and the Palworld 1.0 release certainly seems impressive, all I can think about is Nexus Anima.

Marvel's Wolverine looks like it'll be one of the biggest games of September--which is a stacked month--as the new single-player adventure from Insomniac Games is aiming to put a fresh spin on the iconic mutant. At San Diego Comic-Con, multiple members of Insomniac hit the stage to talk about the game, the dark world that Marvel's Wolverine takes place in, and how the character has been modernized for his first solo outing since 2009. In case you missed it, here's a breakdown of all the new details revealed during the panel, ahead of the release of Marvel's Wolverine on September 15. Earth-1048 is this Wolverine's home dimension While Insomniac has mentioned it before, the studio did once again confirm that its Wolverine game is set on the same alternate Earth as Marvel's Spider-Man, Earth-1048. Insomniac had previously planted seeds for this link in Marvel's Spider-Man 2--alongside many other Marvel Easter eggs--as references to Trask Industries could be found in the game alongside an abandoned hospital in the game covered in graffiti. Take a closer look at those tags, and you'll find a big "X" reference, and one of the walls has "Beast" written on it--which could only be a mention of the X-Men's resident super-genius, Hank McCoy.  Wolverine is 200 years old https://youtu.be/pj9ZIIpesfQ Just like in the comics--and the solo spin-off movie that most of us want to forget--Logan has been around for centuries. He's 200 years old at the start of the game, but he's still sporting a youthful appearance thanks to his mutant healing factor. Decades of war and combat have taken their toll on Logan, and game director Mike Daly said that this has resulted in Logan keeping the people he cares about at arm's length. "Our Wolverine, he's cynical, he's candid. He keeps people at a distance, not because he knows he's dangerous, but because he doesn't want to get hurt again," Daly said during the San Diego Comic-Con panel. "He has a big heart, and that gets him into trouble. Gameplay-wise, we prioritized claw combat because that's an essential part of the character to bring to life and to put into your hands. That meant leveraging his mutant healing factor, bringing forth his deep rage that bubbles up uncontrollably in combat, giving you enhanced senses to track and scout enemies, and feeling his incredibly heavy skeleton as you try to push forth with animalistic strength." The X-Men haven't been formed yet Don't count on Professor Xavier's mutant rescue to save the day, because the X-Men haven't been formed yet in Marvel's Wolverine. Instead, Team X operates in the shadows as a secret group looking to protect mutant interests, but under a very gray compass when compared to the traditional X-Men teams from the comics. Wolverine has amnesia Logan has had several instances in the past where his memory has been fractured. While his mind was eventually healed in the comics, in Marvel's Wolverine, he is still in the dark regarding his past due to some inconvenient amnesia. "Another really important element is his lost memories, and so you'll be peeling back layers of his past, but it's not an origin story," Daly said about this story decision. It looks like Jean Grey will be crucial to helping Logan discover his lost memories, as gameplay suggested that she'll use her psychic powers to guide Logan during dream-like sequences. A plot twist that Wolverine himself is gushing over Don't expect a straightforward story, as similar to its work on Marvel's Spider-Man games, Insomniac wants to drop a surprise or two along the way for fans to discover. "I've always wanted one story to be told that I don't feel like it's ever been kind of investigated as enough," Liam McIntyre--who voices and provides the motion capture for Wolverine--said.  "There's so many twists and turns, and stuff that drops my jaw on the ground. You wait. There's a moment in this that's … I can't wait for you to play it." A modern villain for a modern game Billionaires aren't exactly popular in real life for a number of reasons, and Marvel's Wolverine will see players face off against the mutant-hating Bolivar Trask. Better known as the genius behind the Sentinel program, Trask has funneled all of his considerable resources into exterminating mutantkind. "I know this is going to come as a shock, but billionaires can cause damage. It turns out that having like, unlimited resources and a strong opinion can lead to bad things," said creative director Marcus Smith. "Bolivar Trask is a person who sees mutants as an existential threat to all of humankind, and he will not rest until he's used every ounce of his resources to eliminate every mutant from the planet. And that means he's using his Trask guards--which is basically a personal army--but he's also hired the mercs, the cybernetically enhanced mercenaries, the Reavers." A Jean we've never seen before With mutants being persecuted and on the run, it's up to people like Jean Grey to find a home for her people where they can feel safe in Marvel's Wolverine. "Jean is an unexpected ally that Logan never thought he would have," narrative director Walt Williams said. "This is a Jean that we've never really seen before. She's been out on her own since almost the day that her powers activated, discovering that there are other people in the world out there like her, people with powers, but not necessarily as fortunate, because they can't pass as human. She wants to give them what she didn't have; she wants to give them a home, somewhere safe, where they can just live their normal life. But there really is nowhere safe in the world for mutants at this point in time when someone like Bolivar Trask is out there." There's a spark between Logan and Jean Grey In the comics, the romance between Jean Grey and Logan has always been a forbidden love, given her connection to Scott "Cyclops" Summers. But in Marvel's Wolverine? Even if it isn't romantic at first, there's a spark between the two as their efforts to protect mutants eventually meet. "They cross paths, and she can't believe that this man, this stranger, is willing to do so much to protect people she cares about," Williams said. "Logan has spent as long as he can remember fighting for mutants who either can't or won't fight for themselves, and suddenly, he meets this woman who's willing to stand up and do just that. Not because she thinks it's fun or thrilling like some of his co-workers might, but because she actually cares. I think these two lost and broken souls, they kind of find hope in each other through this, and it gives a little bit of a spark. They don't know if it's going to be romantic or platonic. "They can tell that it's going to be transformative, and it sends them on this path that neither of them are really prepared to go on." Team X has a sinister leader No X-Men? No problem, because Team X is ready to help mutants out--no matter the cost. With a team lineup that consists of Logan, Sabretooth, Mystique, and, in the prequel comic, Sunfire, this black ops group was formed decades ago by Nathaniel Essex. You might recognize that name, because in the comics, Essex is also known as the twisted scientist Mr. Sinister. The prequel comic shows him in his human form, and he operates as the team leader of Team X, who are taking the fight to Trask and his mutant-hunting Sentinel robots. In Marvel's Wolverine, three years have passed since he left the group, but circumstances pull him back into their ranks. Strangely, Essex is one of the few people that Logan trusts, as he found Logan in the woods during one of his feral bouts, helped restore his humanity, and gave him a place on Team X. "I think he respects Essex more than anyone else in the world because Essex has never lied to him," Williams said. Sabretooth and Mystique have some killer threads While it's safe to say that Logan and Sabretooth have some bad blood between them, at least players will get to see the feral mutant in familiar threads. Insomniac Games has updated his signature costume for the game, complete with orange accents and some fur lining. Likewise, Mystique also has a costume taken straight out of the comics, combining her familiar black-and-white garb with skull accents and guns. Go ninja, go ninja, go Marvel's resident ninja death-cult, The Hand, will be one of the factions that menaces Logan during his journey. Currently led by Lady Deathstrike--who Logan has a history with--we'll get to learn more about this secretive sect in the game. In the comics, one of the most notorious acts by The Hand resulted in Wolverine being killed, resurrected, and brainwashed by them to become their ultimate weapon against SHIELD, the X-Men, and the Fantastic Four. There's a Marvel Tokon crossover in Marvel's Wolverine There's a one-two punch of Marvel games on the way in August and September, with Marvel Tokon: Fighting Souls arriving first. The fighting game also features Wolverine, and to celebrate, players can grab a free outfit that dresses the Marvel Tokon version of Logan up in his Earth-1048 costume. When Marvel's Wolverine arrives in September, players can grab a Marvel Tokon-themed outfit that applies a cel-shaded look to Logan. That's probably going to look odd when you're tearing through gangs of Reavers. Wolverine's soundtrack has some The Last of Us DNA https://youtu.be/w6-ZprQ5yHk Award-winning composer David Fleming (HBO's The Last of Us, Superman) has put together the score for Marvel's Wolverine, and if you're curious, you can listen to the first track right now. The full album will launch on August 28.
