Gears Of War Should Have Killed Off Marcus Fenix Years AgoNEW
Gears of War is arguably the most masculine property in video games. A franchise founded on the idea of muscular, refrigerator-sized dudes tearing apart stinky underground monsters with badass chainsa...
Gears of War is arguably the most masculine property in video games. A franchise founded on the idea of muscular, refrigerator-sized dudes tearing apart stinky underground monsters with badass chainsaws until they’re covered in bloody viscera was always destined to attract such a reputation, but this only makes the moments when it subverts our expectations all the more impactful. Instances of quiet within its post-apocalyptic world, or conversations shared between its main cast when everything seems lost, always hit the hardest.  Once you strip away the chainsaw bayonets, you’re left with a profound narrative of grief as Marcus Fenix tries to save a humanity that is already flirting with extinction. While all of his muscles and bravado might make the man seem infallible, Fenix is frequently tasked with the impossible--to save a human race that is constantly under the threat of annihilation. He’s doomed to fail, to eventually fall alongside his comrades until a new generation emerges to continue the fight. This makes perfect narrative sense, and would also give the iconic hero some much needed rest. So, what would happen if The Coalition actually went through with it and killed him? Ever since I first laid eyes on Marcus Fenix as he explored the darkened streets of a fallen city to the melancholic beauty of "Mad World," I have viewed him as a tragic character. He’s fighting an impossible war after being freed from unjustified imprisonment, and spends the entire original trilogy watching close friends succumb to their personal demons or fall to an enemy that shows no signs of slowing. Making the player feel doomed from the outset can be an incredibly powerful storytelling tool, so it’s no surprise that E-Day focuses so heavily on the world falling apart as a younger roster of heroes is forced to take action.  When you turn up to save the day amidst the rubble of Kalona, it’s often about picking up the pieces while walking over fresh corpses of thousands, where finding even two survivors is a reason to celebrate given the circumstances. Not once does Fenix entertain leaving innocent people behind, and you truly feel for him when retreat becomes the only option. Unfortunately, once the excellent opening act concludes, the campaign settles into a predictable routine that can’t help feeling middle-of-the-road. This question has been on my mind for over a decade now, since the character arc of Fenix came to a natural conclusion in Gears of War 3 after the Locust are defeated and humanity is guaranteed a future. Our flawed hero might be stricken with grief by the sacrifices of Dom and his other comrades, but he remains surrounded by people who love and care about him in ways that can now be treasured in a world where survival is guaranteed. But thanks to this need to keep popular franchises alive and avoid disappointing legions of fans, Marcus Fenix has remained integral to Gears ever since.  While the details are cloudy all these years later, there was a time when Gears of War was potentially going to leave the planet of Sera behind in pursuit of pastures new. Speaking to Game Informer as part of a cover story back in 2016, creative director Chuck Osieja claimed that The Coalition was originally thinking about taking Gears to a fresh planet with an all-new cast of characters who were only loosely connected to what came before. So long as Gears focuses on the machinations of conflict with satisfying combat and a worthwhile story, I can see it taking place anywhere, but without the stewardship of Epic Games it settled for the most predictable destination imaginable. Marcus Fenix has a son, the Locust comes back, and humanity once again finds itself on the backfoot. Turns out the original trilogy was for naught.  Gears of War 4 centered on a new trio of likeable characters--JD Fenix, Kait Diaz, and Del Walker--who were all raised in a world without the Locust threat, but still living beneath the thumb of an outdated militaristic society that refuses to leave old habits behind. It’s a more saturated and freeing world, but the very same darkness awaits beneath the surface. As for Marcus Fenix, he has secluded himself in the wilderness after the death of his wife and the trauma of perpetual conflict, resentful of a son who decided to join a military he has actively come to despise. The war may be over, but Marcus has little interest in returning to a world that refuses to learn from its mistakes.  This new trilogy would eventually shift focus away from the Fenix family and instead mostly  on Kait and her strange connection to the new Locust threat, with Gears 5 offering a harsh and sudden player choice in the final act as you’re asked to save the life of either JD or Del. Whoever you don’t choose is murdered before your eyes, but since the trilogy was ditched, at least for now, for a prequel in E-Day, we never get to witness the repercussions. Even Marcus seems pretty unfazed by the death of his only son, when this could have been a killer moment for decades of anger, grief, and resentment to culminate in something meaningful. Instead it’s brushed aside and never mentioned again, joining Halo’s Reclaimer trilogy as stories ripe with potential that Xbox left behind because market relevance was more important. Rather than outliving his son and watching a world he helped save once again fall to ruin, Marcus Fenix should have perished in Gears of War 4 as he passes the torch onto a new generation. Not mauled by a horde of Locust in gruesome fashion as a twisted joke, but giving his life to people he cares about while cementing an enduring hope that things are worth fighting for. Beginning his new character arc as a jaded old man deciding to shield himself from the realities of a world he helped save before reaching a position where he possesses renewed hope in the coming generation would fit the narrative themes while being brave enough to leave our own nostalgia behind. Even in the same universe, we shouldn’t be afraid to fall in love with new characters and new stories, and realise titans like Marcus Fenix are fallible creatures in their own right. Gears of War 4 excelled in making such an iconic symbol of video game masculinity seem vulnerable, but failed to pull the trigger where it mattered most.

Are you ready to unlock the secret ending in Phantom Blade Zero? Every story has its conclusion, including your journey across the Wulin as Soul. That said, there are several requirements that you need to be mindful of, including important quests and points of no return. Our guide discusses every single detail but, because of the topic at hand, you can bet that there will be major spoilers below. It’s pretty much an overarching narrative where you discover the Secrets at the Heart of the Lake, the Herald’s Illusion, and Inanis’s role. These tasks must be completed before you reach the game’s point of no return: Mirage Estate. Here’s a quick summary: After completing the main mission in Pangzhen, you learn that you have to go to the Mirage Estate. However, there’s no way to enter via the steps/thoroughfare. The quest then changes to The Four Serpent Houses. This is when you discern that there’s a hidden passage behind the snake door in the Green Serpent House. After collecting the items needed to open the doorway, you can continue onward to the Mirage Estate. However, if there are still important quests that you need to tackle, you’ll be warned that you have “unfinished business.” You can still fast travel to other regions even if you prematurely enter the Mirage Estate. It’s just that the requirements for the true ending might fail, and you don’t want that to happen. You don't want to enter the Mirage Estate if you still have unfinished story quests in your journal. Early-game quests and overarching plots There are early-game quests that you should tackle to unlock the true ending. These include Deep in the Valley, Crimson Manor, Bloodline Awakening/Long Pillars, and Quick Hand Zhang Treasures. (You can check out these guides we’ve linked for more detailed tips.) Deep in the Valley grants you entry to the lower section of the first region, where you’ll find the Crimson Manor and Long Pillar. Quick Hand Zhang, meanwhile, has you collecting relics in various zones where he has a hideout. From left to right: Deep in the Valley, Bloodline Awakening, and the Crimson Manor. Secrets at the Heart of the Lake: The Village of Lu The Phantom Blade Zero Secrets at the Heart of the Lake is an overarching quest that’s initially started in the Village of Lu. First, you’ll want to collect the six Firefly’s Memory Flowers scattered all over the village. These allow you to enter an optional area near the Temple of the Blue Fireflies, the settlement with a bunch of friendly puppets. Jump down the pit and defeat a boss named Dusk, the Puppeteer to receive rewards and trigger a cutscene. If you defeat an acolyte/scarecrow in the farm fields to the north, you’ll also learn that the objective is part of the quest. These Memory Flowers give you hints regarding a major quest... that's totally optional. Exploration and collectibles: The Illusory Pipa, the Acolyte Masks, and the Herald’s Illusion Certain quests, such as Illusion of Inanis and Sha-Chi-Powered Boat are completed as you continue to explore other regions. For instance, Sha-Chi-Powered Boat leads you to Meigu Lake. As for Whence the Weeping and Pillars of Kunlun, you receive these quests a few hours into the campaign, and you complete them upon clearing the Mountain of Imprisonment. Other quests also play a major factor: A good example is the Illusory Pipa, which lets you dispel impenetrable barriers that open up new areas for exploration. There’s also The Herald’s Illusion, where you seek out the Three-Eyed Puppet’s domains. Lastly, you should acquire all Acolyte Masks, as these items grant entry to a secret area. Note: Completing these tasks nets you two Crystal Puppet Eye items (Yin and Yang). From left to right: The Illusory Pipa, Herald's Illusion, and Acolyte Masks. Secrets at the Heart of the Lake: The Meigu Lake Discordance and Inanis' Realm The aforementioned Acolyte Masks are used to shatter the Four Guardians’ Mirror, a mystical location in the frozen lands in northwestern Meigu Lake. Explore the Discordance realm at your leisure and collect more loot. At the very end, you have to defeat the Blizzard Blademaster. After the Blademaster’s fall, an ominous-looking statue will appear right in front of you. Interact with it and place the two Crystal Puppet Eyes to get transported to the Heart of the Lake. Kill all the cultists to stop their ritual, which then leads you to another encounter with Inanis. This completes the Secrets of the Heart of the Lake. The two Crystal Puppet Eyes teleport you to the ritual site in the Heart of the Lake. What happens when you enter the Mirage Estate and Soulrend Cliff To summarize: If you want to get the secret ending in Phantom Blade Zero, you have to complete all story quests that aren’t tied to the main mission--i.e. those with a purple icon. Once you’ve done all of those, it’s time to enter the Mirage Estate. Doing so causes changes to certain locations, such as the Crimson Forest’s eastern part getting blanketed in snow. Proceed deeper into the Mirage Estate, fighting enemies, taking out bosses, and picking up all sorts of loot. Along the way, don’t forget to complete the following: Bloodline Awakening - The fifth and final Long Pillar is in the estate grounds. Quick Hand’s Request - Quick Hand Zhang has another hideout in the sprawling complex. Eventually, you’ll acquire the Tiger Cannon, a Phantom Edge that can blow up destructible walls. Use it to reach Soulrend Cliff, the last region in the game. There are still lots of enemies, bosses, and rewards in this area, so just keep forging ahead. After defeating what’s supposed to be the final boss, it will be followed by yet another encounter. We don’t want to spoil what happens next, but it should be pretty clear that this is your last foe. Once you’ve vanquished your opponent, sit back, relax, and watch the cutscenes. Discern Inanis' true intentions and proceed further in to the Mirage Estate and Soulrend Cliff. That does it for our guide on how to get the true ending in Phantom Blade Zero. Keep in mind that you can still fast travel to other locations while you’re in the Mirage Estate and also while in Soulrend Cliff (just prior to the last bell). You should be able to find other goodies in the game world, such as the Soul Temper materials for your weapons. Also, don’t forget to battle the Three-Faced Puppet as part of the Triple Threat quest. And, if you’re up for even more challenges, you can try 66 Days Mode after beating the game. You’re a profoundly angry woman who returns to her small hometown armed with nothing but a sledgehammer, which she uses to smash everything in sight. The two mechanics feed into each other to create a fun loop that grows in complexity as new mechanics are introduced. Did I mention the game is also very weird? The silly tale centers on a medieval court designating you the chosen savior to rescue their lost queen (and many other tasks), and the dumb tone adds personality to a sometimes dry genre.
