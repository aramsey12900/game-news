Fields Of Mistria has plenty to keep you busy--from farming to friendship, mining to marriage--but it's also home to a few secrets and cheeky references. We've collated all the things you might have missed about the game, which features plenty of easily overlooked useful features, and options to make your life easier (or more interesting!). Like its contemporaries, Stardew Valley and Story Of Seasons, Fields Of Mistria has plenty of secrets lurking beneath the surface.  This guide does include several spoilers relating to plot and unlockable skills, so if you'd prefer to find them out for yourself you could instead check out our complete Fields Of Mistria crop guide, or our comprehensive guide to romance and marriage.  Secrets in Fields Of Mistria Predict The Weather During Juniper's quest Stinky Stamina Potion, she will gift the player a Crystal Ball. If you're not paying attention, the gift can end up shoved in a corner of your house or--worse--consigned to life in a Chest.  However, the Crystal Ball is one of the most helpful items in the game if you're focused on the health of your farm, as it reliably shows the weather for the next day, meaning you can plan your activities ahead and maximize your useful time. If you interact with the Crystal Ball it'll tell you the weather outright, but if it's prominently displayed, you can also just keep an eye on the icon that shows faintly inside it. The Crystal Ball helps you plan ahead. Secret Caves Later on in the game when you unlock the Dragon's Breath skill, you'll use it for a number of quest-specific purposes, and may even have started using it as a combat strategy in the Mines. But Mistria's map has two secret caves you can only access when this spell is unlocked.  Next to Errol's Cabin is a collapsed cave entrance, which can be blasted with Dragon's Breath to reveal a gold chest and an Animal Sprite Statue. The Animal Sprite Statue can be used to automatically pet your animals every day when charged with Essence--you'll also get a recipe for it.  The other cave is found just past the Beach, and can be found by jumping in the water to the right of the Lighthouse and swimming up and to the right. Once you've blasted open this cave, you'll find another chest, this time containing a Water Sprite statue (and recipe), which allows you to automate the watering of plants.  One of the secret caves, next to Errol's Cabin. A Romantic Gesture If you've picked a date for the Shooting Star Festival, you might not have noticed how much it meant to them. The day after the festival, head to your date's room and check their wall--there's a nice little memento of your time together.  Likewise, you can see the Shooting Star Festival was a treasured moment for one of Mistria's cutest couples, as there's a Star Brooch sitting on their bedside table…  A sweet memento on their wall. Heavy Hints If you're not sure which gifts to give, first off, check out our Fields Of Mistria gift guide, but secondly, there's a really easy way to tell what people want on Saturdays. Just hover outside Darcy's stall and chat to your fellow townies--they'll let you know what drink they're craving, which makes it easy to quickly clean up some Friendship Points.  Gifts From Mimics It can be tempting in the Mines to just hack and slash your way through, especially if you have the fancy armor and a sword infused with magic. But you might be missing out on a secret way to gather free items, recipes, and artifacts. Mimics--the little chest-looking enemies that bite when you approach--can be felled by sword alone, but if you throw something into their mouth before they activate, they'll self-combust and spit out something useful instead.  Mimics look like a cross between a cat and a Chest. Hidden Islands If you've not yet braved the waters of Mistria you might have missed a whole area on the map--albeit a small one. Most people will use their jumping-into-water abilities to get across rivers, but you can actually jump straight into the ocean and go for a swim. Swimming down off the jetty and to the left, then back up again will land you on a hidden island where there is always a dig spot, and often a collection of Ocean Fish waiting to be caught. There's also another smaller island that can be landed on if you want even more solitude.  The hidden islands are easy to miss. Legendary Fish Hack If you keep casting that reel, eventually you'll probably unlock the Fishing Skill Perk that allows you to fish for Legendary Fish. During the morning announcements under the calendar date, the game will now tell you that a "Legendary Fish is nearby". There isn't any more instruction than this, but with a bit of know-how you'll be able to narrow down your search. Each fish is tied to a location, season, and weather type. If you see the "Legendary Fish" pop-up in Spring, it'll be on a windy day where cherry blossoms are blowing around, and you'll be able to find the fish in a pond on the Eastern Road. In Summer, Legendaries arrive on stormy days, and are found in the rivers around The Narrows. Fall Legendaries are found in rivers on the Eastern Road on windy days when leaves are blowing around, and in Winter you'll find a Legendary during a blizzard in the Ocean.  Legendary Fish can be a mystery, but with this tip you'll have them all in no time. Unique Reactions To Hated Gifts You might miss this one purely because you're too good of a friend, but some of Fields Of Mistria's funniest dialogue comes from gifting someone something they really hate. Friendship Points don't go down in the game once you've earned them, so you have a bit of room to experiment if you want to. Try giving Errol a Boiled Egg, for example, or if you're feeling particularly daring, give March a Redhead Worm. Wedding Customization In the days leading up to your wedding you would be forgiven for being too busy to check in on your spouse-to-be, especially as these are the last days you've got to clean up your house for someone else moving in. If you can make a little time for them, though, you'll find you're able to customize your wedding through dialogue choices--although the game will choose for you if you miss the chance.  You can choose your wedding meal and the color of the flowers at the ceremony. The Mournful Clown Painting The Mournful Clown Painting is an item you can only receive if you make a point of listening to everyone yapping in the Sleeping Dragon Inn during Friday Night At The Inn events. A certain conversation between Balor and Nora turns to the painting, and a few more Fridays in, you could be leaving with it. Although, you might not want it.  Easter Eggs In Fields Of Mistria The Little Mermaid If you've learned the Sunken Secrets Skill, you might come across the Mermaid's Comb artifact, which looks suspiciously like a fork. Fans of a certain movie will recognize the Easter egg.  What could it be...? Story Of Seasons/Harvest Moon Reference It's no secret that Fields Of Mistria takes inspiration from the Harvest Moon/Story Of Seasons games--two classics of the genre--but did you spot the direct reference in the character creator? Given as gifts for donating flowers to the Museum in adequate amounts, the Farmer's Cap, Farmer's Overalls, and Farmer Boots when combined are the starting outfit of the default character in Harvest Moon/Story Of Seasons.  If this looks familiar, you're a real one. Extortionate Angel If you've upgraded your town enough to get the second set of Saturday Market vendors, Taliferro and Wheedle, you might have noticed Wheedle stocking a very beautiful but ridiculously expensive Halo cosmetic. Costing 999,999t and coming in five color variants, fans of Gaia Online might have noticed the similarities between this and the Angelic Halo, a basically priceless item in the popular online hangout. We're willing to bet it's a knowing reference. Either way, it's a great way to show off the spoils of your farming empire.

The RE Engine, also known as the Reach for the Moon Engine, has been around for more than a decade. Resident Evil 7: Biohazard, which started a renaissance for Resident Evil games and arguably Capcom in general, was the first game to use it; since then, every Resident Evil game has been developed in it, as have other titles: 2023’s Street Fighter 6 and Exoprimal, 2024’s Dragon’s Dogma 2 and Kunitsu-Gami: Path of the Goddess, and last year’s Monster Hunter Wilds. It’s how Musashi can slice Genma enemies in two, slash through flesh that tears apart, and cut through arms, heads, and more. Way of the Sword reacts to that under the hood with visceral animations that create satisfying deaths for every single Genma in the game, assuming you deplete their stamina like a proper samurai and use Break Issen or one of Musashi’s other finishing moves.  “[This dismemberment] is not something that’s existed in any other title before, and it’s something that, on a technical level, needed to be added to the RE Engine [by Capcom’s engineers],” Yugen says. “Adding this feature was quite difficult because we wanted to combine it with the enemy deaths. Reviewed on: PlayStation 5 Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch, PC Publisher: Humble Games Developer: Chuhai Labs Release: August 13, 2026 (PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC), 2026 (Switch) Rating: Everyone 10+ Wild Blue Skies is not shy about the video game experience it is trying to emulate. You are Bowie, an anthropomorphic dog in a spaceship making your way through a series of levels, most on rails, some taking place in small open areas, to defeat the evil Grimclaw, an anthropomorphic tiger with a giant cyborg head. Developer Chuhai Labs and Vitei Backroom Inc. are transparently presenting their take on Star Fox 64 – a noble and welcome endeavor – but many shortcomings prevent it from reaching the nostalgic heights of its inspirational arcade action game. Mechanically, controlling your ship feels okay, but it dips into slog territory more often than I would like. The perspective of the reticle makes it hard to see what you’re firing at (and there is no first-person option), and hitting the occasional wall bounces you around like a pinball. It can be especially frustrating on the open-ended levels that are not on rails. Locking on and firing your standard lasers are also mapped to separate buttons, meaning you have to choose between the two actions, which is limiting. Also, the range to lock on to fire an extra powerful homing shot is too short. By the time I locked on to enemies, it was often too late, and the enemy had disappeared off-screen.The look of Wild Blue Skies is attractive, though, with bright colors and cool ship designs. I like the look of the main cast and their performances, along with the performances of all the bosses and secondary characters. Everyone’s characterization, however, leaves a lot on the table. The bosses all boast and yell with unfounded confidence, and I felt bad when teammates complained when I accidentally (allegedly) shot them. But even with the quality acting, everyone is undefined and generic. Dialogue could be swapped among the characters, I don’t think I would even notice.The levels offer some visual variety, but are also generic. Moving from a desert level to an ice level is a nice change of pace, but little has been done to make them feel novel. Also, many of the on-rails levels begin with characters pointing out the limited visibility, whether in a storm or from snow. Narratively, I like flying through a hurricane in a game like this, but when the elements keep making things hard to see, it feels like a story excuse for poor draw distance. When you do make it through the hailstorm, or the tornado, or the lava flows, the bosses at the end arguably take too long to defeat. Like its Star Fox 64 inspiration, there are incentives to replay the campaign to chase different challenges and see the alternate paths. Seeing the game to credits is completely doable, but I got frustrated by moments of unfair challenge. Health drops are infrequent, and deaths are often the result of running into a stray enemy, bouncing against walls, or getting overwhelmed by screen-filling danger that I didn’t feel equipped to avoid. It made many defeats feel like the game’s fault rather than my own, which is especially annoying when burning through lives sends you back to the beginning of the campaign.Bowie and pals’ adventure successfully impersonates the Star Fox 64 experience, but it’s an underwhelming impression. Nostalgic moments and satisfying action appear occasionally, but it pays homage to a fault and doesn’t find space to present its own personality. It has the unfortunate timing of releasing and being compared to this year’s Star Fox on Switch 2 – an excellent remake of Star Fox 64. But even if I did not have that game in my immediate memory, I would be hard-pressed to identify anywhere that it has improved on the genre with design lessons learned since 1997, or even risen to meet the ones that already existed even 30 years ago. Half-Mermaid, the studio led by Sam Barlow, the mind behind games such as Immortality, Her Story, and Telling Lies, has announced its next project, Precognition. Described as a sci-fi horror adventure, the game is being made in collaboration with horror filmmaker Branden Cronenberg (Possessor, Infinity Pool).Like previous Half-Mermaid titles, Precognition is a non-linear FMV adventure, meaning it features live-action scenes. The story centers on Diana, a woman who is implanted with an experimental brain implant that allows her to receive visions of the future. Diana is tasked with exploring these visions, focusing on specific moments, and committing them to her present-day memory, which will have consequences affecting how the future ultimately unfolds.“We’ve really been trying to test the limits of non-linear storytelling, focusing on how the game feels to play, while encouraging player agency and freedom,” Half Mermaid co-founder Sam Barlow says in a press release. “We’ve pushed ourselves even harder with Precognition by bringing the feel of investigation and mystery games into a world of cause-and-effect, with a story that spins out as a consequence of your choices .” Half-Mermaid has enlisted Brandon Cronenberg to co-develop and write Precognition. Cronenberg, the son of acclaimed filmmaker David Cronenberg, wrote and directed Infinity Pool (2023), Possessors (2020), and Antiviral (2012). According to Barlow, Cronenberg was the “perfect person” to work with due to his horror filmmaking expertise and his existing familiarity with Immortality and Her Story. “The films I make tend to be subjective and experiential, with the horror emerging as one person’s perspective of the world warps around them,” Brandon Cronenberg says. “The games that Sam and the team have made over the years are subjective experiences by their very nature – no two people will have the same experience with them. So it’s the perfect place to explore these themes further”.Precognition was first teased in January 2024 as Project C, one of two announced projects by Half Mermaid that also includes the yet-to-be-revealed Project D. Interestingly, Precognition had previously been advertised as being published under Blumhouse Games during Summer Game Fest 2024, but that no longer appears to be the case. Kinetic Publishing, the publishing arm of Phasmophobia developer Kinetic Games, has taken on funding duties and announced Precognition as part of a dedicated publisher showcase on August 12. Precognition is set to launch in spring 2028 for PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 2, and PC. To read more about the works of Sam Barlow/Half Mermaid, be sure to read our previous reviews for Telling Lies and Her Story.

Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave Preview - A Slow Build That Hasn't Quite Hooked Me Yet (But I Want To Keep Playing)NEW
...
Platform: Publisher: Nintendo Developer: Intelligent Systems Release: September 17, 2026 I went into the opening chapters of Fire Emblem: Fortune’s Weave with close to zero expectations. I knew there was a colosseum and that fights would happen there… and that’s about it. Now, hours into the game, I never could have expected what’s played out thus far. Fortune’s Weave looks back at the series, bringing forward systems from the past like Fire Emblem Gaiden’s dungeon crawling (which Japanese players experienced in 1992 before it was remade in 2017’s Fire Emblem Echoes: Shadows of Valentia); but it also looks forward, literally, with a sci-fi future where magic can be shot out of guns and flashback plotlines make up the primary narrative. It hasn’t quite coalesced into the gripping marriage of strategy gameplay and war and political intrigue typical of the series, but its early hooks have me searching for my Switch 2 any second of game time I can carve into my daily schedule.  Fortune’s Weave wastes no time, throwing you right into a scenario I’m not sure I can entirely explain. But the hero you create using simple, to-the-point character customization options is playable only for a moment. Eventually, after seeing medieval-dressed characters interact with advanced computer systems and magic guns ripped from the future, you will reach an Obelisk Chamber, and a bright-colored goddess (I think?) named Fortuna who speaks on behalf of Sothis, the young goddess from Fire Emblem: Three Houses. Sothis urges you to dive back into the past and rewrite the histories of fabled heroes so that they might help you in the seemingly apocalyptic present. Again, I think. A lot is going on, and what I’ve played in this preview-designated section is clearly just the placing of Fortune’s Weave’s first few chess pieces on the board. There are four obelisks to choose from, each with an associated hero-turned-fallen-warrior-who-has-now-become-an-evil-flame-lord from the past. After selecting one of the four, you’re thrust into a narrative that leads to Dagsion, the Dagdan Empire’s capital. The Heroic Games, Fire Emblem’s spin on ancient Roman colosseum fights and gladiator tournaments, take place here and it is where each obelisk hero plays a special role. Though I’m not privy to those roles yet, it’s been fun seeing how Leda, a magical bard with a bloodlust for her father’s murderer, arrives there.  Leda’s life is ripped away from her as a girl – with her eyes set on revenge, she travels the world in search of clues and her next coin, wowing alehouse patrons with song and dance. I suspect each hero’s story flows into combat the way I’ve experienced it with Leda, who uses melodies from her mystical vihuela to buff nearby allies on the battlefield. That’s exactly what her Blaze Art does, a character-specific move that turns the tide when used correctly. But because it saps HP to do so, when and how you use it in the series’ trademark strategy grid-based battles matters. The same can be said for Combat Arts, which power up specific weapons but chip away at their durability. These two systems create new strings of tension on familiar grids, which is where the classic rock-paper-scissors style of turn-based strategy gameplay returns. It remains satisfying to create forward pushes, flanks, and the like with intentional advances, and the accompanying battle animations are the best they’ve ever looked; the same can be said for all of Fortune’s Weave, which highlights Intelligent Systems' commanding artistic vision with the boon of power that comes from developing exclusively for Switch 2. While I’m happy to see dungeon crawling return, the associated turn-based combat (notably not grid-based battles) is so far underwhelming – a button to fast forward through these fights or skip to the conclusion screen entirely suggests a lack of confidence in these systems.  Game Informer video editor Eric Van Allen deliberately chose a different obelisk when prompted, instead peeking into the history of Cai, a teenager in search of a better life.Cai is an optimistic, Shonen-esque hero who's stumbled into a greater lot in life than he realizes. It seems clear that he and his childhood pals' path through the Heroic Games will probably not be as lighthearted as they hope. Going on excursions outside Dagsion to build up their renown is enjoyable. Quests, hunts for useful resources, or just a quick trip out to see more of the outlying country net some good returns and spur on the idea of Cai slowly becoming as recognizable and formidable as the other lords.There is still so much Fortune’s Weave left to go for both of us, but the hooks are in – it’s time to see where these obelisks take us, and I’m happy to say I have no idea where that will be.
