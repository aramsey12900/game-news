Gears Of War: E-Day Review In ProgressNEW
The last time we played through a new mainline Gears of War game was 2019, which feels like a lifetime ago. In that time, The Coalition has been churning away at Gears of War: E-Day, a preque...
The last time we played through a new mainline Gears of War game was 2019, which feels like a lifetime ago. In that time, The Coalition has been churning away at Gears of War: E-Day, a prequel story to the original Gears of War games. Marcus Fenix and Dom Santiago stepping back into the spotlight after serving as the anchors for the original trilogy may feel like developer The Coalition waving the white flag on trying to innovate on the franchise following the division surrounding Gears of War 4 and Gears 5, but once you play through the campaign, it's apparent the studio hardly rested on its laurels.Gears of War: E-Day tells the story of perhaps the most pivotal event in the Gears of War universe. Following the conclusion of the Pendulum Wars, Marcus and Dom are in the city of Kalona when the ground shakes and opens to reveal the Locust horde. Watching civilians futilely run for their lives as Locusts rip them apart adds an immediate horror vibe to the overall tone. And even as you are essentially a walking tank of a soldier, that vibe never lets up. Watching the city of Kalona fall in real time as each of humanity's Hail Mary attempts is thwarted by the invading force establishes the Locusts as a true threat. Even as an armored-and-armed-to-the-teeth COG, an overwhelming sense of helplessness colors the entire experience; no matter how many waves of enemies I mow down, there is no end in sight and no salvation for humanity. The action set-pieces are impressive and frequent; one sequence on an airstrip stands out as one of my favorite shootouts this decade, while some less-curated areas that involve traversing Kalona's city streets break up the nonstop action with minor exploration elements and sidequests. Most of the campaign takes place in downtown Kalona, and when you're in the streets, you're free to make your way as directly or circuitously as you see fit. I couldn't help but stop and help stranded platoons or look for nearby collectibles. Uncovering hard cases became my favorite side activity, as they reward mods for your weapons. I did my best to collect every mod I could for each individual in-game weapon; tinkering with the arsenal to match my playstyle became my preferred pastime in between shootouts.Blasting through the relentless hordes is as enjoyable and intense as ever. Whether I was chainsawing enemies to bits with my Lancer, picking them off from afar with my Hammerburst, or outright melting them with an acid-blasting Incinerator, I actively sought out even the optional conflicts. But you don't just fight standard grubs; Gears of War: E-Day throws so many special minibosses that can completely disrupt your best-laid strategies at a moment's notice that you're almost trained to expect the unexpected. Thankfully, you can use new tactical abilities, like a healing injection or an enemy-tagging drone, as well as new traversal and cover mechanics to level the playing field. And though they may stop following you out of nowhere, occasionally get in the way, and yell out cringeworthy one-liners, you always have your squad of four with you even while playing solo; if you're downed, you can be revived by a squadmate a limited number of times per conflict. Those squadmates are compelling components of the overall narrative. Marcus and Dom are dealing with the loss of Carlos, Dom's brother and Marcus' best friend. Mags is a strong-willed former soldier who reluctantly gets sucked into the fray when the Locusts attack. The weak link is Lucas, a newly enlisted soldier who spouts random facts and likes to follow the COG handbook. For too much of the story, Lucas is underdeveloped, but a late-game story involving the newbie really elevated him to stand shoulder-to-shoulder with Marcus, Dom, and Mags; I just wish it happened sooner.Gears of War: E-Day's campaign is one of my favorites of 2026. The characters, action, and story coalesce into something I won't soon forget, thanks in large part to The Coalition's smart utilization of Kalona. Unfortunately, the multiplayer servers weren't turned on before the embargo lift, but if I get as much enjoyment from the PvP and PvE modes on offer, Gears of War: E-Day could easily jump up near the top of my list of favorite games in the series.

Gears Of War: E-Day’s Campaign Is Buried In The PastNEW
The best prequels typically share the same thing in common. Whether it's Red Dead Redemption 2, Yakuza 0, Metal Gear Solid 3, or Halo: Reach, each game tells a story that recontextualizes what came be...
The best prequels typically share the same thing in common. Whether it's Red Dead Redemption 2, Yakuza 0, Metal Gear Solid 3, or Halo: Reach, each game tells a story that recontextualizes what came before. Revisiting a game after playing its prequel should alter the experience, providing you with new knowledge that changes how you perceive certain characters, places, and scenes.  The events of Red Dead Redemption 2 add emotional weight to the first game and John Marston's mission by humanizing his targets, just as Yakuza 0 deepens the bond between Kazuma Kiryu and Akira Nishikiyama, making the first game's tragic finale much more impactful. Gears of War: E-Day doesn't do anything like this.  The Coalition's latest venture back to the world of COGs and Locusts is a prequel to the 2006 original with nothing new to say. You already know where it's going: protagonists Marcus Fenix and Dominic Santiago go to war as the human world of the surface is invaded by the subterranean Locust, and the heroes survive to fight another day. Even then, there aren't any surprises on the way there.  The most notable changes occur within the framework of its third-person, cover-based shooting. It's still distinctly Gears of War--full of meaty men wielding crunchy weapons--just with a few new bells and whistles added to improve aspects of its signature action. It's good, and the increased spectacle is impressive, but it doesn't do enough to elevate to greatness.    For a series that has struggled, or been unwilling, to let go of the past, purposefully going back 14 years prior to the surprise attack that incited the series at least sounded like a solid idea. By tackling the past head-on, with one of the most important days in the series' fictional history, The Coalition gave itself the chance to examine the origins of Marcus, Dom, and the antagonistic Locust Horde from the very moment their fates intertwined.  Instead, E-Day tells a fairly boilerplate story; one that feels like it was written specifically for series newcomers, rather than anyone who's already fought to the conclusion of the Locust War. There's one moment, for instance, when a character explains that throwing a grenade into a Locust-spawning Emergence Hole will close it, while familiar enemy types like Boomers, Tickers, and Reavers--creatures fans have been fighting for two decades--are given grand entrances. Introducing or refamiliarizing players with certain aspects of the Gear's universe isn't a knock on the game in and of itself, but the heavy emphasis on treating these known quantities as novel is indicative of a wider issue. E-Day seems uninterested in surprising longtime fans or appealing to the series' own history.  Marcus is still as gruff, authority-bucking, and no-nonsense as ever, despite a 14-year gap and everything that happened in between, while Dom's still going through the same emotional beats as before, worrying about the fate of his wife Maria. The pair haven't fully developed the close relationship they share in the original game, and mostly bond over their shared grief for the loss of Dom's brother, but their relationship isn't explored deeply enough to add anything interesting to what we already know.  Even something like the invention of the iconic Mark 2 Lancer--the assault rifle with a chainsaw bayonet that has been a staple of the Gears of War games since the beginning--happens off-screen with a character you only briefly meet twice. It's a throwaway scene that demonstrates just how much this prequel feels disappointingly superfluous.   https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=xTxwN-Ck1Zw The story is entertaining at face value, with plenty of intense action and cheesy one-liners. There's a horror-tinged element to some of the early missions, as you witness the sheer brutality of the Locust firsthand. And I also like the two new squad members, Mags Carter and Lucas Reyes, even if the game takes a while to touch on their backstories.  It is odd, however, that neither character is ever put in any peril. This is reserved for characters we already know survive, so the stakes remain relatively low throughout E-Day. The story also ends with more questions than answers, seemingly setting up for a sequel or DLC at the very least. Meanwhile, the cliffhanger at the end of Gears 5 remains unaddressed. Combat is E-Day's strong point. The Locust are still veritable meat bags, with bodies that explode into dense chunks and heads that pop like watermelons–combined with crunchy sound design that's pure dopamine. Classic weapons like the Gnasher shotgun and Longshot sniper rifle are as satisfying as ever, and brutally slicing a Locust in half with the Lancer's chainsaw remains an evergreen delight. You can also find and equip weapon mods this time, too. This adds slightly more depth to combat, letting you tune gun behavior and add scopes, laser sights, and bayonets to weapons that wouldn't otherwise have them. Perfect reloads, the series' classic mechanic in which you get a damage bonus for hitting the reload button in time with an on-screen prompt, can be altered to grant incendiary ammo or increase the weapon's fire rate. You can also find mods that reduce flinch, speed up reloads, increase damage range, and more. It's a heavily customizable system that gives you the freedom to completely alter how some weapons function or simply boost aspects that play to their strengths. It's not just your firearms that have been tinkered with, either. Marcus and company are much more agile than they have been in past games, able to slide, jump, quickly mantle over cover, and climb ledges with relative ease. This ties into the game's renewed focus on verticality in its level design, although being nimbler doesn't meaningfully transform how it feels to play Gears.  E-Day is still a cover shooter. You now have more varied ways of moving from cover to cover, but the moment-to-moment action isn't all that different from what it was back in 2006, with an emphasis on popping up to take opportune shots or maneuvering to flank dug-in enemies. It's surprising how well the Gears formula holds up 20 years later, but also reveals how little has changed. Gears 5 previously broke the series' mold by introducing open sandbox environments that allowed you to tackle objectives in the order of your choosing and explore vast areas aboard vehicles. E-Day follows suit but reins things in considerably, ditching the vehicles and focusing on smaller spaces. The entire game takes place in and around the city of Kalona, alternating between explorable open areas and more linear missions.  When you're free to traverse blocks of the city, you'll find myriad collectibles and occasional side missions, but these sections mostly just bring the game's frenetic pace to a halt. The side missions lack variety, typically tasking you with surviving waves of enemies, and with many shops and apartments sharing the same layout, there's little of interest to find by exploring every nook and cranny. Teaming with other players helps, as you can spread out and find everything much faster, but co-op is obviously optional. It's a shame, because Kalona's architecture is visually fascinating, mixing classical European-style buildings with more modern industrial factories--not to mention a gothic edge. You also get to see it gradually destroyed by revisiting the same locations a few hours apart. Witnessing a street where you once had a firefight turn into a giant sinkhole that cuts through the metro and beyond is an effective sight, showing how quickly the Locust decimated the city.  This is the one area where E-Day does something interesting with its open-ended sections. Otherwise, its linear missions are far superior, honing in on that classic Gears action while going big on spectacle with some cinematic flair. These moments are Gears of War: E-Day at its very best. There might be some new ideas here, but you also get the feeling that The Coalition isn't trying to reinvent the wheel. That's fine, but this also made me realize that my appetite for this kind of game just isn't as strong as it was, say, 10 years ago. There are only so many times you can man a gun turret before you never want to see another one again. For better or worse, E-Day is distinctly Gears, and I had a good time with it, even if the story is disappointing and the open areas are fairly dull. But that's all it is: a good game, propped up by combat that hasn't changed all that much over the past 20 years. That's enough to skate by, but I wish Gears of War would dig deeper.
