It's shown on the bottom left of your screen as a yellowish bar under your HP. It's not quite stamina, as sprinting and using standard attack buttons don't deplete it. However, your more powerful Sha-Chi attacks (Triangle/Y) do deplete the Sha-Chi, as does blocking enemy attacks with L1/LB. Your enemy can also break your Sha-Chi with multiple and/or powerful attacks. When your Sha-Chi is low or depleted, switch to your second main weapon, as it will come out with a stylish attack AND a full Sha-Chi bar. Your other weapon will then have a cooldown (that can be decreased as you level up), but when that cooldown is over, you can switch to the other main weapon with a full Sha-Chi bar once more – it's this rotation that will keep you from ever being left open and helpless with an empty Sha-Chi bar. Try every weapon: Phantom Blade Zero has 30 main weapons and 25 Phantom Edge weapons, which are limited-use (requiring you to build up charges to use). You'll earn some by completing main quests and defeating bosses throughout, but many are optional finds that require you to stray from the golden path. No matter how you find a weapon, you must try every single one you find. I can't tell you how many weapons I found, started using, and quickly said, "Oh yup, this is going to be my new main," only to discover another new weapon that replaced it. There are so many cool weapons and the next weapon you find could be your new favorite weapon. Don't hoard upgrade materials: I know, I know. We all hoard materials, potions, and this and that because we might need them later. But I'm telling you: Do not hoard your materials in Phantom Blade Zero. Developer S-Game wants you to upgrade all of your weapons and discover ones you like, dislike, love, and hate. As you upgrade weapons, new combo abilities and perks are added, making them feel even more unique and powerful. You will discover so many upgrade materials – seriously, like so many. You'll also find plenty of revert items that let you extract every resource poured into a weapon, so you can use those materials on a different weapon. For the most part, though, many of the weapons use their own unique materials that maybe one or two other weapons will use so you can safely upgrade without worrying about sacrificing an upgrade for a different weapon.   Explore everywhere: Phantom Blade Zero has an incredible map, one of my all-time favorites. You can follow the golden path, marked by golden objective markers, but doing so will rob you of plenty of engaging side content. Stray from the golden path to explore caves, hidden alleys, buildings, underground tunnels, and more. Following the story-critical purple side quests, the white side quests, and the white collectible markers will take you to new places to explore. Obviously, you need to do those. You'll also stumble upon quests with purple objective markers. While these are technically optional side quests, I highly recommend completing them all. They might as well be main quests because the only way to get the "perfect" ending (the only one that will be satisfactory, to be honest) is to complete every purple quest before heading toward the game's finale. S-Game says skipping the purple quests means missing "roughly one-third of the game's content and reaching its most basic ending." The game has a "last chance" warning before the finale: Enjoy the game at your own pace and don't stress about completing this or that in the lead-up to the finale because when you reach the final area, the game will make it very clear you're heading toward the ending, with a clear "this is your last chance to clean up and complete side content" type of message. However, some of them have multiple phases (usually just two). Focus on completing the first phase; use all your medicines, abilities, and everything else to take it down, because even if you die in the second phase, the first phase will remain complete. When you die and respawn at the bonfire-like Phantom Bell, return to the boss to start the second phase.   Track down every Memory Flower: Memory Flowers are optional collectibles that really shouldn't be. They're pretty easy to spot while exploring: a single tall, glowing blue flower standing among a circle of smaller blue flowers. Interact with them, and you get to listen to an interesting story vignette. Most of these flowers are part of a set of one, two, three, or more. When you complete a set, you will gain HP. Smaller sets grant a 3% HP boost, while the largest Memory Flower sets grant a 10% HP boost. Interacting with these Memory Flowers is completely optional BUT interacting with them and completing sets is the only way you can gain additional HP in the game. So, like, make sure you find them. If you hear coins jingling, you're near a treasure master: The game hints at this in the second biome, but a mysterious man collects treasures across Wulin. After you talk to him, he will ask you to find three specific treasures (in green-glowing treasure chests) in each region and reward you with an interesting accessory or something else. However, you can't pick up this scavenger-hunt quest in each region until you find the man. The best way to track him down is to listen for coins jingling. If you hear that sound, look around. You should see a sword or three stuck into the environment around you, with a chain of golden coins hanging from it. If you see this, it means the man is nearby. The game has search-action/Metroidvania elements: the aforementioned Phantom Edge weapons are great for combat, but a handful also double as tools for exploration. Some can destroy floors beneath you to reveal hidden tunnels; others can unlock secret staircases, while another lets you grapple to places previously out of reach. As you explore, you will come across areas you don't yet have the Phantom Edge to reach.
