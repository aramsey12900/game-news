That’s because you need vampiric powers like Shadowstep and Planeshift to reach ledges and walk on walls. One is in the northern part of Vale Sangora, while the remaining three are in the southern reaches. Here are some additional tidbits: You can head to these locations regardless if it’s day or night. Your main goal is to follow the windchimes. Hold down the Focus button to see them more clearly. Interacting with the uriashi glyph itself causes two time segments to elapse. The reward from each glyph is a Ravenous Flock Manual. These are the Sacred Stones in the northern (left) and southern (right) parts of the world map. Sacred Stone 1: A Pilgrimage for Power - Bear Trouble Location: This will likely be the first Sacred Stone that you stumble upon in the campaign. It’s west of the Briar Sloughs Scouting Tower. Tip: Save your game once you reach the top of the plateau. You’re about to fight a Ravenous Bear soon. Once the bear is dead, check the uriashi corpse and dagger. Follow the windchimes (left) and be ready to take out a bear (right). Sacred Stone 2: A Pilgrimage for Power - Dark Thing Location: This Blood of Dawnwalker Sacred Stone is near the Westridge Outpost shrine. From the abandoned outpost, keep going north and climb the rocky crag. Tip: You’ll battle an Essence of Grief miniboss--i.e. Vidmo specter. Sacred Stone 3: A Pilgrimage for Power - Fanatics Location: Northeast of St. Tyna’s Grove. It’s near a bandit camp north of one of Ambrus’ outposts. Tip: Take out the wolves as you continue up the sloping path. Then, examine the corpses of the fanatics and the cross that they left behind. The uriashi who killed them will show up after your investigation. You’ll be able to let him go if you wish. You probably don't want to mess with this fella. Sacred Stone 4: A Pilgrimage for Power - The Ritual Location: This Sacred Stone in The Blood of Dawnwalker is in the southern edge of the map. Lightning will strike your surroundings, but keep heading onward until you reach a clearing. Tip: You have to fight a Witch of the Barren Peaks. This miniboss casts lightning spells that are unblockable. Dodge out of the way and strike her down when you see an opening. Note: The Witch of the Barren Peaks drops an Aether Flow Manual, a Crimson Band (epic ring), and Aetheric Essence (epic material). The burning trees (left) lead you to a stormcaller who wields the power of lightning (right). Rewards: Ravenous Flock Manuals As mentioned earlier, each Sacred Stone glyph that you interact with nets you a Ravenous Flock Manual. This is a Witchcraft ability that stuns a target and triggers additional area attacks. With all four manuals, you should be able to level up this ability to its maximum rank, assuming you want to spend the skill points and time segments. That does it for our guide on where to find all Sacred Stones for the Pilgrimage for Power quest in The Blood of Dawnwalker. It involves getting the ingredients and instructions from the herbalist, Anca, and then making the correct brew. Note: This whole process requires two to three Time Segments in the Time Bar, so make sure you have enough to spare before the day ends. How to get the ingredients from Anca You can find Anca in the southeastern part of Laslea Glen. Meet up with her by the river, then chat with her inside her hut. Pay Anca 50 denarii to buy the items. “I really have to go” - This costs 1x Time Segment. Coen leaves with the items. “Gladly. I’ve missed it too” - This costs 2x Time Segment instead. It leads to a short but sweet moment where Coen bonds with Anca. It’s worth seeing if you plan on romancing Anca later on. Anca has the ingredients you need. How to make the correct brew Of course, you still need to brew the potion if you want to save Coen’s mom in The Blood of Dawnwalker: This process costs 1x Time Segment. Before reentering your home, read the Instructions From Anca in your inventory. After the cutscene where Coen starts making the brew, pick “use hot water.” Followed by “three scoops.” Coen will feed Esme the stew, which contains the medicine. Note: After this, you can spar with Coen’s father to receive the Charge Manual and 100 XP. Don't mess up the stew. Esme’s fate When night falls, the Blood Mass begins. Esme’s fate will be drastically different depending on whether or not you were able to cure her. If you neglected this objective or you didn’t follow the brewing instructions correctly, Esme will continue to remain ill. The vampire boyars will notice her ailment and they’ll rip her apart. If Esme was able to drink the potion, she’ll still look weakened, but the vampires won’t discard her immediately. She’ll live, albeit as a prisoner, along with the rest of Coen’s family. Depending on your actions during the prologue, Esme will either perish instantly or survive as a prisoner. That does it for our guide on how to save Coen’s mother in The Blood of Dawnwalker. Stuntman: Hollywood Is Easier Than The Original – And That's A Good ThingNEW
...
Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC Publisher: Saber Interactive Developer: Saber Interactive Release: TBA Released in 2002 for the PlayStation 2, the original Stuntman was a great concept that, for me anyway, proved far too challenging to be fun. Perfection was demanded, and extreme specificity was required for progress. It took all the fun out of controlling a car in a video game, which I imagine is how movie stunt drivers probably really feel after the fifth take of the same sequence, so in that way maybe it was a perfect simulation of the stunt driving experience.But I have always remained enamored with the fun concept. Who wouldn’t want to drive a stunt car in a Hollywood movie without the need to sign personal injury waivers and the danger of bodily harm? I played through four levels of Stuntman: Hollywood at Gamescom, including, perhaps most notably, the recently announced Jurassic World level. The team building the game at Saber Interactive, I believe, took away the same opinion I had back in 2002: if this game were more forgiving, it would be a lot more fun. That isn’t to say Stuntman: Hollywood is a total breeze. With the exception of the opening tutorial level, I had to retry each level at least once, but the challenge doesn’t feel as insurmountable as it did 24 years ago. Perfection is encouraged but not demanded, and specificity is only required in certain places.In practice, the game almost feels like a slowed-down rhythm game crossed with Simon Says. As you’re driving through the sets – perhaps the biggest sets ever constructed for any movie ever – a director shouts instructions in your ear: drift here, get close to that obstacle there, hit that ramp, drive through that other obstacle there. Along with the director’s voice, there are also plenty of on-screen indicators directing you where to go and telling you what to do. The first run through a level is frantic as you try to quickly respond to every directive, and it can be overwhelming, but there is a joy to being successful, and I maybe had the most fun replaying a level after completing it at least once. I played two levels that drove me through cities, one where I was trying to escape a collapsing factory during an earthquake, and finally the Jurassic World level. In that level, I drove a motorcycle away from an angry volcano while dinosaurs ran beside me, ramp-jumping over an angry Tyrannosaurus rex and sliding under the occasional obstacle. I haven’t been on many movie sets, but I can confirm from visiting the film set of Detective Pikachu that the animated monsters are not usually there when filming. My understanding is that they’re usually added later in something called “post”. But what do I know, and who cares? I would rather have the dinosaurs there to race with me and jump over, accuracy be damned. Despite all the bombastic action happening on screen, one of the most notable elements of the Jurassic World level was the fast-paced, percussive remix of the classic Jurassic Park theme. It was bizarre, and you would never catch me listening to it outside of the game, but in context the absurd arrangement worked pretty well.My big takeaway from Stuntman: Hollywood is that the two-decade-old video game concept might have finally found its footing. For those who want to pull off an undeniable take and nail every moment perfectly, I believe that still exists here. But for those like me who just want to participate in an admittedly hyperbolic simulation of being a stunt driver on a movie set, Stuntman: Hollywood is the most welcoming version of the experience the franchise has offered to date.

Ace Combat 8: Wings Of Theve Multiplayer Hopes To Bolster The Series' Long-Term SupportNEW
...
Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC Publisher: Bandai Namco Developer: Bandai Namco Release: October 2, 2026 Rating: Teen The Ace Combat series is riding high on the tailwind of its seventh entry's success, and October will see Ace Combat 8: Wings of Theve take off. So far, we've seen quite a bit about its single-player campaign, but now, the Project Aces team is unveiling its multiplayer side.For players like myself, Ace Combat has frequently been a single-player affair, centered on the campaign and missions. The options to play with friends were always there, but I rarely spent much time exploring them. Ace Combat 8: Wings of Theve is trying to change that. Speaking to the developers at Gamescom 2026, they cited the response to Ace Combat 7: Skies Unknown as a reason why they wanted to enhance the multiplayer offering this time around."Because Ace Combat 7 was really successful and became a long-tail title, we also identified that it's really critical for the players to be able to play for the long term," said producer Manabu Shimomoto, via translator. "So for that, to enable that, the online mode and environmental platform needs to be very solid." Ace Combat 8: Wings of Theve is planning 100-player online lobbies where you can customize a pilot avatar and walk around, hanging out with other fliers. More options will open up as you complete missions and build up your hangar. I'm both cautious and curious about the cosmetic aspect. Bandai Namco confirms there will be a battle pass – Ace Pass –  with free and premium options, and there's also a supply shop where you can obtain items that have a crate-opening aesthetic.Of course, the main attraction is actually flying and fighting. Co-Op Assault and Co-Op Warfare are the two big pillars of Ace Combat 8's multiplayer, allowing players to dip into PvE or PvP, respectively. Co-Op Assault will launch two teams of four into missions competing for the highest score, with live players as your wingmen.In Co-Op Warfare, teams of four battle against each other alongside allied land, air, and sea units. Once again, you compete for high scores, with escalating scores increasing threat level and netting you a score multiplier. Higher threat also means the enemy gets more points for shooting you down, though, so big swings can happen in the middle of a match."There are some players who are not really big fans of playing against people, so PvE is really critical for those players," Shimomoto tells me. "Whereas some players really enjoy a competitive environment, so [options for both players] is really critical." Both modes will allow several aircraft roles as well, ranging from Fighters and Attackers to Multirole all-rounders and support-driven Electronic Warfare aircraft. You'll be able to progress along tracks for your chosen aircraft, building them up as you would within the single-player campaign. There are also online-exclusive options and visual customization.Additionally, Project Aces has some special modes, dubbed Scramble and Emergency Sortie, that can pop up in the multiplayer lobby. Scramble pits teams of four against a tough rival NPC squad, while Emergency Sortie hosts special fights against iconic Ace Combat bosses and superweapons.All of this circles back to the lobby, where a jukebox with music from past games and an arcade with a selection of Namco classics awaits as well. The team wouldn't confirm which games are being selected for inclusion outside of Pac-Man, which you can see in the reveal trailer, but did say we'll see some games from Namco's history in there.It's certainly exciting to see the Aces team build out such a large multiplayer offering. The PvPvE and special sorties look like cool reasons to revisit Ace Combat and to get other friends into the series as well. Hopefully this adds to an already impressive-looking Ace Combat. If you want to learn more about the upcoming entry in Bandai Namco's flying-shooter series, check out our preview from Issue 379 of Game Informer.
