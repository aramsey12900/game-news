Activision Should Hire Former Halo Devs For Next Mainline Game, Former Franchise Director SaysNEW

Now that development of future Halo games is being handled by Activision Blizzard's hands, some former developers of the series have spoken out to share their thoughts on the shocking but not entirely surprising mix-up. This includes the former franchise creative director, who has provided some salient advice to the Call of Duty publisher. Frank O’Connor has worked on Halo for over 20 years, first working as a writer and community manager before serving as the game's franchise creative director from 2003 to 2023. This is a guy who knows Halo like the back of his hand, and with all that series expertise, he took to LinkedIn on September 25 to share some thoughts on how to make the next Halo great. "Pro-Tip: Do NOT tie it to hardware/services launch cycles, do not try to force it into a shape (or aging platform) that it can't fit and do NOT abandon sandbox," O’Connor said in response to Xbox's former social media manager, Genevieve Harrison, who was pontificating on Activision Blizzard's development tactics. "Pro-Pro-Tip: Hire Bungie, Halo Studios and other laid-off folk who are bursting w. talent and ideas that got nerfed or shut down over the years." In laying out these tips, O’Connor gave three ways the next Halo game could bring the franchise back to its former glory. The first is a "classic" Halo game that focuses on a sandbox, movement, and shooter experience. It'd be "tailored to a core audience [and] scaled to be successful with that core audience and attractive to a broader one so it can grow." A second option could be "something new [and] modern" that reinvents the game. O’Connor suggests this "surprising but fantastic" reinvention could be an action-sandbox game that's a "new take" on the FPS or an "action RPG" that's fundamentally different, but its success comes from a strong story and IP use. Lastly, O’Connor thinks that an Activision Blizzard-esque Halo game could be viable. It could be a sandbox game with "player, progression, and business models" that the publisher is well-known for with the likes of CoD. Given that experience, maybe this idea could be "a big annual or semi-annual spectacle" with consistent content. O’Connor couched these ideas, saying that none of them are "easy, guaranteed, or necessarily viable." Still, he believes that one "could be successful" now that Activision is driving the Warthog. O’Connor isn't the only former developer who shares his opinion on the CoD publisher taking over the Halo reins. Co-creator Marcus Lehto also weighed in, saying on September 23 that he is "shocked but not entirely surprised" by the move. Like Lehto, we're all "eager to learn more about what’s next," and for right now, all we know thus far is that the next Halo game will be a "full-feature, mainline installment" in the long-running series.

Diablo 5 Seems To Be Fixing Diablo 4’s Biggest MistakeNEW
One of the biggest surprises of the year was the reveal of Diablo V at this year's BlizzCon. Sure, we'll have to wait until 2029 to get our hands on it, but take that decade-long wait cycles for new D...
One of the biggest surprises of the year was the reveal of Diablo V at this year's BlizzCon. Sure, we'll have to wait until 2029 to get our hands on it, but take that decade-long wait cycles for new Diablo games!  After sharing a first look at the game, Blizzard took the opportunity to talk about the improvements they’re making to Diablo V's gameplay design based on feedback from Diablo IV. Chief among them: the return of procedurally generated dungeons and worlds.  Diablo IV was touted as the first “open-world” title in the series, and with that came the limitation of having a single pre-established world map; it never changed and players were forced to engage with it for hours and hours. And listen, I get the intent. It set Diablo IV apart from previous installments and made players feel like there was a compelling reason to move on from Diablo III. Even though that game had a huge focus on its world, it steered away from an interconnected, open-world map in favor of making parts of its world feel like dungeons themselves.  Yet Diablo IV just feels truncated as a result. Is the map varied? Yeah, you have different biomes and events throughout, but despite these variances, it largely feels like you're always engaging with the same things, over and over again. “But wait, isn't that the point of Diablo?” you might be asking. Well, to an extent, yes! But that is just one aspect of the series. Take it from a degenerate Diablo player like me, who has put thousands upon thousands of hours into each title: Diablo IV felt like it was missing a key ingredient. Prior to IV, my friends and I would share stories of the random dungeons and encounters that the games would generate. By the end of Diablo III’s lifecycle, there were so many random layouts and themes for rifts, that we would hear stories about dungeons that we refused to believe even existed. And that's to say nothing about the world of Diablo II and the random generation of its map. The fact that players couldn't rely on a map to reach their next destination and were forced into a life-or-death battle was among one of many things that made the game so fulfilling. Rolling up a hardcore character (meaning you lose all progress if you die) and praying you made it to the next checkpoint is a feeling that few games have yet to replicate, even in the Diablo series. Diablo V's Plague Knight So it’s refreshing to hear that Blizzard is bringing procedural generation back in a meaningful way. During BlizzCon's  Diablo V panel, its lead developers talked about how, because Diablo has taken over Sanctuary, the world is constantly “Terror-forming,” meaning that the heroes of the game will need to navigate an ever-changing world. Without a set map--and with the added pressure of leading an entire caravan--players could be forced to endure more tension and uncertainty than ever before. Bearing that in mind, I imagine it will be far easier to engage with Diablo V's changing world for thousands of hours than it was to travel across Diablo IV's static map. Yet maps aren't the only thing that Blizzard seeks to improve with Diablo V, as the team also gave a very early, work-in-progress look at itemization and new double slot items. They also revealed that the game will launch with the most amount of character classes for any entry in the series before. The team seems to be taking the criticisms of Diablo IV very seriously and is working towards making Diablo V a more varied experience right from the jump. So I can’t help but wonder: Was Diablo IV a mistake? The game notoriously launched with lackluster itemization and loot systems that required full reworks (though admittedly Diablo III really suffered from this too); the lack of map variety meant that by hour 100 I was sick of visiting the same locations over and over. Hell, in a review for a previous outlet, I questioned what Diablo IV actually did better than Diablo III. With expansion support ending and the next mainline title already being revealed, I can’t help but feel that Blizzard knows what a misfire IV was and how its core design prevents it from being fully reworked. Here’s hoping Diablo V brings down the house in a blaze of hellfire glory.

Renown is one of two stats needed to recruit new team members in Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave. While the Support Level requirements never go above level 3, you may be intimidated by characters who require level 10 Renown. It's vitally important to note that gaining Renown will take a lot of time. Unlike the Support Levels, each Flame Lord's campaign builds their Renown separately. This requires patience so you don't get blindsided. Seeking a short-term Renown boost may risk potentially sacrificing other rewards that are far more important. While our Gifts Guide covers the basics of wooing each NPC to your cause, you'll still need to gain a ton of Renown to get the best characters to join your ranks. There are plenty of other Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave tips we've found on our long journey; from weekly activities to each Flame Lord's unique ways of getting famous, here is everything to know about Renown. Theodora's counter-attack instantly knocks the rider off his steed. Complete main story battles The biggest chunk of your renown you can gain comes naturally from finishing the main battles. If they take place in the arena, you'll gain at least 500 or so points. Obviously, this method alone won't get you all the way to rank 10, but if you have exhausted all other methods and run out of time, it may be reassuring to know that you'll get a significant boost for the next chapter. Dietrich's Answerer's Cry summons powerful enemies to appear nearby. Use your Flame Lord's exclusive Renown-gaining method Each of the four Flame Lords (main characters) whose story you follow in Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave has their own method of getting Renown. This is the primary way to reach higher Renown ranks, shifting the focus of free time periods to suit each Flame Lord's strengths. Cai's is by far the simplest to understand. In addition to the subquests, some people in Dagsion have a pin with a hand above their heads. These are his Kindness Quests and only require you to head to the designated location, either in Dagsion or on the World Map, and accomplish the objective. Dietrich has two ways to gain Renown, and both involve tons of fighting. The first is using the Answerer's Cry to make enemies appear for two turns. Consecutive summoning of foes with the Answerer on the world map gradually reduces its range, which recovers slowly each turn you don't use it or instantly if you enter or exit a base or explore a dungeon. Defeating enemies this way gives you a small amount of Renown and Echo Shards to bolster Combat Arts. You can also fight Dread Warriors in dungeons by accepting challenges the Headhunter in Dagsion gives you, for a larger amount of Renown, as well as learn new Combat Arts. Theodora has Supply Quests, which give you a quota of materials to gather and turn in at Bonaventure. To complete these, recruit a healthy number of battalions and assign them to gather the smaller numbers in each quest. Every space on the World Map with an icon lists all obtainable resources, so make sure you've got the right place for each one on your shopping list. Theodora can also search spaces or explore dungeons for more resources, and even if you defeat all enemies and loot all chests, materials in dungeons respawn with every entry. Finally, Leda has Perform Quests, which are relatively similar to Dietrich's location-based quests. However, instead of fighting strong foes in the middle of dungeons to gain renown and learn new Combat Arts, she performs dances in taverns to earn renown and Blaze Arts, while performances outside of Dagsion may reveal secret spots on the map that have rare materials. Subquests initially don't give much Renown, but later ones do give significant boosts. Complete subquests and meet the gods at temples In Dagsion, there are a couple more ways that are universally available but yield a very small amount of Renown. Subquests are probably worth knocking these out anyway, as they give other rewards besides Renown, but this is a relatively minor way compared to the others. One important caveat you need to know is that you technically only need to finish a subquest once with any of the four Flame Lords, provided you start the next campaign from the very beginning. As soon as you enter your first free time, you'll get all the Renown for finishing side quests instantly. However, if you swap campaigns mid-flow, you won't, and you need to manually complete them again. If a temple on your in-game map in Dagsion glows yellow, remember to visit it before leaving. Visiting these temples allows you to seek an audience with that temple's god for a small renown gain. You can meet with a temple god once a week. Not all Flame Lords can participate in a Paralogue, mostly because the NPCs you need to talk to in order to start them are ones that aren't recruitable by those characters. For example, Cai can't do his own Paralogue as he can't talk to himself. Deadpool’s a weird character. Like fellow mutant Wolverine, Wade Wilson is a trained killer whose primary superpower is a healing factor that lets him recover and reform from any wound, even if bisected or decapitated. Unlike Wolverine, Wade knows he’s in a fictional medium, be it a comic book, movie, or game, and physically can’t stop quipping about that fact and referencing things in and out of his world. It’s up to the writer whether this is sheer coincidence (as in, he’s genuinely just psychotic and his insane ramblings happen to be the truth), or a true meta awareness granting him power over narrative itself. This is a trait that Marvel T&omacrkon: Fighting Souls handles smartly, which in a way is surprising, because it usually makes him quite annoying.
