But should you prefer not to drive yourself, you do have a choice, and it doesn't involve taking taxis or relying on public transportation. It can be easy to miss, due to how it's accessed, but there is an auto-drive option you can enable so your current character drives themselves. Here's how to enable it: Hold Down on the D-Pad while in a vehicle Press Options/Start to enable Cinematic Mode Hold Circle/B to turn on auto-drive By default, your character will seemingly just drive themselves around aimlessly. However, if you set a destination on the map, they'll instead drive there. That's useful not just on the roads, but also with other vehicle types. For instance, you can hop on a boat and mark the location for a diving mission, and spare yourself the oft-uneventful process of manually driving to it. Cinematic Mode gives you nice camera angles from which to enjoy the visuals, and not having to pay attention to where you're driving means you can spend more time gawking at all of the details littered throughout the world. Or you can just pretend you're the passenger and enjoy playing with the radio, without having to spend exorbitant sums on a taxi ride. Auto-drive can be canceled at any time, so you're not locked into a full trip if something catches your attention along the way. One thing you can't do is play around on your phone while in auto-drive. To do that, you'll need to be manually driving, which means getting pulled into a first-person perspective that makes the prospect even more precarious. While in auto-drive, you can adjust between three speed settings depending on the urgency with which you want Jason or Lucia to drive. That makes this more of a viable way of getting around quickly, though don't expect them to pull off the same extreme maneuvers you might use to speed around. Keep in mind, there is an extensive public transportation system available as well (if only we could all be so lucky), though that's less useful if you're insistent upon arriving at a very particular spot.

GTA 6 Has The Best Use Of PS5’s DualSense Controller YetNEW
I expected Xbox Series X|S's Quick Resume to be the defining feature of this console generation, but it's been the unique features offered by PS5's DualSense controller that have actually provided the...
I expected Xbox Series X|S's Quick Resume to be the defining feature of this console generation, but it's been the unique features offered by PS5's DualSense controller that have actually provided the biggest edge for me. GTA 6 is not only the latest such example of this, but it's also the best example of leveraging the DualSense yet. The controller's speaker is used for some nice touches, like hearing your phone ring or chatter on the police scanner, but as is often the case, it's the DualSense triggers that make the more meaningful difference. These are leveraged in myriad ways that can significantly enhance the experience of whatever you're doing. For instance, the home-repair tasks you can complete early in the game see the triggers react differently based on what you're doing. It just feels more immersive for the triggers to not behave normally when you're installing screws with a driver; that's an action that should not feel identical to firing a gun or hitting the gas in your car. Similarly, there's an added weight to the strength-training activities you can take part in that lend an added sense that Jason and Lucia are actually picking up heavy items, not just manipulating weightless objects made of 1s and 0s. But where I think the DualSense triggers really shine is in driving. The triggers have a soft lock that allow you to pull and maintain a speed that is appropriate for the road you're on. Certainly in the early hours, when money is at a premium and alternative vehicles are not easy to steal, keeping your car in good shape is made easier by obeying traffic laws, and that is easier if you can comfortably pull the trigger without maxing out the RPMs. You can just as easily pull the trigger past that point to put the pedal to the floor and go as fast as you want, but that little bit of resistance lends that dangerous decision a bit of extra excitement, almost like a piece of physical feedback that you're choosing to break the law. Part of what makes these interactions work so well is how many years we've spent interacting with triggers on a game controller in a very narrow way: You might pull it partway, but generally you're just mashing it down into the plastic of the controller without much regard. By varying the resistance you encounter based on what you're doing, the abstraction of interacting with a video game world via a controller is pushed ever so slightly in the direction of realism. And for a game like GTA 6, where all of the minor details are what make it so notable, it's just one more way in which it feels less like a video game.

So, this gave us a good platform on the main flow to expand particularly intense or emotional moments they experience with variation that can expand on their romantic relationship, while not losing anything if that doesn’t exist. In short, we did develop variation in scenes and interactions that range from subtle to more significant, but done in a way that should feel authentic to the player’s choices, relationship-wise and also criminal profile.One example is after Lucia helps Kordell’s gang pull off an intense armored truck robbery she shares the information with Raul’s crew. Afterwards Jason pulls Lucia aside to see how she's coping. This moment carries several variations where the performance direction was captured multiple ways to cover a range emotional energy between them. The actors deliveries are brilliant and nuanced – these small tender moments are where we tried to craft pieces that when added up together over the course of the story, author an authentic version of the player’s Jason and Lucia.” Were there any fictional couples or real-life couples that the team looked at for inspiration in shaping the Jason and Lucia dynamic, whether those couples came from big crime stories or not?Rupert Humphries, Senior Vice President of Narrative:“We definitely started by looking at all the “couple on the run” movies. There are a lot of them but the ones that we kept coming back to were Warren Beatty and Faye Dunaway in Bonnie and Clyde, McQueen and MacGraw in The Getaway (the Jim Thomson book is also great), True Romance, Thelma and Louise. Clooney and JLo’s dynamic in Out of Sight, as well. And then a lot of buddy movies actually – Riggs and Murtaugh, Butch and Sundance, Mike Lowry and Marcus Burnett, etc. for their back and forth together. In terms of having a game where the characters can change based on how you play and whether you really lean into and enjoy the crime spree we thought about how both Pulp Fiction and Natural Born Killers start with a couple in a diner pulling out guns, but then how different those couples are and how different those scenarios play out. Jason and Lucia are a lot tougher than Tim Roth and Amanda Plummer but we liked the idea of telling a story where Jason and Lucia could end up being closer to either end of that spectrum based on what the player was doing.”Sex and intimacy are notoriously complicated and challenging to present in games. How did you aim to strike a balance with their private moments together that felt authentic? Any thoughts about how or why it’s so challenging to present adult relationships in video games?Rupert Humphries, Senior Vice President of Narrative:“This is definitely something we thought a lot about and spent a long time trying to get right. The actors who play Jason and Lucia were so good, and they had a natural chemistry and part of it was just trying to capture some of that.There’s this trade off in making games between player control and then scripted scenarios. We try and give the player as much control as we can because we are working in an interactive medium and actually doing something rather than watching it is going to feel more powerful. But then you have the ability to really mess around and break the illusion of a tender or romantic moment or whatever it is. So the relationship aspect of this story is there if a player wants to engage in it, but if they don’t the story can play out differently.” In its best moments of genuine support, regular connection, open communication, and flirty or engagement with one another, Jason and Lucia can come across as a pretty ideal model of good relationships – even while they’re simultaneously committing major crimes together. You are really living with these characters – they’re eating, sleeping, showering, throwing away their trash, or not. The relationship needed to feel real to sit alongside all of that. We wanted to create a world that held up a mirror to that, showed that fever pitch of craziness that we are all maybe a couple of bad decisions away from, but we wanted the story we were going to tell to have something positive at its core and the message that human connection is better than division.”The game automatically moves you to play one character or another at certain points, but for many sequences, you can choose your playable lead.
