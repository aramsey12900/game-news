Even then, 14 years before the studio’s eventual closure, anxiety about EA’s stewardship of the company was high. In a 2016 interview, BioWare General Manager Aaryn Flynn told Game Informer that, following the departure of the studio’s founders, the team felt the need to prove “we weren’t going to follow the apocryphal stories of EA crushing every other studio it buys under its thumb.” The studio itself was an ever-changing Ship of Theseus of creative talent, facing EA-mandated layoffs, subsidiary studio closures, staff departures, and the occasional return. The good news for fans of BioWare--no matter which era they hold dear--is that many of those creatives are still out there developing games with distinctly BioWare flair. Arcanaut Studios If you’re looking for the project with the most direct BioWare lineage, look no further than Arcanaut Studios’ Star Wars: Fate of the Republic. As well as being an explicit spiritual successor to BioWare’s iconic Star Wars RPG, Fate of the Republic is helmed by Mass Effect director Casey Hudson, with a team full of BioWare vets--including people who worked on the original KOTOR. Alongside Hudson, Arcanaut’s ex-BioWare talent includes: Senior technical designer Dan Fessenden, who worked on both KOTOR and The Old Republic as a programmer Chief technical officer Ryan Hoyle, who was a programmer on KOTOR and most of BioWare’s games from Neverwinter Nights all the way through to Dragon Age: Veilguard Director of external development Melanie Faulknor, who was a project manager on The Old Republic, as well as multiple Mass Effect and Dragon Age titles  Director of production Caroline Livingstone, who worked as a voice director at BioWare on the Mass Effect and Dragon Age games, as well as others as early as Jade Empire We’re still facing a long wait for Fate of the Old Republic, though Hudson has promised that it will definitely be out before 2030. Of course, Old Republic fans can still enjoy the BioWare-developed MMORPG Star Wars: The Old Republic, which was saved from BioWare’s fate when EA handed management of the online game over to Broadsword Online Games in 2023. The MMO even has a new expansion, Masters of the Force, releasing soon. Archetype Entertainment The other AAA studio carrying BioWare’s banner is Archetype Entertainment, the Wizards of the Coast subsidiary that tapped a number of notable BioWare alumni. Archetype’s BioWare veterans include: Former studio head James Ohlen, who was the lead designer on BioWare’s Baldur’s Gate games, as well as KOTOR, Neverwinter Nights, and Dragon Age: Origins Creative director Jesse Sky, who worked as the lead designer on The Old Republic post-launch VP and general manager Chad Robertson, who worked at BioWare as a head of technology and studio director Lead writer Drew Karpyshyn, who was the lead writer on KOTOR and Mass Effect 1 and 2 Archetype is currently working on Exodus, an epic sci-fi project that’s showing more than a little Mass Effect influence in its design and worldbuilding. Though the timing may just be coincidence, Exodus released a new tease just after the BioWare shutdown was announced. Though Exodus is rarely mentioned without some comparison to Mass Effect, early previews suggest the game has what it takes to move out of the shadow of BioWare’s sci-fi behemoth to stand on its own two feet. Set to release on April 7, 2027, Exodus feels well-timed to take some of the sting out of Mass Effect’s cancellation. Ohlen stepped back from Archetype after a major Exodus reveal at The Game Awards, with the former studio head saying he felt his work on the game was complete. Ohlen has shifted his focus back toward tabletop gaming, and his TTRPG company Arcanum Worlds also boasts a number of ex-BioWare employees, including Karpyshyn, Sky, and writer Brent Knowles. Beamdog One of the earliest BioWare spin-off studios, Beamdog was founded in 2009 by BioWare co-founder Trent Oster and former BioWare lead programmer Cameron Tofer. The studio has employed a number of ex-BioWare talent over the years, including Knowles and writer David Gaider. The studio has stayed close to its roots through much of its history, publishing remasters of BioWare’s classic RPGs including Baldur’s Gate 1 and 2, and Neverwinter Nights. Beamdog published an original roguelite based on '80s fantasy cartoons called Mythforce in 2023, though the game received mixed reviews.  Summerfall Studios On the indie side of the industry, Dragon Age lead writer Gaider co-founded Summerfall Studios in Melbourne, Australia in 2017, following his departure from Beamdog. Summerfall released Stray Gods: The Roleplaying Musical in 2023, with a DLC, Stray Gods: Orpheus, following in 2024. The Greek mythology-themed game featured a BioWare-esque dialogue wheel, allowing players to influence the direction of musical numbers in real time. In 2025, Summerfall released a roguelite deckbuilder called Malys. Yellow Brick Games Mike Laidlaw, who served as creative director on the Dragon Age series, founded indie studio Yellow Brick Games in 2020, which released its debut game Eternal Strands in 2024. Citing games like Shadow of the Colossus, Breath of the Wild, and Tears of the Kingdom as inspirations, Eternal Strands features an intricate physics-based combat system. Inflexion Games Inflexion Games is another studio with BioWare heritage, having been founded by former BioWare GM Aaryn Flynn. The studio’s debut game is Nightingale, a survival game set in a unique Victorian-inspired gaslamp fantasy setting, which tasks players with adventuring out into the inhospitable Faewilds. Nightingale released into early access in 2024 and has been generally well received by critics and players in its beta form, though it has struggled with player retention.  Earlier this year, Inflexion revealed it has been working as development partner on Conan Exiles Enhanced, which resulted in Nightingale being sidelined for the foreseeable future. North Mist Studios Other post-BioWare projects are still in their early days, with a few indie studios setting up to release their first games. One of these is North Mist Studios, a collaboration between former BioWare writers Mary Kirby and Sylvia Feketekuty, and cinematic director Sarah Hayward. Far from following a traditional game development pipeline, North Mist Studios has been developing the world for its first game project, The Concierge--a spy-thriller office drama about Canadian assassins--through a series of narrative podcasts. While development on the game is coming along slowly, North Mist recently announced that it had secured funding to prototype a smaller game in the same universe called Alone With The Deep, based on its horror audio drama Kinbasket. Worlds Untold Mac Walters, the writer who brought Garrus Vakarian to life in Mass Effect 1 and 2, founded the studio Worlds Untold in 2023, with financial backing from NetEase. Worlds Untold had started teasing a new sci-fi intellectual property when NetEase pulled funding in 2024--in the same week as Hudson’s former studio Humanoid Origin faced the same fate. Worlds Untold has been quiet since 2024. However, it still has a website and social media pages. “While we're pausing for now, this isn't goodbye--there's more to come, and we look forward to sharing the next chapter with you when the time is right,” Walters wrote on the studio’s LinkedIn page at the time. BioWare’s Legacy While this is just a selection of studios and projects that can be traced directly back to BioWare, the studio’s impact on the games industry as a whole can be seen across the RPG space--even on games with no ex-BioWare staff. The industry is full of creatives who grew up on games like Baldur’s Gate, Knights of the Old Republic, Dragon Age, and Mass Effect, and brought that influence into their own work. If you want concrete evidence of BioWare’s legacy outliving its own messy history, look no further than the incredible success of Larian Studios’ Baldur’s Gate 3. BioWare’s influence is plainly visible throughout BG3, in the way it combines its epic fantasy story with the more personal narratives of relationship building.  “BioWare led the way in making RPGs big,” Larian boss Swen Vinke said in a recent post, reacting to BioWare’s closure. “It's almost unimaginable that we're now entering an era where the name BioWare won't show up anymore when discussing upcoming RPGs.”

Where the great city of Lordaeron once proudly stood, only desolate ruins remain. The good news is that this makes it the perfect location for some good ol’ fashioned plundering, and a cromulent quantity of Scourge-slaying. In World of Warcraft: Forever, the Ruins of Lordaeron serves as a low-level instance that sits just above the Undercity in Trisfal Glades.  While this may intrinsically mark the Ruins of Lordaeron as a Horde-exclusive dungeon, there’s plenty of joy to be found if you take the plunge on your Alliance toon. As we’ll come to explain in the guide, getting to the instance isn’t nearly as daunting in Forever as it was in Classic. Of course, we’ll also give you the full Lordaeron lowdown, including all the quests you can cash in, bosses you can challenge, and, most importantly, all of the goodies you can potentially acquire for your efforts. For a comfortable time in RoL, we’d recommend steering clear until level 18, though you can always jump in as soon as you’ve snapped up the quests.  How to get to Ruins of Lordaeron Ruins of Lordaeron is located in Trisfal Glades, outside the entrance to the Undercity. Horde-side, Undead will have the easiest time reaching the ruins, seeing as they’re parked on the doorstep of the Undercity. A quick Hearthstone back to base (provided you haven’t relocated to The Sepulcher or elsewhere) is all you need. If you’re a Skyborne, Orc, Troll, or Tauren coming from Kalimdor, then hop on the zeppelin stationed near Orgrimmar’s entrance, which will drop you off just outside the city walls. If you’re part of the Alliance, you best take your sea sickness meds as all races will need to head out on a cruise across the ocean at some point. If you’re stationed in Stormwind (Humans, Skyborne at this point), take the shiny new ship from the harbor over to Auberdine in Darkshore. There, you’ll find a vessel that travels on a route rotating between Auberdine, Menethil Harbor (Wetlands), and Southshore (Hillsbrad Foothills). Kick your feet up and wait until you reach the latter of the three destinations, before making the run up through Silverpine Forest, and into Trisfal Glades. Alternatively, you can head directly north through the foothills, looping around the back of Dalaran, and swim across Lordamere Lake to the glades. For Night Elves, it’s a similar story, though you’ll likely already be in Darkshore at this point. Again, hop on the ship to Hillsbrad and go for a run (or dip) when you arrive -- nice and easy. Dwarves and Gnomes, conversely, have the most legwork to do. If you haven’t already been to Stormwind, you’ll first need to take the Deeprun Tram down there from Ironforge. From there, follow the steps outlined above and you’re good to go.  It should be noted that you will often find Horde players patrolling the dungeon’s entrance, ready to one-tap you. If you fall foul of these shenanigans (you’re on their turf, to be fair), then try and resurrect as close to the instance portal as possible and leg it. Ruins of Lordaeron Map Map by Santiago Reyes (2026) Your party will enter the Ruins of Lordaeron to the north-east, before working its way around clockwise in a ‘U’ shape. The pathway marked on the map best indicates the dungeon’s general flow, with a double-back in and down once you reach the north-west quadrant. There’s some optional verticality to the ruins, too.  For example, if you climb the stairs up to the top of the tower in Market Street, you can follow the rooftop along and drop down directly behind one of the bosses, Bjork. It’s worth noting that you will need to clamber over the wall situated just below where it says ‘Lordamere Overlook’ on the map, so don’t think you’ve hit a dead end when you clear the eastern section. However, we've been making liberal use of the Atlas addon to achieve this. You'll also need to install the Kalimdor & Eastern Kingdom map pack to get it working. Ruins of Lordaeron Quests There are five Horde-only and three Alliance-exclusive quests to undertake in Ruins of Lordaeron. There is also a secret quest that can be found by both factions inside the dungeon. While only one of the Horde tasks is picked up inside RoL, all of the Alliance ones are found within the instance. Detailed gear stats are listed in the loot section of this guide. Ruins of Lordaeron Bosses There are six bosses in Ruins of Lordaeron: Witherfang, The Baron, Viktor The Vile, The Abandoned, Bjork, and Rath’mael. Both Viktor The Vile and The Abandoned are spawned in, with the former being an optional encounter -- more on that in their respective sections. Witherfang King’s Alley might as well be called Spider Alley, because that’s all you’ll be dealing with in RoL’s opening section. Witherfang patrols the street with a few of her brood in-tow, so make sure you’ve taken out enough trash to ensure it doesn’t get dragged in when the fight kicks off. Witherfang uses Leech Poison, which drains health over time from its target, so make sure that gets cleansed from your tank (or whoever has it applied to them). Aside from that, it’s a simple case of holding fast and dishing out damage. The Baron The Baron is essentially an oversized Flesh Golem, and has a similar knock back ability to its mini-me iterations. However, Knockout is a considerably more powerful move that deals devastating damage and can stun the target for several seconds. Obviously, as a tank, being stunned is less than optimal as it creates openings for aggro to drop. Do what you can to regain his attention should this happen. Damage dealers and healers should also keep a close eye on how much threat they’re generating in this window, though the latter should, of course, ensure they keep their team healthy. Generally speaking, it’s a pretty chill encounter, but there’s a small chance chaos may ensue. Viktor The Vile In the bottom-left corner of the dungeon, before you head into Market Street, you’ll find the remnants of a house with a large, prominent fireplace punctuating it. Make sure your group’s fully prepared, as interacting with the kindling inside of it spawns five waves of enemies, before Viktor The Vile makes his introduction.  Viktor himself isn’t much to write home about. In fact, he only carries the same Leech Poison ability used by Witherfang. The only notable difference is that his melee attacks deal some solid damage, so make sure you keep your tank topped up. The Abandoned As you first enter Market Street through the archway, a lantern will flick on, prompting three waves of mobs to spawn. Once they’ve been dealt with, The Abandoned will emerge from the statue to the right of the courtyard. Sporting both Frost Nova and Drain Life, this boss can be a little irksome if you’re not properly positioned. For the most part though, it shouldn’t pose too much of a threat. Bjork No, we’re not quite taking on everyone’s favorite visionary Icelandic singer. There’s nothing magical about this Bjork; in fact, quite the opposite. The only ability this lumbering creature possesses is Anti-Magic Shield, which has a very short duration. Hit it, hit it some more, and it’ll keel over without much fanfare.  Rath’mael Ruins of Lordaeron’s grand finale is the fearsome-looking Rath’mael, who is, perhaps disappointingly, more bark than bite. He casts Flamestrike where your tank is, which can be interrupted. If he does get it off, simply reposition and go back to whacking him until he’s dead.
