Reviewed on: Switch 2 Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch, PC Publisher: Square Enix Developer: Square Enix Release: October 22, 2026 Rating: Everyone 10+ RPG publisher Square Enix has been doing the Lord's work lately by rescuing their discontinued mobile games and rehydrating them into full-fat RPG experiences. Last year's Octopath Traveler 0 is one such triumph, and it's safe to say Final Fantasy Resonance is another victory for reusing assets instead of throwing them in the garbage. More importantly, it's a great RPG experience with a fun cast and character customization options that will make fans of the Final Fantasy V job system rabid with delight. Resonance is based on the first season of the now-defunct Final Fantasy Brave Exvius mobile game, which ran four chapters in total from 2016 to 2025, but Resonance is no mere port. For starters, it received an HD-2D injection that gussied up Exvius's already-impressive sprites. Resonance wears the HD-2D aesthetic confidently; Square Enix has obviously listened to past criticisms of its beloved graphical style, and it paid off. Resonance is an HD-2D game, but its highly detailed character sprites and occasional jaw-dropping pixel-based "cinema" scene differentiate it from the Octopath Traveler series and the Dragon Quest HD-2D remakes.  Resonance's distinctions are its greatest strength, in fact. It's easy to assume at first blush that its story about a boy and his best friend is nothing but a table to prop retro Final Fantasy references upon. It's true that a key part of Resonance's gameplay involves summoning "Visions" into battle, said visions being projections of characters from other Final Fantasy games. But that's all they are: Projections. They teach transferable skills, à la Final Fantasy V's job system. They can offer characters permanent stat boosts as they level up, like Final Fantasy VI's espers. And when conditions are met, they can be summoned to perform a super attack via a CGI spectacle that will remind you Square Enix have been masters of game cinema direction since day one.Each Resonance character can equip a Vision that lends them their powers in a fight, and also grants passive upgrades and skills. Rain, for example, is a heavy hitter with innate fire attacks, but if he wears Cecil Harvey as a backpack, he also gets access to light and dark magic. Cecil gets stronger as they fight together, and bestows skills that can be equipped as wanted. If you're a Final Fantasy V job-head, you've come home.But Final Fantasy Resonance is Rain's story despite its many callbacks to past games. Yes, Rain is an amalgamation of past Final Fantasy heroes, from Cloud's spiky yellow hair to Tidus's daddy issues, to Zidane's sunny attitude (with a hint of shadow), but that just makes him easier to root for. Rain's roommate, Lasswell, contains notes of my beloved Kain Highwind from Final Fantasy IV, but there's a troubled nobility to him that's purely his own. They're gradually joined by a cast of characters who click with each other (and thus, with me) thanks in part to comedic side-discussions delivered with quality voice acting. References to past Final Fantasy games are doled with clever subtlety. As a Final Fantasy XIV fan, I had to peel myself off the ceiling more than once when I encountered some choice shoutouts. Resonance's battle system grants it another distinction. It's a classic turn-based system that shows off who's next in line to attack, like Final Fantasy X. Every character brings a fighting style and elemental flavor to the party. Lasswell is a samurai who favors ice, Jake is a gunner attuned to the wind, and Lid will rain the fattest of steel chocobos upon your head in an earth-based attack.Resonance is a snappy game, which might be its most welcome trait. Fast travel is generous, and dungeons are conquered in just the right amount of time. You're looking at a 40-hour experience, give or take, which is downright digestible for an RPG in this day and age. On the flipside, Rain's world lacks depth. Its towns are beautiful, unique, and filled with characters and quest-givers who are worth talking to, but the overworld connective tissue is thin and its dungeons lack pizzazz. An auto-battle option would lessen the frustration of random encounters, but said encounters don't pop up nearly as often as they do in ancestral Final Fantasy games. Plus, the random battles are spaced in a way that makes grinding unnecessary unless you're maxing out a Vision, which I appreciate, but sometimes you're just trying to get to a place.I went into Resonance expecting to like it for its salute to Final Fantasy characters across the decades, but I wound up loving the game for showing me a whole new canon. In a time when mainline Final Fantasy is still figuring itself out, Final Fantasy Resonance is easy to recommend. It has one foot planted on the solid foundation of Final Fantasy's history, but with its rich graphics and likeable cast of characters it builds up from that history instead of merely pillaging it.  Score: 9 About Game Informer's review system

Reviewed on: Switch 2 Platform: Publisher: Nintendo Developer: Nintendo Release: October 22, 2026 Rating: Everyone 10+ Originating as a tech demo for the Wii’s motion controls, Nintendo’s Sports series has always found novel and intuitive ways to emulate various athletic endeavors with motion-based video game controllers. Like its predecessors, Switch Sports Resort is a compilation of sports-based minigames built around approachability. Though rightfully known for its enticing multiplayer options, Switch Sports Resort adds new single-player objectives and modes to offer a well-rounded experience for all player counts, making it perhaps the strongest entry in the series to date.Switch Sports Resort delivers 12 main events to choose from, each with distinct controls, strategies, and rules. Mainstays like bowling, archery, boxing, and golf were my initial destinations to attempt to relive old glories, but newcomers like thumb wrestling, skateboarding, power cruising, and prop plane made me glad for their inclusion. Skateboarding, power cruising, and prop plane form a new category called leisure sports, which allow for timed exploration of the newly renovated Wuhu Island by land, sea, and sky, respectively. I spent several sessions seeking out landmarks and collecting Spocco Points, the in-game currency used for character customization. And since all the sports’ controls are so intuitive and newcomer-friendly, it was a breeze to jump between activities. Though they all boil down to moving your arms in something approximating motions you’d make playing the real sport, Nintendo has smartly implemented more variety in its control schemes, all while keeping them easy to pick up. Before the tutorial even explains how to hold the Joy-Con, you can likely accurately guess what the grip and motion will be; even more involved control schemes like basketball and volleyball are easy to remember. Skateboarding is particularly innovative, as it uses Switch 2’s Joy-Con’s mouse mode to emulate the kick-push of a skater. Meanwhile, prop plane has you holding your Joy-Con like a paper airplane as you fly around blasting balloons and visiting landmarks. However, holding the Joy-Con that way for several minutes inevitably cramped my hand. And though boxing remains one of my favorite sports in the bundle, I needed to retrain my brain, as throwing a punch the correct way doesn’t work with how the game wants you to do it; if I turn my wrist when punching, my character sways instead of attacking.Playing with friends, both online and in-person, remains the highlight of Switch Sports Resort. The online friend lobby system receives a boost in the form of allowing different sports to take place simultaneously within the same lobby, and when you add in special variants like simultaneous archery, 100-pin bowling, skateboard racing, and prop plane balloon battles, you have a lot of fun options with which to jump in.Whenever I play solo, the Island Crown is where I spend most of my time. Here, you climb the ranks across nine sports, sometimes with special rulesets, vanquishing NPC opponents until you are the champion. Whether it was taking to increasingly outlandish bowling lanes, windier archery ranges, or simply facing off against more strategic table tennis players, I relished in improving my skills en route to the championship.  Because these minigames are relatively surface level, they have diminishing returns once you reach a certain skill level. While I loved it when my tennis opponents started challenging me to use better angles and my fellow golfers stopped air-mailing their tee shots straight into the trees, higher-skill opponents rarely energized me; instead, sports like bowling that include fun, off-the-wall variants were my favorite ladders to ascend, as they truly put my skills to the test as I was forced to adapt to moving obstacles and ramps. I just wish Nintendo injected more fun twists to keep the experiences fresh.After completing each activity, you’re awarded Spocco Points, which you use to unlock themed customization elements for your characters. Cartoon-like Sportsmates replace the iconic Mii characters introduced in Wii Sports, but you can still use your Mii if you choose. Though I have fondness for Mii characters, after two entries featuring the more expressive, customizable Sportsmates, I now prefer those character models.Nintendo Switch Sports Resort delivers a capable compilation of quick-hit sports minigames that can be easily enjoyed regardless of how many people are holding Joy-Cons. Though I certainly have my destination sports, there’s not a loser in the bunch. Island Crown mode does a great job expanding the single-player options, but this package’s legs are in its multiplayer experience. And since Switch Sports Resort is chock-full of easily approachable minigames, I can see it becoming a cherished inclusion in the rotation of party mainstays.
