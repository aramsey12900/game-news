The New, Non-GTA 6 Games You Should Be Playing Right NowNEW
While Grand Theft Auto 6 is set to dominate what GameSpot covers for the next month (at least), it doesn't mean there aren't plenty of other games out there that those of us are playing at the same ti...
While Grand Theft Auto 6 is set to dominate what GameSpot covers for the next month (at least), it doesn't mean there aren't plenty of other games out there that those of us are playing at the same time (or in the cases of those of us who aren't big GTA fans, what we're playing instead). Whether it's a new demo for a game we're eagerly anticipating, an indie title that's bravely facing off against Rockstar's juggernaut of a title, or just something that made us smile, we want to take a chance to highlight those games too. Below, you'll find a list of smaller or lesser-known games that we're playing alongside or instead of GTA 6, as well as links to our coverage of those titles. As we get into the thick of Grand Theft Auto 6 season, we'll keep coming back here to add additional impression pieces we want to highlight. Memorable Demos A fantastic demo can reinforce your decision to go ahead and wishlist or preorder a game you already have your sights on, but the best-of-the-best are those that surprise. Sometimes you have no idea what a game is going to be like or whether you'd even like it, and then you try out the free demo at the last in-person E3 you ever attended and suddenly the game becomes your entire personality for the next six years. Over the past month or so, we've played demos in both camps. None of these games are out yet, but they're all scheduled to launch either later this year or sometime next year. If you've got a spare hour or two, these snippets are an excellent way to spend your time. At Fate's End My most anticipated game of 2026 was At Fate's End. I say "was" because it was delayed to 2027. Thankfully, a demo for the game was released alongside the announcement of the delay, allowing me to at least experience a morsel of what's to come. Spoiler: It's fantastic. This is the next game from Spiritfarer developer Thunder Lotus, which sees you embody the role of a warrior princess who has to duel her hurting siblings with sharp swords and even sharper words--it's the exact emotional devastation I crave. And both the art and music direction are nothing to sneeze at. This is a pretty game to both look at and listen to. Draftula So you took GameSpot editor Steve Watt's wonderfully written Castlevania: Belmont's Curse review to heart, bought the game, and enjoyed fighting Dracula as yet another member of the Belmont family. But now what? Well, you could go help rebuild Dracula's castle in preparation for the next invasive adventurer. Writer Dave Irwin enjoyed the demo for Draftula, an upcoming roguelite that sees you needing to build Dracula's misshapen castle over and over, higher and higher, in order to waylay any would-be heroes. Exterminauts Okay, technically not a demo, but a beta test that you can join right now is demo-ish. GameSpot editor Mark Delaney compares Exterminauts to Helldivers 2, although this three-person player-versus-environment shooter stars regular schmucks obsessed with do-it-yourself projects, not patriotic starship troopers. The game otherwise plays very similarly to Helldivers 2, so the beta feels more like a means of testing to see whether the narrative flavor is to your liking. If you already like Helldivers 2, you'll probably like the gameplay of this shooter too. My Arms Are Longer Now GameSpot managing editor Chris Pereira and I often muse in our weekly one-on-one meetings that we rarely have time to destress via gaming, since the portal by which we primarily play games is also the machine we sit at all day for work. So any weird game that gets Chris to relax makes me happy and is more than deserving of a shout-out. My Arms Are Longer Now is a bizarre game that's strangely compelling--between the ridiculous humor of controlling an arm that stretches belief and the silly hijinks that you're able to get up to, this will likely remain the funniest demo in this roundup.  Nirvana Noir Genesis Noir is one of the most memorable games of 2021. Its use of abstract art to frame a compelling noir detective story within the explosion of the big bang created an experience that was quite novel. The next game, Nirvana Noir, aims to tell an even weirder story. Writer Khee Hoon Chan likes how the vibrant color of Nirvana Noir makes for an eye-catching sequel, one in which a detective hops between parallel realities (one in which the Big Bang happened, and one in which it didn't) to unravel a conspiracy centered around the final moment of Genesis Noir. The game looks gorgeous, and the demo already indicates that this game's puzzles and mysteries are going to be just as spellbinding as the first game. Paranormal Activity: Threshold Mark puts it perfectly: The scariest game of the year is a demo for a game that isn’t even coming out. Like Silent Hills before it (and Devotion seemingly until it surprisingly did come back), Threshold is a fantastically spooky good time that's just never going to release in full. But at least we still have this great demo to experience a tiny glimpse of what was going to probably be a strong first-person horror game. It's really no big surprise that this demo is good--Threshold was being developed by DarkStone Digital, the same studio behind the extremely popular (and oftentimes way too scary for me) The Mortuary Assistant.  New releases There aren't just new demos to play right now. Some studios are bravely staring down the Goliath that is Grand Theft Auto 6, the sling that is their much smaller title in hand. Releasing in the weeks leading up to GTA 6 or in the weeks post-GTA 6's launch is a bold choice, but those of us who aren't huge into sandbox games are thankful that they're here. Too much of just one thing for a long time isn't always fun. These games fall into their own niche--you're not going to find any games listed here that feel like GTA 6 but smaller. We imagine most of those games were delayed to 2027 or beyond in order to not be overshadowed by Rockstar's action-adventure. Deadlock Deadlock is the latest in a now dwindling field of hero shooters, but unlike the swing-and-a-miss mishaps of Concord and Highguard, Deadlock seems to have the juice to stick around. The game's player numbers saw a huge surge in September with the City Never Sleeps update, and it's basically been doing just fine ever since despite technically still being in early development. Fingers crossed that continues to be the case. Deadlock has some of my favorite lore of any shooter ever, which is only amplified by the impressive world design of its maps and phenomenal character art direction. Writer Ana Diaz said that Deadlock is the game that convinced her we should all be role-playing in competitive games, and after seeing so many people doing just that in Deadlock and posting those conversations on YouTube, I'm inclined to agree! Hunger Unlike most of these games that are releasing just before GTA 6, Hunger is scheduled to launch just after Rockstar's game comes out. Set to be released in Steam Early Access on December 3, Hunger is an extraction role-playing game in which you navigate a plague-stricken Napoleonic Europe (so weird that we got two first-person zombie-adjacent Napoleon-centered games in 2026). If you're anything like me, you see the words "extraction shooter" and want to log off, but hold on! After playing Hunger for eight hours, writer Cameron Koch points to the extraction RPG's promise of permanent progress as its most compelling feature. You're doing everything else you would in other extraction games--looting, encountering other players, trying to get away--but there's a bit of roguelite-inspired gameplay at work here too, ensuring you can retain permanent upgrades not only from run-to-run, but season to season.  Tanuki: Pon's Summer A bike-riding, mail-delivering raccoon dares to challenge GTA 6, and we should all support him. Mark played this game in the week leading up to GTA 6's launch and spoke with game director Liam Edwards as to how the team decided that releasing their third-person adventure game between The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake and GTA 6 was the right move. Blending together BMX tricks and Japan-inspired locales, Pon's Summer sees the titular tanuki racing through four cities to deliver the mail as quickly (and as cool) as he can.  Thank You Bus Driver Friendslop is all the rage these days but, as the name implies, you do need friends to play with in order to enjoy them. Or I guess you could cheat like Mark and just have children. But not all of us are so lucky to be blessed with the circumstances to create people to play games with. That's what writer Jade King enjoys most about Double Fine's Thank You Bus Driver--this wacky first-person driving adventure game feels like playing friendslop, but it's single-player. The game sees you playing as the titular bus driver and utilizing 1930s animation logic to safely get your passengers to their destination--stretching your arms to absurd lengths to smack troublemakers in the back of the bus, for example, or utilizing magical trinkets to transform how the bus handles. Queen's Domain Quite a few of us at GameSpot are obsessed with Queen's Domain--it's quickly gone from a "I'll try to play this game at some point" to "I should play this before GameSpot's Best Of deliberations." GameSpot senior video producer Jean-Luc Seipke first put the game on our radar with their argument that Queen's Domain is gonna be a cult classic. Really hard to argue that I don't need to check out a game with a statement like that. But the pressure to check this game out only grew when Jade added that Queen's Domain has the most terrifying mimic ever, and that the whole game just has some of the best enemy creature design of any action-adventure game this year. You should join me in trying out this game this November. Village in the Shade First released in Japan on July 30, Village in the Shade released in North America and Europe more recently on October 30. Featuring a hand-painted art style and cute-looking characters, Village in the Shade looks like another in a long line of relaxing farming sims. But it's not. Instead, this game is another in a long line of "looks like a cozy game but is 100% not." Your farmer is immediately inundated with strange rules: don't talk to strangers and don't go out after 11 PM, among other similarly strange limitations. When writer Heidi Kemps checked out the game, she noted that it doesn't take long for Village in the Shade's otherworldly, encroaching horror to take hold, and you can't help but want to unravel the mounting mystery of the town.
