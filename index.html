One of the biggest talking points in gaming today is the use of artificial intelligence, including generative AI. It's a hot-button issue, and few companies have faced the level of criticism about it as Saber has. The developer recently found itself in hot water after CEO Matthew Karch lashed out at Rideshare Stimulator's former writer who said they were laid off and replaced by ChatGPT. Now, Saber's chief creative officer, Tim Willits, has addressed the situation as well, promising that, "We will never fire someone and replace them with AI." For anyone just catching up, Rideshare Stimulator is an upcoming Saber game where players take on the role of an Uber- or Lyft-like driver and interact with them as they take them to their location. The developers freely admit to using generative AI in various capacities, and now Willits has shed more light on the situation. He said the game's main campaign features dialogue written by humans, but there is also a "free ride" mode where Rideshare Stimulator wants to be able to reference current events in the real world. "We use AI to generate that because we want to do it so fast," Willits said, adding that the plan is for weekly or biweekly updates with this kind of AI-generated content. Willits also clarified that some of Saber's other games, including the new Hellraiser, Turok, Stuntman, and Space Marine games, also do not use AI. Saber, like many other game companies, is experimenting with AI in various ways. "We want to figure this new technology out, and we don't know exactly where it's gonna go, and our players don't know where it's gonna go, so we're all ... all of us are trying as a society to figure this out together," he said. "We want to experiment with it. I personally believe somebody [at] some game company will come up with such a brilliant idea, and we'll all be like, 'That's it.' We just need to get there." When asked specifically about claims that Saber is firing people and replacing them with AI, Willits said this is not true. "I promise you we have never let anyone go and replaced them with AI. We hire people all the time," he said. A spokesperson for Saber previously told PC Gamer that neither Saber nor Rideshare developer Unigine replaced any writers with AI for that game or any others. "In fact, Saber's staff of writers has tripled since 2023. Rideshare's story was written entirely by real people," the company said. Rideshare's "experimental, free-ride mode" does use generative AI "by necessity," Saber said, because "the number of passengers in the game is infinite." "We are firm believers in using AI tools to improve or enhance gameplay experiences and improve NPC behavior as we have done since Saber was founded in 2001. As the industry has continued to contract, Saber continues to hire. None of our 3000+ employees have to worry about being replaced by AI," the developer said. The writer in question, Stella Sacco, said Karch reached out to "apologize unreservedly" for how he spoke about Sacco. Karch had said, "Stella who? I had to ask Claude because I have never spoken to her or seen her." Sacco added in a statement that Karch said "he regretted escalating and how people online were speaking about me." "I'm not worried about being called slurs by random commenters--I'm a trans woman; that's every day for me. But it stunk to have my skill and reputation built of 20 years in games publicly maligned by someone with institutional power."

Hellraiser: Revival Is More Than Just Cenobites and Gore – 10 Things We Learned From Saber InteractiveNEW
October wouldn't feel right without a spooky game or two, and Hellraiser: Revival is shaping up to be a terrifying--and horny--dive into the nightmarish fiction of author Clive Barker. So far, the gam...
October wouldn't feel right without a spooky game or two, and Hellraiser: Revival is shaping up to be a terrifying--and horny--dive into the nightmarish fiction of author Clive Barker. So far, the game is looking like the kind of Hellraiser experience that defined the franchise throughout the '80s and early '90s, and at Gamescom, Saber Interactive sat down with GameSpot to talk about the game, how far they're willing to go to nail the vibe of Hellraiser, and more. Hellraiser: Revival launches for PC, PS5, and Xbox Series X|S on October 6. Cliver Barker was a "permissive" partner The legendary creator of Hellraiser needed some convincing before he started collaborating with Saber Interactive, but once he was assured that the developers had a grasp of the Hellraiser franchise, he gave the studio the freedom to tell their own tale within that dark universe. "Clive Barker was such a permissive partner to work with," narrative director Anthony De Fault said. "He was really supportive of our ideas. He basically just wanted to make sure we understood Hellraiser as a franchise, and then, once he knew that we had the, we were channeling the same spirit that he was channeling back in the '80s, he just let us go." Capturing the soul of Hellraiser's greatest hits  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=6Ad6CybXBC4 For the game, Saber Interactive aimed to capture the "soul" of the franchise, drawing not only from the recognizable visual elements established in the Hellraiser movies, but also from Clive Barker's original novella, The Hellbound Heart, which served as the source material for the franchise. "This game is a response, specifically to The Hellbound Heart, the first film, and Hellbound: Hellraiser 2, the second film," de Fault said. "So it's just the things that Clive was involved with that resonated really strongly with people back then. We wanted to make something that renewed that and had the same soul, but for a new medium and 40 years later." Barker was also credited as an executive producer on the third and fourth Hellraiser movies, but had no active production or creative involvement in the franchise until 2022's Hellraiser reboot. That gap saw six Hellraiser movies released, although fans usually consider this period a low point for the franchise. Saber Interactive isn't afraid of pushing ratings boards to the limits https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=YCTOqk9fzO0 From what we've seen so far, Hellraiser: Revival looks like it'll be going all in on the extreme subject matter of Barker's work. Saber Interactive has confirmed that the game is going to be gory and sexy, as the studio didn't want to compromise on their vision--even if that meant it faced the risk of being denied classification in several regions. Why go that far? Because doing anything less was seen as a disservice to Hellraiser fans. "We knew what Hellraiser ought to be. We knew how disappointed Hellraiser fans would be if we did compromise, so it was just never really on the table," de Fault explained. "We wanted to pitch it at a point where I don't know if the executives would necessarily have agreed with this at the time, but we always felt like, if we get denied a rating in one or two places, we've probably done our job right. It was just important to us to do everything with purpose. All of the depraved stuff that you might encounter in the game, I think everything in there is defensible in terms of there's a reason it's there. It's not just for shock value. "It's like a good discomfort." "Comforting discomfort," Esov added. So far, it looks like the game will be available in the US, at least, as it has received an ESRB rating. It was all about execution While all of that comforting discomfort is designed with a purpose in mind, don't be too surprised if Hellraiser: Revival makes you reach for a bucket. One scene in particular might be hard to stomach, as de Fault described it as a moment of "consensual mutilation" in the game. "There's a consensual mutilation scene. In early versions of this, we failed to adequately convey to players that this was consensual. So, some people came away thinking it wasn't and that it was pretty distressing, and so we had to course-correct that. I think our intent was fully realized in the game. There wasn't really anything we wanted to do that we scaled back or anything like that. We just had to correct the execution in some places." Hellraiser: Revival takes place in the ancient year of 2006 https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=0rA40lqttO8 Hellraiser: Revival is set in 2006, in the small town of Claireport. Saber Interactive chose this time period as a bridge between '80s Hellraiser and the modern era, while also ensuring there wouldn't be any "super high-techy stuff" to distract players. "We still wanted it to feel like an older franchise, but just without going all the way back to the '80s," de Fault added. Pinhead has an agenda Like most Hellraiser movies, Revival revolves around a pair of sex freaks who get more than they bargained for when they use one of the infamous puzzle boxes created by the Cenobites--the all-new Genesis Configuration in this instance. Playing as Aiden, it'll be up to players to rescue his girlfriend Sunny from the Cenobites, and you can expect to run into Pinhead at several points in the story. "He plays a really substantial role," de Fault explained. He appears near the start of the game with all the other Cenobites, responding to the fact that Aiden and his girlfriend, Sunny, have used one of the configuration boxes from the game during their sexual play. In Hellraiser fashion, [the Cenobites] demand a soul, and they take somebody away who happens to be Sunny. "I probably shouldn't go further than that, but Pinhead, you encounter him multiple times. The deal that you have with him has its twists and turns, and it evolves." Pinhead is also voiced by Doug Bradley, the actor who played him in several Hellraiser movies over the decades. The "Genesis Configuration" box is fueled by suffering While previous Hellraiser media revolved around the Lament Configuration puzzle box, the Genesis Configuration is a completely new creation for the game. It serves as both a plot device and a weapon, but to use it, you'll need to fuel it with the suffering of enemies and Aiden's own anguish. Saber Interactive isn't spilling the beans on how it fits into the overall story of Hellraiser: Revival, but they did tease that it was made for a specific purpose that will be revealed eventually. "It's all in the name of this glorified violence, and basically, it gets powered by the suffering of yours and others," game director Emil Esov explained. "When there is suffering in the vicinity, you get more energy into the box, and you can perform these abilities." Cenobites can't be trusted Don't expect to be taking on iconic Cenobites in Hellraiser: Revival. They serve a variety of roles in the game, helping Aiden to unlock new powers for the Genesis Configuration or acting as unstoppable antagonists you'll have to escape. The female Cenobite--sometimes referred to as the Fallen because she was a nun who was drawn to sin in her human life--will also serve as a devil on Aiden's shoulder when she's not unlocking new powers for the Genesis Configuration. We're not worthy, we're not worthy! There's no shortage of enemies to deal with in Hellraiser: Revival, as Aiden will regularly come into conflict with the "Unworthy Ones" from the Cult of the Scarlet Church. Denied access to the Labyrinth, the worshippers of Leviathan are dangerous, but they're still fragile mortals who can be shot, skewered, and burned to death. Hellraiser: Revival's puzzles serve as a nice change of pace between all of the horror So what kind of game is Hellraiser: Revival? Esov and de Fault described as being a "puzzle-moderate" game, as when Aiden ventures into the depths of the Labyrinth, players will have solve the deadly challenges within that structure.. "There is like two to two-and-a-half hours of Labyrinth gameplay, and there is no combat in the Labyrinth," Esov said. "We intentionally designed it so the Labyrinth itself is something that is menacing, that is scary. Sometimes it's going to be, traps. Other times, it's going to be you using the Genesis Configuration to actually manipulate the Labyrinth. And then, sometimes it's the combination of the two. It's puzzle-moderate, but it's nothing like brain-crunching puzzles that require a lot of focus. "I think most of the puzzles are kind of 10-second to two-minute breathers between action or exploration sequences, that just force you to tickle your brain a little bit," De Fault added. "I'd say they occur fairly frequently, but they're not head-scratchers."

When Gears of War first debuted in the Xbox 360 generation, it introduced the world to the majesty of the Lancer weapon. A gun with a brutally effective chainsaw mounted to it, the Lancer has become the defining symbol of the franchise, and in Gears of War: E-Day, players will get to see just how that weapon came to be. In the established Gears of War lore, the weapon was created a year after the events of Emergence Day. That created something of a dilemma for the developer, The Coalition, as they had to give players something that reminded them of the classic weapon while telling a story that takes place during Emergence Day. The Solution? A Lancer prototype that's introduced in the game, and has roots in Gears of War literary stories. "Wherever possible, we've just done everything we can to respect the canon, right? The scene where Tai uses a chainsaw in a grocery store to kill a Locust is part of the creation story that was in a book, but we've lifted it--like all the dialogue--everything is intact in our game," creative director Matt Searcy said to GameSpot. "So even if we have to do like a small retcon, we just lift it and drop. We took the moment, we took the dialogue, we took everything about it and just said if we have to make a change, we're going to respect it as much as we possibly can, and if we need to connect the dots between two things, we're going to." https://youtu.be/SRFE0gCsgLg "We have to have a Lancer in this game. The book that helped inspire the story of Gears of War: E-Day is the 2008 novel Gears of War: The Aspho Fields by Karen Traviss. In it, Gear soldier Tai Kaliso uses a power saw in combat, and that action eventually goes on to inspire the creation of the chainsaw attachment for Lancer rifles. The book also covers the battle in which Dominic Santiago's brother, Carlos, dies, giving The Coalition an established starting point for building Marcus and Dom’s relationship throughout E-Day. "What it gave us was a really rich jumping-off point," Searcy explained. "So we knew E-Day is a plot point, two-thirds of the population dies, the monsters come out of the ground, and it's a cool concept. But we needed something rich for the characters to be based on, and that gave us a really strong starting point in terms of understanding where Marcus and Dom were before this game." While the story campaign has been a big focus for the early marketing of Gears of War: E-Day, we're not entirely convinced yet if it'll tell the definitive Gears story that sets up the entire franchise. The multiplayer might be where this prequel truly shines--especially with the overhauled movement systems--and you can check it all out for yourself when Gears of War: E-Day launches on October 6 for Xbox Series X|S and PC.
