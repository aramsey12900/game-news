An official Phasmophobia movie is on the way; we learned as much last summer. But what if I said you could watch something quite like Kinetic's ghostbusting co-op game right now?  That's the elevator pitch I bring you, fellow horror fans, less than two weeks out from Halloween. The movie is Never After Dark, a gorgeously shot, cleverly creepy ghost story that Phasmophobia fans shouldn't miss. Never After Dark tells the story of Airi, a young woman who can commune with ghosts. She works as a traveling intermediary between the dead and the living, convincing spirits to move on and freeing residents from their hauntings. But on her latest gig, she discovers she's taken on a much harder assignment. And wow, that assignment looked really familiar in such a fun way. Never After Dark has been described as a "J-Horror revival" project, recalling the ghost stories frequently imported to the US from Japan at the turn of the 21st Century. Movies like Ringu, Pulse, Dark Water, and Ju-On: The Grudge were a big deal in Japan and earned a lot of praise (and remakes, for better or worse) here in the States, too. I've never been a big fan of the term J-Horror, but I can see how it works as shorthand for that particular bundle of films.  Sorry, Airi, but there's no option to stay in the van and watch the cameras. Director Dave Boyle is an American, but he has spent much of his career making movies in and about Japan. Some of those aforementioned inspirations for Never After Dark are my favorites in the genre, and Boyle's new film does indeed feel like them at times, though I was more often struck by how much it felt like I was playing a round of Phasmophobia. Even Airi, who works alone most of the time, finds herself with a small group of other characters, like a solo player hopping into an open lobby in Phasmophobia. I won't spoil the movie, but suffice it to say that many of the game's core mechanics and moments are reflected in it. Some scenes even feel pulled straight from the game. Of course, this is partly because both Kinetic's game and Boyle's film draw on real-life theories about ghosts and how to interact with them, rather than the movie intentionally cribbing the game's core identity. Still, it made me wonder how the eventual Phasmophobia movie might handle some similar situations.  Never After Dark is in theaters across the US right now, albeit in limited release, so check that it's playing near you. Alternatively, you could also stream it at home soon. I haven't seen any announcement about whether it'll end up on any subscription service, but its rent-at-home premiere is set for October 28, making it a smart choice for Halloween weekend if you feel like you've seen everything scary and need something new. I went into the movie because I'd heard about its nearly perfect aggregate review score and because I thought it might feel like something from my favorite director, Kiyoshi Kurosawa. It definitely checked that box, I'm happy to say, but just as central to its vibe is a fascinating look at what a Phasmophobia movie might look like--before we get the real thing in the (hopefully near) future. After Beating Castlevania: Belmont’s Curse, Help Dracula Rebuild In This Awesome RoguelikeNEW
Let’s face it, if you are a Castlevania fan or just love metroidvania games in general, you’re probably sinking your teeth into Belmont’s Curse. And why not? After all, it just got a glowing review fr...
Let’s face it, if you are a Castlevania fan or just love metroidvania games in general, you’re probably sinking your teeth into Belmont’s Curse. And why not? After all, it just got a glowing review from our own Steve Watts, and like plenty of the rest of you, I’ve also been eagerly waiting for a new Castlevania adventure for over a decade. One indie team in particular seems keen to grab your attention once your time with Belmont's Curse is over, having recently released a demo for their Castlevania-adjacent roguelike. That game is Draftula, which asks, "What if Dracula commissioned you to build his labyrinthine castle?" Draftula’s demo is available on Steam for everyone to try right now. It plays like a deckbuilder, but instead of collecting cards, you earn tile pieces that are castle rooms. The goal of each run is to place each room tile in such a way that you build a castle tall enough to prevent the vampire hunter from knocking it down at the end of the night. You can also inhabit each room with monsters, which can further slow the vampire hunter down. Each piece has a coin cost so you need to be strategic in how you construct Dracula's castle, and depending on your performance each run, you can earn hearts that can be spent between runs to unlock permanent upgrades. It may sound easy, but the catch is that Dracula’s powers are somewhat weakened; he’s just woken up from a long slumber. Because of his diminished capabilities, you can't just immediately build the castle as a tower--to overcome certain height restrictions, you'll be required to use specific tiles to break the wards blocking you. Runs can conclude as quickly as several seconds or take as long as a few minutes, depending on which rooms and monsters show up and how much gold you can earn with each tile placement in order to place more rooms or monsters.  Of course, all of this is in vain. After each night, a heroic knight named Sir Del Montes ruthlessly tears down Dracula’s newly built castle. Players will then go to the hub area: a cliff overlooking the building site, where Dracula and various vendors reside. Some of these vendors sell new castle pieces for the hearts you collect over multiple runs, which in turn open opportunities to expand the castle beyond its current limits. Some building pieces are given temporarily, such as the one in the current demo build: the innkeeper, who wants his room surrounded by at least five other tiles. Del Montes even has some gentle words of encouragement for you, though he also says that he’ll knock down whatever you put up! So far, almost every part of Draftula is meticulously calculated, from the roster of available parts to its pleasing retro vibe and chill music. The demo isn't very long, consisting of only a handful of runs before the lack of available room tiles halts progression--you just can't reach the next height requirement. However, of what's there, Draftula feels like a roguelike that rewards planning, and I hope the full game expands on its awesome gameplay loop. My one big criticism is that Draftula needs an undo button just in case you make a mistake. It can be frustrating to accidentally misclick where I wanted a tile to go, so hopefully a way to circumvent that will be included in the full game.  But don't let my one complaint put you off trying out the game; the Draftula demo is great and it's only getting better. The demo has just been updated with a new Nightly mode, for example. This update adds a set number of tiles, a map, and mods that change the rules. These include bonuses that are granted whenever you place a room, such as randomly putting an Evil Eye room (which re-adds all other rooms in its row back to your available pieces) on the board after every fourth room you place, not being able to place more rooms in the same column of space during the turn you already placed a room there, and randomly distributing a copy of one room you’ve just played. The scoring method in Nightly mode is also predetermined, with bonuses calculated for the number of rooms placed, the castle’s height, and any coins and gems earned for that run. It’s a good way to keep practicing before the game’s release. You can retry as many times as you like, but you can only submit your score to the leaderboard once.  There’s currently no release date set for Draftula. However, if you fancy a chill construction game on theme with the latest Castlevania adventure, or just need a break from its many difficult boss battles, consider giving this demo a go!

A new Pokemon is landing soon in Pokemon Go. The meteorite Minior is making their debut in the mobile game beginning October 19, but you won't encounter them like a typical Pokemon. Rather than periodically spawning in the wild, Minior's appearance is fittingly tied to meteor showers. The celestial Pokemon will only spawn on certain evenings when a real-world meteor shower is taking place, so you'll need to fire up the game on specific nights if you want to catch a glimpse of the new monster. To help you add the falling star to your collection, here are some tips on how to catch Minior, including a schedule of upcoming meteor showers, how to change Minior's form, and whether the meteorite Pokemon can be shiny. In This Article How To Get MiniorMinior Shower ScheduleHow To Change Minior's FormCan Minior Be Shiny? How To Get Minior Minior is a little different from other common Pokemon in that they won't just randomly spawn in the wild; you'll only have a chance to encounter one on evenings when a meteor shower is taking place. During these specific nights, Minior will spawn on the map between 5 PM and 11:59 PM local time. From there, it's simply a matter of waiting for one to appear, then tapping on them to initiate a catch encounter. You can increase your odds of successfully catching the Pokemon by first feeding them a Razz Berry. Minior Shower Schedule Minior will appear in the wild during the following meteor showers: Orionids October 19 5-11:59 PM local time Leonids November 14 5-11:59 PM local time Geminids December 11 5-11:59 PM local time One Minior color will debut in the Northern Hemisphere, and another color will debut in the Southern Hemisphere Eta Aquariids May 3 5-11:59 PM local time Southern Delta Aquariids July 28 5-11:59 PM local time Perseids August 10 5-11:59 PM local time According to the official Pokemon Go website, a different Minior core color will debut during each of the aforementioned events, so you'll need to play the game during every meteor shower if you're hoping to add all seven core colors to your collection. A couple of bonuses will also be available during the meteor shower events. You'll receive extra Stardust for catching Minior, and you'll see shooting stars streaking across the in-game sky from 5-9 PM local time. The game will also offer a free Timed Research that rewards you with a Star Piece and additional Stardust when completed. How To Change Minior's Form Minior doesn't evolve into any other Pokemon, but the meteorite Pokemon does come in several different variants.  Every Minior you encounter will initially be in Meteor Form, with the same brown outer shell. Once the Pokemon sustains enough damage during battle and their HP drops below 50 percent, their shell will break to reveal a different colored core. There are seven core colors in total, one for every hue of the rainbow: red, orange, yellow, green, blue, indigo, and violet. One core color will debut during each meteor shower. Can Minior Be Shiny? At the moment, shiny Minior is not yet available in Pokemon Go. Shiny variants are typically introduced several months after a Pokemon first makes their debut, so shiny Minior may arrive down the line as part of some future event. However, nothing has been announced yet. We're coming up on the end of October, but Pokemon Go still has a few events lined up for this month, including its annual Halloween celebration. Castlevania games typically have relatively simple combat. You press a button and a whip, sword, or other weapon usually comes out swinging. It's not usually complex with combos outside of the Lords of Shadow era, with the series typically focused more on exploration than a thrilling set of moves. Castlevania: Belmont's Curse significantly changes these combat mechanics as Rose has access to seven weapon families, complete with buffs and perks. Each type has four different possible upgrades you can find on your journey, with subsequent weapons recovered from grand chests. To find the best weapons in this metroidvania, you'll occasionally need to think outside the box and find hidden rooms in seemingly unassuming passages. Each weapon type in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse has four slots, with those further right being more powerful. All Castlevania: Belmont's Curse weapons While you start with the humble Longsword, rather than the Vampire Killer whip a Belmont would typically wield to smite Dracula's monstrous army, Rose is proficient with seven different types. Here are all the weapons in the game and their locations: Sword The sword is a quick weapon type that will do more damage to enemies if they're hit immediately after a backdash or slide. You can also use the Arcane Whip on enemies with the sword equipped to dash through them when it connects. Longsword: Paris Streets - Bank of the River Seine (Unlocked at the beginning of the game.) Bastard Sword: Paris Streets - Justice District Sewers (From the area that leads east toward the Main Castle gate, instead turn back left and head toward the Ferryman warp. In this room, you'll need to time your rope swinging to avoid blades and go through Chaos Shards. Lower the bridge to the left and back up the Chaos Shard column to reach the top of the room and the chest.) Joyeuse: Notre Dame - Path of the Penitent (In this section, there is a puzzle where you need to dodge lots of horizontal lasers. On the way back toward the save room, you'll flip a switch that will begin counting down. Keep going left past the Chaos Shards and use the Smash ability on the breakable platform below you. If you make it in time, the sword is in the chest to your left.) Valmanway: Paris Streets - Palace of Justice (Complete the Justice Arcana's test to get this weapon.) Shortsword & Shield These two weapons bridge attack and defense, with regular presses hitting enemies with the shortsword and holding down the attack button blocking attacks from Rose's front. Hold down the button long enough to charge up a shield-dashing attack. Attacking while using the Arcane Whip will execute a shield bash, swatting away enemies in the air. Rusted Harpé and Aegis: Holy Innocents' Cemetery - Serpent's Lair (Outside the Medusa boss fight after defeating her) Fleuret and Tower Shield: Library - Condemned Shelves (At the easternmost point of the Library, light up all but the bottom two glyphs around the globe to make a larger one appear. Clear the platforming section above to drop onto the chest.) Arondight and Lancelot's Shield: Notre-Dame - Tower Entrance (As you climb the left side of the Cathedral, you'll see the gold chest locked away behind a breakable floor. You'll need to quickly make both of the wooden floors above that disappear, then execute a smash to obtain this chest.) Alucard's Sword and Shield: Main Hall - Alucard's Tower (From the door near the Alucard painting near the Castle Entrance, head up and solve the rotating door puzzle so that you clear the highest path. Keep going up until you reach the door back to the Hall and open the shortcut by hitting the cog with your weapon. Head up the left side and go right until you see the Alucard painting. Go up and right. You'll first want to drop down between the two crumbling platforms and swing on the rings until you reach the area underneath Alucard's Tower. There is a blue torch here, so make sure you break it. Afterward, make your way back up and this time jump across all breakable platforms. Break all the remaining blue torches here to unlock Alucard's bedroom, complete with a Dragon Helm easter egg, and the sword and shield.) Spear This long-range weapon has both melee and ranged capabilities. Press the attack button to thrust the spear into the nearest foe. Hold down the button to aim with a reticle showing where it will land, and release it to fling it. The Arcane Whip attack is a pushing attack that makes foes stumble backward. Lance: Holy Innocents' Cemetery - Fountain of the Innocents (Just after the Fountain, head west and you'll see some ledges leading to the top of a building. The Lance is inside the chest.) Halberd: Clock Tower - Mechanic's Ascent (This one is a little misleading location-wise because technically it's at the end of the Western Maintenance Shaft.
