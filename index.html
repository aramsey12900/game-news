Publisher Critical Reflex was founded six years ago and has since curated one of the most unique, strange, and interesting game catalogs. If I had to guess, the game that put Critical Reflex into your game publisher lexicon was probably 2024's Mouthwashing, which launched the same year the publisher released other indie hits like Buckshot Roulette and Arctic Eggs. During Tokyo Game Show last week, I spent a couple of hours at the Critical Reflex booth, which was themed like an underground neon-punk bunker, and it was there I played four games that I immediately added to my Steam wishlist (even though one of them was so grody that it made me gag).Below, I'll be discussing my thoughts on the following four games after going hands-on with each for roughly 30 minutes: Tiny Chills – Not From Ear, Grobnopolis | Last Days, Sated, and Flawless Abbey. Enjoy!Tiny Chills – Not From EarsTiny Chills – Not From Ear I don't know if Tiny Chills – Not From Ear is my TGS game of the show, but it is certainly the only game I played there that made me gag... so that's something. Don't worry, though – that's not a turn-off for me. In fact, I can't wait for more. From developer Docktopus Games, Tiny Chills is a horror/freak game set in a small town where earthquakes don't stop, and neither do the human disappearances. Nobody knows what's going on, and it's freaking everybody out, but that doesn't stop you from getting in a fight at school and getting suspended. Stuck at home, you help your mom at her ear-cleaning clinic, using a variety of tools to dig through patients' horrendous, wax-filled canals to clean out gunk, bugs, monsters (???), and more. The world has an overall Coraline vibe: something is off about my parents and the way they treat me. And Tiny Chills' grainy, shadow-filled, dreary lo-fi look sets it apart from other games in the ear-cleaning genre. At your mom's decrepit and lonely clinic, patients bang on the door as if the world is ending (and it might be?) and they're desperate for one last ear-cleaning before it all goes up in smoke. The knocks on the doors scared me every time – it was seriously so loud. Patients explain that something is itchy or ringing in their ears, and in no time at all, you're looking through their canals from a first-person perspective, guiding tools to the eardrum.  You can use a suction tool to suck up clumps of wax and a hook-like instrument to latch onto bigger pieces, which you must then carefully drag out of the ear. There are other tools, but ultimately, your job is to clean up the ears and get whatever is clogging them out, and it is always. so. dang. nasty. Between the actual act of cleaning these ears, the squelchy, moist noises that accompany it, and the overall sickening vibe of Tiny Chills' atmosphere, my tolerance for grodiness reached its threshold, and I couldn't help but gag. It's disgusting; it's awesome. Besides cleaning ears, you do some strange things in your house, like catching a pet crow that flies around and figuring out where your mom keeps disappearing to. Something/everything is off about this world, and I cannot wait to learn more about just what the heck is going on in Tiny Chills. In the meantime, I'm going to prepare my stomach for whatever else lurks in the ears of this game's patients.Grobnopolis | Last Days Grobnopolis | Last Days Grobnopolis | Last Days uses a word – Grobnopolis – that you will say out loud once and then say it out loud hundreds of times afterward because of how good it rolls off the tongue. After playing it for roughly 20 minutes, I think Grobnopolis is also a game you should keep on your radar if you're into atmospheric horror games that have you asking yourself, "Just what the hell is going on here?" Set in Huta-Grobno in the year 1983, you are an apartment caretaker who's just trying to do his job while the world outside becomes blurrier and blurrier with suffocating smoke, the result of something gone wrong in a mine somewhere in town. Inside my humble apartment, painted with drab browns and depressing greys, I interact with various objects to learn the lay of this oppressive land. I eventually reach a radio, and by scrolling through live stations, I hear strange opera music, workers mumbling as they piece together what's gone wrong, and broadcasts warning of impending danger, a Church cult, and more. This radio seems key to Grobnopolis's narrative, as I'm clearly listening to stations I shouldn't have access to and learning things I shouldn't know.  I interact with other people who call this apartment block home, and while none of them are outright sinister, something is definitely up, and they all freak me out. If I were this caretaker, you wouldn't catch me leaving my apartment, but specific tasks, like finding cords and other objects in the hallways to connect up some equipment in my place, force me to. All the while, a large crack or hole in my kitchen keeps growing, whispering things I don't yet understand. "From the black veins of the old mines rises a thick, blood-red ichor the locals whisper of as 'Soup,'" the game's Steam description reads. "It climbs through foundations, stains stairwells, and pools in places that should not hold liquid. Some things should never be unearthed. Some things were meant to remain buried."  As this mysterious "Soup" makes its way to more cracks and crevices in Huta-Grobno, the local Church gets involved, bussing certain civilians out of town and out of danger, while asserting its control to gain power in the decaying city. I genuinely have no idea where any of this is heading, but Grobnopolis has my attention, and I look forward to learning more about what's going on here. SatedSated Of the four games I played during my Critical Reflex preview during TGS, I think Sated has the most potential to be the publisher's next hit. It mixes something the indie space loves – roguelites – with monstrous horror fused with a little food-inspo by way of Cooking Mama. This demo wasted no time, putting me in front of a giant mechanical shark who hopped out of the water to hand me his hand/fin-written order. He requests some strange but not surprising ingredients, and though I don't know exactly what each is, each has a symbol associated with it. I take that order to my nearby fridge, which acts like a slot machine with a lever I can pull for another random drop of ingredients, and grab what I need based on those symbols. Sated smartly neglects to detail exactly how much of each ingredient is needed, instead giving you an ambiguous bar that shows how much of this or that ingredient should make up the entire recipe. This keeps you on your toes, because to make the most satisfying meal, you need to guesstimate as close as possible to what the hungry monster requires.  After feeding the monster, you're given a score indicating how much they liked or disliked the meal you prepared. After that, you head to your underground train's captain's seat, select your next destination on a typical roguelite map/chart, and see what monster awaits their next meal there. Unfortunately, at the next stop, I fail to make a decent meal for the monster and, as a result, become the meal. I'm eaten alive, and my run is over. The hooks are there, and I'm ready for my next run; now I just need to know when that next feeding run will happen.Flawless AbbeyFlawless Abbey Flawless Abbey is both a surrealist, dreamlike kaleidoscope of colors and adventure, and a moody and oppressive mystery, and I'm interested in both pieces of its puzzle. In Flawless Abbey, you are a dreamseeker who lives inside a purple-and-grey facility where you hook yourself up to strange pods that allow you to explore an obelisk known as a dream-tower. While that sounds fantastical, it's just another day on the job for you. I go from door to door, trying to find today's pod of choice, only for it to break down when I finally find the one I'm tasked with sitting in. I walk to the IT person/being of consciousness who clearly loves the company it works for and is able to reboot the pod in question. I make my way back there, plug in, and find myself aboard a mythical train soaring through the stars and swirling sky. Aboard this train, I solve typical environmental puzzles, like one that tasks me with opening a door using a special mechanism I can only understand after using a train ticket as a symbol key. Throughout Flawless Abbey, I answer questions by selecting dialogue choices that feel less like answers for this game's world and more like ways for the game to learn more about me, the player. It's odd and thought-provoking, even if I don't really know what's going on. Visually, though, I'm sold, and the painterly, patchwork-style art, coupled with a far-out score, has me intrigued enough to figure out exactly where this train is headed.  The game's Steam page promises more, like corporate benefits and health insurance, a beast trying to tear down the dream-tower, a personal assistant who will guide me through dreams (even though they are also terrified about what's going on), and other dreamseekers to meet. "Something isn't right," the game's description reads, and I agree, but I'll have to wait for the game's launch to learn what's wrong.

Ace Attorney fans have been hoping for the announcement of a new entry for many years now, but today, we got a surprise instead. Ace Attorney: Dual Destinies VR is a virtual reality version of the fifth mainline game in Capcom's lawyer-themed adventure series, and it's coming to Meta platforms next year. You can watch the reveal trailer below. This port gives Dual Destinies a more hands-on approach, allowing players to pick up evidence to inspect, examine environments for clues in first person, and use gestures and voice commands during cross-examinations: You can literally yell "Objection!" to object to a witness' contradiction. You don't physically inhabit the protagonist 100% of the time, however – dialogue during trials still involves seeing characters in third person, meaning you'll still get to see Apollo, Phoenix, and Athena's over-the-top animations. According to the game's store page, it will come to Meta Quest 3S, Meta Quest 3, Meta Quest Pro, Meta Quest 2, and Meta Quest, though developer Amata K.K.'s description of the game omits the original Meta Quest from that lineup. It drops sometime in 2027. Personally, I find this to be an odd move for the series. Capcom has been generous and thorough with its HD collections of the older Ace Attorney titles. After the Ace Attorney Investigations Collection dropped in 2024, the only remaining Phoenix Wright adventure you couldn't play on modern platforms was his crossover with Professor Layton. As such, it seemed like the only next step was a brand new entry, so a VR adaptation is particularly surprising. Ace Attorney also isn't a series that needs to be a VR title. As fun as it might be to yell "Objection" every couple of minutes during a cross-examination, the vast majority of gameplay is non-voiced dialogue. Unlike the highest quality VR games, which work best in short bursts, Ace Attorney games are long, and wearing a headset just to mostly read text for a few hours doesn't sound appealing. Finally, Phoenix Wright: Ace Attorney – Dual Destinies is my least favorite Ace Attorney game, but I understand why it was their pick here. It's the series' fifth entry, but it was the first foray into 3D, so it's the earliest release that could be reasonably set in VR. You can play it without playing prior titles, but I'd always recommend the original trilogy's HD collection over this one.The best game to get the VR adaptation would have been The Great Ace Attorney Chronicles, as it's a better standalone adventure and it's been longer since that game's release (2021) compared to the HD re-release of Dual Destinies, which dropped in 2024's Apollo Justice: Ace Attorney Trilogy. But even though The Great Ace Attorney Chronicles is a better game, Dual Destinies is shorter and has more of the series' iconic characters, so it's a more reasonable first project.The silver lining is that Capcom is neither developing nor publishing this game (that's all being done by Amata K.K.), so there's a decent chance Ace Attorney 7 is still in the works – the development team won't be preoccupied with finishing this VR title first. Maybe this VR version will drastically change my opinion of Dual Destinies, but it's unlikely. I'll find out when the game drops sometime next year.

The release of Sonic Mania in 2017 signaled to many fans that Sega and the Sonic the Hedgehog franchise were listening to fans. Announced as part of the series' 25th anniversary in 2016, the title was developed as a collaboration between Sonic Team and a group of independent developers led by designer Christian Whitehead. Following the release and subsequent overwhelming acclaim of Sonic Mania and its upgraded version, Sonic Mania Plus, Sonic Team entered into conversations with Evening Star, the studio founded by Whitehead and several other members of the Sonic Mania dev team in 2018, but despite getting into the prototyping phase, that sequel never happened. In 2023, a Sonic Mania sequel felt like a distant hope, as Evening Star announced its own platformer, Penny's Big Breakaway, while Sega released its next 2D Sonic game, Sonic Superstars, which was developed in collaboration with Sonic co-creator Naoto Ohshima's studio, Arzest. When I broached the Sonic Mania 2 topic with Iizuka a few years ago, he confirmed that those initial conversations occurred with Evening Star. "There was a period after the development of Sonic Mania when we worked with them to consider concepts for the next generation of Classic Sonic," Iizuka said in 2023. "It's too bad that it didn't become a finished game. Still, I'm pleased that the next generation of Classic Sonic that we talked about with them has been achieved with Superstars." Sonic Superstars Iizuka mentions that he wanted to go a different route with Mania's successor, instead relying on 3D models instead of pixel-art graphics to appeal to a wider audience. Whitehead, for his part, backed up Iizuka's retelling of the events. "When we started Evening Star, it was always our goal to move beyond 2D pixel art into 3D games, and develop all-new, original IP," he says. "Evening Star did work with Sega to explore possible directions Classic Sonic could go after the success of Sonic Mania. Sonic Mania 2 was never in development, though, because we actually agreed early on that we should try to make something fresh, like hand-drawn 2D or 2.5D. At the same time, Evening Star was developing our new Star Engine, so we also ended up making a cool Sonic prototype that played with depth in 2.5D, and some other gameplay ideas. As Iizuka-san said, though, [Evening Star] did not continue to full production on the game, at which point Arzest took the helm and made Superstars alongside Sonic Team. We moved forward with our plans to develop an original 3D title, which is, of course, Penny's Big Breakaway!"Now, three years following that conversation, Sonic Team is collaborating with TecoPark for Sonic Pico Park, releasing this November. When I ask Iizuka what he looks for when selecting a development partner, he says studios and developers that can fill gaps where Sonic Team isn't as skilled or experienced are an important factor. "Sonic Team is really proficient at making action games; they make action game after action game," Iizuka says. "The Pico Park team, they’re really good at making that action-puzzle format. That's something Sonic Team has never made before, so I wouldn’t want to say, 'Hey, Sonic Team! Go make an action-puzzle game.' But the Pico Park team, they're really good at doing it." Sonic Mania With Sonic Mania perhaps the strongest example of a project Sonic Team worked on collaboratively, it was natural to steer the conversation toward an update on the potential of Sonic Mania 2. While it isn't planned at the moment, Iizuka doesn't think the door is completely shut. "We're still really close with them," he says. "We've gone out eating and drinking with them because relationships are important to us. They also have a lot of projects that they're working on for what they want to do and what their business needs are. We don't have anything to talk about now, but there's definitely a desire to want to work with them again, and we think they could make a great game. It's not off the books yet, but it's not something that's moving forward currently."I reached out to Evening Star chief technology officer Hunter Bridges to ask his reaction to the quote. To my surprise, he had already seen the quote from Iizuka when he read it in the latest issue of Game Informer. After reading it, he emailed Iizuka with the subject line "Eating and Drinking" (referring to his quote) to try to set up some time to do just that while Iizuka was in town for Tokyo Game Show 2026. Unfortunately, the timing didn't work out for Bridges and Iizuka to meet up in Tokyo this month. "It's as he says, though, we have a good relationship with Sega and keep in touch," Bridges tells me. "We are working on something exciting right now, but it's not Sonic this time. But if the right opportunity lines up on both sides, we'd be happy to make another great Sonic game. Whether that would be Mania 2 is up for debate; we are an ambitious team!" Sonic Mania released in 2017 to widespread critical acclaim, earning an 86 on reviews aggregator Metacritic, including an 8.5 from Game Informer. Neither Sonic Superstars nor Penny's Big Breakaway reached the same level of acclaim, earning a 73 and 74, respectively, including a 7.5 for Superstars and a 7 for Penny's Big Breakaway from Game Informer. Neither Sonic Team nor Evening Star has publicly announced their next project, though Iizuka recently told 4Gamer that he wants to continue evolving both the 2D and 3D sides of Sonic.

Platform: Publisher: Bloober Team Developer: Broken Mirror Games Rating: Teen Bloober Team has been on a hot streak lately, bringing Silent Hill back into the positive-convo zeitgeist with its Silent Hill 2 remake in 2024, followed by another survival-horror hit last year, Cronos: The New Dawn (which is getting an action-focused DLC called Lazarus this year, too). All of this is on the heels of other Bloober games like The Medium and the team's original Layers of Fear series. Regardless of how you feel about the Poland-based studio's horror output, you can't deny it stays busy, so it came as no surprise to see yet another new Bloober game revealed during a Nintendo Direct earlier this month. Dubbed Onyx: The Dark Grip, this 2.5D "dark adventure" game is launching exclusively on Nintendo Switch 2 sometime next year. After speaking with Bloober's senior brand manager Dawid Podgorski, I'm convinced this is one of the most Nintendo-sounding games hitting the console in 2027, and that's courtesy of a suite of console-specific gestures and mechanics that remind me of the Wii and DS era.   Bloober Team CEO Piotr Babieno started selling Nintendo games at a local game shop 15 to 20 years ago, becoming one of the first to do so in Poland, where Nintendo hardware and software had barely entered the market. Babieno even organized the first Nintendo fan convention in Poland during the GameCube era, "so we've always had an idea to make something specifically for Nintendo," Podgorski tells me, explaining Bloober's Nintendo history. Podgorski works for Bloober, but Onyx: The Dark Grip is actually in development at the studio's subsidiary Broken Mirror Games, which Podgorski says Bloober uses to "make a game we cannot make on our own." Broken Mirror Games consists of Bloober specialists and external devs – it's also behind the upcoming 3v1 asymmetrical horror game Saw: Genesis. Onyx: The Dark Grip could only exist as a Switch 2 exclusive, Podgorski explains, and that much is very clear after my quick 20-minute meeting. "Survive as the Vessel, Intervene as the Player" is the slideshow pitch for Onyx: The Dark Grip, and that's because you possess Holden, a "regular guy" human whom you, as a god-like creature, need to find something. Onyx: The Dark Grip is set in a small, isolated town gripped by a relentless winter, based on Poland's real-world Bieruń. However, curiously, Holden doesn't know he's possessed – he only feels that he needs to keep going (to where you drive him to) but has no idea why.  The game has been in development for three years and is an homage to classic Nintendo consoles and experiences found on the Wii and DS and other consoles like the PlayStation Vita, all of which uniquely took advantage of what the consoles could do. As the Vessel – Holden – you will explore the town, solve puzzles, and fight using melee and ranged attacks. Podgorski stops short of calling it a full-on horror game, instead calling it a "dark adventure" experience, but the reveal trailer suggests Onyx: The Dark Grip will, at the very least, be spooky. As the god, you will manipulate reality to accomplish what you need using Holden, and that's where all the Nintendo-sounding things happen.Players will use god-like powers to move things in Holden's world, allowing him to progress forward; you will touch the screen with both thumbs to act as an electrical conductor for the generator Holden is interacting with in the game; you will have to whistle and blow into the microphone to make Holden warmer when he gets cold; and when Holden's mental health is afflicted by terror and fear, moths will cover the screen, and you will need to swipe those away to clear the screen and Holden's headspace.  You can even read NPC minds as a god and put your own thoughts into their brains. Plus, you can use what is essentially a "Talk With The Dead" spell to learn key information from corpses in the town, and you can also swipe the screen to throw fireballs and start fires with sparks. Podgorski says your "Void" ability also lets you crush enemies emerging from beyond by tilting the Switch 2 back and forth using the built-in gyroscope. Podgorski emphasizes that you are not playing as the character on the screen, but as yourself too – the god of Holden's world. And, like you with a Switch 2 in hand, Onyx: The Dark Grip's god, as it were, will play with Holden's world like a handheld game device, metaphorically. "Everything happening there is there, and you can intervene, but you are not there," Podgorski tells me about Holden's world.  Inspirations for Onyx: The Dark Grip include Eternal Darkness (horror that breaks the fourth wall), Silent Hill (psychological instability and perception horror), Inside (2.5D cinematic clarity and restraint), Resident Evil 2 (pressure and pacing), and True Detective: Night Country (winter isolation and grounded dread). Polish composer and multi-instrumentalist Jan Borysewicz will back the adventure with a score recorded with the Prague Philharmonic Orchestra. That's a lot to be excited about for Onyx: The Dark Grip, but I didn't get to see the game in action; I only learned these things from Bloober's senior brand manager (a point Podgorski acknowledged). That said, the premise is enough to keep it on my radar as we head into 2027. The more games that remind me of the Vita, Wii, and DS era, the better, and Onyx: The Dark Grip seems set on doing just that.
