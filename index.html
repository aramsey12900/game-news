World of Warcraft: Forever will avoid repeating WoW Classic's mistakes and will have tools to prevent "dead" or "unsustainable" server rulesets, Blizzard said. In the past, various WoW Classic servers became "dead" over time, as players either stopped playing or transferred to other servers. Those massive drops in population had major ramifications for players left on the server being unable to find groups for raids or dungeons, as well as for the server's economy and auction house. In some extreme cases, Blizzard gave away free character transfers so players could leave their "dead" servers behind. While Forever is going a different route with what are essentially mega servers tailored around specific rulesets, some players might still have anxiety when it comes to picking a ruleset that will be a healthy place to play long-term.  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=BVyhnixdPQg In the latest episode of the WoW: Forever podcast, Blizzard said it has tools it can use if a ruleset's population becomes "unsustainable." Senior game designer Josh Greenfield gave the example of Forever's separate RP and (newly added) RP-PvP server rulesets. He said there were initially concerns about splitting up the RP community, fearing that one or both of the rulesets wouldn't be able to stand the test of time. He said Blizzard now believes "it will be fine," but does have options should the worst happen.  "If someday down the line, if something happens and one or both of those populations can't quite sustain themselves, we have options if we do need to say link up their auction houses or allow them to group for instanced content," Greenfield said. Greenfield said it's not something Blizzard will jump to unless absolutely necessary and that it's committed to its current separate-ruleset path. Blizzard debated the idea of rulesets during Forever's development and discussed letting players from different rulesets group together for instanced content or combining auction houses. However, it landed on wanting to maintain distinct communities, in part because of the differences in server economies when it comes to PvE and PvP rulesets, with Greenfield using an example of how it's easier to farm for gold and items on a PvE server. That in turn opened up debates on whether these cross-ruleset players would be able to trade items with one another and the need for other restrictions. "Ultimately, these are different mindsets, these are different rulesets," Greenfield said the team decided. "Let's keep them separate for now. That is a part of original WoW that we don't want to just throw out completely. But I think the tech is set up in such a way that if we ever did really feel like we needed to make a change, we could do so." He said those options are merely "levers we can pull if we need to" and that it's important for Blizzard to "stay flexible." That's good news, and will likely give players worried about choosing the wrong ruleset some relief.  Blizzard is keeping busy in the handful of days left before Forever's launch. It previously detailed how it's making improvements to Forever's divisive layering system in time for release, and announced major changes to racial abilities.  In a first for WoW, Forever will be fully playable on a controller. WoW executive producer and vice president Holly Longdale recently said Forever's controller support is a step towards WoW being playable "wherever you want" and "on whatever you want." WoW: Forever arrives November 4. If you want to be ready for launch day, you can technically make up to three WoW: Forever characters right now.

Phantom Blade 0’s 66 Days Mode Is BrilliantNEW
When I start action games, I typically start on harder difficulties, just so I can have the game push my limits and strategies a bit more. So when I first booted up Phantom Blade Zero, I was surprised...
When I start action games, I typically start on harder difficulties, just so I can have the game push my limits and strategies a bit more. So when I first booted up Phantom Blade Zero, I was surprised to find that you do have the option to start on Wayfarer, Pathbreaker, or Hellwalker, aka: easy, medium, or hard, but not 66 Days mode. This initially confused me. Coming to a concession by starting on Hellwalker, I found that you can just switch difficulties whenever you like, which I’m sure can be for better or for worse, depending on who you talk to.  But after 40 hours beating the game on Hellwalker, I wasn’t sure if the 66 Days mode would be worth replaying the game for. 66 Days mode is the same difficulty as Hellwalker, the hardest difficulty that I had already played, but with the added rule that you are locked into that difficulty and only have 66 lives. If you die 66 times, it's game over--permadeath. You have to start all over.  Hesitant to go through the game all over again, I hovered over the option for a moment before curiosity got the better of me and I gave it a try. The first thing I noticed is that you get to keep all of your gear, all of your upgrades, all of your builds, but no quest items. This was surprising, as I thought you would start from scratch and work your way up again. But as I kept going, the second thing I noticed changed my impression. The moment I got hit for the first time, a switch flipped in my brain telling me, “Hey, don't f**k this up." This thought did not occur at all during my initial playthrough, as I would just mindlessly fly through swarms of trash mobs, and if I got sloppy and died, I’d just run back and clean up the rest of the mobs. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=Qhzva8rV9Q4 This is the moment I understood 66 Days mode. In a sense, your first playthrough is a teacher who teaches you the tools on how to tackle the problems, and learning the history and stories. There’s no worry about asking questions or experimenting to see if something works; there’s no risk and it's okay to be wrong. Just try again. This also ensures you complete Soul’s journey all the way to the end, no matter what, and you learn the truth of the story. But 66 Days mode is the true test. You have all the knowledge you need of how every encounter works now, and you have your best/favorite builds that you’re familiar with to go through every fight. And you can’t mess up, because you only have 66 chances.When I died for the first time in 66 Days mode, I truly felt like it was my fault. I fought all these bosses before, I know how they work, I have my favorite build, I have all the tools and knowledge at my disposal. If you die now, there’s nobody to blame but yourself, and I love that. Being in new situations for the first time and learning new things can be both an invigorating and frustrating experience. At best you understand that it’s part of the learning process, and at worst you just straight up blame the game. But in 66 Days mode, you can only point the finger at yourself given how powerful and knowledgeable you have become, and it felt so much more rewarding to fly through bosses back to back with that power. This comes back to a general point in game design: restriction, risk, and power, when done just right, are an amazing tool. They can recontextualize the exact same situation in a completely new light, and many other games balance these things to great effect, such as Arc Raiders' loot, Elden Ring's runes, and Blood of Dawnwalker's time limit. Probably the biggest point of contention at this point is that players don’t want to invest 30-40 hours just to get to the mode they want to play. But I would say this is purpose built this way; every game is designed differently, and this intentional choice for Phantom Blade 0, which didn’t make sense to me before, is now clear. All that is to say, if you enjoy your first playthrough of Phantom Blade Zero, and you feel competent enough to try another streamlined playthrough skipping all the cutscenes and focusing only on the game’s crown jewel, the glorious combat, give 66 Days mode a try. I sure didn’t regret it.

It's shown on the bottom left of your screen as a yellowish bar under your HP. It's not quite stamina, as sprinting and using standard attack buttons don't deplete it. However, your more powerful Sha-Chi attacks (Triangle/Y) do deplete the Sha-Chi, as does blocking enemy attacks with L1/LB. Your enemy can also break your Sha-Chi with multiple and/or powerful attacks. When your Sha-Chi is low or depleted, switch to your second main weapon, as it will come out with a stylish attack AND a full Sha-Chi bar. Your other weapon will then have a cooldown (that can be decreased as you level up), but when that cooldown is over, you can switch to the other main weapon with a full Sha-Chi bar once more – it's this rotation that will keep you from ever being left open and helpless with an empty Sha-Chi bar. Try every weapon: Phantom Blade Zero has 30 main weapons and 25 Phantom Edge weapons, which are limited-use (requiring you to build up charges to use). You'll earn some by completing main quests and defeating bosses throughout, but many are optional finds that require you to stray from the golden path. No matter how you find a weapon, you must try every single one you find. I can't tell you how many weapons I found, started using, and quickly said, "Oh yup, this is going to be my new main," only to discover another new weapon that replaced it. There are so many cool weapons and the next weapon you find could be your new favorite weapon. Don't hoard upgrade materials: I know, I know. We all hoard materials, potions, and this and that because we might need them later. But I'm telling you: Do not hoard your materials in Phantom Blade Zero. Developer S-Game wants you to upgrade all of your weapons and discover ones you like, dislike, love, and hate. As you upgrade weapons, new combo abilities and perks are added, making them feel even more unique and powerful. You will discover so many upgrade materials – seriously, like so many. You'll also find plenty of revert items that let you extract every resource poured into a weapon, so you can use those materials on a different weapon. For the most part, though, many of the weapons use their own unique materials that maybe one or two other weapons will use so you can safely upgrade without worrying about sacrificing an upgrade for a different weapon.   Explore everywhere: Phantom Blade Zero has an incredible map, one of my all-time favorites. They're pretty easy to spot while exploring: a single tall, glowing blue flower standing among a circle of smaller blue flowers. Interact with them, and you get to listen to an interesting story vignette. Most of these flowers are part of a set of one, two, three, or more. When you complete a set, you will gain HP.
