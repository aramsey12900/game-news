Leonida is a state with plenty of powerful machinery. From high-powered weapons to devastatingly fast vehicles, you can have quite the arsenal at your disposal in Grand Theft Auto 6. However, if you're searching for the ultimate combination of weapons and transportation, then you'll want to get your hands on a tank. For decades in GTA, tanks have been the vehicle that players use when they want to go on a true six-star Wanted level rampage. Tanks are extremely difficult to take down, have a variety of weapons to use, and can crush anything in their path. Naturally, a vehicle as powerful as a tank is going to be difficult to obtain, but in GTA 6, there's just one location where you can acquire one. Where to find a tank in GTA 6 As far as we've been able to tell so far in GTA 6, the only location where you can get in and drive a Hauners Rhino tank is at the Mile Zero Air Station in the Leonida Keys. The Mile Zero Air Station is located west of Key Lento in the far southwestern part of the map, surrounded by small islands. While the story will eventually bring you to this particular location, you can attempt to explore it right from the start of the game. Of course, that's no small feat, as this is a U.S. Army base more guarded than any other location in Leonida. You'll find dozens of armored and well-armed soldiers on the ground, as well as a few weaponized choppers in the air providing air support. To make matters worse, the Mile Zero Air Station has fences all around it that are impenetrable; you can't climb over them or drive a vehicle through them. If you want a tank badly, though, you'll have to somehow make it into the military station. We can confirm there are a couple of tanks that you can take control of at Mile Zero, and so far we've noticed they spawn on the north and south sides of the base more often than not. They're also usually parked inside a hangar, so make sure to check those instead of searching outside areas. Managing to sneak into the base undetected allows you to find a tank and take control of it without having to kick another NPC out of it. If you get spotted breaking into the base by a guard, you'll immediately receive a five-star Wanted level, and soon at least one tank will make its way to your position. Tanks can make their way to you both inside and outside the base, so it doesn't matter where you're located. While the tank's missiles can kill you in a single hit, having the vehicle come to you can be advantageous. If you can climb aboard the tank, kick the driver out, and take control of it for yourself, you don't have to do any work searching for it in the base. Regardless of how you get inside the war vehicle, it appears the Hauners Rhino tanks at Mile Zero are the only ones in Leonida. How to use cheats to spawn a tank in GTA 6 Getting a tank the normal way is fairly difficult, but this is GTA, so you know there's a cheat code that you can use to spawn the vehicle for yourself. However, cheat codes work differently in GTA 6, and you can no longer press some buttons at any point to completely break the game. Instead, cheats are only available to players who beat the main story. Then, each individual cheat code is unlocked by completing a specific challenge. For a Hauners Rhino tank, you need to complete "All Survival Challenges" in GTA 6. Once you've done that, you'll see which cheat code spawns a tank. At the moment, the cheat code is unknown, as no one has completed all survival challenges yet. Whenever the cheat code becomes public, though, you can find it below. How To Save Guns And Weapons You Find In GTA 6NEW

Weapons are arguably the most important items that you can equip for Jason and Lucia in Grand Theft Auto 6. Whether it's a pistol that helps with sticky situations or an assault rifle for elaborate robberies, you need a weapon on both characters at all times to survive in Leonida. While GTA 6 features over 100 unique weapons to find and use, just picking one up doesn't automatically mean you get to keep it equipped for the rest of the game. Rockstar Games has completely reworked the weapons system, and you need to take some extra steps to save any weapons that you find or have used in the past. In This Article How to save your weapons in GTA 6Stow weapons in your vehicle's trunkManage weapons at Ammu-Nation or a garage How to save your weapons in GTA 6 There are a few different ways you'll find weapons in GTA 6. The safest and easiest way is to simply purchase them from your local gun store, which is often an Ammu-Nation distributor. Here, you can buy all of the default weapons in the game, including pistols, SMGs, ARs, snipers, shotguns, and more. Once you buy a weapon here, you can choose to simply "buy and equip it," which immediately equips that gun to your loadout. The other ways to find weapons consist of looting them off dead (or knocked-out) bodies on the ground, stealing them from cop cars or military armories, or finding legendary weapons (marked with a gold gun icon) in compounds, like Kingfisher hideouts. You'll also get the chance to keep some weapons from story missions. Regardless of how you come across weapons in GTA 6, though, you need to be able to save them so you can access those weapons whenever you want. In many situations in GTA 6, you end up with a weapon equipped, and then it disappears due to mission progression, sleeping, or another factor. If you want this to stop happening to you, there are a few ways to make sure you save weapons in GTA 6. Stow weapons in your vehicle's trunk In most scenarios, the easiest way to make sure you save a weapon is to stow it in the trunk of your personal vehicle. This mostly goes for longarm weapons. By opening the trunk of any vehicle, you get the option to "Stow Longarms." However, you want to make sure you're stowing weapons in your personal vehicle so you can always have them nearby or a phone call away. Storing weapons in a vehicle you don't own means they'll remain in that vehicle, which usually gets lost during a play session. By stowing weapons in your trunk, you're giving yourself the chance to re-equip them through your loadout. The loadout can be accessed through the trunk, and stowing weapons allows you to scroll through the longarms loadout box to equip them. This is often the best way to save weapons in GTA 6, but it's not the only method. Manage weapons at Ammu-Nation or a garage Whether you bought and customized a default gun at Ammu-Nation or found a legendary weapon at a drug hideout, GTA 6 often saves these weapons without you even knowing it. If you find that a particular weapon is no longer equipped when you view your loadout, stop by an Ammu-Nation and check its weapon locker. You can also visit a garage you've purchased if it has a weapons locker. The weapons locker is tall and green, and there's one at every Ammu-Nation store in Leonida. At a weapons locker, you can choose to "Manage Weapons" or go through your loadout. If you select "Manage Weapons," you'll see a menu that shows every single weapon you own in GTA 6. You can view your weapons by their class (pistol, SMG, AR, etc.), and selecting a class lets you see all the individual weapon types in it. From here, you can hover over a weapon type, such as the Duke .556 Assault Rifle, and press "Triangle/Y" to see its "Variants." The variants tab shows all the different weapons you own for that weapon type. If you feel like you've lost a weapon in GTA 6 or can't find it in your loadout, it's most likely stored in the variants tab within your weapons locker. When you find the weapon you're looking for in the locker, you can press "X/A" to equip it to your current loadout. If you've checked your vehicle's trunk, loadout, and weapons locker and still can't find a particular weapon, it might be lost for good in GTA 6. As long as you know what to do to save weapons in the future, though, that shouldn't happen again. Exclusive – Behind The Romance: Rockstar Talks GTAVI’s Jason And LuciaNEW
After dozens of hours exploring Leonida, I have a lot of positive things to say about Grand Theft Auto VI, and shared as much in our review of the game. But for my part, the feature that elev...
After dozens of hours exploring Leonida, I have a lot of positive things to say about Grand Theft Auto VI, and shared as much in our review of the game. But for my part, the feature that elevated the game most prominently above Rockstar Games’ other (excellent) games wasn’t its graphics, missions, or action – it was the storytelling and interplay between the two lead protagonists. Jason and Lucia bring heart, emotional depth, and a sense of genuine threat to the crime fantasy, as you grow attached to them and start cheering for them against all the odds stacked against them.We reached out to senior members of the Rockstar Games team to ask about their thoughts on Jason and Lucia, and this is what they had to share.During my playthrough, the Jason and Lucia dynamic really elevated the experience, adding heart and emotional weight to the expected mix of action, driving, and activities around Leonida. Was a couple or romantic relationship the plan or goal for the game from very early on in development, or did that come later? Do you see the focus on Jason and Lucia’s relationship as one of the features that separates Grand Theft Auto VI from your prior work on storytelling in the game series?Stephen Donkin, Vice President of Design: “The dual protagonists and the relationship between the two of them was there from very early in development, but exactly how that was expressed in the game did change over time. It was an enormous challenge for us to get them working together in the open world together and finding exactly how that would work. As we started to layer in more mechanics, dialogue, and incremental features across all teams, it just clicked at some point. The ability to be together in the open world also allowed us for new ways to tell the story of the game and to bring some of the exposition and narrative into the open world in a way we hadn’t been able to do before. Hearing these little bits of dialogue as you are just tooling around the world that have specific context to what you as a player have just been doing in the story really knits it all together.” The player has a choice for how to develop the relationship between the two leads, but I imagine most players are drawn toward helping it thrive. In early playtests, is that what you observed?Katie Pica, Design Director: Player Systems & Progression:“Overall, we’ve seen that players are invested in supporting Jason and Lucia’s relationship but we don’t favor a specific path through the game, it is completely down to player choice.”It feels like a lot of the game’s driving narrative beats factor for a close connection between Jason and Lucia. Was it challenging to craft variations that included a more tense or distant connection between the two? Is that even possible?Luke Howard, Senior Design Director: Missions & Visualization:“One of the core goals of this game was to tell the story of Jason and Lucia with a base line relationship where they are amicable, if just platonic, as they still share the same ultimate goals and need each other to achieve them. So, this gave us a good platform on the main flow to expand particularly intense or emotional moments they experience with variation that can expand on their romantic relationship, while not losing anything if that doesn’t exist. In short, we did develop variation in scenes and interactions that range from subtle to more significant, but done in a way that should feel authentic to the player’s choices, relationship-wise and also criminal profile.One example is after Lucia helps Kordell’s gang pull off an intense armored truck robbery she shares the information with Raul’s crew. Afterwards Jason pulls Lucia aside to see how she's coping. This moment carries several variations where the performance direction was captured multiple ways to cover a range emotional energy between them. The actors deliveries are brilliant and nuanced – these small tender moments are where we tried to craft pieces that when added up together over the course of the story, author an authentic version of the player’s Jason and Lucia.” Were there any fictional couples or real-life couples that the team looked at for inspiration in shaping the Jason and Lucia dynamic, whether those couples came from big crime stories or not?Rupert Humphries, Senior Vice President of Narrative:“We definitely started by looking at all the “couple on the run” movies. There are a lot of them but the ones that we kept coming back to were Warren Beatty and Faye Dunaway in Bonnie and Clyde, McQueen and MacGraw in The Getaway (the Jim Thomson book is also great), True Romance, Thelma and Louise. Clooney and JLo’s dynamic in Out of Sight, as well. And then a lot of buddy movies actually – Riggs and Murtaugh, Butch and Sundance, Mike Lowry and Marcus Burnett, etc. for their back and forth together. In terms of having a game where the characters can change based on how you play and whether you really lean into and enjoy the crime spree we thought about how both Pulp Fiction and Natural Born Killers start with a couple in a diner pulling out guns, but then how different those couples are and how different those scenarios play out. Jason and Lucia are a lot tougher than Tim Roth and Amanda Plummer but we liked the idea of telling a story where Jason and Lucia could end up being closer to either end of that spectrum based on what the player was doing.”Sex and intimacy are notoriously complicated and challenging to present in games. How did you aim to strike a balance with their private moments together that felt authentic? Any thoughts about how or why it’s so challenging to present adult relationships in video games?Rupert Humphries, Senior Vice President of Narrative:“This is definitely something we thought a lot about and spent a long time trying to get right. The actors who play Jason and Lucia were so good, and they had a natural chemistry and part of it was just trying to capture some of that.There’s this trade off in making games between player control and then scripted scenarios. We try and give the player as much control as we can because we are working in an interactive medium and actually doing something rather than watching it is going to feel more powerful. But then you have the ability to really mess around and break the illusion of a tender or romantic moment or whatever it is. So the relationship aspect of this story is there if a player wants to engage in it, but if they don’t the story can play out differently.” In its best moments of genuine support, regular connection, open communication, and flirty or engagement with one another, Jason and Lucia can come across as a pretty ideal model of good relationships – even while they’re simultaneously committing major crimes together. Was that juxtaposition of a healthy romantic relationship against an external world of illicit activity an explicit goal for how the narrative came together?Rupert Humphries, Senior Vice President of Narrative:“That was the goal, yes. You are really living with these characters – they’re eating, sleeping, showering, throwing away their trash, or not. The relationship needed to feel real to sit alongside all of that. Then even just as a development team, we live with these characters for years and years. We wanted them to be people we could like, care about, and root for. And they needed to like each other. But then the world itself is so crazy and chaotic and seemingly on the brink of collapse.When we set out to make this game, and go into that multiyear development marathon, we said we wanted to spend our time working on something with heart, and with some characters you could care about and root for. It’s barely worth even stating, but we are living in a time when people are divided along almost every line, and feelings about the future are pretty bleak. We wanted to create a world that held up a mirror to that, showed that fever pitch of craziness that we are all maybe a couple of bad decisions away from, but we wanted the story we were going to tell to have something positive at its core and the message that human connection is better than division.”The game automatically moves you to play one character or another at certain points, but for many sequences, you can choose your playable lead. In your experience playing and watching others play, do most players gravitate toward one character or another as “their character?”Stephen Donkin, Vice President of Design:“It really varied in our playthroughs. They are both such great characters and performances that people seemed to gravitate to both of them in different ways. But overall, we have found the split of time between characters to be relatively even on a per-playthrough and also at a macro level.”

While Grand Theft Auto 6 is set to dominate what GameSpot covers for the next month (at least), it doesn't mean there aren't plenty of other games out there that those of us are playing at the same time (or in the cases of those of us who aren't big GTA fans, what we're playing instead). Whether it's a new demo for a game we're eagerly anticipating, an indie title that's bravely facing off against Rockstar's juggernaut of a title, or just something that made us smile, we want to take a chance to highlight those games too. Below, you'll find a list of smaller or lesser-known games that we're playing alongside or instead of GTA 6, as well as links to our coverage of those titles. As we get into the thick of Grand Theft Auto 6 season, we'll keep coming back here to add additional impression pieces we want to highlight. In This Article Memorable DemosAt Fate's EndDraftulaExterminautsMy Arms Are Longer NowNirvana NoirParanormal Activity: ThresholdNew ReleasesAustralia Did ItDeadlockHungerTanuki: Pon's SummerThank You Bus DriverQueen's DomainVillage in the ShadeVolvy's Adventure: Reslimed Memorable Demos A fantastic demo can reinforce your decision to go ahead and wishlist or preorder a game you already have your sights on, but the best-of-the-best are those that surprise. Sometimes you have no idea what a game is going to be like or whether you'd even like it, and then you try out the free demo at the last in-person E3 you ever attended and suddenly the game becomes your entire personality for the next six years. Over the past month or so, we've played demos in both camps. None of these games are out yet, but they're all scheduled to launch either later this year or sometime next year. If you've got a spare hour or two, these snippets are an excellent way to spend your time. At Fate's End My most anticipated game of 2026 was At Fate's End. I say "was" because it was delayed to 2027. Thankfully, a demo for the game was released alongside the announcement of the delay, allowing me to at least experience a morsel of what's to come. Spoiler: It's fantastic. This is the next game from Spiritfarer developer Thunder Lotus, which sees you embody the role of a warrior princess who has to duel her hurting siblings with sharp swords and even sharper words--it's the exact emotional devastation I crave. And both the art and music direction are nothing to sneeze at. This is a pretty game to both look at and listen to. Draftula So you took GameSpot editor Steve Watt's wonderfully written Castlevania: Belmont's Curse review to heart, bought the game, and enjoyed fighting Dracula as yet another member of the Belmont family. But now what? Well, you could go help rebuild Dracula's castle in preparation for the next invasive adventurer. Writer Dave Irwin enjoyed the demo for Draftula, an upcoming roguelite that sees you needing to build Dracula's misshapen castle over and over, higher and higher, in order to waylay any would-be heroes. Exterminauts Okay, technically not a demo, but a beta test that you can join right now is demo-ish. GameSpot editor Mark Delaney compares Exterminauts to Helldivers 2, although this three-person player-versus-environment shooter stars regular schmucks obsessed with do-it-yourself projects, not patriotic starship troopers. The game otherwise plays very similarly to Helldivers 2, so the beta feels more like a means of testing to see whether the narrative flavor is to your liking. If you already like Helldivers 2, you'll probably like the gameplay of this shooter too. My Arms Are Longer Now GameSpot managing editor Chris Pereira and I often muse in our weekly one-on-one meetings that we rarely have time to destress via gaming, since the portal by which we primarily play games is also the machine we sit at all day for work. So any weird game that gets Chris to relax makes me happy and is more than deserving of a shout-out. My Arms Are Longer Now is a bizarre game that's strangely compelling--between the ridiculous humor of controlling an arm that stretches belief and the silly hijinks that you're able to get up to, this will likely remain the funniest demo in this roundup.  Nirvana Noir Genesis Noir is one of the most memorable games of 2021. Its use of abstract art to frame a compelling noir detective story within the explosion of the big bang created an experience that was quite novel. The next game, Nirvana Noir, aims to tell an even weirder story. Writer Khee Hoon Chan likes how the vibrant color of Nirvana Noir makes for an eye-catching sequel, one in which a detective hops between parallel realities (one in which the Big Bang happened, and one in which it didn't) to unravel a conspiracy centered around the final moment of Genesis Noir. The game looks gorgeous, and the demo already indicates that this game's puzzles and mysteries are going to be just as spellbinding as the first game. Paranormal Activity: Threshold Mark puts it perfectly: The scariest game of the year is a demo for a game that isn’t even coming out. Like Silent Hills before it (and Devotion seemingly until it surprisingly did come back), Threshold is a fantastically spooky good time that's just never going to release in full. But at least we still have this great demo to experience a tiny glimpse of what was going to probably be a strong first-person horror game. It's really no big surprise that this demo is good--Threshold was being developed by DarkStone Digital, the same studio behind the extremely popular (and oftentimes way too scary for me) The Mortuary Assistant.  New Releases There aren't just new demos to play right now. Some studios are bravely staring down the Goliath that is Grand Theft Auto 6, the sling that is their much smaller title in hand. Releasing in the weeks leading up to GTA 6 or in the weeks post-GTA 6's launch is a bold choice, but those of us who aren't huge into sandbox games are thankful that they're here. Too much of just one thing for a long time isn't always fun. These games fall into their own niche--you're not going to find any games listed here that feel like GTA 6 but smaller. We imagine most of those games were delayed to 2027 or beyond in order to not be overshadowed by Rockstar's action-adventure. Australia Did It A fear of being spoiled on GTA 6's story is pushing Chris to try and focus on Rockstar's action-adventure game, but a very different game has been slowly clawing its way into his heart. Between its fun unit-combining mechanics and hilarious conveyor belt of doom, Australia Did It has been pulling Chris away from GTA 6. Part turn-based tactics game, part tower-defense game, part survivorslike, Australia Did It seems tailor-made for GameSpot's resident tactics aficionado, but don't let Chris have all the fun. Be sure to check out this game yourself! Deadlock Deadlock is the latest in a now dwindling field of hero shooters, but unlike the swing-and-a-miss mishaps of Concord and Highguard, Deadlock seems to have the juice to stick around. The game's player numbers saw a huge surge in September with the City Never Sleeps update, and it's basically been doing just fine ever since despite technically still being in early development. Fingers crossed that continues to be the case. Deadlock has some of my favorite lore of any shooter ever, which is only amplified by the impressive world design of its maps and phenomenal character art direction.
