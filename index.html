The Fortnite item shop has always been kind of annoying, because it's never had a consistent inventory you could browse like a normal store. Fortnite gift cards no longer exist You used to be able to buy a Fortnite gift card that would redeem directly for V-Bucks, but Epic stopped making those in 2025. While Epic does still have an item that it refers to as a "Fortnite Gift Card - Powered by the Epic Games Store," these are not actually Fortnite products at all. Instead, a current "Fortnite gift card" simply grants you credit in the Epic Games Store, and you can spend it on anything they sell on mobile or PC, including V-Bucks. So in reality these are EGS gift cards, and there is no current way to gift V-Bucks directly. This distinction is important because the physical "Fortnite gift cards" that are sold in stores (in denominations of $15, $30, $50, and $100) don't line up with any V-Buck purchase amounts. So if you receive a $30 card for your birthday, you can buy a 2,400 pack of V-Bucks and have $7 left over, or you can throw in $7 of your own money and get a 4,500 pack. Epic gift cards do have the added benefit of a 20% rebate in EGS credit when you put it toward Fortnite--but that's still an additional layer of stuff you have to think about beyond the original purchase, and all it really does is make you take extra steps to counter the most recent V-Buck price increase.  Oh, and if you play on Switch or Switch 2, you can't even use V-Bucks purchased with an EGS gift card on your device, because your V-Buck balance doesn't transfer to Nintendo's systems. Instead, you'll have to shop in a web browser and then restart your game to receive the items. More extra steps. Prices are a mess since the latest price change The latest price increase, back in the spring, was an example of shrinkflation--the prices stayed the same, but the amounts of V-Bucks you'd receive for your purchase went down. It used to be that $23 got you 2,800 V-Bucks, but now it's 2,400, and the other standard amounts have been adjusted similarly. On top of that, Epic added a new "exact purchase" feature that would allow you to purchase only the specific amount required for the thing you want to buy. That sounds good in theory, but it's a horrendous value given the way Epic implemented it. How it works is that it charges you two different rates within the same transaction. Let's use the new Lil Tecca bundle, which costs 2,600 V-Bucks, as an example. If you use the "exact amount" feature to purchase 2,600 V-Bucks, you'll pay $27--the first 2,400 costs $23 ($.95 per 100 V-Bucks), and the other 200 costs $4 ($2 per 100 V-Bucks). That might be tolerable in small amounts, but the value becomes increasingly terrible the more you're spending. Fortnite caps the price to use the "exact amount" just below that of the next tier of V-Bucks, meaning that if you're buying less than 4,500 V-Bucks with the "exact amount" feature, you will always pay less than the $37 that a 4,500 pack costs. But since using this feature is so exorbitantly expensive, you'll reach that cap by purchasing just 3,100 V-Bucks. And that means that using the "exact amount" feature to purchase any amount of V-Bucks between 3,100 and 4,400 will cost exactly $36.49. So if you were to use this feature to buy the Addison Rae bundle for 3,600 V-Bucks, you'd be shorting yourself 900 V-Bucks to save 50 cents. The price change and the expensive top-up are changes that work in tandem, because so many past shop bundles were oriented around the 2,800 V-Buck pack. Even collabs frequently suffer a similar fate--just ask any of the Attack on Titan or Transformers or God of War fans who've been waiting years to see those skins return. Similarly, they seem to only sell Star Wars cosmetics in May now.  The takeaway here is that it's not easy to shop for the things you want in Fortnite, because only a tiny portion of the game's cosmetics are available at a time. For Epic, that's always been the point--the FOMO system encourages you to watch the shop change every day and leap into action when something you want comes back. But if there's too much friction in that process, or if buying stuff routinely requires too much math and other mental effort, it can sour folks on the shop entirely. Released in 1997 for personal computers and developed by Microprose, the eponymously named Magic: The Gathering sought to capture the excitement of the tabletop game to players all across the digital realm. Magic: The Gathering’s first video game was a single-player RPG. Arzakon provides the greatest challenge in the game as he has the highest life total, the most powerful spells in the game, and access to all five colors of Magic. Though the Planeswalker is unable to fully defeat Arzakon, they are successful in sealing him away for a set number of years, depending on how much damage they are able to land in the final battle. Musashi won’t be utilizing his famous dual-wielding style as he’s still early in his journey – in his 20s – and hasn’t yet discovered/created it at this point of his life in Way of the Sword. “Where Musashi is set in this game is before he’s really mastered that, so there’s more focus on that one-sword action, but we did want to include elements of his familiarity with other weapons, which is why we have the Oni Armament skills that come into play.” During my time playing Way of the Sword, I used two different Oni Armaments. The Two Celestials are small blades that deal straight damage, while the Earth-Shaker hammers I later unlock in my playtime inflict heavy stagger damage. Equipping different Oni Armaments based on the enemies before you is a crucial part of Musashi’s combat kit. During a fight with the Nue, which consisted of two Chimera, mythical beasts inspired by various animals, using the Earth-Shaker hammers was key to my (eventual) victory against them. Yugen says stories about Musashi tell of how he used whatever means necessary to achieve victory, which is why his kit includes more than just a sword. You can parry enemies into torches, setting them on fire as a result, or flip tables to use as large shields – after you’re done using it as a shield, you can throw it into enemies, too, knocking them off balance. “These are all elements that were kind of inspired by the real stories of Musashi,” Yugen says. Some stories of Musashi, who is famous for an undefeated record of 62 sword duels and traveling around Japan specifically to reach that number, tell of an attitude somewhere between confident and cocky. That’s apparent in Way of the Sword’s Musashi, who is also searching for the next duel – in fact, he wants to get rid of the Oni Gauntlet on his arm because he feels like using it is cheating in these duels, a far cry from the likes of Samanosuke or Jubei, who want to use their Oni powers to save the world in Onimusha: Warlords and Onimusha 2: Samurai’s Destiny, respectively.  Yugen says he and the team stressed this confidence/cockiness to the motion capture artists Capcom hired to perform the actions of Musashi and his enemies. But, given how early in Musashi’s journey Way of the Sword takes place, they also took great care to make his action “a little rough around the edges” and “not quite as defined,” according to Yugen. One thing that helped was to imagine each fight in the various scenarios Musashi finds himself in as a Street Fighter bout. Leaning on one of Capcom’s other franchises, Yugen says the Street Fighter angle helped them define what Musashi would be thinking about as each fight begins, how he would walk up to enemies and approach them with sword in hand, and more. “It was towards the end of the project that we really started introducing those elements to flesh out the uniqueness of him, even down to the way he walks,” Yugen adds. “We didn’t want him to walk with this tension; he has a bit of swagger to him, and that’s something we really paid attention to in design.” Continuing, Yugen says, “Musashi doesn’t really hide his feelings. He’s very blunt, he’s very direct, and when something is a pain in the ass, he’ll say, ‘God, this is a pain in the ass.’”Some of that is apparent in the Way of the Sword demo that you can play right now, and a lot of that was apparent in my extended hands-on time with the game.
