Are you ready to unlock the secret ending in Phantom Blade Zero? Every story has its conclusion, including your journey across the Wulin as Soul. That said, there are several requirements that you need to be mindful of, including important quests and points of no return. Our guide discusses every single detail but, because of the topic at hand, you can bet that there will be major spoilers below. It’s pretty much an overarching narrative where you discover the Secrets at the Heart of the Lake, the Herald’s Illusion, and Inanis’s role. These tasks must be completed before you reach the game’s point of no return: Mirage Estate. Here’s a quick summary: After completing the main mission in Pangzhen, you learn that you have to go to the Mirage Estate. However, there’s no way to enter via the steps/thoroughfare. The quest then changes to The Four Serpent Houses. This is when you discern that there’s a hidden passage behind the snake door in the Green Serpent House. After collecting the items needed to open the doorway, you can continue onward to the Mirage Estate. However, if there are still important quests that you need to tackle, you’ll be warned that you have “unfinished business.” You can still fast travel to other regions even if you prematurely enter the Mirage Estate. It’s just that the requirements for the true ending might fail, and you don’t want that to happen. You don't want to enter the Mirage Estate if you still have unfinished story quests in your journal. Early-game quests and overarching plots There are early-game quests that you should tackle to unlock the true ending. These include Deep in the Valley, Crimson Manor, Bloodline Awakening/Long Pillars, and Quick Hand Zhang Treasures. (You can check out these guides we’ve linked for more detailed tips.) Deep in the Valley grants you entry to the lower section of the first region, where you’ll find the Crimson Manor and Long Pillar. Quick Hand Zhang, meanwhile, has you collecting relics in various zones where he has a hideout. From left to right: Deep in the Valley, Bloodline Awakening, and the Crimson Manor. Secrets at the Heart of the Lake: The Village of Lu The Phantom Blade Zero Secrets at the Heart of the Lake is an overarching quest that’s initially started in the Village of Lu. First, you’ll want to collect the six Firefly’s Memory Flowers scattered all over the village. These allow you to enter an optional area near the Temple of the Blue Fireflies, the settlement with a bunch of friendly puppets. Jump down the pit and defeat a boss named Dusk, the Puppeteer to receive rewards and trigger a cutscene. If you defeat an acolyte/scarecrow in the farm fields to the north, you’ll also learn that the objective is part of the quest. These Memory Flowers give you hints regarding a major quest... that's totally optional. Exploration and collectibles: The Illusory Pipa, the Acolyte Masks, and the Herald’s Illusion Certain quests, such as Illusion of Inanis and Sha-Chi-Powered Boat are completed as you continue to explore other regions. For instance, Sha-Chi-Powered Boat leads you to Meigu Lake. As for Whence the Weeping and Pillars of Kunlun, you receive these quests a few hours into the campaign, and you complete them upon clearing the Mountain of Imprisonment. Other quests also play a major factor: A good example is the Illusory Pipa, which lets you dispel impenetrable barriers that open up new areas for exploration. There’s also The Herald’s Illusion, where you seek out the Three-Eyed Puppet’s domains. Lastly, you should acquire all Acolyte Masks, as these items grant entry to a secret area. Note: Completing these tasks nets you two Crystal Puppet Eye items (Yin and Yang). From left to right: The Illusory Pipa, Herald's Illusion, and Acolyte Masks. Secrets at the Heart of the Lake: The Meigu Lake Discordance and Inanis' Realm The aforementioned Acolyte Masks are used to shatter the Four Guardians’ Mirror, a mystical location in the frozen lands in northwestern Meigu Lake. Explore the Discordance realm at your leisure and collect more loot. At the very end, you have to defeat the Blizzard Blademaster. After the Blademaster’s fall, an ominous-looking statue will appear right in front of you. Interact with it and place the two Crystal Puppet Eyes to get transported to the Heart of the Lake. Kill all the cultists to stop their ritual, which then leads you to another encounter with Inanis. This completes the Secrets of the Heart of the Lake. The two Crystal Puppet Eyes teleport you to the ritual site in the Heart of the Lake. What happens when you enter the Mirage Estate and Soulrend Cliff To summarize: If you want to get the secret ending in Phantom Blade Zero, you have to complete all story quests that aren’t tied to the main mission--i.e. those with a purple icon. Once you’ve done all of those, it’s time to enter the Mirage Estate. Doing so causes changes to certain locations, such as the Crimson Forest’s eastern part getting blanketed in snow. Proceed deeper into the Mirage Estate, fighting enemies, taking out bosses, and picking up all sorts of loot. Along the way, don’t forget to complete the following: Bloodline Awakening - The fifth and final Long Pillar is in the estate grounds. Quick Hand’s Request - Quick Hand Zhang has another hideout in the sprawling complex. Eventually, you’ll acquire the Tiger Cannon, a Phantom Edge that can blow up destructible walls. Use it to reach Soulrend Cliff, the last region in the game. There are still lots of enemies, bosses, and rewards in this area, so just keep forging ahead. After defeating what’s supposed to be the final boss, it will be followed by yet another encounter. We don’t want to spoil what happens next, but it should be pretty clear that this is your last foe. Once you’ve vanquished your opponent, sit back, relax, and watch the cutscenes. Discern Inanis' true intentions and proceed further in to the Mirage Estate and Soulrend Cliff. That does it for our guide on how to get the true ending in Phantom Blade Zero. Keep in mind that you can still fast travel to other locations while you’re in the Mirage Estate and also while in Soulrend Cliff (just prior to the last bell). You should be able to find other goodies in the game world, such as the Soul Temper materials for your weapons. Also, don’t forget to battle the Three-Faced Puppet as part of the Triple Threat quest. And, if you’re up for even more challenges, you can try 66 Days Mode after beating the game. Join Soul as he faces numerous threats in the Wulin and be sure to check out more tips in our Phantom Blade Zero guides hub.

Virtue And A Sledgehammer Marcus Stewart This Devolver Digital-published game has a simple but head-turning premise. You’re a profoundly angry woman who returns to her small hometown armed with nothing but a sledgehammer, which she uses to smash everything in sight. But why is she doing this? Why is she so mad? A big part of Virtue and a Sledgehammer’s allure is its fascinating and well-written tale about the protagonist’s strained relationships with her sister and mother. Titanium Court On Switch 2 Marcus Stewart This bizarre indie blends fun match-3 puzzle-solving with tower defense gameplay wrapped around a funny but surreal story. Depending on how the board looks when the puzzle portion ends, you send troops (sustained by the resources you collected) to attack/defend against rival castles and gather more items; board positioning determines how well this can go. The two mechanics feed into each other to create a fun loop that grows in complexity as new mechanics are introduced. Did I mention the game is also very weird? The silly tale centers on a medieval court designating you the chosen savior to rescue their lost queen (and many other tasks), and the dumb tone adds personality to a sometimes dry genre. Titanium Court charmed and impressed us when it launched on PC earlier this year, and now console and mobile players get their chance to hold court. Humans are drawn to horror in all forms, real life and fiction. Whatever the reason, we flock to movies where supernatural entities and puppets on tricycles upend otherwise lovely lives, TV shows where murderous cults kill and stump local detectives, and books where the words on the page seem to follow you to bed. Games, however, go a step further, letting you enter the horror and control it (or become its victim), and you could argue that, as a result, they’re the most immersive form of fiction. We at Game Informer love a healthy dose of horror from time to time, and we often review games like this. Some terrify us. Others… don’t. And the best ones stick with us for years and years after the credits. Below is a list of 10 of the best horror games we’ve played recently. This isn’t an all-time list, so don’t fret if your personal favorite isn’t here. The Village in the Shade takes things back to the homestead, giving you (a child who emerged from a hole in the ground and was immediately given the responsibility of running an entire farm by yourself) the freedom to plant, sow, reap, ranch, and more. Just follow the important rules: don't go out at night, don't share secrets, and don't leave the village among others. You wouldn't want to break the rules, right? You could, theoretically, go about your idyllic rural living. Just don't forget to water your turnips before you go sneaking out again.
