Having made your way into Dracula's Castle, Castlevania: Belmont's Curse throws a rather huge curveball almost immediately: a long ascent up the Clock Tower. Now, veterans of the metroidvania series will know making it to the top is no easy feat, with these areas typically housing some fiendish spike traps, flying enemies, and large drops. The Clock Tower in Belmont's Curse is the most devilish yet, with spinning blades ready to slice you up if you so much as brush past them. On top of that, you've been challenged by the Zealot, a previous boss who says he'll only fight you at the summit. Naturally, you want all of Dracula's minions dead, so here's how to reach the Clock Tower's peak. Occasionally, you'll need to use enemies as stepping stones to reach the top. How to reach the top of the Clock Tower From the get-go, there are perils to heading up the Clock Tower. The first room has giant iron fists that pound the ground whenever you get close. They won't kill you, but they do hurt a lot. The first vertical room has some Medusa Heads you can latch onto to get some height, but be careful of the spinning blades in the middle of the giant wheels. You'll also need to duck under the spikes at the top to avoid being sent back to the bottom. The next room introduces two new things: the iron fists can push switches to rotate platforms (more on that later), and there are many Nova Skeletons blasting full-screen lasers out of their bellies in the latest Castlevania game. The next vertical shaft requires you to leap across the rotating platforms at the top while avoiding the laser beams, but is otherwise unremarkable. When you reach the top of the next room, there is a faint glow leading to the left. Follow it to find a save room and a shortcut back to the bottom. From this point, you can go east until you reach the Library, but you also have plenty of Clock Tower to climb. Notable obstacles include the wheels of spinning saws in the Eastern Steps that slowly roll down slopes. You can duck under the blades at specific points. You'll also find giant cogs that you can attack to reveal optional pathways in Daedalus's Crossing. This stone golem mini-boss blocks the way to the top of the Clock Tower. As you cross to the other side after the area with the cog, you'll face the mini-boss of the area: a stone golem. This thing hits like a truck, with the ability to cause earthquakes and throw rocks. It's slow-moving, so use the grapple shots to leap over the boss rather than slide underneath. When it eventually dies, watch out for the rocks that scatter, as they can deal a rather annoying deathblow if you're not careful. The last section has a bunch of platforms that have iron fists dangling above them, which you can use to swing across to grab the Claymore, one of the many hidden weapons. You can then climb back up the platforms as intended to reach the Clock Tower Bridge, marking the top of the tower. Just head left from now on. Don't worry about that clock face and the swirly stuff around it. Surely nothing can go wrong! There's a way to make this iron fist crash through the breakable floor below by flipping switches. How to find the real Clock Tower boss So... as it turns out, Death had a nasty trick up his sleeve, but the Ferryman has guided you back to the nearest save point. What can you do? Well, as it turns out, there's a little more to the Clock Tower than meets the eye, and you'll need to start heading downward. On the way to the bridge leading to the Library, there's a large room with iron fist switches that flip three sets of platforms. Head to the top and flip the third set first so that they are vertically aligned. You'll need to make sure you're on the right side for this to work. Keep going down, making sure that the platforms are vertical as you head down, and eventually you'll trigger the mechanism at the bottom to send the iron fist crashing down. Head into this newly opened area to continue, breaking the lock behind the shortcut just in case you die on the way. Keep going down, avoiding the iron fists and defeating the enemies until you reach the Smelting Room. This has a lot of grapple points to latch onto as you try to leap between the crushing fists. Just keep your cool, and you'll eventually get the timing down. About halfway through the next room, crack open the breakable wall to eat some much-needed chicken, and after the next trap room (which is relatively easy), head past the Axe Knight through the door on the left to open a path to the rest of the Clock Tower from the bottom. However, your journey to the bottom is not done. Head back into the previous room and deal with the enemies below. After several gauntlets, you'll reach a room labelled "Final Trap". Head immediately through the door to the right and continue up the elevator to reveal a much-needed save room. You can then go back to the Final Trap and make your way past the last set of crushing fists before you reach the boss fight. Defeat this titan to get the ability to crash through glowing floors and other objects.

Getting into Notre Dame in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse has its challenges. However, the Cathedral itself is home to a giant organ that the Shadow Arcana boss has used to seal himself away from you. Of course, this is Death, and he doesn't make it easy, but finding the ghosts in Notre Dame will give you the three melodies you need to play to unlock the door and fight him. But what if we told you that, despite beating Death, there is actually a fourth melody you can play? The Shadow Arcana spells hint at another tune, but this time there's no ghost to tell you which keys to press. You'll need to venture into one specific room that's thankfully close to a save room, so we'll explain where to go and how to figure out which keys to press to finish this metroidvania's most fiendish puzzle yet. Paying attention to background details will reveal a bunch of hidden information. How to find and decipher the hidden melody The first step to completing this puzzle is to visit the Stained Glass Gallery in Notre Dame. From the Western Triforium save point, head right, jumping down the hole at the top, past the Ferryman room, and into the Sealed Scriptorium. From there, go right and crash through the Stained Glass Gallery's ceiling. Notice the symbols at the top of the windows? These are in the same order as the keys on the Giant Organ, while the candles below them are the sequence for playing the hidden melody. In total, the tune consists of ten key presses, and you'll definitely need the double jump and dash power-ups to play them. Now that you know the tune, you can play it to unlock a bunch of stuff at the same time! How to play the Notre Dame hidden melody Go to the Giant Organ and make your way to the Crescent Moon key. From here, you'll need to play the following keys: Moon Libra (Right one key) Scorpio (Right one key) Aquarius (Right three keys) Capricorn (Left one key) Virgo (Left six keys) Sun (Right one key) Leo (Left two keys) Cancer (Left one key) Aries (Left three keys) After playing this tune, you'll unlock two things. The first is a point in Death's Arcana, so go ahead and spend that as soon as possible. After you're done, head to the left, and by the statue, a new path has opened underneath. Go down and follow it to obtain the War and Famine legendary dual-swords weapon from the chest at the end.

However, one early example you may have seen is a giant painting of Notre Dame Cathedral, just hanging there in a giant hallway. Those who have played Castlevania: Portrait of Ruin might have an inkling of what this means, but you'll need to gather a few key items together before you can explore this hidden area. When you eventually find all the components, return to this painting to bring it to life. Where is Notre Dame in Castlevania: Belmont's Curse? Surprisingly, finding Notre Dame is remarkably simple. In the Main Hall, as you progress toward the Clock Tower area, you'll come across a large empty hallway with a giant painting of the famous French cathedral. You won't be able to do anything with this artwork right now, but it's essential that you do visit at some point if you want to finish the game. With the power gained from Talos, you can destroy this grotesque marble statue. Requirements for unlocking Notre Dame Your first objective is to find and defeat Talos. He is an extremely well-hidden boss that requires a bit of puzzle-solving to reach his lair, and the fight is relatively tricky too. You can find more detail on how to get to the Talos boss fight toward the end of our How to Ascend the Clock Tower guide, as this is the true main boss of the area. Once you have beaten Talos, you'll obtain his technique that breaks specific barriers and objects with a yellow glow. This is needed for the next step: unlocking the Artist Arcana. In the Main Hall area, you'll find several Alucard Paintings that hint at secret passages, and one of them just so happens to reveal the hidden entrance to Brauner's Atelier. Smash his latest 'masterpiece' to smithereens, and he'll appear to complain. There is no boss fight this time, which for fans of this metroidvania series is a bit of a shame, but you'll get the Arcana with little fuss. Finally, you need the Painter's Brush, which has been pinched by another boss of the castle: Carmilla. To reach her, you'll need to explore the library and find the clues that unlock the astral glyph mechanisms. Once you have all three combinations, return to the central chamber near the save point and activate the glyphs leading to the rafters. At the top of this short tower, you'll find the Blood Witch Carmilla, whose boss fight has a rather nasty gimmick you need to overcome to beat her. Make sure you collect this paintbrush after defeating Carmilla in the Library. How to unlock Notre Dame Once you have all three key components needed to open the way, return to the massive painting of the building in the Main Hall area. Equip the Artist Arcana and press the on-screen prompt that appears when you approach the middle. You'll get a small cutscene before being transported to the entrance. Sadly, the cryptic puzzles don't end there; many more lie between you and the Notre Dame resident boss, and even beyond the castle walls.
