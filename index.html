In this guide, we'll show you how to collect Irisalis Petals, craft the legendary Aniipod, and catch Irisalis for your Aniimo collection. Who is Irisalis? Irisalis legendary Aniimo Irisalis is the legendary evolution of Iris, and this Aniimo can only be caught during the seasonal Walking with Irisalis event. This is a Nova stage Aniimo with the DPS role and Grass Element type. Irisalis has two skills to use. Irisalis Shadow creates clones of Iris, while Whirling Blossom Rain makes the clones fire floral light beams. Only legendary Aniimo can unlock the transmogrify ability. This allows you to use Radiant Dreamshadow to change the visual appearance of Irisalis. Walking with Irisalis event details Walking with Irisalis event The Walking with Irisalis event is live from now until December 10, giving you over two months to complete the limited-time objectives. This requires collecting Irisalis Petals, crafting a special legendary Aniipod, and defeating Irisalis in battle before you can catch it. How to complete the Walking with Irisalis event The material needed for the Walking with Irisalis event has actually been in the game since launch, so you might already have a good start on this. You'll need a total of 99 Irisalis Petals to craft the legendary Aniipod. If you're just getting started with the game, you'll have a grind ahead of you, but we'll give you some tips on how to get Irisalis Petals easier. How to collect Irisalis Petals There are nine permanent ways you can earn Irisalis Petals: Completing main story objectives (12) Completing Journey Quests (10) Complete Elite Pathfinder challenges (7) Earning Silver Badges (14) Completing Tracing the Trail event quests (5) Alpha First Clears (17) Branch Nurture (18) Completing Aniimo Topics (16) You can get all 99 Irisalis Petals just by completing all the objectives above, but there are additional ways to earn the petals faster. A new weekly rotation of options will be available during the event, allowing you to score several Irisalis Petals quicker. Breezy Mission Letters gift boxes In week one, you can purchase Breezy Mission Letters gift boxes for 428 Glimmer each. The shop currently has a limit of four purchases of this item, but each one will give you an Irisalis Flower. Go to your inventory and select "Use" on the Irisalis Flower. This will give you five petals per flower, giving you a maximum of 25 Irisalis Petals to add to your total count, assuming you buy all five Breezy Mission Letters gift boxes. Irisalis Petals in the Stamp Shop The stamp shop also has Irisalis Petals at a cost of 50 Windborn Stamps each, and you can purchase up to four petals this way. Visit the Walking with Irisalis event tab each week to see what new options are available in the event's weekly rotation. How to craft the legendary Aniipod Crafting a legendary Aniipod for Irisalis Once you have 99 Irisalis Petals, head back to your menu's event tab and select the Walking with Irisalis event. You'll now be able to select the option to craft the legendary Aniipod. You can only craft one of these special event Aniipods, meaning you can only catch Irisalis once. Crafting the legendary Aniipod will unlock the ability to teleport to a rift, and this will take you to Irisalis. You'll now need to defeat Irisalis in battle. Defeating Irisalis Irisalis is a grass-type Aniimo, so you'll want to pick your best team with Dark, Fire, and Wind elements. Also remember this makes her resistant to Earth, Grass, Water. It's worth having a healer in your party as well. Glacy is a water-type Aniimo, but they are a powerful healer to pair with the elementals you'll need. Defeating Irisalis will give you a prompt to catch them with the legendary Aniipod you crafted, and you'll now have the game's very first legendary Aniimo. Diablo 4 players are decking themselves out in full sets of Mythic Unique gear as part of Season 15, resulting in characters that are more powerful than ever before. Whether that was Blizzard's goal, or this is simply a mistake of some kind, is currently up for debate. It's about as big of a change from one season to the next that Diablo 4 has ever experienced. Back in Season 14, Blizzard added the "crafted" item tag to Mythic Uniques made via the Horadric Cube, Blacksmith, or Jeweler, with a limit of only one crafted Mythic being equippable at a time. Even if you had the Resplendent Sparks resource needed to make more Mythics, you couldn't use them. Instead, you had to rely on good old-fashioned luck to find natural Mythic drops without the crafted tag. The changes weren't popular, so Blizzard walked it back mid-season, and has made further improvements as part of the latest season, Hell's Legacy. Mythics made by the Blacksmith or Jeweler are no longer considered "crafted," while ones made via the cube still are. Once again, players could have several Mythics of your own creation equipped at once, much like the previous seasons. However, for players with more sparks than they know what to do with, there is technically a way to get around the "crafted" tag. It takes combining three of the same Mythic Unique (not exactly an easy task) in the cube using the Recycle Uniques recipe, resulting in a new version of that same item. While the item will have all new stat rolls, the key here is that this recycled Mythic Unique won't have the "crafted" tag, letting players equip as many of them as they can create. The result is players running around with full sets of Mythic gear, something we've never seen before in Diablo 4. This would be a huge problem and could completely break the game in many ways, if it weren't harder to do than it sounds. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=u9IHlOYCrbg Considering it costs 15 Resplendent Sparks to just create one Mythic Unique this way, it's definitely a huge time investment. To do this trick with all 12 gear slots would cost an enormous 180 Sparks, something that seems nearly impossible for an average player and would require an ungodly amount of grinding and farming. That hasn't stopped some of the game's most dedicated content creators from doing exactly that, however, as highlighted by Wudijo.  Whether the ability to create non-crafted Mythic Uniques this way is an intentional choice on Blizzard's part or an oversight remains to be seen. Diablo 4's next update is slated for September 29, but the 3.2.2 patch notes make no mention of this trick and instead are mostly bug fixes. Overall, Season 15 seems to have been well-received by players after the largely unpopular Season 14. The season finally added Diablo to Diablo 4, as players relive past Prime Evil encounters from previous Diablo games and rub shoulders with none other than the legendary Deckard Cain.  Diablo 4 won't receive new expansions going forward, but it is set to receive a new class, the Amazon, in the first half of next year. Meanwhile, Blizzard is hard at work on Diablo 5, set to release in 2029, and has already revealed three of its playable classes.

Onimusha: Way Of The Sword Made Me Realize I’ve Been Playing Action Games WrongNEW

There are a number of fights in Onimusha: Way of the Sword that get on my nerves. Greater Nue. Byakue for the, like, umpteenth time. Sasuke Uchiha--I mean, Sasaki Ganryu. The stupid last boss, who I thought would literally never die. I felled them, one after the other, most times on the first or second try, but it forced me to rethink how I played the game because the greatest enemy of all was actually the fickle camera. On multiple occasions, the camera would get stuck in a wall or phase through an enemy’s torso. Seeing where I was and who I was fighting became a battle itself as I fought on the actual battlefield--until I did one simple thing: I stopped locking on to what was in front of me. Suddenly, I felt like a true samurai warrior, my eyes observing the full combat zone as I more accurately predicted and reacted to my enemy’s movements. With this new freedom, I don’t know if I’ll ever go back to locking on to everything again. An Old Tactic In A New Game "What's that, Shizuka? You want me to stop locking on?" For the early stages of my time with Capcom’s macabre samurai adventure, I clicked the right stick in to lock onto the enemy who stood in front of my sword. It didn’t matter the number of enemies that were there; that lock-on was literally always toggled, and I could very easily switch my target from one to the other if necessary. It wasn’t until about seven hours into the game that I began to reconsider this tactic. During the game’s 11th story mission, What Lurks in the Shadows, you encounter two Raikou-looking monsters named Nue (Blue) and Nue (Red). You battle one of these quadrupedal beasts earlier in the mission, so you should be familiar with their moveset, but fighting two of them, with their lightning-fast movement, sucks. I died. A lot. It’s frustrating because neither the fight (nor the game itself) is all that challenging, but the camera kept getting in the way, making it difficult to keep one Nue in focus while the other charged me from offscreen. This is where I tried something different. I toggled my lock-on off, and before I knew it, those Nues were no more. Clicking The Lock-On Off Just Clicks This isn’t a new tactic in action-adventure and action-RPGs. Players have been fighting without lock-on for decades, and Souls players in particular have long discussed the advantages of doing so. A netizen in the Dark Souls subreddit asked two years ago why people hate locking on in the game, and user Gabosh responded that unlocking “simply [allows] you to manually control the camera or aim spells” and is “useful against giant enemies or 1v2 boss fights.” Someone in the Dark Souls 3 GameFAQs forum asked a similar question nine years ago, explaining that Souls games become “SO much easier if you don't lock on and just learn to free aim.” And even in Elden Ring’s Steam discussions page, someone decried how sick they’ve grown of the lock-on system two years ago, with user Sockwallet saying that you should “NEVER use lock-on for melee in Souls games, [because] it gets you killed.” Evidently, I never got the memo that locking on was bad. But many of these discussions highlighted two things I learned in my own testing: control and freedom. I thought that, as I fought through Onimusha’s hell-transformed world, locking on was the way to go. It squared me up with the enemy in front of me, ensuring that I was always looking ahead at what was coming. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=qbmpcYkUh_U Sometimes, though, what’s coming isn’t in front of you, and when you’re locked on, you’d never see that. I learned that the hard way during my fight with those two annoying Jolteon knock-offs. By unlocking, I had much more freedom over the camera, giving me greater awareness of my surroundings. On top of the freedom, I gained a greater sense of control over the flow of battle and protagonist Miyamoto Musashi’s movements. While locking on let me react to the enemy, employing free aim let me react to the fight as a whole. Instead of the camera dictating who I fought and where I was positioned, I was able to scan the arena, reposition Musashi based on enemy tactics, and make informed combat decisions in response to what was happening around me. I stopped circle-strafing and stood my ground more. I stopped dodging haphazardly and parried more effectively. I stopped letting the camera determine my next target and, instead, thinned the pack based on enemy priority. In essence, I became a combat strategist, analyzing the battlefield and enemy movements to attack and defend at the perfect moments. A Tactic For One Is A Tactic For All I really hated this boss fight... Ever since my encounter with the Nue Twins, I’ve opted for free aim in almost all combat scenarios. Whether I’m fighting a horde of monsters, two bosses at once, or one big bad, my lock-on is typically off now. I’m enjoying the control and freedom too much to relinquish it to the automation of Onimusha’s lock-on system. I’ve gotten so accustomed to it that, after beating the game in roughly 40 hours, I went back to other action games like Ghost of Yotei and Mortal Shell 2 to see just how effective free aim is. And yep, free aim works! I don’t know why I never considered not locking on before this, but I’m glad to have experimented with it now. Locking on certainly has its place--don’t get me wrong. The final boss in Onimusha: Way of the Sword is a real piece of s**t and a great example of this. It’s a 1-on-1 duel, locking onto your opponent makes sense here. But even then, toggling the lock-on off during certain segments of the fight--particularly the second phase--provides a better sense of control and freedom, and those two traits are important in action games. I’ll continue to lock on when necessary, but from now on, I’m entering fights with no target in mind, allowing myself to flow like water from one enemy to the next, regardless of what game I’m playing.

Few video game openings make the impression City Escape does in Sonic Adventure 2. After jumping out of a helicopter, Sonic uses a piece of debris to snowboard down the city streets as the generational earworm, "Escape from the City" blasts. It was a defining moment for gamers, whether they were playing it on the original Dreamcast in 2001 or GameCube in 2002. It's also one of the best-designed levels in 3D Sonic history. Ahead of its appearance in Sega and iam8bit's 35 Years of Speed: The Sonic Art Exhibit this weekend in Los Angeles, we got ahold of the design document Sonic Team used to lay out just how this iconic level would eventually play out while Sonic Adventure 2 was still in development. Originally codenamed Project GAIA 2, Sonic Adventure 2 had the unenviable task of following up the acclaimed Dreamcast launch title, Sonic Adventure. Not only was that a daunting task on its own, but Sega found itself in dire financial straits as the PlayStation 2 was decimating the Dreamcast in sales. Sega, on the brink of exiting the hardware business, dramatically downsized the development team behind Sonic Adventure 2 and relocated them to San Francisco, California, which heavily inspired the hilly urban design of the game's opening level. "Our team of 120 got pared down to 11, sent to America, and told to go make Sonic Adventure 2," Sonic Team creative officer and Sonic Adventure 2 director Takashi Iizuka told me in 2016. "Part of what we had to resolve was 'How are we going to make the same game with 11 people when we have less than a tenth of the staff on-board?' I was really tasked with trying to make the impossible possible."Regardless of the challenges, Sonic Team, under Iizuka's leadership, was able to clear the hurdles and deliver one of the Dreamcast's most beloved games in just a year and a half of development time. According to several long-tenured members of Sonic Team, including Iizuka, art director Hiroshi Nishiyama, and sound director Jun Senoue, Sonic Adventure 2 was their favorite game they've worked on."It was a small development team so we were able to condense all the good elements from the previous game, and deliver a story and game that was satisfying to players everywhere," Iizuka told me in 2022. Nishiyama followed up with, "I had a lot of new ideas for the game because of all the new stimulus and experiences I got by living in America and was able to make a game with a lot of great moments together with the other members." While Sonic Adventure 2 is often remembered primarily as the game that introduced the world to Shadow the Hedgehog and songs like "Live and Learn," City Escape is up there as one of the most iconic elements of the popular title. The design document showcases various elements of the Hero Side's opening mission, including the aforementioned snowboard sequence, plus the chase sequence involving the massive GUN truck. You can check out the City Escape design document from Sonic Adventure 2 below, and if you need a little accompanying music as you flip through the pages, we recommend hitting play on the "Escape from the City" YouTube video above. If you're in the Los Angeles area the weekend of September 25, you'll be able to see this in person, alongside other historical pieces of art from the Sonic franchise through the 35 Years of Speed: The Sonic Art Exhibit showcase at ARK/8. Admission is free, but attendees should register through Eventbrite.
