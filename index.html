Wherever you click on Game Informer today, you're sure to find some insightful Gamescom writing, and we can't wait to bring all the German gaming home to you. Without further ado...The Most Dope Games We've Played At Gamescom 2026Below, we'll list the games we've played in order of when we've actually seen them. For easier navigation, use the Table of Contents on the side. About Fishing About FishingBy Eric Van AllenDeveloper The Water Museum has been making a mark with their particular brand of games. 2024's Arctic Eggs was took the physics of frying an egg in a pan to its utmost limits, and About Fishing seems to be doing the same for the leisurely past-time of fishing.A mixture of lo-fi PS2-era graphics and well-honed fishing controls made this a joy to demo. The craftiness, tension, and struggle between an angler and their nautical prey might have been enough to impress me; it was easy to get lost in finding the perfect way to drop my hook right in the path of a fish, and the system even encouraged creativity, like hooking a line around a rock in a current to hold it in place for a big catch.The narrative teases, though, baited the hook for me. About Fishing has both the fascinating gameplay and standout story beats to make me very, very curious about where it will go. It's jumped quite high on my watch-list, and I recommend checking out its Steam demo if you're at all interested in the wave of throwback-styled indie gems. Agni: Village of Calamity Agni: Village of CalamityBy Wesley LeBlancAgni: Village of Calamity is a survival horror game set in Indonesia, developed by Indonesian studio Separuh Interactive, and based on Indonesian horror films. Agni is looking for a friend who's gone missing in a remote village, and she runs into strange creatures, Remedy Entertainment-inspired phenomena, and a stalking middle-aged man who wants to chop her body to pieces. It's not doing anything too different from your typical survival horror game, but the Indonesian setting and inspiration provide a new lens to look through – I'm excited by that and look forward to playing some more. Control Resonant Control ResonantBy Wesley LeBlancLet me say right off the bat: I am stoked for Control Resonant as a Remedy-verse fan, but I still have not played the game. Unfortunately, that hasn't changed at Gamescom 2026, because my preview of a new section of Control Resonant was hands-off. This didn't inspire confidence in me, especially as Control Resonant marks a big departure for the studio with its melee action combat and RPG elements. However, what I watched the Remedy Entertainment employee play was another exciting look at the game. My colleague and senior associate editor Marcus Stewart did go hands-on with it at SGF and came away with positive thoughts about actually playing the game, so I do still have hope, but alas. Okay, that's enough about that – here's what I actually saw. Dylan Faden, the brother of Control protagonist Jesse, is out of the FBC's containment alongside all of the Hiss the Oldest House tried to keep at bay. There have been plenty of looks at the Hiss' role in Control Resonant, but for this hands-off preview, Remedy showed off a Manhattan park area covered in a blue mold, complete with various mold enemies, too. Dylan is tasked with finding a Resonant here – the big bosses of the game.  Curiously, Remedy says you will fight the same first and last boss no matter how you play the game, but that all the Resonants you fight in between can be tackled in any order you choose. In this preview, we're after a moldy Resonant boss, but first, we have to eat some mold to learn more about it. If you didn't already know, the mold in Control Resonant is "delicious," according to Remedy, and hard to resist. As such, Dylan passes all kinds of bodies who look dead but are actually paralyzed in a euphoric state (because they've willingly eaten the mold). En route to the mold Dylan needs to eat, we pass an FBC Field Content Research facility before shifting gravity so we can climb up a vertical wall.  Up ahead, there's a group of mold enemies. The standard mob enemies are easy to take out, but they're great for farming Dominance, an effect activated by Dylan's combat kit that increases damage after killing an enemy. After garnering some Dominance, we tackle the massive garlic bulb-looking enemy skulking about, placing mold mines in the area. This enemy is about the size of an elephant and shoots mold mines and other projectiles out of its "trunk," for lack of a better term. Here, Remedy explains that the equipped Dual Blades have a critical chance buff, and that the drill transformation for his transforming Aberrant weapon does some serious Falter damage. Every enemy has a yellow Falter bar below their red health bar – Falter is essentially a Stagger meter. When you fill it up, you can perform an execution by pressing L3 (on PlayStation 5).  After taking this enemy out and clearing out the others, we make our way toward the mold Dylan needs to eat. On the way, we see a traffic barrier behaving weirdly, rotating in a glitchy fashion; Remedy says this indicates a nearby world event, but we don't have time to complete it.At this point, I ask Remedy if there are real-world locations from Manhattan in Control Resonant. They say no, and that instead of actual named locations, the places within Control Resonant are meant to feel inspired by the real Manhattan, but with a "twisted" Remedy spin on it. We continue forward, fight some additional enemies, and I see Dylan's shoulder button abilities on display; R2 deals fire damage by way of Ignite, while L2 uses the Barrage ability, which pummels enemies with rocks to greatly increase Falter damage. R1 is Push, and it's basically a Jedi force push. I also learn here that there is no parry and no block – there's just a dodge (and you can perfect dodge to slow down time and deal extra damage). Remedy wants Control Resonant players to be aggressive; that's why you can only heal by defeating enemies, as they drop blue orbs that add health to your HP bar. A few minutes later and we finally reach the mold for Dylan to eat. He eats it, and we get some strange dialogue choices to select. Remedy says dialogue choices won't change the game's ending – there's only one ending – but that some side quests can have different endings depending on your choices. Plus, your dialogue choices will change how NPCs perceive you. It's not a Mass Effect-like system or anything like that, but Remedy says you will notice NPC perception in their attitude toward you.  After eating this mold, Dylan has an idea of where the Resonant he's after is hiding. We find it underground; it's called the Collective, and it's a giant pile of mold shaped into various frowning faces. During this boss fight, Dylan can't do any damage to the Collective. Instead, you have to attack the Collective's tentacles attached to the walls in the arena. To do that, you need to gravity shift so that you can walk on the walls and hit the tentacles. All the while, the Collective is firing moldy projectiles at you and summoning enemies to slow you down. After damaging all of the tentacles, weak spots open up, and you finally do some actual damage to the Collective. I don't get to see the fight's conclusion, but I'm excited to do so myself in the final game. It's not the wildest boss, but seeing Remedy's spin on the "attack weak points to defeat the boss" formula is neat. I expect a lot of weird and strange when I play a Remedy game and so far, Control Resonant seems like it's going to deliver all that and more. Control Resonant launches digitally on PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, and PC on September 24. Grand Theft Auto 6 has finally had its first big gameplay reveal, giving us our first real look at Rockstar's massive open-world and the amount of detail it holds. It's one that might be the most visually-impressive game we've ever seen, according to the experts at Digital Foundry. Digital Foundry's Oliver Mackenzie and Richard Leadbetter have praised GTA 6's visual presentation, with Mackenzie specifically going as far to call it the "most impressive example of real-time game rendering" he's ever seen. The pair praised a slew of details showcased in the gameplay, including standout character rendering, especially the hair physics and skin details across not just the game's protagonist, but many NPCs too. Ray tracing use was also praised, with the pair pointing out how Rockstar has delicately implemented it in the right places for the biggest impact, and is leaning on older conventional methods in the pursuit for a better balance between fidelity and performance. Performance was a big talking point too, especially so given how poorly GTA 5 performed on Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3 when it initially launched. Rockstar has always seemingly favored the best looking image quality over the smoothest, but that seems to have changed with GTA 6. Digital Foundry noted that while the game was rendering at 30 frames per second, that target seems to be consistently hit, with the team rarely finding dips during its analysis of all 48,242 frames. According to Digital Foundry's calculations, the gameplay preview of GTA 6 was rendering at a native 1440p and then upscaled to 4K without using a modern upscaling technique, leading to a slightly soft image that can hopefully be improved before launch. The team also reaffirmed its prediction for several months ago, saying that it doesn't expect Rockstar to release a 60fps performance mode for GTA 6 on this generation of consoles, even with significant compromises to the outstanding visual presentation on offer. It expects the PlayStation 5 Pro to offer better examples of ray-traced effects or potentially higher resolution targets, but the CPU remains the biggest bottleneck in a world with so much simulation that would make halving the rendering time nigh impossible without a significantly more capable processor. That's something that a PC release in the future might showcase.
