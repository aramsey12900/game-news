High Stakes, Higher Boots: Here Are Horror Games’ Best-Dressed WomenNEW

While the female horror-game protagonist fends off demons and other predators, she is also serving looks. This is the rose-covered cross women are made to bear: We must inspire others with our appearance even while covered in gore. Within the broader scope of patriarchal society, this expectation, which then bleeds into fashionable horror games like Silent Hill 3 and the Fatal Frame series, is unfair. Women actively experiencing horror-game scenarios (a hateful god in Silent Hill 3, homicidal spirits in Fatal Frame, etc.) are already dealing with the stress of needing to do things like craft bullets and practice healthy inventory management. It seems cruel for developers to make them think about how their knee-high socks are rolling down mid-duel, too. But for women like me, who love horror games as much as they appreciate clean drinking water, horror-game protagonist fashion is also a uniform we can use to show we're united--both in our pain and in our resilience. Anyway, it takes a bold personality to make sure your hair is brushed and blouse is buttoned before steel-piping an enemy into dust. So I've collected eight of the best female horror-game protagonist outfits across major games like Resident Evil 4 and cult-favorites like Koudelka, ranked based on distinctiveness and influence. This is not a place for practicality.  8. Koudelka Iasant, Koudelka (2000) The psychic Koudelka had incredible fashion foresight: Her horror RPG takes place in a monastery in 1898, but she's wearing the perfect amount of polyester lace for a goth club in the '90s. This asynchronicity is just one of the many awkward details that stopped Koudelka from becoming a big deal at launch (GameSpot's Koudelka review from 2000 complains of "lackluster gameplay"), but it's also what makes her outfit special. Koudelka wears a jean skirt so short that her Victoria's Secret panties peek out like a ghost, plus a see-through black corset and brassy bangles that drunkenly gesture at her 19th-century setting. She's a witch, and confidently so. 7. Jessica Riley, Until Dawn (2015) Until Dawn is one of the best horror games of the PS4 generation, though its middling 2024 remake and unpopular 2025 movie adaptation has since rusted its reputation. But the appeal of fan-favorite Jess has always been as clean as coal. She wears her winter coat with its faux-fur collar popped out like a lion's mane, as bitchy as possible, and has her low-rise skinny jeans just high enough on her sharp hips to be practical. Like Jess herself, the outfit is smug in how overly trendy it is. But a woman with a little ego is nonetheless something to admire.  6. Fiona Belli, Haunting Ground (2005) Fiona was misunderstood for a long time, both by Haunting Ground's initial audience and the demented men in the game, who chase her around her own family's castle trying to get her pregnant. But, among new fans who have discovered Haunting Ground 20 years later, Fiona's outfit has finally earned her empathy--and respect.  Her captor forces her to wear what is now seen as the prototypical horror-game protagonist armor in tall leather boots, a short skirt, and a medieval-inspired blouse that reveals her delicate waist and the more vulnerable organs. Her top includes corset lacing on the back using a powder blue ribbon, nearly the same color as the gleaming stone set in the bodice of the blouse and Fiona's light eyes. But they are brighter than the stone, which she hasn't chosen for herself.  5. Alice Liddell, Alice: Madness Returns (2011) Developer American McGee's Alice: Madness Returns seems to have become more popular each year since publisher EA rejected plans for a sequel, and part of that frenzy is motivated by Alice's singed-effigy look. There are parts of her appearance that are unreplicable, like the glare in her olive eyes and the permanent, unhappy curve of her mouth.  But her blood-speckled tea dress, the petticoat under her gown, and dirty apron on top of it remind me of the invigorating Japanese Lolita fashion subculture. This fashion style uses frilly, dolly dresses and ribbons to accentuate the truth of the woman that wears them: Her mouth is unhappy like Alice's. She's using her dress-code-appropriate skirt length to question why she's ever been told to wear something more revealing, like Fiona. Instead, the unbelievable height of Alice's buckled platform boots suggest you will die before you see what's under that skirt.  4. Maria, Silent Hill 2 (2001) Opposite to Alice, the mysterious dream girl Maria uses her body as a lure. Her outfit has been replicated by fans for decades (though it originated in a Christina Aguilera red carpet look, which Konami devs decided to borrow), but it requires unique nakedness of both the soul and body: Unlike other horror game protagonists, Maria ditches socks completely. Her knees are bare, a mulberry cardigan squeezes around her chest, and her midriff is also exposed, save for the soaring butterfly tattoo on her hip. As most outfits featuring a pink cheetah-print skirt tend to be, it's memorable and good motivation for being daring within your closet--though Maria, the ghost, hides behind the feeling. 3. Ashley Graham, Resident Evil 4 (2005) Big, brown leather boots. Skirt that ends at the thigh, a tight sweater vest that's perfect for the apple-crisp middle of October. Ashley looks like the standard horror-game protagonist, but that's because the plucky president's daughter helped define it. Developer Capcom established modern survival-horror gameplay with its mega-influential shooter Resident Evil 4--and the successful 2023 remake reminded everyone of that fact--but Ashley handled the style.  Her outfit is that of a mature schoolgirl, reflecting both the college-campus chic seen in contemporary horror films like Urban Legend (1998) and Scream 3 (2000), as well her cloistered upbringing. Ashley is ultimately an unwilling victim, but not when it comes to fashion. She probably smells like Marc Jacobs and a drop of nervous sweat. 2. Heather Mason, Silent Hill 3 (2003) I have an unfounded theory that Heather Mason's outfit--forest green cargo skirt, Scooby Doo orange sweater vest, and those classic horror girl brown boots--might have inspired Ashley's matching color palette, which is why I've ranked her outfit slightly higher for originality. Heather also means something different when she wears her elevated schoolgirl look; Ashley's wealth makes her seem glamorous, but also naive. Heather, instead, is a one-bedroom realist. She has high endurance, though she'd prefer the cult that raised her to stop trying to use her womb to grow evil God. Within the chaos, her simple autumn outfit and practical puffer vest--with pockets!--remind her and fans who relate, she's a regular girl who can handle what she must. 1. Fatal Frame: Mask of the Lunar Eclipse remake (2023) Ultimately, when a girl these days talks about a "horror-game protagonist outfit," she's likely referring to Fatal Frame: Mask of the Lunar Eclipse from 2023, specifically. Koei Tecmo's remake finally freed its formerly Japan-exclusive Wii horror game after 15 years, and a new generation of stylish women have now met the spiritually tortured protagonists Misaki, Ruka, Madoka. The girls are saturated in death--poltergeists eat the cursed island they've been foolish to return to--but they are also caught up on Japanese mall fashion. Their outfits include the knee-high socks and microskirts, as required by the women of horror before them, but also more embroidery, chiffon bows, and milk froth-like dresses than ever before, fitting in perfectly with the Y2K nostalgia of other 2026 fashion trends.  Fatal Frame just adds a bloody border to the decade's classic silhouette: its fitted tops, bottoms, and airy cardigans. Fans of the fashion online and vintage resale sites recommend 2000's J-fashion brands like Axes Femme to get the delicate look, which is the best example of how your soul and your clothes touch with soft cotton hands.

Life-Sim Meets Otherworldly, Encroaching Horror in Village In The ShadeNEW
Cozy life-sim games where you get to retreat to a rustic village for a relaxing, self-sufficient life on a farmstead are hardly in short supply--and, with so many stellar entries in this genre out the...
Cozy life-sim games where you get to retreat to a rustic village for a relaxing, self-sufficient life on a farmstead are hardly in short supply--and, with so many stellar entries in this genre out there, a game following this formula needs a really good hook to stand out. Village in the Shade, a new release from NIS America on Switch, Switch 2, PlayStation 5, and PC, certainly looks the part of a cheerful, cozy farming game. But, as the phrase goes, looks can be deceiving--and, in this case, that deception runs deep.  You can tell something is off right from the tutorial, which sees you chasing after an escaped chicken, only to discover something rather distressing. What happens next only leaves you with more questions and some highly unsettling imagery. Somehow, after all this, your player character emerges, stumbling into Kagatsu Village in urgent need of care.  The Kagatsu villagers seem very attentive at first, but it quickly becomes clear that the people in charge aren't entirely altruistic. It feels like they see you less as a suffering victim and more as an engine to reap profits. You're thrown onto dirty farmland overrun with debris--which has been oh-so-kindly leased to you--and forced to sleep in a run-down shack that looks like it came from a match-3 mobile game ad. If you want to stay with the villagers, you're told, you will need to become one of them. And you'll have to work for it. Nobody will so much as speak to you until then.  When you finally raise your first, scant batch of turnips--and offer them in a ritual sacrifice ceremony--you're made an official Kagatsu citizen. But then come the rules. The rules must be followed. They're strange. Arbitrary. Controlling. Almost cult-like. "Do not expose another person's secrets." "Do not search for what was lost." "Do not approach strangers." And, the one most commonly emphasized: "Do not go outside after 11 PM." But the people here seem nice enough, now that they're actually talking to you. Rokkaku's a single parent who runs the local restaurant and cares deeply for his adopted niece Yoh. Hasumi might not have a medical license, but she does seem to care deeply about everyone's health. Sazanka runs the general store and wants to provide the tools for your success. Kisuke might be crass and grumpy, but you can see that the builder has a good heart underneath it all. They'll teach you how to fish, how to set up animal pens, how to hunt for wild game, and how to build a variety of tools. They all seem like a pleasant bunch, even if they can be standoffish at first. (Well, except that sleazeball Konno. Guy has "manipulative crypto bro" written all over him.) And yet, it feels like everyone's hiding something. You catch glimpses of strange behavior, snippets of conversations likely not meant for your ears. There are noises at night. People knocking on your door at ungodly hours, screaming incoherently. Oh, no, it's just Yoh; she's lonely and wants to sleep with the baby chick she adopted that lives in your pen. No harm will come of that, right? Eventually, circumstance forces you to break that most sacrosanct of rules, and you step out during midnight. It's here that you come face-to-face with otherworldly abominations, chasing you, obstructing your vision, making the haptics on your controller go absolutely wild. You've seen something--many things, actually--that shouldn't have been seen by humans. You suspected it, expected it, and yet it’s still no less disturbing. You begin second-guessing the motivations for everything. Submitting your produce at a town gathering for a public evaluation contest seems innocuous enough, but it happens mere days after you've been asked to either tithe or submit to a blood-gathering "medical exam" for reasons unknown. You can't help but wonder if hunting some animals might actually be an act of mercy, given what you know is lurking out there. And there are all of those areas that are conveniently, conspicuously blocked off. Sure, you’re going to open them as you progress, but what kind of progress do you have to make?  This is the reality of Kagatsu: a place that hides horrors beneath deceptively warm and inviting coziness. The graphics are beautifully drawn in a 2D, painterly style that's immediately appealing. The day-to-day life-sim gameplay is easy to pick up, and it's satisfying to build your farmstead enterprise from nothing. But even in its brightest moments--when you raise a bumper crop, finally craft that new setup that lets you make you make a new kind of foodstuff to sell, or help the townsfolk with their errands or give them thoughtful gifts--there's always that creeping, pitch-black shadow welling underneath.  I've been enthralled by Village in the Shade since I picked it up. It weaves its themes of deception and unease into a routine, leaving you in a bizarre emotional state of feeling both comfortable and on-edge. The contrast between the familiar warmth of a life-sim game and a hideous, horrifying underbelly is enthralling, and I find myself both eagerly anticipating and dreading every new development in my cozy town life. If you’re looking for a different kind of life-sim--or a different kind of horror game--you’d do well to take up your tools and join the village.

The Top 10 Best Horror Games To Play This HalloweenNEW
Humans are drawn to horror in all forms, real life and fiction. Perhaps it’s the desire to get as close to death as possible without actually dying; perhaps it’s the morality of life and know...
Humans are drawn to horror in all forms, real life and fiction. Perhaps it’s the desire to get as close to death as possible without actually dying; perhaps it’s the morality of life and knowing death comes for all of us; perhaps all our heads are just a little miswired. Whatever the reason, we flock to movies where supernatural entities and puppets on tricycles upend otherwise lovely lives, TV shows where murderous cults kill and stump local detectives, and books where the words on the page seem to follow you to bed. Games, however, go a step further, letting you enter the horror and control it (or become its victim), and you could argue that, as a result, they’re the most immersive form of fiction. We at Game Informer love a healthy dose of horror from time to time, and we often review games like this. Some terrify us. Others… don’t. And the best ones stick with us for years and years after the credits. Below is a list of 10 of the best horror games we’ve played recently. This isn’t an all-time list, so don’t fret if your personal favorite isn’t here. Instead, this is a collection of 10 horror games ordered by release date that we think are great for spooky season and beyond. Let us know in the comments what you would add to the list! Village In The Shade PlayStation 5, PlayStation 4, Switch, PC The cozy corner of the horror genre seems to have exploded overnight, and now tons of new slice-of-life horror games are in various stages of development. That said, the team behind newcomer The Village in the Shade is no stranger to the form; they made the Yomawari series, a cute-and-terrifying collection of creepy virtual tales. The Village in the Shade takes things back to the homestead, giving you (a child who emerged from a hole in the ground and was immediately given the responsibility of running an entire farm by yourself) the freedom to plant, sow, reap, ranch, and more. Just follow the important rules: don't go out at night, don't share secrets, and don't leave the village among others. You wouldn't want to break the rules, right? You could, theoretically, go about your idyllic rural living. But as you unravel the threads of what this village is, who you are, and why you're here, it's hard not to get drawn into the strange, mysterious, delightfully disturbing truths hiding in its shadows. Just don't forget to water your turnips before you go sneaking out again. Tenebris Somnia PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch, PC Fans of survival horror and old-school FMV adventure games will find plenty to love (and fear) in Tenebris Somnia. When a young woman visits her ex-boyfriend’s apartment and discovers he's trapped in a hellish dream world, it kicks off a well-executed survival-horror trek of inventive puzzles and resource management to overcome dangerous monsters. High-production, live-action cutscenes help tell the story of a deteriorating relationship and the toll an indie horror film took on its creators, and they play an important role in gameplay. Tenebris Somnia may not look like much due to its classic pixelated presentation, but its scares and story captivate. – Our Review Resident Evil Requiem PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 2, PC Resident Evil Requiem is not just one of the best Resident Evil games, but it’s also one of the best horror games ever made. It features a unique dual-protagonist structure. Grace Ashcroft is timid, new to the whole “shooting zombies in the head while dealing with Umbrella viruses” thing, and is generally just trying to do her job. Leon S. Kennedy, however, is a veteran of all that and more. Requiem splits its runtime between the two characters, letting you experience both sides of Capcom’s marquee survival-horror style. Grace’s sections leave your hands clammy and your blood pressure high as you desperately try to survive. Leon’s sections leave your hands shaking with adrenaline and your blood pumping with excitement as he mows down zombies (sometimes literally, courtesy of those pesky chainsaws). The result is a Resident Evil game that blends two typical RE experiences – terrifying and tough or exhilarating over-the-top action – into one cohesive package. It’s perfect for someone who wants to feel scared and then soon after receive the catharsis of being the one in control, like being the tragic victim who dies in Act One of the horror movie and the last one standing who lives to tell the tale. – Our Review Reanimal PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch 2, PC Little Nightmares developer Tarsier Studios has a knack for delivering creepy side-scrolling adventures, but Reanimal eschews any whimsy to become its scariest game yet. While mechanically it is similar to Little Nightmares and still puts small children in unimaginable peril, Reanimal is capital-H horror thanks largely to its terrifying threats and disturbing animal-themed imagery (this probably isn’t the game for animal lovers; some messed-up stuff happens to these critters). Escaping the game’s island setting is thrilling and chilling whether you’re playing alone or with a friend in co-op. Months after release, we’re still thinking about Reanimal’s memorable final sequence, which is worth the price of admission on its own. – Our Review Silent Hill 2 (2024 Remake) PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC 2001’s Silent Hill 2 is considered one of the best horror video games of all time for good reason. At release, it was a graphical powerhouse, using the PlayStation 2's horsepower to create a setting that begged to be explored, even though every bone in your body said you shouldn’t. The remake from developer Bloober Team took on a big task in modernizing the game without losing the original’s soul, and it did a great job. Among all the recent Silent Hill releases (Silent Hill f, Silent Hill: Townfall), the remake is our favorite. The story of James Sunderland and his troubling relationship with his departed wife is complex, emotional, and – important for the purposes of this list – unsettling and scary. – Our Review Alan Wake 2 PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC Alan Wake 2 is an excellent pick for some spooky madness, in the vein of David Lynch’s seminal Twin Peaks mystery-surrealist series. Don’t worry too much about the “2” part of the game’s title. You just need to know that the titular Alan Wake character is a not-great writer who acts like he’s really great, his wife is missing, and he got trapped in the ambiguous Dark Place. He’s only one half of the Alan Wake 2 equation, with his segments set in a dream-like version of New York City. Playing as him feels the most Twin Peaks-y. The other half is Saga, a police officer who arrives in the Pacific Northwest town of Bright Falls to investigate a murder that might be linked to a cult. You can expect more traditional survival-horror gameplay from her. But this is still a Remedy Entertainment game, and without spoiling anything, it goes places. And along the way, you can expect plenty of scares. Turn the lights down low (or off), light a candle, and sit back and enjoy one of Remedy’s best games. – Our Review  Paranormasight: The Seven Mysteries of Honjo Switch, PC, iOS, Android Square Enix has gone two-for-two with its Paranormasight series, an anthology of stories about the strange and occult. And while the more recent Mermaid's Curse is a brilliant tale all its own, the first entry – The Seven Mysteries of Honjo – brings significantly more scares. A group of people in the city of Honjo find themselves caught in a spectral battle royale, using powerful objects and conditions to curse one another, with the allure of a prize at the end. Its magic isn't just in the flowchart timeline, where you can hop between different perspectives and protagonists to see how events play out from every angle, but also in the gorgeous stereoscopic environments and narrative puzzles. Adventure games are a classic genre, and Paranormasight will remind you of what the genre is capable of the moment you slowly spin your camera around, your eyes peeking out from behind your fingers.  Signalis PlayStation 4, Xbox One, Switch, PC Survival horror can contain multitudes, and as the years have worn on, the genre has grown to encompass a wide range of action-combat mechanics. Signalis feels like a re-centering in that respect. It's an isometric survival-horror indie project from developer rose-engine, filled with clever puzzles, scarce resources, limited inventory slots, and looming, oppressive dread. As the Replika unit Elster, you delve deep into a remote complex filled with horrors and monsters, searching for something, or someone, waiting down below. While love for classic horror games and other hallmarks of genre fiction will certainly draw you in, the stylish presentation and devastatingly human core will keep you forging ahead to the finale. Once you've breached the surface of Signalis' retro-future super-structures, you won't be able to turn back. Immortality PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC, iOS, Android In horror movies, audiences often groan at a protagonist for putting themselves in seemingly grave danger just to learn more about a cursed amulet or bloodthirsty killer. But in Immortality, you are the protagonist, and despite a lingering sense that you shouldn't be digging into the archives of these abandoned films, each eerie new scene makes you want to dive deeper. It's a game about Marisa Marcel, an actress who almost starred in three movies in the late 20th century, but has since disappeared. None of the movies were ever released due to mysterious circumstances, but you have access to the archived, unedited live-action footage, including behind-the-scenes conversations. By scrubbing through footage, you can select people or items in each scene and jump to random footage from any other movie that includes the same person or item. The approach to puzzle-solving is unique and keeps you appropriately disoriented. The mystery at the center is an unsettling treat to unravel, and scary in new and unexpected ways. Immortality is one of a kind and an easy recommendation for horror fans who want to try something a little different. Dead By Daylight PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PlayStation 4, Xbox One, Switch, PC, iOS, Android In the decade since launch, Dead by Daylight has continued to succeed, thanks to a dedicated (and newcomer-friendly) fanbase and its towering roster of original and licensed personas from across pop culture horror. The asymmetrical multiplayer title has become an anthology of horror’s biggest faces, including Freddy Krueger, Ghostface, Pyramid Head, and even the Xenomorph. Whether you play as a Survivor, trying to escape all manner of nightmare locations through stealth and cunning, or as a Killer, whose experience feels more like a hunting club, you’ll understand why Dead by Daylight has become a recurring destination for fans of the gruesome and bizarre. Read about the making of Dead by Daylight and how it sunk its teeth into millions of players in our six-page exclusive feature in Issue 368 (p. 42), available in our magazine archive to anyone with a free Game Informer account.

Electronic Arts shut down the legendary RPG studio BioWare last week, ending development on an untitled fifth Mass Effect game. Since then, we've seen what appears to be a leaked trailer for the game, which suggests it might have been called Mass Effect Universe, and heard a sneak peek at what Grammy-winning composer Austin Wintory was cooking up for the beloved sci-fi RPG series. Over the past week, fans across the internet have tried to piece together how a publisher could decide to shut down a studio as prolific as BioWare, but according to a new Bloomberg report, the answer is Star Wars. More specifically, Bloomberg writes that, presented with the opportunity to develop Star Wars Battlefront III with Disney, the publisher, which went private last year after a $55 billion acquisition,  chose to invest its money into that instead of BioWare's in-development Mass Effect sequel. The publication writes that EA looked at its portfolio for the coming years and had two massive sci-fi investments it could continue: Star Wars Battlefront III or BioWare's next Mass Effect. Even though Mass Effect was well into development, and even though the Battlefront threequel hasn't even entered development, EA chose to continue with the latter. And so BioWare was shut down, and Mass Effect development ended, though Bloomberg's sources say there are other, less significant reasons the studio was shuttered, too.  Those other factors include the poor reception to 2017's Mass Effect: Andromeda, the failure of Anthem in 2019, and 2024's Dragon Age: The Veilguard, which, despite garnering positive critical reception, apparently missed EA's sales expectations by 50%. There's also the uncomfortable reality that BioWare made progressive LGBTQIA+ positive games but was a studio within a publisher house now partially owned by the Public Investment Fund of Saudi Arabia, a government fund from a country where same-sex activity, including marriage, is criminalized, sometimes punishable by death; these harsh laws extend to other facets of the LGBTQIA+ community. Another investment partner in this acquisition of EA is Affinity Partners, a Miami-based investment firm founded and run by former White House senior advisor Jared Kushner, who is the son-in-law of President Donald Trump, a political figure who imposes laws that discriminate against the LGBTQIA+ community, especially the 1% of Americans who are transgender; Trump has also been known to mock this community in various ways. No official statements say BioWare shut down because of its progressive leanings, but one has to wonder if that was a factor, especially after reports that BioWare scrubbed diversity from demos to avoid negative feedback from EA (thanks, Kotaku).When BioWare closed last week, only 50 employees remained, all working on the next Mass Effect, after years of layoffs at the studio. A Mass Effect TV series is still in the works at Amazon, even though EA seems to have hung up Commander Shepard's video game suit for good. [Source: Bloomberg]
