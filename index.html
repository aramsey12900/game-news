Modern Warfare 4 Isn’t Blowing Me Away, But Its New Smoke Sure IsNEW
Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4's early access beta is giving players their first hands-on with the newest entry in the juggernaut FPS franchise, and while it mostly feels like business as usual, I did...
Call of Duty: Modern Warfare 4's early access beta is giving players their first hands-on with the newest entry in the juggernaut FPS franchise, and while it mostly feels like business as usual, I did come away impressed with one particular change in this year's installment: smoke. In many shooters, smoke works one of two ways: It's either close to useless, failing to provide sufficient cover, or entirely too effective, making items like smoke grenades feel mandatory.  Modern Warfare 4 takes a different approach. This isn't your granddaddy's smoke. Rather than just shrouding an area in fog, equipment like smoke grenades and field upgrades like the smoke wall create actually interesting gameplay scenarios thanks to developer Infinity Ward's new volumetric smoke tech. Far from being static, this smoke interacts with its environment, moving and morphing as players run, shoot, and slide through it. Explosions will disperse the smoke, too. This opens up all kinds of tactical possibilities, both offensively and defensively. Attackers can use it to block sightlines while still making temporary kill holes by shooting through the smoke. It's a dynamic that works on defense as well. Throwing a smoke grenade and seeing where bullets are opening up holes in the smog gives you more information on where hostiles are coming from. Being able to disperse smoke with bullets and grenades also presents players with decisions to make in the middle of a firefight: Do you shoot through the smoke to try and clear sightlines but give away your position? Do you throw a grenade and try to disperse the smoke? Or do you wait patiently and try to take full advantage of the shroud the smoke provides? These moment-to-moment gameplay questions are present to a lesser extent in previous Call of Duty games, but this new volumetric smoke, and all the ways players can interact with it, is a small game changer. It's an addition I would love to see Infinity Ward double down on and design more of the game around. It makes Modern Warfare 4's objective modes in particular more engaging, with more choices when it comes to attacking enemy capture points or positions. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=yaNr1hHAg2M It's tactical and clever in a way that most of Modern Warfare 4's multiplayer isn't, and strangely enough, feels at odds with the overall speed and pace of this year's Call of Duty. Infinity Ward seems to want to have its cake and eat it too. The newer Modern Warfare subseries that started in 2019 is known for being more grounded, realistic, and slower-paced than its Black Ops cousin. In many ways, Modern Warfare 4 delivers on those expectations. Guns feels weighty and powerful, with impressive visuals and excellent sound design. But when it comes to gameplay, Modern Warfare is faster and less tactical than ever before, with blisteringly fast (and fluid) movement. One match of the beta is all it takes to reveal players are jump firing and sliding around the battlefield like they're pilots from Titanfall. Movement isn't as over-the-top as it has been in recent Black Ops games with their (very fun) omnimovement system, but Modern Warfare 4 still feels like a far cry from the "boots on the ground" of the 2019 Modern Warfare reboot. Infinity Ward's new volumetric smoke, for all its impressiveness, feels a little out of place as a result. After all, why play tactically around this impressive new smoke tech when you could just constantly slide around shooting people in their kneecaps instead? https://www.youtube.com/shorts/czlDkpBL8C4 Modern Warfare 4 isn't the first shooter to use volumetric smoke, of course. The most obvious comparison to make is the upgraded smoke that developer Valve debuted as part of Counter-Strike 2 back in 2023. Therein, however, lies the point. Counter-Strike 2 is a tactical shooter, one where smoke creates scenarios that skilled players can play around. In Modern Warfare 4, smoke, while visually impressive and extremely cool, feels at odds with the rest of Infinity Ward's fast-paced vision. As it stands, Modern Warfare 4's new volumetric smoke is one of the few aspects of this year's game that feels new to the series and stands out. There's still more beta, and the full game, to play, so it remains to be seen how useful or powerful Infinity Ward's new smoke tech will be post-launch. Nonetheless, it's a cool addition for a franchise that too often feels the same year after year, and one I hope Infinity Ward does more with in the future.

The Best SNES RPG You Likely Never Played Is Getting A Modern Re-releaseNEW
The Super NES had no shortage of amazing RPGs, though only a fraction of them saw release outside of Japan. There was one particularly strange case, though: Terranigma, the third game in a trilogy of ...
The Super NES had no shortage of amazing RPGs, though only a fraction of them saw release outside of Japan. There was one particularly strange case, though: Terranigma, the third game in a trilogy of popular action-RPGs that included Soul Blazer and Illusion of Gaia, only saw an English-language release in Europe. 30 years later, however, that's finally changing, as Terranigma has been announced for a global release by publisher Clear River Games on PS5, Xbox Series X|S, Switch, Switch 2, and PC in early 2027. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=e5rWXIzACtM Terranigma puts players in the shoes of Ark, a youth who lives in a secluded village. A mysterious box he opens unleashes a curse on everyone in his hamlet, setting him off on a journey that becomes an epoch-crossing mission to revive the world and humanity as we know it. As a late SNES release, it offers some of the finest pixel-art graphics and music seen on the platform, along with an ambitious narrative and visual design supervised by manga creator Kamui Fujiwara. While any sort of changes and additions to the game were not explicitly detailed, the re-release appears to be faithful to the original's pixel art and music. The press release announcing the game mentions "bringing a host of upgrades, tweaks and bonus features" -- hopefully that means one the game's most notorious difficulty spikes might be toned down. Terranigma was originally created by now-defunct developer Quintet and published by Enix. While an English translation was commissioned, Enix's US subsidiary had closed by the time the game was set to release. Nintendo of Europe picked up the game for publishing in that region--even localizing it into additional languages--but it was passed over by Nintendo of America, resulting in an unusual case of a SNES RPG being released in Europe and Japan, but not North America. It's since become one of the most sought-after European SNES releases. Due to Enix's merger with Square and Quintet's dissolution, some (including former staff) assumed that rights issues were holding up a potential re-release. It appears that any such issues have been resolved, as Clear River states that Terranigma is being re-released "in cooperation with Square-Enix." While we'll probably never know what sort of wrangling went on behind the scenes, it's cause for celebration that such a underappreciated classic of a game will finally get the worldwide release it deserves.

Blood Dungeon Review - Skilled Ugly ChaosNEW
Reviewed on: PC ...
Reviewed on: PC Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, Switch, PC Publisher: Messhof Developer: Messhof Release: August 25, 2026 Rating: Mature Talking about Blood Dungeon is impossible without making at least some reference to Vampire Survivors. Blood Dungeon is clearly born from Vampire Survivors’ undeniable loop and sense of progression, but developer Messhof addresses arguably the primary shortcoming of the 2022 Bullet Heaven game (I think that’s what we’ve agreed to call these, right?): movement. Moving in Blood Dungeon is fun and it sets the game apart within the genre and demands enjoyable skill from the player, but some balance annoyances keep the game from soaring. Blood Dungeon is a functional platformer before you are even awarded the first upgrade to your starting weapon. The nine characters play similarly (though there are two that feel fairly different), and they leap with ease, can climb any wall or ceiling, clamber along fences, and even swim. If there weren’t sometimes hundreds of enemies on screen, I can confidently say that Blood Dungeon would probably work in the platformer genre.Despite the quality platforming, however, Blood Dungeon is more about powering up and staying alive. Like Vampire Survivors, you defeat enemies, collect the titular blood, level up your weapons, acquire new ones, and choose between a series of passive upgrades that appear randomly. If you’ve played a game in this genre, you know what to expect, and it does the style well. Some upgrades feel useless, but many more are easy selections when they appear in the roulette. A few, however, feel required. I don’t know that I ever beat a final boss without the basketball (which sends a flurry of orange balls bouncing around the environment) or the upgrade that doubles everything. I hit a point where I was rerolling frequently to get those two upgrades, and when I couldn’t, it often led to disappointing failure. I would have preferred the weapons to feel similarly powerful, even if they all played differently, which I recognize is a difficult balancing act.Blood Dungeon is also surprisingly challenging, which has its pros and cons. The genre is successful at rewarding players who make smart decisions more than their ability to dodge enemies. The power fantasy is staggering at the end of a great run in a Bullet Heaven game, but I never quite felt all-powerful here, even when I successfully defeated the three bosses of any given level (which is how you beat a level). That’s the con, but the pro is that Blood Dungeon is a much more active and intense game, and when your platforming skills are what save you more than randomly getting the basketball, that is rewarding. Bones awarded at the end of each run can be used for permanent upgrades, which is great; I eagerly invested in the elements of my characters I wanted to improve, but I hit a wall earlier than I expected. I ran out of upgrades to buy, and the requirements to access the few unlockable upgrades were unclear. This led me to amass a large bank of unspent bones while I hoped I'd triggered something to unlock the other purchasable upgrades, which felt random rather than rewarding.The final element of Blood Dungeon that sets it apart from comparable games is its art style, which I don’t love. The characters are purposefully ugly, and it all feels intentionally gross. The fart cloud, which casts a very useful periodic cloud of poison, for example, is an upgrade I would sometimes avoid just because I wasn’t eager to hear my character fart every few seconds. But all that said, it is undeniably unique and perfectly fits the game’s overall mood and tone. The style just isn’t going to work for everyone, and I happen to technically be an everyone.Blood Dungeon is a refreshing take on the genre Vampire Survivors popularized only four years ago, and it effectively establishes its own identity both with its art style and platformer-focused mentality. It is fun to play, whereas many of these games are ultimately fun to experience passively – a build to the point where you can put the controller down and watch chaos blaze across the screen. You can’t ever put the controller down in Blood Dungeon, and that’s why it’s so easy to spend your bones (if you haven’t already purchased everything) and jump right back in for another run. Score: 8 About Game Informer's review system
