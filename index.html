World of Warcraft: Forever is approaching itemization and "best-in-slot" (BiS) gear differently, with developer Blizzard instead wanting players to have more boss- or biome-specific choices. In the latest episode of the WoW: Forever Podcast, Blizzard dove deep on the MMORPG's itemization changes, how it approached designing Forever's new items, class changes, and more. On the subject of raid gear and chase items, Forever senior game designer Aidan Moon said something that could be seen as controversial in certain circles of the game's community. "I essentially want to get rid of BiS," Moon said. "I think that BiS is not a good thing for a video game that's supposed to be about [the] role-playing aspects of it." https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=67dDe3QgJtM BiS is WoW shorthand for essentially the best item that could possibly be in a given gear slot for a specific class. Look up any WoW guide for WoW Classic or modern WoW, and you'll find class BiS gear lists telling players exactly what they should be wearing in order for their character to be as powerful as possible. Endgame in many ways revolves around acquiring your BiS gear, first your "pre-raid" BiS from gear available in max-level dungeons, and then BiS gear, like tier sets, from raids. It's for that exact reason Forever introduced new items that grant additional stats or effects against certain enemy types or even in certain locations. Moon gave an example of specific items that deal more damage against Murloc enemies, and that players might want to have more gear tailored to fighting against a Murloc boss in a raid encounter.  Forever's raids will feature multiple distinct biomes within them, Moon revealed, giving players more factors to consider when choosing what gear to use for specific parts of each raid. One wing of a raid may be snowy, while another may be cavernous, changing what gear could be more optimal for those encounters. "I think your BiS should be different [for] every single boss if you're actually making a theoretical BiS list," Moon said. "It should be based on the creature and the biome and a mixture of those two. You might have an item that's really good against snowy, and then another item in that same slot that's really good against Murlocs. Then there's a Murloc boss in a snowy area and you're just like 'Which one do I wear?' And then eventually you get to a point where you're swapping those things out or it's too much cognitive load and you don't want to do that. It's for those players that really want to optimize."  Trying to get rid of BiS lists is a big change for WoW, which has revolved around the idea for over two decades. Players can already encounter some of the itemization ideas Moon discussed while leveling to 60, with low-level gear from dungeons and quests dealing more damage against specified enemy types or even granting additional movement speed in specific zones. "I don't want you to have to ever think about BiS, I just want you to think about the fight right in front of you and how you're going to fight it," Moon said. Of course, having more biome- or enemy-type-specific items means hoarding more pieces of gear as opposed to selling old ones when an upgrade comes around. That will no doubt quickly fill up the inventories and banks of players looking to min-max for every possible scenario. Classic WoW is already notorious for having limited bag space, and players running around with multiple sets of hyper-specific gear will only make that pain point worse.  Blizzard sounds like it's aware of that problem. Principal game designer Kris Zierhut said bigger bags are on that way, but that it's not needed right now immediately post-launch. However, Blizzard knows players will eventually need more bag space as well as better ways to organize and search for owned items to make putting together encounter-specific sets of gear easier. It's a massive change, but the strategy Blizzard is employing seems to be working. Forever's launch broke WoW concurrent and weekly player records, and more than 15 million characters have been created in the MMO so far. Blizzard recently lifted the veil on how Forever's new PvP system will work, revealing how classic WoW's infamous rank grind will now be a thing of the past.

Technically, Devolver did fail to release Volvy at the same time as GTA 6, as it initially promised, but the day seems like the more important milestone, so I still give it credit for pulling this off. Volvy's Adventure: Reslimed Not only that, but it promoted Reslimed's release with a promotional Game Boy Color demo. Reslimed is positioned as being a remake of an unreleased GBC game called Volvy, featuring the mascot Devolver has begun using in recent years. In reality, that GBC color game does not exist. However, an alleged discovery of a "weird game collection" was shared recently on Reddit; the nature of this was initially mysterious, but those questions were answered when the poster dumped a ROM for Volvy, a playable GBC game. I tried out Volvy on Analogue Pocket last night and was pleasantly surprised. It's a classic platformer with some modern-leaning twists, including walljumping, which was something of a rarity for the time period the game was meant to have been developed. More notable is a punch ability that can be used to propel yourself through levels by effectively latching on to breakable blocks or enemies and pulling you through them and across gaps or into the sky. I was disappointed to find out this was only a one-level demo, though it increased my interest in checking out Reslimed once it was released today. Reslimed isn't much longer--a full playthrough is said to take about two hours, though I've been playing at a more deliberate pace, and there's plenty of replayability thanks to collectibles, a completion timer, and the possibility for no-kill runs--but it does scratch the itch I got from playing the GBC version. Walljumping is gone here, rightly putting the emphasis on the punch-based navigation that remains consistently fun and gives the game a distinct feel.  I'm a sucker for any game, particularly a platformer, that does something novel with its movement mechanics, and Reslimed succeeds in that regard. What look like impossibly long gaps for a character who has a limited jump--even with a sort of propeller ability to slow your descent--are actually no problem at all, as you punch your way through a series of blocks or enemies, letting you move so quickly that I'm having to intentionally slow myself down in order to hunt for secret items (some of which are, perhaps annoyingly, required in order to unlock subsequent levels). There's a great sense of momentum to the way you move using the punch, lending an almost puzzle-platformer element to the action. Each level so far feels like it's got something new to show you. Would there be enough here to sustain a 15-hour experience? Maybe not, but for a $5 game (currently discounted to $3.74), there's more than enough here to offer a recommendation. At the very least, if you have a means of doing so, you should check out the free GBC game, which I might actually prefer to Reslimed, if only due to the novelty of playing a "new" GBC game on my Pocket. Don't let GTA 6's release keep you from playing another solid game from Doinksoft, the makers of other quality 2D experiences like Gunbrella and Gato Roboto. After dozens of hours exploring Leonida, I have a lot of positive things to say about Grand Theft Auto VI, and shared as much in our review of the game. Jason and Lucia bring heart, emotional depth, and a sense of genuine threat to the crime fantasy, as you grow attached to them and start cheering for them against all the odds stacked against them.We reached out to senior members of the Rockstar Games team to ask about their thoughts on Jason and Lucia, and this is what they had to share.During my playthrough, the Jason and Lucia dynamic really elevated the experience, adding heart and emotional weight to the expected mix of action, driving, and activities around Leonida. Was a couple or romantic relationship the plan or goal for the game from very early on in development, or did that come later? Do you see the focus on Jason and Lucia’s relationship as one of the features that separates Grand Theft Auto VI from your prior work on storytelling in the game series?Stephen Donkin, Vice President of Design: “The dual protagonists and the relationship between the two of them was there from very early in development, but exactly how that was expressed in the game did change over time. It was an enormous challenge for us to get them working together in the open world together and finding exactly how that would work. As we started to layer in more mechanics, dialogue, and incremental features across all teams, it just clicked at some point. The ability to be together in the open world also allowed us for new ways to tell the story of the game and to bring some of the exposition and narrative into the open world in a way we hadn’t been able to do before. Hearing these little bits of dialogue as you are just tooling around the world that have specific context to what you as a player have just been doing in the story really knits it all together.” The player has a choice for how to develop the relationship between the two leads, but I imagine most players are drawn toward helping it thrive. In early playtests, is that what you observed?Katie Pica, Design Director: Player Systems & Progression:“Overall, we’ve seen that players are invested in supporting Jason and Lucia’s relationship but we don’t favor a specific path through the game, it is completely down to player choice.”It feels like a lot of the game’s driving narrative beats factor for a close connection between Jason and Lucia. Was it challenging to craft variations that included a more tense or distant connection between the two? Is that even possible?Luke Howard, Senior Design Director: Missions & Visualization:“One of the core goals of this game was to tell the story of Jason and Lucia with a base line relationship where they are amicable, if just platonic, as they still share the same ultimate goals and need each other to achieve them. So, this gave us a good platform on the main flow to expand particularly intense or emotional moments they experience with variation that can expand on their romantic relationship, while not losing anything if that doesn’t exist. In short, we did develop variation in scenes and interactions that range from subtle to more significant, but done in a way that should feel authentic to the player’s choices, relationship-wise and also criminal profile.One example is after Lucia helps Kordell’s gang pull off an intense armored truck robbery she shares the information with Raul’s crew. Afterwards Jason pulls Lucia aside to see how she's coping. This moment carries several variations where the performance direction was captured multiple ways to cover a range emotional energy between them. Clooney and JLo’s dynamic in Out of Sight, as well. And then a lot of buddy movies actually – Riggs and Murtaugh, Butch and Sundance, Mike Lowry and Marcus Burnett, etc. for their back and forth together. In terms of having a game where the characters can change based on how you play and whether you really lean into and enjoy the crime spree we thought about how both Pulp Fiction and Natural Born Killers start with a couple in a diner pulling out guns, but then how different those couples are and how different those scenarios play out. Jason and Lucia are a lot tougher than Tim Roth and Amanda Plummer but we liked the idea of telling a story where Jason and Lucia could end up being closer to either end of that spectrum based on what the player was doing.”Sex and intimacy are notoriously complicated and challenging to present in games. How did you aim to strike a balance with their private moments together that felt authentic? Any thoughts about how or why it’s so challenging to present adult relationships in video games?Rupert Humphries, Senior Vice President of Narrative:“This is definitely something we thought a lot about and spent a long time trying to get right. The actors who play Jason and Lucia were so good, and they had a natural chemistry and part of it was just trying to capture some of that.There’s this trade off in making games between player control and then scripted scenarios. We try and give the player as much control as we can because we are working in an interactive medium and actually doing something rather than watching it is going to feel more powerful. But then you have the ability to really mess around and break the illusion of a tender or romantic moment or whatever it is. So the relationship aspect of this story is there if a player wants to engage in it, but if they don’t the story can play out differently.” In its best moments of genuine support, regular connection, open communication, and flirty or engagement with one another, Jason and Lucia can come across as a pretty ideal model of good relationships – even while they’re simultaneously committing major crimes together. Was that juxtaposition of a healthy romantic relationship against an external world of illicit activity an explicit goal for how the narrative came together?Rupert Humphries, Senior Vice President of Narrative:“That was the goal, yes. Grand Theft Auto VI Review - Outrageous FortuneNEW
Reviewed on: PlayStation 5 ...
Reviewed on: PlayStation 5 Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S Publisher: Rockstar Games Developer: Rockstar Games Release: November 19, 2026 Many superlatives will be heard in the discussion around Grand Theft Auto VI – biggest world, most activities, best graphics, deepest narrative – but its real triumph is the way all those features and others work in service of a cohesive, believable immersion in a criminal fantasy that is hard to grasp until you’ve played it for yourself.Grand Theft Auto VI – Video Review: Rockstar’s latest returns players to a dramatically expanded version of the Vice City seen in a prior game, now largely unrecognizable after real and in-game years have passed. Leonida is a joy to explore, filled with a wealth of activities, both mundane and illicit. The game’s design makes many ordinary tasks as compelling as the illegal ones. Go fishing off the boat, brew coffee to wake up, work out at the gym, scuba dive for long-lost treasure, dust crops, motocross in the mud, go dancing at the club. As you move between those activities, the world feels alive with minor events, from traffic accidents to shady individuals a little too interested in your walk to the ATM. GTAVI delights in life's minutiae, with animations, voice lines, and background characters that keep things fresh even dozens of hours into the story. The level of detail everywhere is astounding and, at times, overwhelming. Getting sidetracked is easy.The focus on detail carries into the visuals. Lucia’s hair blows in the wind, and she absently pushes it back behind her ear. A drug den is littered with paraphernalia and empty containers. A shifty contact quirks a smile – your only hint that things are about to go south. Sweat glistens on your character’s skin after a hard workout, their breathing and heart rate noticeably heightened. Clouds roll together in the sky, creating realistic weather simulations. The commitment to an authentic simulated world is encompassing, thoughtful, and intentional, a testament to hand-crafted and meticulously painstaking development work, paying off in an absorbing play-space that is hard to step away from.While the day-to-day simulation is fascinating, it also adds pacing and counterpoint to the explosive march of major missions, narrative threads, and set-piece encounters that drive the game forward. Not even one of these meticulously choreographed sequences deserves to be spoiled; they run the gamut from amusing shenanigans at a strip club to intense chases and escapes fueled by raging gunfights and explosions. Many of the best moments leverage an expert sense of musical accompaniment, weaving a slick original score alongside one of the most impressive and wide-ranging licensed soundtracks ever heard in a video game – often to surprisingly emotional effect. Ranged combat is punchy and enjoyable; smart use of cover and time-slowing focus makes most battles manageable. On-foot navigation is in a similar boat – simple to pick up and uninterested in needless complexity or challenge; the focus is on choosing smart moments for stealth, knowing when to run and when to fight, and selecting the precise instant for a burst of sudden action. Driving is fast and intense, made more appealing by a huge variety of functionally distinct vehicles to enjoy. If there’s a fault to the core and optional story missions, it’s that many feel a bit on rails regarding where to go and what to do at each step. At the same time, that directed approach keeps the pace relentless, and the action and outcomes thrilling. Over many hours, the game morphs and throws new wrenches into the mix at key junctures, teasing and then playing out criminal experiences from petty theft to group heists to illicit spec ops, and punctuates those distinct vibes with memorable and singular moments.Like its predecessors, GTAVI simultaneously lampoons and celebrates life in modern America, taking aim at targets like social media, wellness lifestyles, rap and music culture, manosphere content, and dozens more. The caricatures land so well because, in many cases, they’re (sadly) barely over the line of the absurdity we see reflected in real life, and because Rockstar so ably captures individual subcultures, dialects, and situations. Behind it all is a scathing critique of wealth inequality and the inherent unfairness of a system that sets most up to fail. It’s a bitter message delivered in honey-wrapped, exhilarating gameplay, but it shines through.Of all its significant feats, the relationship between Lucia and Jason elevates the game to new heights, lending this installment a heart, tension, and emotional core missing from prior entries. This is, without a doubt, video gaming’s most richly drawn and compelling romance, featuring two leads who are immediately relatable and compelling to watch. Player actions dictate the strength of their bond, deepening the sense of engagement. Like the best crime fiction, you’re left surprised by how much you cheer for two people who regularly commit terrible acts. The two collectively suffer the slings and arrows of a world that always seems stacked against them; the fortune they amass is not a power grab, but a desperate attempt to cling to each other and come out the other side together, and the player can’t help but cheer them on and dread how fate might stand in the way.Grand Theft Auto VI is a generational game that stands toe-to-toe with many of the great crime stories and satires across other media. It showcases both the entertainment value of interactive play and the current high-water mark for technical and artistic detail in games. Hunt for and count up all the minor faults or foibles you like, but then note how you just spent another hour lost in this remarkable escape into criminality, wanton violence, egregious sex, and the elusive potential of true love. If you've got a spare hour or two, these snippets are an excellent way to spend your time. At Fate's End My most anticipated game of 2026 was At Fate's End. I say "was" because it was delayed to 2027. Thankfully, a demo for the game was released alongside the announcement of the delay, allowing me to at least experience a morsel of what's to come. Draftula So you took GameSpot editor Steve Watt's wonderfully written Castlevania: Belmont's Curse review to heart, bought the game, and enjoyed fighting Dracula as yet another member of the Belmont family. But now what? Well, you could go help rebuild Dracula's castle in preparation for the next invasive adventurer. Writer Dave Irwin enjoyed the demo for Draftula, an upcoming roguelite that sees you needing to build Dracula's misshapen castle over and over, higher and higher, in order to waylay any would-be heroes. Exterminauts Okay, technically not a demo, but a beta test that you can join right now is demo-ish. GameSpot editor Mark Delaney compares Exterminauts to Helldivers 2, although this three-person player-versus-environment shooter stars regular schmucks obsessed with do-it-yourself projects, not patriotic starship troopers. The game otherwise plays very similarly to Helldivers 2, so the beta feels more like a means of testing to see whether the narrative flavor is to your liking. If you already like Helldivers 2, you'll probably like the gameplay of this shooter too. My Arms Are Longer Now GameSpot managing editor Chris Pereira and I often muse in our weekly one-on-one meetings that we rarely have time to destress via gaming, since the portal by which we primarily play games is also the machine we sit at all day for work. So any weird game that gets Chris to relax makes me happy and is more than deserving of a shout-out. My Arms Are Longer Now is a bizarre game that's strangely compelling--between the ridiculous humor of controlling an arm that stretches belief and the silly hijinks that you're able to get up to, this will likely remain the funniest demo in this roundup.  Nirvana Noir Genesis Noir is one of the most memorable games of 2021. Its use of abstract art to frame a compelling noir detective story within the explosion of the big bang created an experience that was quite novel. The next game, Nirvana Noir, aims to tell an even weirder story. Writer Khee Hoon Chan likes how the vibrant color of Nirvana Noir makes for an eye-catching sequel, one in which a detective hops between parallel realities (one in which the Big Bang happened, and one in which it didn't) to unravel a conspiracy centered around the final moment of Genesis Noir. The game looks gorgeous, and the demo already indicates that this game's puzzles and mysteries are going to be just as spellbinding as the first game. Paranormal Activity: Threshold Mark puts it perfectly: The scariest game of the year is a demo for a game that isn’t even coming out. Like Silent Hills before it (and Devotion seemingly until it surprisingly did come back), Threshold is a fantastically spooky good time that's just never going to release in full. But at least we still have this great demo to experience a tiny glimpse of what was going to probably be a strong first-person horror game. It's really no big surprise that this demo is good--Threshold was being developed by DarkStone Digital, the same studio behind the extremely popular (and oftentimes way too scary for me) The Mortuary Assistant.  New Releases There aren't just new demos to play right now. Some studios are bravely staring down the Goliath that is Grand Theft Auto 6, the sling that is their much smaller title in hand. Releasing in the weeks leading up to GTA 6 or in the weeks post-GTA 6's launch is a bold choice, but those of us who aren't huge into sandbox games are thankful that they're here. Too much of just one thing for a long time isn't always fun. These games fall into their own niche--you're not going to find any games listed here that feel like GTA 6 but smaller. We imagine most of those games were delayed to 2027 or beyond in order to not be overshadowed by Rockstar's action-adventure. Australia Did It A fear of being spoiled on GTA 6's story is pushing Chris to try and focus on Rockstar's action-adventure game, but a very different game has been slowly clawing its way into his heart. Between its fun unit-combining mechanics and hilarious conveyor belt of doom, Australia Did It has been pulling Chris away from GTA 6. Part turn-based tactics game, part tower-defense game, part survivorslike, Australia Did It seems tailor-made for GameSpot's resident tactics aficionado, but don't let Chris have all the fun. Be sure to check out this game yourself! Deadlock Deadlock is the latest in a now dwindling field of hero shooters, but unlike the swing-and-a-miss mishaps of Concord and Highguard, Deadlock seems to have the juice to stick around. The game's player numbers saw a huge surge in September with the City Never Sleeps update, and it's basically been doing just fine ever since despite technically still being in early development. Fingers crossed that continues to be the case. Deadlock has some of my favorite lore of any shooter ever, which is only amplified by the impressive world design of its maps and phenomenal character art direction. Writer Ana Diaz said that Deadlock is the game that convinced her we should all be role-playing in competitive games, and after seeing so many people doing just that in Deadlock and posting those conversations on YouTube, I'm inclined to agree! Hunger Unlike most of these games that are releasing just before GTA 6, Hunger is scheduled to launch just after Rockstar's game comes out. Set to be released in Steam Early Access on December 3, Hunger is an extraction role-playing game in which you navigate a plague-stricken Napoleonic Europe (so weird that we got two first-person zombie-adjacent Napoleon-centered games in 2026). If you're anything like me, you see the words "extraction shooter" and want to log off, but hold on! After playing Hunger for eight hours, writer Cameron Koch points to the extraction RPG's promise of permanent progress as its most compelling feature. You're doing everything else you would in other extraction games--looting, encountering other players, trying to get away--but there's a bit of roguelite-inspired gameplay at work here too, ensuring you can retain permanent upgrades not only from run-to-run, but season to season.  Tanuki: Pon's Summer A bike-riding, mail-delivering raccoon dares to challenge GTA 6, and we should all support him. Mark played this game in the week leading up to GTA 6's launch and spoke with game director Liam Edwards as to how the team decided that releasing their third-person adventure game between The Legend of Zelda: Ocarina of Time remake and GTA 6 was the right move. Blending together BMX tricks and Japan-inspired locales, Pon's Summer sees the titular tanuki racing through four cities to deliver the mail as quickly (and as cool) as he can.  Thank You Bus Driver Friendslop is all the rage these days but, as the name implies, you do need friends to play with in order to enjoy them. Or I guess you could cheat like Mark and just have children. But not all of us are so lucky to be blessed with the circumstances to create people to play games with. That's what writer Jade King enjoys most about Double Fine's Thank You Bus Driver--this wacky first-person driving adventure game feels like playing friendslop, but it's single-player. The game sees you playing as the titular bus driver and utilizing 1930s animation logic to safely get your passengers to their destination--stretching your arms to absurd lengths to smack troublemakers in the back of the bus, for example, or utilizing magical trinkets to transform how the bus handles. Queen's Domain Quite a few of us at GameSpot are obsessed with Queen's Domain--it's quickly gone from a "I'll try to play this game at some point" to "I should play this before GameSpot's Best Of deliberations." GameSpot senior video producer Jean-Luc Seipke first put the game on our radar with their argument that Queen's Domain is gonna be a cult classic. Really hard to argue that I don't need to check out a game with a statement like that. But the pressure to check this game out only grew when Jade added that Queen's Domain has the most terrifying mimic ever, and that the whole game just has some of the best enemy creature design of any action-adventure game this year. You should join me in trying out this game this November. Village in the Shade First released in Japan on July 30, Village in the Shade released in North America and Europe more recently on October 30. Featuring a hand-painted art style and cute-looking characters, Village in the Shade looks like another in a long line of relaxing farming sims. But it's not. Instead, this game is another in a long line of "looks like a cozy game but is 100% not." Your farmer is immediately inundated with strange rules: don't talk to strangers and don't go out after 11 PM, among other similarly strange limitations. When writer Heidi Kemps checked out the game, she noted that it doesn't take long for Village in the Shade's otherworldly, encroaching horror to take hold, and you can't help but want to unravel the mounting mystery of the town. Volvy's Adventure: Reslimed Devolver is just going to act like Devolver sometimes. The publisher's latest marketing stunt saw it announcing that it would release a game the same day as Grand Theft Auto 6. And, true to its word, it did just that. Chris spent all of GTA 6's launch day playing this retro-style platformer, and he doesn't seem to want to stop despite the game only being about two hours long. Apparently there are secrets that he still needs to find, and using the titular protagonist's massive punch to propel through puzzles and platforming challenges hasn't lost its novelty yet.
