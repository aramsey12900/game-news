When it drastically increased the price of Game Pass Ultimate on October 1, 2025, Xbox made a promise in a now-deleted Xbox Wire post: “Ultimate subscribers now get access to over 75 day-one releases a year.”  In the year since, a lot has changed at Xbox. It has hired a new CEO, laid off thousands of developers, spun off several studios, and scaled back its multiplatform plans. This past year has been one of pivots and changes in strategy for Xbox, so I wondered if that day-one release commitment for Game Pass Ultimate fell by the wayside.  Over the past year, I have tracked every title added to Game Pass Ultimate the day it launched. As of Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy’s launch on August 27, 2026, Xbox has met the goal it set for Game Pass Ultimate content last year. Xbox looks very different now than it did a year ago, though, so reflecting on the past year of Game Pass Ultimate also means reflecting on what else has changed at Xbox over that time.  This promotional image from October 2025 promotes "75+ day one games a year" as an Xbox Game Pass Ultimate benefit October 2025: 9 Games Added Sopa: The Tale of the Stolen Potato Little Rocket Lab Ball X Pit Keeper  Ninja Gaiden 4 The Outer Worlds 2 Powerwash Simulator 2 Bounty Star:  Super Fantasy Kingdom Following the price-increase announcement and 75+ game commitment, Xbox came out swinging in October. This was the busiest month in terms of first-party and second-party releases on the service over the past year, as Keeper, Ninja Gaiden 4, and The Outer Worlds 2 were all published by Xbox Game Studios. The Xbox ROG Ally also released this month and was positioned as a portable PC tailor-made for Game Pass. Fans and former Xbox developers did not take too kindly to the Game Pass Ultimate price hike, though, which turned what could’ve been a triumphant month for Xbox into one of controversy.  November 2025: 12 Games Added Football Manager 26 Dead Static Drive Egging On Whiskerwood Pigeon Simulator Winter Burrow Call of Duty: Black Ops 7 Moonlighter 2 Total Chaos Young Suns Monsters Are Coming More day-one games would come to Game Pass Ultimate in November 2025 than any other month. That said, the lineup was mostly made up of indies like Winter Burrow and Total Chaos. The first-party offering of the month was Call of Duty: Black Ops 7, the second Call of Duty game to come to the subscription service the day it was released. Black Ops 7 would receive mixed reviews from critics and the community and didn't make the grand Game Pass splash Xbox needed in the wake of the price hike. December 2025: 3 Games Added Marvel Cosmic Invasion Routine Death Howl Not many games came to the subscription service in December, though Marvel Cosmic Invasion and Routine had a fair bit of hype. Xbox also did not show anything of note at The Game Awards. The defining Xbox moment of December is when Circana sales report data pointed out Black Ops 7 had underperformed in North America. Activision announced it would no longer release Modern Warfare or Black Ops games in back-to-back years, a clear indication that the Xbox and Activision Blizzard merger was not going as planned.  Call of Duty: Black Ops 7 (Activision) January 2026: 2 Games Added Quarantine Zone: The Last Check Mio: Memories in Orbit January of this year would add fewer games than any other month, although MiO: Memories in Orbit is quite good. It was a busy month for Xbox, though, as it held an Xbox Developer Direct to show off Fable, Forza Horizon 5, Kiln, and Beast of Reincarnation. Previous Xbox leadership did have to answer some questions about their confusing multiplatform approach and willingness to cancel games, but January would ultimately prove to be the calm before the storm at Xbox. February 2026: 7 Games Added Menace Relooted Blazblue Entropy Effect X Starsand Island High On Life 2 Aerial Knights’ Drop Shot Dice A Million In what was an otherwise standard month for Xbox Game Pass, some earth-shattering changes were made at Xbox. Phil Spencer, who had led the Microsoft division since 2014, stepped down as CEO of Microsoft Gaming and was replaced by Asha Sharma. Sarah Bond, an executive closely tied to Xbox’s multiplatform strategy, also exited the company. At the time, Sharma wrote in a Microsoft Blog post that she wanted to “recommit to our core Xbox fans and players, those who have invested with us for the past 25 years,” leaving us wondering when, not if, big changes would come to Xbox. March 2026: 3 Games Added Planet of Lana II Nova Roma Super Meat Boy 3D Outside of an Xbox Partner Preview showcase highlighting third-party games, March was pretty quiet on the Xbox Game Pass front. Sharma made a big splash immediately after taking over by announcing Project Helix, Xbox’s next-gen console. The controversial “This is an Xbox” marketing campaign, which deemphasized consoles, was also sunset at Sharma’s direction. While component shortages mean we still don’t know much about Project Helix, this month gave us our first indication of what Xbox’s recommitment to itself was. April 2026: 9 Games Added Replaced Aphelion Vampire Crawlers Kiln Hades 2 inKonbini Heroes of Might and Magic: Olden Era Terratech Legion Sledding Game This was the best month for Game Pass yet in 2026, headlined by Double Fine’s multiplayer game Kiln. This would go on to be the last game released by Double Fine under Xbox ownership. A report from The Verge also revealed that Sharma held a meeting where she teased big changes to come at Xbox, telling employees that they “have to fix the fundamentals on console and PC.” We would see how that played out over the next couple of months. Kiln (Double Fine) May 2026: 11 Games Added Mixtape Motorslice Forza Horizon 6 Outbound Black Jacket Call of the Elder Gods Subnautica 2 Luna Abyss Echo Generation 2 Crashout Crew Kabuto Park May 2026 was the most chaotic month for Game Pass within the past year. In terms of games, Forza Horizon 6 and Subnautica 2 both had very successful launches, while Mixtape was met with critical acclaim. More importantly, Xbox also made some important changes to Game Pass by backing off its commitment to adding Call of Duty games to the service on day one. In turn, it also brought the monthly price of Ultimate down from $30 to $23 (still higher than before the previous price increase). “Our players cover a wide breadth of geographies, preferences, and tastes, so while there isn’t a single model that’s best for everyone, this change responds to a lot of feedback we’ve gotten so far. We’ll continue to listen and learn,” the Xbox Wire announcement for this explains. No mention of the 75+ game commitment was made in that post. June 2026: 6 Games Added Frog Sqwad Solarpunk Beatdown City Survivors Beastro Junkster Starseeker: Astroneer Expedition While June was as milquetoast a month as it could be in terms of new Game Pass titles, it was full of massive ups and downs for Xbox. A showcase was held during Summer Game Fest that celebrated Xbox and confirmed a recommitment to Xbox console exclusives with Gears of War: E-Day and Clockwork Revolution. At the time, Xbox admitted it had lost “millions” of Game Pass subscribers since the October price increase and had only recently begun to regain them after the price decrease in May.  Then, almost as soon as Summer Game Fest ended, an Xbox Wire post from Sharma explained that Xbox needed to “reset the business” due to a low “accountability margin,” the hardware-component crisis, and being “over extended” with the number of studios it owns. This sparked lots of speculation and rumors about layoffs, studios being shut down or spun off, and Game Pass investment disappearing for third-party studios. It was a preview for what was to come in July. July 2026: 8 Games Added Ascend to Zero Wuthering Waves Denshattack Shift at Midnight Tears of Metal Halo: Campaign Evolved  Corsair Cover Mistfall Hunter Halo: Campaign Evolved, a remake of Halo: Combat Evolved, was not nearly enough to take attention away from all of the shocking moves Xbox made in July. Xbox announced plans to cut 3,200 jobs over the next fiscal year, sold off Ninja Theory and Undead Labs, and spun off Double Fine and Compulsion Games as independent studios.  As for where Game Pass fits in all of this, Sharma admitted that Game Pass and other parts of the Xbox business “did not grow at the pace we expected.” Bloomberg revealed that Xbox had been spending over $1 billion a year on Game Pass-related deals. While Xbox is still committed to Xbox Game Pass, this month made it clear that the plan to center the entire Xbox business around the subscription service truly didn’t pan out as intended.  Xbox CEO Asha Sharma at the Xbox Games Showcase (Microsoft) August 2026: 4 Games Added Beast of Reincarnation Sandustry Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy Blood Dungeon As of this month, we’re still dealing with the fallout of Xbox’s July layoffs. Both Compulsion and Double Fine have reestablished themselves as independent studios and regained publishing rights to their games, although those titles remain on Xbox’s subscription service for now. Beast of Reincarnation and Resonance: A Plague Tale Legacy are some of the bigger day-one releases of the year for Game Pass, although Beast of Reincarnation did not garner fantastic reviews.  September 2026: At least 4 Games will be added Minecraft Dungeons 2 Dune: Awakening  Forever Ago Speedrunners 2 Right now, four day-one additions are slated for Game Pass Ultimate. These games will affirm the “over” part of the “over 75 day-one releases” promise that Xbox made last year. As of August 2026, we know that at least 78 games will have been added to Game Pass Ultimate on day one between October 2025 and September 2026. Xbox has not made any particularly bold proclamations about the subscription service or its future at Gamescom. It has also neither posted online to boast that it surpassed the 75-game milestone nor publicly committed to delivering over 75 day-one releases on Game Pass Ultimate per year beyond 2026. A source tells GameSpot that the company plans to continue bringing day-one games to the subscription service, though. A lot has changed at Xbox over the past year, as everything from Xbox Game Studios’ developer count to the price and offerings of Game Pass Ultimate looks very different from just one year ago. Still, in a year full of mayhem for Xbox as a company, it managed to deliver on a Game Pass promise made before all of this chaos ensued.

Mega Man: Dual Override Preview - Mega Man's Dual Override Mode Makes It Feel Easier Without It Actually Being EasierNEW

Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PlayStation 4, Xbox One, Switch, PC Publisher: Capcom Developer: Capcom Release: 2027 Since its announcement, Capcom has made it fairly clear that despite the Dual Override subtitle, this entry in the Mega Man series is not a spin-off. It is a mainline Mega Man game despite being the first to not end in a number, but I wanted to be completely sure. “Yes, that is correct. This is part of the mainline Mega Man series,” producer Hiroyuki Minamitani (who says Zero is his favorite Mega Man game) told me during an interview following some hands on with the game at Gamescom.Mega Man series producer, Shingo Izumi (Star Force: Leo/Dragon/Pegasus is his favorite Mega Man game) says that it really comes down to making sure new players who maybe discovered Mega Man through Super Smash Bros. Ultimate or Fortnite (or maybe even the recently released Pragmata skins) aren’t intimidated by history. “As the number increases over time, certain players who have never had a chance to play past Mega Man games may feel some sort of resistance as we feel many new players will be joining in with Dual Override," Minamitani says. "We felt that we want the title to be more approachable for that audience, as well. So, the title is a combination of the main title and the subtitle, as well. […] We have hope that people introduced to Mega Man outside of Mega Man games will hopefully give Dual Override a shot now that they’re familiar with the character.” From left to right, Dual Override producer Hiroyuki Minamitani, director Shinya Ikuta, and Mega Man series producer Shingo Izumi That effort to welcome new players to a mainline Mega Man game seems to extend to the story, as well. Mega Man has never had a complicated story, but it has maintained a canon of sorts across its 11 games. I don’t think we can expect any unexpected narrative revelations. “The Dual Override story kind of stands alone within the entire canon. I don’t think there is a chronological aspect to it, but it is a standalone story that fits within the overall Mega Man world without having to play the others,” Minamitani says. It will have plenty of callbacks and references to past games, though, as it is intended to be a celebration of Mega Man’s 40th anniversary.One of the ways this manifests is in a new structure. I played through three stages leading up to a final conformation with Twinkle Girl – a first for a Mega Man game – and each of those three stages featured a boss. The second stage boss was Astro Man R, which Mega Man even commented before the fight started that they made a copy of him. Astro Man was a boss in Mega Man 8.“If you have played other Mega Man titles in the past you might notice that as you progress through the stage, things become more and more challenging as you get further along to the end of the stage,” director Shinya Ikuta (Mega Man 2 is his favorite) says. “That’s when you feel that sense of accomplishment for defeating the boss. For this title we wanted to provide more segmented senses of accomplishment so you feel that after you defeat the boss at the end of that particular area within the entire robot master stage.” The intention is effective as I did, in fact, feel accomplished as I made it through each of the difficult (but not too difficult) stages, each being shorter than the typical Mega Man level, but each still full of pattern recognition and combat challenges. It also just means more room for more bosses in general. “Yeah, exactly. That creates an opportunity to include more than just the brand new bosses. There is an opportunity for returning bosses, going back to the decision of, what can we do to celebrate the 40th anniversary,” director Ikuta says.Among the bosses I played, I had the most trouble with Twinkle Girl, an exuberant performer who fires off exploding fireworks as a main attack, but does plenty of moving and dashing which also must be avoided.The titular Dual Override ability allows players to build up a meter to activate some limited, extra firepower. I had it set to activate automatically when I reached a certain low health threshold, which I liked. It gave me a “one last shot!” feeling without reducing the challenge too much. Even if you’re shooting stronger blaster shots, your health is still low, so you still need to play defensively. In Dual Override mode, if you can charge your weapon up enough, you also can fire an even more powerful shot. I appreciated the Dual Override mode and definitely see myself activating it basically whenever it is available. I also appreciate that the flashy cutscene that plays when you activate it can be skipped. I enjoy the core Mega Man games, and I walked away from Dual Override eager to see the rest and what kinds of new bosses it will offer. But my heart will always belong to Mega Man X. The last time we received a new mainline Mega Man X game was X8 in 2004. Mega Man may be celebrating 40 years, but I am commiserating 22 years of no proper new Mega Man X games.“From my perspective, yes, that is something we would definitely love to visit,” series producer Izumi says when I ask not if one is being worked on or if there is anything to tease, but rather, if a new X game is something he would be open to at least considering. “Mega Man X is an important series within the Mega Man brand. We definitely value that series, and if there is a chance, we would always love to consider that.”

Grand Theft Auto 6 has finally had its first big gameplay reveal, giving us our first real look at Rockstar's massive open-world and the amount of detail it holds. It's one that might be the most visually-impressive game we've ever seen, according to the experts at Digital Foundry. Digital Foundry's Oliver Mackenzie and Richard Leadbetter have praised GTA 6's visual presentation, with Mackenzie specifically going as far to call it the "most impressive example of real-time game rendering" he's ever seen. The pair praised a slew of details showcased in the gameplay, including standout character rendering, especially the hair physics and skin details across not just the game's protagonist, but many NPCs too. Ray tracing use was also praised, with the pair pointing out how Rockstar has delicately implemented it in the right places for the biggest impact, and is leaning on older conventional methods in the pursuit for a better balance between fidelity and performance. Performance was a big talking point too, especially so given how poorly GTA 5 performed on Xbox 360 and PlayStation 3 when it initially launched. Rockstar has always seemingly favored the best looking image quality over the smoothest, but that seems to have changed with GTA 6. Digital Foundry noted that while the game was rendering at 30 frames per second, that target seems to be consistently hit, with the team rarely finding dips during its analysis of all 48,242 frames. According to Digital Foundry's calculations, the gameplay preview of GTA 6 was rendering at a native 1440p and then upscaled to 4K without using a modern upscaling technique, leading to a slightly soft image that can hopefully be improved before launch. The team also reaffirmed its prediction for several months ago, saying that it doesn't expect Rockstar to release a 60fps performance mode for GTA 6 on this generation of consoles, even with significant compromises to the outstanding visual presentation on offer. It expects the PlayStation 5 Pro to offer better examples of ray-traced effects or potentially higher resolution targets, but the CPU remains the biggest bottleneck in a world with so much simulation that would make halving the rendering time nigh impossible without a significantly more capable processor. That's something that a PC release in the future might showcase.
