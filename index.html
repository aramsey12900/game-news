Crimson Forest: Red Serpent House This Phantom Blade Zero Serpent House will likely be the first that you stumble upon in the campaign. It’s due north of the Thieves’ Camp, but you’ll only be able to enter it once you have the Flaming Mane. The Phantom Edge comes from the Lion Dance Grandmaster boss in northwest Pangzhen, so just do the main quest objectives there until you obtain it. The Flaming Mane is used to burn the brambles in this area. Once you’re inside, pick up the loot, including: Red Serpent Tongue (quest item) Loong Emblem: Yin (quest item) Serpent Rattle: Red (fragment) Revisit the Crimson Forest to enter the Red Serpent House. Village of Lu: Black Serpent House Next, use the Flaming Mane to torch the brambles in the Village of Lu and grab the following in the den: Black Serpent Fang (quest item) Loong Emblem: Yang (quest item) Serpent Venom Sac: Black (fragment) Reinforced Bowstring (Soul Temper - Feather Bow) Return to the Village of Lu for the Black Serpent House. Meigu Lake: White Serpent House The Lóng Emblems that you’ve collected are needed for the Serpent House in Phantom Blade Zero. The den is on an island at the eastern part of the lake, and the emblems can unlock the main area. You’ll find the following items there: Treasure Basin (accessory) - Inside the small building to the right. Sha-Chi Amber Fragment (upgrade) - Go to the back of said building. Serpent Slough: White (fragment) Sundering Dual Axes (dual-wield weapon) - Dropped by Heartpiercer, Elite Shadow Warden. White Serpent Eye (quest item) 2x Leng Tu’s Memory Flowers Note: The four fragments that you’ve collected are automatically combined to create the Serpent’s Memory, a Soul Temper material for the White Serpent & Crimson Viper. Sail across Meigu Lake and gain access to the White Serpent House. The Serpent Door and the Mirage Estate - Point of no return You can place the three quest items--Red Serpent Tongue, Black Serpent Fang, and White Serpent Eye--in the ornate door in Pangzhen’s Green Serpent House. Doing so opens a hidden passageway that leads to the Mirage Estate. To be clear, this is a point of no return. When you go through the passageway, you’ll receive a warning telling you that you still have “unfinished business.” If you proceed further, you’ll arrive at the Mirage Estate, though it’s possible that several quests will also fail abruptly. That’s why it’s important to tackle key objectives before you proceed. In any case, that does it for our guide on where to find all Serpent Houses in Phantom Blade Zero. Amid the countless games flooding Steam at all times, a new release this week is not to be missed: Queen's Domain. The action-adventure game from Freshly Baked Games takes more than a little inspiration from the earliest titles made by From Software, the King's Field series, but it manages to stand out from other modern fantasy games. That's the takeaway from senior producer Jean-Luc Seipke, who goes into their impressions of the game in the video below. Jean-Luc is not a King's Field player but very much enjoyed the opening 10 hours of the game--an amount that somehow only equates to about 5% completion, according to an in-game counter. The gorgeous retro visuals evoke a game released somewhere right between the PlayStation 1 and 2 eras, and is light on story overall, although it leans into environmental storytelling in a way that feels very true to the style of From Software. Jean-Luc notes the gameplay may not properly sell why the game is such a joy to play. The combat is decidedly slower-paced than many other games, even requiring you to be at full stamina in order to deal damage. There's also no block or parry, instead requiring you to physically move out of the way of an attack to avoid damage. This results in a very deliberate style of combat, where you have to really pay attention to animations and be calculating with your every move.  https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=WB0C8z2j6nA What interests me most from Jean-Luc's rundown is the exploration. You eventually unlock a weapon, the Holy Sword Endu, which provides you with the ability to dash forward or backward in mid-air and can be upgraded to provide additional charges. What initially might feel like a linear world becomes something far more dense and interesting as you recognize the way the sword progressively lets you reach more and more areas as you poke and prod at its environments to see where it can take you. You should be aware that it can be easy to lose progress, as there are no checkpoints or auto-saves. Die, and you're sent back to your last manual save. (If that's off-putting to you and are looking for something vaguely soulslike-adjacent, the newly released Phantom Blade Zero might be what you're looking for.) Jean-Luc recommends Queen's Domain, and you can hear more about why (and see some very upsetting-looking bipedal fishmen while you're at it) in the video above. Queen's Domain hits Steam tomorrow, October 30, for $20 and is deemed Verified on both Steam Machine and Deck. A demo is available now.

This here piece is for you. Breaking Down Phantom Blade Zero's Graphics Options Phantom Blade Zero has three visual modes on PS5 Pro: Quality, Balanced, and Performance. I tested all three. My personal favorite was Balanced, which gives you an upscaled (checkerboarded) 4K resolution at 40 FPS, as long as you have a 120Hz TV with VRR. If you have a 120Hz TV, it likely has VRR because most major manufacturers include it with 120Hz. I like this mode because the upscaled 4K looks beautiful, just a hair less than native 4K, and if you aren't someone super trained on those details, I'd wager you won't notice the difference. The best part is that this mode offers 40 FPS gameplay, which, contrary to how math works, is halfway between 30 FPS and 60 FPS; I'm not kidding, look it up. As such, 40 FPS feels much smoother than 30 FPS, and you're still getting crisp 4K resolution. It's a win-win for me and my go-to mode when a game offers it. That said, if you don’t have a PS5 Pro, you won’t see this Balanced mode. The Performance mode offers an upscaled 2K resolution with a pretty locked 60 FPS. Phantom Blade Zero is an extremely fast, fluid action game, so I think many people will want the buttery smoothness of 60 FPS gameplay. If that's you, pick the Performance mode. Sure, its resolution is lower, and it will look blurrier and less crisp than the other two modes, but if you aren't switching back and forth often or looking at side-by-side images, you likely won't notice. Quality mode is native 4K resolution (absolutely beautiful, the best visual image you can get playing Phantom Blade Zero), but at 30 FPS... and for a game like this, I don't think 30 FPS does it justice. I don't really recommend this mode for combat, but if you're just waltzing around Wulin, grabbing screenshots and such, turn it on and bask in the wonders of developer S-Game's art team. Note: There is an option for ray tracing on PS5 Pro. I tested all three graphics modes with it on and off and noticed zero performance changes or issues.
