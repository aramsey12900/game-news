It's been a busy few weeks for Rockstar and Take-Two following a series of leaked GTA 6 gameplay videos and images. Beginning in mid-August 2026, an anonymous individual or group calling itself CyberLeek began publishing unreleased Grand Theft Auto 6 material. The first wave included gameplay videos and apparent images of the GTA 6 map. The situation is still unfolding, so we've got a roundup of all of the events of the most recent GTA 6 leak in chronological order, along with what the footage appears to show, and CyberLeek's communications so far. Ahead of the footage being posted more widely, several Reddit users believe that infrastructure including the main CyberLeek domain and backup spelling variants were registered as early as August 14. They also claim that a $CYBERLEEK token, liquidity pool, website, and voting program were created around August 15. August 18 Jason plays basketball The first clip was around a minute long, and showed protagonist Jason Duval outside the oceanside house that's already been seen in official Rockstar marketing material. It showed off a Focus mechanic, where making a basket increased Jason's Focus level, as shown on the HUD. It's unclear whether this Focus system is present across all of the gameplay, or only in minigames such as basketball. It was initially unclear whether the footage was genuine, but as Rockstar begin issuing DMCA takedown notices, it appeared to verify the legitimacy. CyberLeek's manifesto Accompanying the first leak were some "commandments" from CyberLeek, including: Thou Shalt Not Sell Digital Preorders Thou Shalt Not Sell Fake Single-Player DLC Thou Shalt Preserve Single-Player Content CyberLeek's clearest complaint is around digital preorders. It argues that this doesn't benefit customers since the product is a digital download that cannot sell out. GTA 6 will not be released on a physical disc, despite having physical premium editions available. Jason's road confrontation The second clip to surface on August 18, titled Random Video 1, showed Jason driving before becoming involved in an altercation with an NPC. It appeared to show that a wide variety of items can be used as weapons, including a wrench. So far, CyberLeek has only shown footage of Jason's gameplay. The map Also on August 18, CyberLeek published images purporting to show the map of GTA 6's city, Leonida. GTA mapping communities have extensively researched the images and found that numerous details line up with years of reconstructed information from Rockstar's official marketing materials. The map appears to be made up of a series of in-game screenshots stitched together to show the entire open world. August 19 Taser video A third video appeared to show Jason breaking into a facility and using a taser, which would be a new type of weapon for the series if it's in the final build. Many theorized this clip could be from 2025 at the earliest, since it featured a track by Tate McRae that was released in January 2025. It shows Jason could be capable of cloning keys or smashing windows when near vehicles. Jason also seems to collect a hat from the ground and put it on. Vehicle-health and fuel-status bars are shown, too. After a short cut away, Jason and his friend drink beers on a boat while laughing about using a VPN. Quite apt. Junkies A fourth video, titled Junkies, showed Jason kicking a police officer off a motorcycle before stabbing him and driving away on it. It showed a potential wanted level of six stars, something that didn't feature in GTA 5. He then approached a community living underneath a bridge, and as Jason's wanted level increased, a warning appeared on the UI stating that the cops are looking for him and have his description. It also showed the use of something called Zombix, which will apparently have a weaker effect on your health the more you use it. Bystanders also seem to have the ability to report your crimes. Stop Killing Games distances itself Stop Killing Games--an international consumer campaign that hopes publishers will keep purchased games in a playable state after official support ends--has distanced itself from CyberLeek and its tactics. "Using illegal means to make a point is unacceptable to us," it said. https://twitter.com/StopKilingGames/status/2089892615923622375?s=20 It also urged people to not buy the CyberLeek memecoin. "Do not send these people your money, no matter how much sympathy you may feel for their actions," it explained. The polls begin CyberLeek has a memecoin called $CYBERLEEK that appears on all of the watermarked footage. This cryptocurrency's value depends on internet speculation or attention, and CyberLeek said that its token would fund a "secret project." There's been no word so far on what that secret project might be. As an incentive for people to purchase the token, CyberLeek began running polls for users to decide on the footage they wanted to view next. Whichever poll option had the most tokens purchased against it would be the next released footage. One poll asked users to choose between a car during the day, a car at night, a motorcycle at night, or a plane during the day. The plane won, with 64.4% of the total, leading to the release of the plane video. August 20 Extended gameplay This video was the one that really got people talking, as some theorized that the leaker may have access to a playable build of the game. As Jason flew over the city in a plane, it showed the density of the environment, and added new locations. A few radio stations are scrolled through, giving a better idea of what the GTA 6 soundtrack could sound like. However, the most important moment was when the character apparently was directly controlled and used gunfire to spell "Leek" on a nearby wall. Ending threats At the same time, CyberLeek threatened to release further material, including potential story and ending spoilers, unless Rockstar and other publishers change their approach to digital games. Some of the videos feature watermarked threats such as, "No physical discs? Then more leeks!" August 21 Subpoenas Rockstar parent company Take-Two filed multiple subpoenas requesting that Microsoft and Discord assist it in discovering CyberLeek's identity by providing information on its users. One subpoena filed with the Southern District Court of New York on August 20 showed that it was issuing DMCA takedown notices in order to "obtain the identity of an alleged infringer or infringers," adding that the information would only be used to protect Take-Two's rights. Take-Two has asked Microsoft and Discord to come up with the internal records before September 4, asking for information including Discord account IDs, registration emails, IP addresses, linked connections, and more. Seeking advertisers A new "Contact" tab appeared on the CyberLeek website on August 21 that stated that the individual or group responsible is looking for funds "through community support and strategic partnerships" by selling ad space on its forthcoming leaks. It stressed that this is not a "ransom," though, so that's okay. It'll set those interested back a 400 XMR (Monero) fee, which currently converts to just shy of $165,450. And that's just the fee to contact those responsible. As far as content goes, it's one of the more uneventful leaked videos, but it does give a good idea of how the city could look at sunset. The existence of a third Ori game would be news to Thomas Mahler, CEO of Moon Studios and director on both previous Ori titles. Mahler went on to explain that Moon Studios has been discussing the possibility of purchasing the Ori IP back, suggesting that no other studio could do much with it. A few hours after, Mahler went a step further and called the Ori 3 rumors "nonsense," while sharing that he recently had dinner with Ori composer Gareth Cocker and that both share a desire to bring the series back. Mahler hasn't been directly involved with Microsoft, however, since the launch of Ori and the Will of the Wisps. It's called Nuevo Paraiso--The Forgotten Frontier, and a new trailer highlights what fans can expect from the project. She's a prominent character from Red Dead Redemption 2 who mentions moving to South America. They are responsible for a number of different Red Dead Redemption 2 mods, including an earlier Mexico expansion that served as the starting point for Nuevo Paraiso. Moving in Blood Dungeon is fun and it sets the game apart within the genre and demands enjoyable skill from the player, but some balance annoyances keep the game from soaring. Blood Dungeon is a functional platformer before you are even awarded the first upgrade to your starting weapon. The nine characters play similarly (though there are two that feel fairly different), and they leap with ease, can climb any wall or ceiling, clamber along fences, and even swim. If there weren’t sometimes hundreds of enemies on screen, I can confidently say that Blood Dungeon would probably work in the platformer genre.Despite the quality platforming, however, Blood Dungeon is more about powering up and staying alive. Like Vampire Survivors, you defeat enemies, collect the titular blood, level up your weapons, acquire new ones, and choose between a series of passive upgrades that appear randomly. If you’ve played a game in this genre, you know what to expect, and it does the style well. Some upgrades feel useless, but many more are easy selections when they appear in the roulette. A few, however, feel required. I don’t know that I ever beat a final boss without the basketball (which sends a flurry of orange balls bouncing around the environment) or the upgrade that doubles everything. I hit a point where I was rerolling frequently to get those two upgrades, and when I couldn’t, it often led to disappointing failure. I would have preferred the weapons to feel similarly powerful, even if they all played differently, which I recognize is a difficult balancing act.Blood Dungeon is also surprisingly challenging, which has its pros and cons. The genre is successful at rewarding players who make smart decisions more than their ability to dodge enemies. The power fantasy is staggering at the end of a great run in a Bullet Heaven game, but I never quite felt all-powerful here, even when I successfully defeated the three bosses of any given level (which is how you beat a level). That’s the con, but the pro is that Blood Dungeon is a much more active and intense game, and when your platforming skills are what save you more than randomly getting the basketball, that is rewarding. Bones awarded at the end of each run can be used for permanent upgrades, which is great; I eagerly invested in the elements of my characters I wanted to improve, but I hit a wall earlier than I expected. I ran out of upgrades to buy, and the requirements to access the few unlockable upgrades were unclear. This led me to amass a large bank of unspent bones while I hoped I'd triggered something to unlock the other purchasable upgrades, which felt random rather than rewarding.The final element of Blood Dungeon that sets it apart from comparable games is its art style, which I don’t love. The characters are purposefully ugly, and it all feels intentionally gross. The fart cloud, which casts a very useful periodic cloud of poison, for example, is an upgrade I would sometimes avoid just because I wasn’t eager to hear my character fart every few seconds. But all that said, it is undeniably unique and perfectly fits the game’s overall mood and tone. The style just isn’t going to work for everyone, and I happen to technically be an everyone.Blood Dungeon is a refreshing take on the genre Vampire Survivors popularized only four years ago, and it effectively establishes its own identity both with its art style and platformer-focused mentality. It is fun to play, whereas many of these games are ultimately fun to experience passively – a build to the point where you can put the controller down and watch chaos blaze across the screen. You can’t ever put the controller down in Blood Dungeon, and that’s why it’s so easy to spend your bones (if you haven’t already purchased everything) and jump right back in for another run. Rebel Wolves, developers of The Blood of Dawnwalker, put out a trailer last week showing off console gameplay of their upcoming action role-playing adventure, but the new footage ended up backfiring. The video revealed that PS5 and Xbox Series X versions of the game would not be able to run at 60 frames per second, instead targeting 40, which frustrated many, as 60 frames is considered the standard for action games like this. Today, developer Rebel Wolves issued a new announcement: The Blood of Dawnwalker will run at 60 fps after all, thanks to a day one patch coming to consoles."Performance Mode was already in the works, but it was still being tested," a post on the official Dawnwalker social media pages reads. "We simply weren’t ready to promise it yet. We heard your feedback. It was clear how much this mattered to many of you, so we focused on finishing the next round of tests. Those results gave us the confidence we needed to confirm Performance Mode for Day 1." The developers go on to state that the patch will now allow the PS5 and Xbox Series X versions of the game to target 60 fps in Performance Mode, 40 fps in Balanced Mode, and 30 FPS in Quality Mode. The PS5 Pro version of the game will include "overall visual enhancements," though Rebel Wolves doesn't elaborate exactly on what that means. Finally, the less-powerful Xbox Series S will be stuck at 30 FPS.Personally, I hope Rebel Wolves succeeds on this day one patch, though I do find it surprising that it expects to turn this around by September 3, the game's release date – it seems like a tall ask in a short window. Still, I (and most everyone else, I assume) would prefer to play The Blood of Dawnwalker at 60 FPS, so I'm looking forward to seeing the studio pull it off.Rebel Wolves was founded by developers who formerly worked at CD Projekt Red on beloved games like The Witcher and Cyberpunk 2077, so the hype around its upcoming project is substantial, even alongside the fall's other major releases.
