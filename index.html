Retailer GameStop has released its latest earnings report, and it was a record-breaker for the company. The Texas-based retailer's biggest number for its fiscal Q2 quarter that ended August 1 was $160.2 million. That's how much GameStop made in operating income for the period, and it was the highest Q2 operating income level since the company went public in 2002. Net income was up, too, reaching $298.7 million, compared to $168.6 million last year. GameStop benefitted from continued growth in its biggest sales category--collectibles--which saw net sales jump a whopping 57% year-over-year to $356.3 million. For this latest period, collectibles made up 45.1% of GameStop's net sales. GameStop was once primarily known as a video game retailer, and while it still sells games, consoles, and accessories, the company's biggest revenue-driver today is collectibles. Anyone who has walked into a GameStop store recently can attest to how much shelf space is dedicated to collectibles. GameStop's next-largest revenue-driver was its "video game" category, which reached $263.2 million for the period, down sharply from $494.6 million during the same period last year that benefitted from the Switch 2 launch. Finally, GameStop's pre-owned and refurbished business made up $170.7 million during the period, down from $250 million last year. Some might have thought that Sony's decision to end support for physical game discs, and GTA 6 not releasing on a disc, would be a death knell for a company like GameStop. However, physical video game sales are "irrelevant" to the company, CEO Ryan Cohen has said. He has also suggested GameStop could expand its business into the "live commerce" category that is popular on TikTok. The executive has also discussed the possibility of GameStop becoming a marketplace for re-selling digital gaming items. While GameStop's operating profit jumped, net sales for the company overall fell from $972.2 million in Q2 last year to $790.2 million this year. GameStop said the decreases was primarily attributable to the launch of the Switch 2 last June, an event that benefitted GameStop. The downturn in overall revenue was also attributable to store closures and GameStop getting out of its business operations in France, the company said. GameStop's share price tumbled after the report came out. GameStop also scaled back selling, general, and administrative (SG&A) costs during Q2 this year, with those expenses reaching $187.1 million, compared to $218.8 million during the same period last year. GameStop is forecasting even rosier times ahead, projecting $650 million in adjusted earnings before interest, taxes, depreciation, and amortization (EBITDA) for its fiscal year ending January 30, 2027. That's up from a previous forecast of adjusted EBITDA of $600 million. In another move, GameStop is trying to buy the auction site eBay, but so far, its proposals have been rejected buy eBay's board. GameStop owns more than 43 eBay shares, worth about $4.9 billion today.

Blizzard president Johanna Faries hasn't been shy about hyping up this year's show in interviews, calling 2026 Blizzard's "biggest year yet." She said its various development teams are "cooking on big swings" across its key franchises and that this year's BlizzCon will reflect the studio's goal of being the "best gaming and entertainment company in the world." BlizzCon's announcements will deliver the "heart and soul" of what fans want out of a BlizzCon, she said, while still "bringing some very new things to the table." What, then, should fans expect from what is being billed as the biggest year in Blizzard history? Let's dive into what to expect from BlizzCon 2026, as well as a few predictions. A Whole Lot Of Warcraft For years, BlizzCon essentially served as a new World of Warcraft expansion vessel. Blizzard would get on stage, announce a new expansion with a jaw-dropping cinematic, and then dive into all the details of the new expansion. That will still likely happen this year, though we do already know the name and setting of WoW's next expansion, The Last Titan, since Blizzard announced an entire trilogy of expansions back at BlizzCon 2023. The reveal of the expansion's cinematic and more details, including its revamp of Northrend, major new features, and possibly a release date, is a given. Expect to learn more about Midnight's 12.2 patch as well. However, there's likely to be more Warcraft this year than perhaps any other time in Blizzard's history. In addition to The Last Titan, it's not exactly a secret that Blizzard is cooking up a new version of WoW Classic, unofficially called Classic+ by the community. What form this version of Classic will take is still unknown, but many fans expect it to serve almost as an alternate timeline for WoW where the game didn't release new expansions every few years and instead turned its focus to the core regions of Azeroth fans were already familiar with. That could mean Blizzard finishing or expanding zones from original WoW, class redesigns, additional endgame content, or even new playable races and classes. Dataminers discovered a reference to a new build for Classic called "Camelot" within Blizzard's files earlier this year. That codename at first glance didn't mean much but was quickly connected to a once-dismissed Warcraft release slate leak from last year that mentioned Camelot by name. That connection suddenly made the leak much more credible overall, and it shows Camelot could possibly release this year shortly after BlizzCon. This, of course, wouldn't be out of the ordinary for WoW Classic. The game's previous experiment that many saw as a testing ground for Classic+, Season of Discovery, was announced at BlizzCon 2023 in early November and was then released only a few weeks later. It certainly seems like history could repeat for Classic+, Camelot, or whatever Blizzard officially calls it.  But Classic+ is far from the only new Warcraft project included on the alleged leaked release slate. According to the alleged leak, Blizzard may also be making a new Warcraft RTS titled Warcraft Chronicles for 2027, as well as an Arthas RPG spin-off for 2028.  Those would be huge news on their own, but beyond games, the leak additionally lists a tie-in television show for each game. The idea of Blizzard working on television shows isn't as far-fetched as it sounds. Faries said in an interview earlier this year Blizzard has had "exciting conversations" about Hollywood adaptations of its IP, while Blizzard VP and executive producer for WoW Holly Longdale has been on record that the Warcraft IP is "underutilized" and that Blizzard wants to expand it beyond just WoW. It's possible the leaked slate is years old and no longer accurate (or just an extremely coincidental fake), but it wouldn't be surprising if all of what it detailed and more appeared at BlizzCon. StarCraft Is Back In A Big Way StarCraft has been a largely dormant franchise for close to a decade. The last major DLC for StarCraft 2 released in 2017, the same year StarCraft: Remastered released. Since then, Blizzard's sci-fi franchise has been relegated to cosmetic crossovers in other Blizzard games like Diablo 4 and Hearthstone. That looks to be finally changing at BlizzCon this year. Persistent reports have indicated Blizzard is working on a StarCraft shooter of some kind, though additional details on what kind have been scarce. There are also reports Blizzard is partnering with Korean video game giant Nexon for a licensing deal that could potentially see Nexon develop new StarCraft games.
