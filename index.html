Blizzard Workers Win Historic Union Contract That Could Set A New Standard For Game DevelopersNEW
A group of 1,900 employees at Blizzard Entertainment has successfully signed a union contract with Microsoft, securing several guarantees for members. Represented by the Communications Workers of Amer...
A group of 1,900 employees at Blizzard Entertainment has successfully signed a union contract with Microsoft, securing several guarantees for members. Represented by the Communications Workers of America (CWA), the contract has given the Blizzard workers more workplace flexibility and guaranteed wage increases, but several other industry-first agreements could make this a blueprint for other video game unions to follow. One of the big wins for the Blizzard union is artificial intelligence guardrails, as the contract stipulates that Microsoft cannot use AI to automate or replace jobs without first talking to the union. Another big win are the job-recall rights for employees, because if they're laid off, Blizzard must offer them an open role within 14 months and inside the union's network before hiring someone from outside. Laid-off union members will also get an additional four weeks of pay--regardless of how long they worked at the company--hybrid schedules that allow them to work from home, and formal systems to settle disputes within the workplace. "It's a new day at Blizzard, where greater power for workers is translating into more innovative games for players," CWA president Claude Cummings, Jr. said to Game Developer. "I am so proud of CWA bargaining teams, taking on tough fights and winning strong contracts that set new standards for working people across our industries." In an interview with Aftermath, Blizzard workers spoke about how the negotiating process with Microsoft dragged on. As one Blizzard employee explained, Microsoft eventually agreed to ratify the union contract after it was convinced that it would be mutually beneficial. Union leaders and members mobilizing outside of Blizzard headquarters to protest also helped seal the deal on the contract. These protections come at a time when Microsoft has been called out for mass layoffs at Xbox Game Studios, which started with 1,600 retrenchments and will see another 1,600 people let go throughout the year. It's not the first time rolling layoffs have hit Xbox either, as thousands of jobs have been cut since the company finalized its acquisition of Activision Blizzard. The gaming industry has also historically had very few unions, leaving workers with very little say during these dark times. Things might be slowly changing, as unions steadily gain more power in the industry. Across the pond, a group of former Grand Theft Auto 6 developers who accused Rockstar Games of union-busting are preparing for a legal battle against the developer. A hearing at the Glasgow Tribunals Centre will begin on September 10 and could run until October 15, while the Independent Workers' Union of Great Britain will hold a rally to support the staff it also represents.

Ultimate on its list of "free updates for select Nintendo Switch games." Here are the games that did add "Nintendo Switch 2 Edition" to their names. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=52Fei-sLLQc&pp=ygUIcGlrbWluIDQ%3D New Nintendo Switch 2 Editions Six games are now getting the Switch 2 bump after today's Direct: three first-party titles and three third-party games. Voice commands for the dog Oatchi and a new Dandori Academy mode will be added to the game on November 12. Xenoblade Chronicles 3 was the third first-party game to receive the Switch 2 Edition treatment. The upgraded version will add a new hero called Shimmer, and a Hero's Vault mode where players fight through waves of enemies in order to enhance the party's abilities. On the third-party side, The Witcher 3's upcoming Remastered Edition will hit Switch 2 in September, while cozy sim Palia will add seasonal updates, community events and new Echo Expeditions with its Switch 2 Edition in December. Finally, Crisis Core - Final Fantasy VII Reunion, which originally launched for Nintendo Switch in 2022, joined the Switch 2 Edition family today at the end of the Direct. An Ultimate omission All of those games getting new enhanced editions is cool! The Switch 2 has proven to be a very viable platform for the resurgence of older games. However, there is a glaring hole where the Super Smash Bros. Ultimate Nintendo Switch 2 Edition should be standing, and it's puzzling to say the least. What, if anything, could be holding Nintendo back? Now, the existence of an all-new Smash Bros. was rumored in the lead-up to today's Direct, along with a new Nintendogs and 3D Mario. None of those things happened, so who knows if those "leaks" had any credibility. However, even if this is true, and the next Smash will be something new, a Switch 2 Edition of Ultimate still makes sense, if for no other reason, than to buy Nintendo time. To quote myself in that op-ed from last year, with a Switch 2 edition, "Nintendo can push back the clock on that question until [Smash Bros. lead Masahiro] Sakurai–or perhaps an unnamed successor–can figure it out." It's possible that Nintendo was waiting for Sakurai to be finished with Kirby Air Riders. The Direct said that the two new Amiibo figures coming to Air Riders are the last ones, so that work may finally be ending. Or, if someone new is taking the reigns, that extra time could allow Nintendo and the new lead to figure out what the future of Smash Bros. looks like. There is a possible explanation, though, to why Nintendo would leave its highly successful fighter tied up, and the binding is all red tape. It's likely that, even if Nintendo wants to update Ultimate, doing so would be a logistical nightmare in terms of licensing. I don't know what those initial contracts said, obviously, but if consideration for future updated editions wasn't built into the agreements for some of those characters, then getting them back wouldn't be possible without a new agreement. Some of those parties have gone through major changes, and opening up negotiations may be impossible--Banjo-Kazooie and Minecraft's Steve in particular could be tough, considering Xbox's renewed penchant for keeping Xbox things on Xbox. Whatever the reason, Smash Bros.'s lack of presence on Switch 2, even this early in the console's lifespan, is glaring. With every Nintendo announcement that doesn't include Smash Bros., be it an Ultimate Switch 2 Edition or a brand-new game, that void will only grow larger.
