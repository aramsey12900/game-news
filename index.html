Bruce Straley, best known for his work at Naughty Dog on the Uncharted and The Last of Us franchises, has apologized to God of War: Laufey studio Santa Monica Studio, after he said he was "not excited" about what the game was offering, at least in some respects. Straley told Edge that he thinks "every pixel" in Laufey looks "mindblowing and that he is "blown away" by the lengths Santa Monica Studio went to. However, Straley said he is "not excited" by "what you're doing in the game." Beyond Laufey, Straley said he feels there is "no opportunity to inject fresh, innovative ideas" in AAA games generally. A scene from Straley's new game, Coven of the Chickenfoot. In a new statement shared on social media, Straley said, "I want to apologize to the Laufey team for dragging you all into this uninvited and unnecessary negative attention." He said he was trying to make a point about his concerns about the AAA games industry generally, and acknowledged that he made a mistake by naming Laufey specifically. "I have thoughts about what’s wrong & the possible serious harm done to our industry & how we might be able to fix it. But in trying to have a thoughtful conversation I stuck my foot in my mouth & mentioned Laufey, and that was my mistake," he said. Straley said games like Half-Life 2, Gears of War, and BioShock--along with games he worked on, including Uncharted 2 and The Last of Us--"set the 'modern AAA' mold." He said these games felt like "they were trying to creative a new experience within AAA." Secondarily, Straley observed that the quest for "games-as-a-service" in a bid to make the next Overwatch or Fortnite is "not a viable strategy and frankly shows these 'industry leaders' don't know their own brand." Straley went on to say he believes the gaming industry is suffering from a "lack of good leadership, clear direction, and conviction from the people at the top." These people are in charge of the money, and therefore determine the stability of the business, he said. "I believe if they understand their brand, they could have honest, inspiring conversations with creators to steer our medium to innovate," Straley said. Straley is now working on much smaller games than he made at Naughty Dog. His new independent studio, Wildflower Interactive, is working on the puzzle-platformer Coven of the Chickenfoot. The game has players taking on the role of an elderly witch named Gertie, who works alongside a "creature-companion" that Gertie cannot speak with. Instead, Gertie needs to "discover the creature's true intentions" in other ways, like through environmental storytelling. Xbox was originally set to publish Coven of the Chickenfoot before dropping it, according to another developer. It's still in the works, backed by an unknown financier.

As a series, Fire Emblem has had two standout features compared to other tactical RPGs. First, characters can build relationships with their allies, and in more recent entries, even become romantically involved. Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave doesn't focus on the seduction side at all, preferring to show the camaraderie between teammates. The second feature, one that's been there since the first NES game, is that characters can permanently perish in combat. Fortune's Weave gives you this option at the beginning of the campaign as Classic Mode. Be warned: this is only for those seeking a brutal challenge. Casual Mode, on the other hand, is a more forgiving way to play through this gargantuan RPG, taking away the stress of potentially leading your allies to their demise. You also choose Normal or Hard difficulty modes, options that are separate from the Casual/Classic choice, so below we'll explain the differences between these key decisions. Fire Emblem Fortune's Weave difficult mode selection. Normal vs Hard Difficulty Mode The first choice is actually between Normal and Hard modes. The game describes Hard mode as "For experienced players", while Normal is for newcomers and "intermediate" players alike. If it's your first playthrough, we recommend going with Normal. If you wish for a challenge, Hard Mode adds extra constraints and ups the difficulty in several key factors. Here are the differences between them both: Normal ModeHard ModeEnemies you face are around the recommended level.Enemies are at least three levels above the recommended cap.Fortuna's Blessing costs no Divine Sand.Fortuna's Blessing costs Divine Sand.Other Blessings cost a small amount of Divine Sand.Other Blessings cost more Divine Sand compared to Normal Mode. Fire Emblem Fortune's Weave classic or casual selections. Casual Mode vs Classic Mode If you've never played a Fire Emblem or similar tactical RPG before, go with Casual Mode. This eliminates all permadeath from the game, instead forcing fallen friendlies to retreat from the battle, returning only after its conclusion. Another more universally accepted term for Classic is 'Ironman Mode". If anyone in your party dies in combat, that's it. While you can always reverse time, either by restarting the chapter via the pause menu or sprinkling some of the Time Dust Fortuna bestows on your current Flame Lord early in their campaign, it's more useful here if any one of your roster kicks the bucket. However, Fire Emblem: Fortune's Weave adds a wrinkle to Classic Mode's definition of character death, and that's what happens when guest characters in any Heroic Games match fall in battle. If anyone who isn't designated as a group leader is defeated in combat, they will retreat. Since they're not currently part of your roster, you can still find them in Dagsion, give them gifts, invite them to share a meal, and eventually recruit them to your ranks. Progression can be carried over if you played the prologue prior to release. Can I Change These Settings Mid-Campaign? Yes, but it will take some time for them to kick in. On the title screen, provided you saved in the Obelisk as your named character (the default name is Eshmel), you can select the 'Descend Again' option. This lets you replay the prologue while giving you the opportunity to change the difficulty, swap between Casual and Classic modes, and tinker with your protagonist. The catch is that you need to replay the entire prologue. Thankfully, you can skip many things with the plus button, from cutscenes to battle screens and most dungeon fights. You will need to replay two full battles, but they're not that difficult to complete because your party is overpowered even in Hard Mode. Once you reach the Obelisk Chamber Base, you can continue each Flame Lord's story where you left it. You can also reduce the difficulty from Hard to Normal at the Shrine of Causality (the big glowing sun at the top of the stairs in the Base), but not increase from Normal to Hard.
