Collecting bones in Phasmophobia is a great way to earn extra rewards and set yourself an additional (sometimes frustrating) challenge, but it's also crucial to achieving a perfect investigation. Each contract, one bone randomly spawns in the investigation area, offering you and your ghost hunting team the option to find it. But, unlike cursed objects, bones have no set spawn locations, so locating them—especially on larger maps— can be hard work. But it’s well worth finding them, even if they don’t count as evidence, as collecting a bone offers a cash and XP reward, with the latter critical to achieving Prestige level. On smaller maps, like Tanglewood, you gain $25 and 25XP for finding the bone; on medium maps, like Point Hope, you gain $35 and 35 XP; while larger maps, like Sunny Meadows, offer $50 and 50 XP. What’s more, if you photograph the bone before collecting it, you can earn an additional media cash and experience reward, while collecting all the bone types will unlock an achievement. Below, we break down all the bone types available in Phasmophobia, tips for finding bones, and everything we know on the conduit collectibles that will replace bones when 1.0 releases in 2027. All bone types in Phasmophobia There are 21 bone types to collect to unlock an achievement. There are 21 bone types available to find in Phasmophobia. Each contract, one random bone will spawn; each time you photograph and pick up a new type of bone you haven't collected before, it will be added to the bone trophy cabinet in your lobby. By collecting them all, you unlock the "Bone Collector" achievement. If you're unsure about which bones you're missing, you can check your cabinet to see where there are gaps. Below are all the bone types you need to complete the cabinet and unlock the achievement:  Spine Skull Jaw Right Scapula Left Scapula Right Hand Right Humerus Right Ulna Right Radius Ribcage Left Radius Left Ulna Left Humerus Left Hand Right Foot Right Fibula Right Femur Pelvis Left Femur Left Fibula Left Foot How to find bones in Phasmophobia Some bones are easier to spot than others. In addition to the type of bone being randomized each contract, the bone’s spawn location inside the investigation area is also random each time, which can make it hard to track down, especially on larger maps. But there is one major rule the bone spawn follows: it only appears in a location accessible to the player and the ghost, so you won’t find a bone inside a closed container like a cupboard or locker. We typically find the bone spawns on the floor or on top of a piece of furniture, such as a table, but sometimes it can appear in weird places like behind a door or in a bathtub. Note that bugs and glitches can mean that, even if the bone is meant to spawn in an accessible location, this sometimes may not be the case. With that in mind, if you’re manually hunting down the bone, it’s sweeping the map room by room, checking all the nooks and crannies, and being mindful that certain patterns (like the floor in Tanglewood) can make it harder to spot. However, if you’d prefer not to waste time, there are a couple of Phasmophobia tips you can use to help you find the bone. The first is to simply ask the Ouija board where the bone is; note, asking this question will drain 20% of your Sanity, and it will only tell you the room, not the exact location. Alternatively, you can utilize a dead player to find the bone and have them tell you its location via Discord or (my personal favorite) throwing random objects in its direction. Bones are being replaced in Phasmophobia 1.0 Conduits will replace bones when 1.0 releases in 2027. In its announcement revealing that Phasmophobia’s 1.0 release has been delayed to 2027, developer Kinetic Games revealed that, when 1.0 does come around, bones will be replaced by conduits. According to the developer, conduits will be a new collectible item that provides “new lore to the ghost and the overall world of Phasmophobia.” When this mechanic is introduced, rather than finding bones during a contract, you’ll find unique items that are paired with a particular ghost model. These items (or conduits) will take the form of seemingly everyday objects, with Kinetic’s sneak peek revealing some of these items include a burned letter, a locket, a watch, a smashed lightbulb, and a set of children’s bracelets held together by a medical wristband. The developer hasn’t yet confirmed exactly how these conduits will work or the rewards finding them will grant players.

In Phasmophobia, the Spirit Box is a piece of equipment that you can use to gather Spirit Box evidence, helping you to identify the ghost you're dealing with. When used under the right conditions, players can use the Spirit Box to communicate with the ghost by asking valid questions or saying recognized phrases. If the ghost has Spirit Box as one of its pieces of evidence, it will likely respond--though higher difficulty levels with less evidence may impact this. Its responses may not necessarily prove useful in identifying it, but any response counts as valid Spirit Box evidence and takes you one step closer to determining your spectre. In addition, using the Spirit Box can also be useful when trying to identify a certain ghost’s unique abilities. For example, if the ghost’s age response changes across the course of the contract, you likely have a Thaye on your hands, while a heavy breathing response is specific to the Deogen. There are a lot of questions you can ask the ghost through the Spirit Box in Phasmophobia, but below, we’ve rounded up those most likely to get a response and what that response could be--though you may not like it. The most important thing to note about the Spirit Box is that not all ghosts will respond to your questions; only those with the Spirit Box as evidence will successfully do so. However, being able to rule out the Spirit Box as evidence is just as useful, so always make sure to try it. You can’t just run in and start bothering the ghost with your questions, though; certain conditions need to be met to ensure you get a successful response from eligible ghosts. Before you start using the Spirit Box, check (if available) the board in the van that reveals who the ghost responds to, either ‘everyone’ or ‘people who are alone’ (requiring you to be in the room alone when using the Spirit Box). If this information isn’t available and the Ouija Board cursed object is available, you can ask the Ouija Board to tell you--but it’ll cost you some Sanity. With this determined, take the Spirit Box to the ghost room and turn off the lights in the room, ensuring you’re alone if needed. Toggle the Spirit Box on and use your selected voice input to ask the ghost a question. Note that the Spirit Box needs to be within a certain range of the ghost for it to respond: three meters for Tier 1, four meters for Tier 2, or five meters for Tier 3. These tier levels also impact the chance of the ghost responding, with higher-tier Spirit Boxes having a greater chance to elicit a response. If the ghost gives an audible response through the Spirit Box, then you can mark it down as evidence. If you see the ghost icon light up white (Tier 1), a ghost icon appear (on Tier 2), or a line of white LEDS (Tier 3), this also counts as a successful reading. If you see a red ghost icon light (Tier 1), an X instead of a ghost icon (Tier 2), or a line of red lights (Tier 3), this means the question was valid, but the ghost didn’t respond--in this case, it’s worth trying a few more times before you rule it out. Anything else means your question was either invalid or there’s an issue with your mic/voice recognition. While you can stand and hold the Spirit Box, asking questions one at a time, my advice if you’re using voice chat is to activate the box and then place it on the floor, allowing you to ask questions and await responses while searching for other evidence. Spirit Box questions and responses Try to use the Spirit Box as early as possible, once you've found the ghost room. There are three main categories of Spirit Box questions in Phasmophobia: how aggressive is the ghost, its location, and its age. The ghost will not provide an answer to your specific question, but the category of that question will determine its potential response, which typically takes the form of a news report sound bite. For example, if you ask "Are you friendly?", a ghost won't say yes or no, but it may(if eligible) respond with a short phrase that includes the word "kill" or "death," indicating it's probably not friendly. But it's not necessarily the response that matters here, but whether the ghost does respond. So, really, you can get what you need from the Spirit Box by remembering three main questions: "Are you friendly?," "Where are you?," and "How old are you?" These cover all three categories and are valid questions that the ghost, if eligible, should respond to. However, below, we provide a list of other popular questions you can ask and the responses the ghost might give. As the 18-year-old Shiro, you’re the latest arrival to a domed AI-controlled city called D-topia, a human paradise according to its blissful human citizens. Designated as a Facilitator, your job is to maintain the city by helping residents with any problems and repairing mechanical issues; going to work involves solving increasingly diverse math/number puzzles that the AI tells you have no practical application other than keeping you productive.Each day you wake up, go to work, roam the city’s sleek and pristine districts to interact and bond with a colorful cast, and go to bed. But what makes D-topia intriguing is how much its residents enjoy living a life where machines do their thinking for them. Despite the apparent lack of struggle, confronting situations that challenge D-topia’s euphoria, such as deciding the fate of a citizen who harmlessly ate more than his allotted snacks or finding a cat whose existence violates the AI’s strict laws, adds a potentially sinister undertone I’m gradually trying to excavate.
