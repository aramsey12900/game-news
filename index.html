Dragon Ball Xenoverse 3 – Release Date, Gameplay, And Everything We KnowNEW
While many game studios chase the elusive idea of a "forever game," Bandai Namco cracked the formula a decade ago with Dragon Ball Xenoverse 2. Pitting fan-favorite characters from the franchise again...
While many game studios chase the elusive idea of a "forever game," Bandai Namco cracked the formula a decade ago with Dragon Ball Xenoverse 2. Pitting fan-favorite characters from the franchise against custom-made warriors, Xenoverse 2 had an extraordinarily long run following its release in 2015, as constant updates and new story content kept the experience fresh for years. A decade later, Dragon Ball Xenoverse 3 is aiming to move the series forward. Following teasers for "Age 1,000" and the subsequent unveiling of Dragon Ball Xenoverse 3 earlier this year, publisher Bandai Namco has begun to steadily reveal more details about the action-RPG, its new gameplay mechanics, and where it fits into the Dragon Ball timeline. If you haven't been keeping up, then here's a closer look at everything that has been revealed so far. Dragon Ball Xenoverse 3 release date Dragon Ball Xenoverse 3 doesn't have an official release date yet, but it is expected to launch in 2027. Dragon Ball Xenoverse 3 platforms Dragon Ball Xenoverse 3 will launch for PC, PS5, and Xbox Series X|S. Dragon Ball Xenoverse 3 story https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=vmh6wJrK9cs While the first two Xenoverse games saw players join the Time Patrol and take part in major Dragon Ball events--chronal shenanigans that were largely the result of Dragon Ball Online villains Towa and Mira--Xenoverse 3 is set several hundred years in the future. It's up to the Great Saiyan Squad to keep the peace in West City and the rest of the world. It's not long before you're drafted into their ranks, and the game has a very distinct superhero influence. This was one of the final projects by the late creator and illustrator of Dragon Ball, Akira Toriyama, who helped design the characters and lore for Xenoverse 3. It's the furthest point in the Dragon Ball timeline that has ever been explored, and the developers have also emphasized a theme of connection throughout the game--more on that in a bit. Dragon Ball Xenoverse 3 gameplay https://youtu.be/wqHmtPxiBJQ Like the previous Xenoverse games, Xenoverse 3 is a 3D brawler that mixes RPG mechanics with beat-'em-up action. Players will get to create their very own warrior, with different races like Saiyans and Humans catering toward specific playstyles with their unique strengths and weaknesses. Humans, for example, are all-rounders who can quickly fill up their potential meter, while Saiyans have raw power and their signature transformations. Beyond the core beat-'em-up gameplay, players can also unleash powerful skills, like beam attacks and area-of-effect ki explosions. While you needed to charge up your Ki Meter to pull these off in previous games, in Xenoverse 3 you can use these attacks at any time and wait for them to recharge after a cooldown period. Ki now acts as a stamina gauge, while some of the transformations we've seen so far include the Super Saiyan and Kaioken power-ups. Ultimate Attacks also allow you to tidy up the battlefield pretty quickly of bad guys or deliver flashy beatdowns to bosses during a clutch moment. It's an ability that does take a while to charge up, so it's best kept as an ace up your sleeve. Outside of combat, it looks like West City will serve as the social space for Xenoverse 3, and it'll be full of activities for you to take part in during your downtime. Soul Shift The biggest additions in Xenoverse 3 are the Soul Assist and Soul Change awakenings. While out on patrol, Soul Assist allows you to use the powers of a legendary Dragon Ball warrior, while Soul Change allows you to transform into a powerhouse like Trunks or Vegeta, who have quicker cooldown timers for their signature powers. You'll need to charge up a meter to become a Z Warrior, and this same meter is also tied to your Awakening ability, so you'll need to choose wisely when it comes to activating this power-up. Dragon Ball Xenoverse 3 multiplayer While Dimps has confirmed that there will be multiplayer in Xenoverse 3, the developer hasn't shown this off yet. Xenoverse 3 is focused on single-player first, but if it's anything like the previous entries, expect modes that allow players to team up for challenging missions and some good ol' fashioned 1v1 action. Insomniac’s Spider-Man Reveals What Brand New Day Is MissingNEW
Spider-Man: Brand New Day has swung into theaters and shattered numerous records; everyone and their Aunt May went to see this film, it seems. And thankfully, it’s good! After a trilogy of Tom Holland...
Spider-Man: Brand New Day has swung into theaters and shattered numerous records; everyone and their Aunt May went to see this film, it seems. And thankfully, it’s good! After a trilogy of Tom Holland movies that purposefully went in a different direction than what fans had come to expect of the character in prior adaptations--and to divisive results--Brand New Day ties up this iteration of Peter Parker in a much more familiar web.  In the latest film, the hero is devoted to his career of fighting crime, with zero time left for a personal life; he has to wrestle with his moral compass in order to stop the bad guy while remaining the hero; and he lives in a rundown Manhattan apartment that likely still costs multiple thousands of dollars. However, one of the movie’s greatest strengths also doubles as a point of weakness, particularly if you return to the Marvel's Spider-Man games developed by Insomniac after watching it. Despite Brand New Day’s best intentions to move Spider-Man toward his roots and away from his ties to the broader Marvel Cinematic Universe, the movie is still absolutely a part of the MCU machine. It sees Mark Ruffalo’s Hulk return to the films, while Jon Bernthal’s Punisher finally gets to make his big screen debut. We also get a new iteration of a popular character from Marvel comics, who ultimately acts as the film’s big bad.  Spider-Man and Punisher team up in Spider-Man: Brand New Day. I call this one of the movie’s greatest strengths, because their inclusion, in my opinion, is how Marvel should be handling these kinds of crossovers. Their appearances feel natural and meaningful to the narrative rather than to simply satisfy contractual obligations or have them filmed on green screen to be inserted into existing scenes. Their interactions with Peter, both as himself and his heroic alter ego, help bolster the webhead’s story while teaching the characters meaningful lessons of their own. However, the inclusion of all these other characters also opens up the film to some critique. While I enjoyed the story director Destin Daniel Cretton and his crew chose to tell with the film’s shadowy villain, the narrative could have been much better served if that role was actually filled by a member of Spider-Man’s rogues gallery.  Early on in the production of the film, it was heavily speculated Brand New Day's story would include Mr. Negative, the villainous alter ego of Martin Li who, when not organizing crime, helps run a charity for the homeless called F.E.A.S.T. While there’s the simple connection that Mr. Negative was introduced in the Brand New Day arc of Spider-Man comics, there’s also the fact that the villain of the movie is able to inhabit the minds of other people.  While Mr. Negative cannot possess others directly, he does have the ability to persuade them to do what he says, and with some tinkering to the script, he easily could have served as the movie’s antagonist while keeping a lot of the other elements of its story intact. Instead, the film features a character in that role that will leave Peter’s world and go onto serve as a foundation in a different corner of the MCU, and because of that choice, Brand New Day is ultimately tainted by sins akin to those in the first Tom Holland trilogy.  Mr. Negative as depicted in Marvel's Spider-Man 2. Over four films, many characters have come and gone from Peter’s orbit, pulled away by the gravity of other MCU projects or have exited due to narrative developments, some of which occur outside of the Spider-Man movies themselves. But one of the big draws to Spidey as a character is how interconnected his web of both allies and enemies is, and that is expertly displayed in the Marvel’s Spider-Man games from Insomniac. Martin Li is integral to both Marvel's Spider-Man and Marvel's Spider-Man 2. In the first game, his actions as Mr. Negative lead to the death of Miles Morales’ father midway through its campaign. Miles, who becomes another Spider-Man in his own game and serves as one of the protagonists in Marvel's Spider-Man 2, has one of the best moments in all three titles when confronting Li toward the end of that game. Li, who is trying to repent for his past crimes, teams up with Miles in an attempt to save Peter’s life. When it looks like Li might have to sacrifice himself in order to do so, Miles decides to try and reconcile with the former villain, despite having mixed feelings about it. “I won’t forgive you. It’s just not in me,” Miles says. “But I can’t carry this hate for you anymore, man. So let’s set things right.” It’s just one of the many powerful moments across the trilogy of games, and that power comes from the series’ excellent world-building. In Brand New Day we get a glimpse at Spider-Man’s rogues gallery with an opening montage of the superhero’s escapades since the events of 2021’s No Way Home. However, the only villain who gets any real screen time is Scorpion. Michael Mando’s Mac Gargan, who had a brief appearance in the first Tom Holland movie Homecoming, finally returns as a full-fledged supervillain. Yet despite being a classic Spider-Man antagonist and having been established in the MCU, Gargan serves as nothing more than a possessed puppet in Brand New Day. In the comics, as well as other adaptations, Gargan becomes the Scorpion in an experiment funded by J. Jonah Jameson, the editor-in-chief of the Daily Bugle who absolutely despises Spider-Man. The procedure twists Gargan’s mind, as the Scorpion suit is permanently grafted to his body. As such, the villain repeatedly targets not only Spidey, but Jameson as well, often forcing the former to have to defend the latter, and neither party is ever happy about it. Spider-Man and Scorpion in their classic comic style. While the Scorpion is also featured in both of the Insomniac games, he is similarly an underdeveloped character there as well. However, he is a big part of an important moment in Marvel’s Spider-Man 2 when Kraven dispatches him with ease, demonstrating the hunter’s skills and savvy, setting him up as a formidable opponent for the wall-crawler to have to deal with. Jameson also has appeared in the Holland movies too, with J.K. Simmons reprising the role after portraying the character in the original Tobey Maguire trilogy. But Simmons’ portrayal of the raging news editor in No Way Home is a far cry from his iconic interpretation of the character in the movies from the early 2000s, acting as little more than the face of an anti-Spider-Man campaign. He has little impact in the story, even if his tirades against Spider-Man do partly motivate Peter’s decision to wipe everyone’s memory of him as the wall-crawler. Holland and Zendaya provide excellent, well-rounded portrayals of Peter and MJ, but they’re the only Spider-Man characters in this quadrilogy of movies that I can really say that about. Marisa Tomei was an inspired choice for Aunt May, but in this iteration, the existence of Uncle Ben and his tragic death--which canonically only occurs because of a selfish decision made by Peter--is completely ignored or maybe didn’t even happen. The removal of Uncle Ben takes a tantalizing aspect of the young hero's development and his relationship with his aunt completely off the table.  Jacob Batalon’s Ned is a fun character, but has incredibly limited development compared to the likes of Harry Osborn, the traditional choice for Peter’s best friend and whose father also happens to be Spider-Man’s greatest foe. And speaking of foes, while the villains in the Holland movies have been mostly great, they are either--in the case of Mysterio--dead, or stuck in another universe. That includes Homecoming’s Vulture, who was inexplicably transported into the world of Morbius as a way to try and connect Sony’s disastrous non-Spider-Man Spider-Man movies to the Hollandverse. The Vulture as depicted in Spider-Man: Homecoming. For the most part, every other character of consequence that has appeared in the Holland Spider-Man movies–Tony Stark, Happy Hogan, Nick Fury, Doctor Strange–has been an imported MCU character, and no matter how well those crossovers are pulled off in Brand New Day, half of the film’s cast falls into that bucket. In comparison, the Insomniac Spider-Man games develop a wealth of Spidey-first characters; Miles Morales, his mother Rio, Harry and Norman Osborn, Otto Octavius, Martin Li, Yuri Watanabe, Felica Hardy, Kraven the Hunter, Wilson Fisk--I could go on. And while a game has a lot more breathing room to develop characters, I would hazard to say that, for all their own faults, both the Maguire and Andrew Garfield cinematic outings at least attempted to grow Peter Parker’s inner and outer circles. When the Holland movies have the opportunity to do that, a slot that could be filled by one of Spider-Man’s own characters is instead given to an extended Marvel family member. I’m not expecting a two-to-three-hour movie to be able to deftly juggle as large an ensemble as the Insomniac games do over their dozen plus hour campaigns. However, the effort those titles put into fleshing out Spider-Man’s world pays off in dividends for all of its characters, while also laying fertile ground for future installments to grow from. Each of the Holland movies is narrowly focused on building up Peter as a character, and plugging in other MCU heroes where it makes sense at the behest of that goal. Tom Holland’s Spider-Man might live in a friendly neighborhood, but it’s a disappointingly small one that, after four movies, should be wall crawling with a litany of the comic run’s most iconic characters.

As explained in a new "Director's Take" blog post by Overwatch product director Chris McCabe, Blizzard said it recognizes that due to Overwatch's ever-expanding roster of 50+ heroes, not every battle pass will have rewards for the heroes a particular player is most excited about. The solution Blizzard is introducing is to not only break the battle pass into smaller chunks so players can more quickly pursue the rewards that interest them the most, but also offer an option to change some of the rewards entirely. https://www.youtube.com/watch?v=DiIquLrEqC4 Starting in Season 4, players will be able to, once per premium battle pass purchase, "hack" a legendary skin at the end of a reward track and replace it with a "curated list of past Overwatch shop and loot box skins," McCabe said. "So, if you're a Tank player who's not interested in the Damage and Support skins in your battle pass, you can use your Tier Hack to swap that Cassidy skin with one you'll appreciate more," McCabe said. "Perhaps a Legendary Reinhardt skin with more crayon and duct tape than chrome? Up to you." The pool of skins that can be used to replace one from the battle pass will change every season, and will always include skins for heroes across all three of the game's roles. Season 4 will feature 12 legendary skins as part of the Tier Hack pool to choose from.  This new Tier Hack system will go hand-in-hand with Overwatch's new "track"-based battle pass. Instead of one, linear, 80-tier list of rewards players need to unlock in order, the battle pass will be divided into six chunks. Players will need to complete a shorter introductory track for each season but can then choose to make progress towards any of the other five reward tracks. What track is active can be changed at any time. After all six tracks are complete, players will progress towards prestige titles and rewards as usual. A look at Overwatch's new battle pass interface. Blizzard's free-to-play hero shooter is hardly the first to employ this type of battle pass system. It's also been done in other live-service games like Call of Duty, Battlefield 6, and Blizzard's own Diablo 4, just to name a few, something Blizzard is more than aware of. McCabe said Blizzard isn't trying to reinvent the wheel, but give players more choice over what rewards to go for first.  Despite the new track system and a UI/UX makeover, Blizzard made clear the number of rewards in the battle pass, and the amount of work required to make progress, isn't changing. "Instead, our goal is to give you more control over what rewards you unlock, and in what order," McCabe said. Overwatch Season 4 launches August 11, and alongside its battle pass changes, will introduce a new MEKA Tank hero, D.Mon, to the roster. In other Overwatch news, Blizzard announced last season that the game's newest core game mode, Stadium, would not receive new content going forward. 1666: Amsterdam – Hands-On With The Assassin's Creed Creator's Long-Awaited Passion ProjectNEW
...
Platform: PC Publisher: Panache Digital Games Developer: Panache Digital Games Release: August 25, 2026 Assassin’s Creed creator Patrice Désilets has moved mountains to bring his passion project 1666: Amsterdam to fruition. The witchcraft-focused action-adventure game was originally conceived over 15 years ago while Désilets was still at Ubisoft. Through a series of tumultuous events, the project has changed studios and been at the center of a years-long lawsuit over rights ownership between Ubisoft and Désilets. Now that 1666: Amsterdam will finally see the light of day, I traveled overseas to its namesake city to play an hour-long build Panache Digital Games prepared for media, which I do at the Amsterdam Dungeon, a local horror attraction that’s part Universal Halloween Horror Nights, part history lesson. Amid the muffled screams of guests enduring the dungeon’s creepy rooms and spirited live performers, it creates the perfect atmosphere to dive into Panache’s occult adventure.  The demo begins immediately after the free prologue chapter that launched alongside the game’s re-reveal at Summer Game Fest. I control Noa Brooklyn, a young crimson-clad witch who has just completed her initiation into the Zaindaris, a magical faction that raised her to become a Collector. Noa’s primary purpose is to identify and eliminate entities called Originals, powerful beings who can assume human form to live amongst us, sometimes for centuries. The Zaindaris granted the Originals their power long ago by bestowing Nux, an ancient magic, upon chosen individuals in an attempt to elevate humanity; instead, the Nux corrupted many of them over time, and they now pose a danger to the world. The Zaindaris have since been engaged in a generations-long cycle of training a Collector to reclaim the Originals’ power to restore balance. Now, it’s Noa’s turn. Noa’s journey won’t be the only time period players will experience. In a fitting twist from the mind behind Assassin’s Creed, the game prominently features a more contemporary setting. It’s New Year's Eve in 1999, and a young man named Aaron visits Amsterdam with his lover. Turns out his partner is secretly a Zaindaris. They engage in a sex ritual that somehow transports Aaron’s consciousness to 1666, where he awakens in the body of a cat during the night of Noa’s ritual; Noa chooses him as her familiar, or Union Cat. This begins a fascinating partnership between the steadfast Noa and reluctant Aaron as they must work to help one another. But there’s one more era at play. If you played the prologue, you met a woman named Clio, seemingly in the 2020s, investigating a letter from her father. Clio’s dad, it turns out, is Aaron, and his message recounting his and Noa’s adventure serves as the narration. Clio’s full role in 1666: Amsterdam is unclear, but given she was evidently conceived during the ritual that kicks off the game’s events, it’s clearly important.  After completing the ritual, the gathering is interrupted by witch-hunting militia. Chaos ensues, giving me the first sample of combat. The right shoulder trigger unleashes a basic combo attack, with Noa pummeling foes using her staff. Stunning them at the brink of death triggers a flashy final blow. A dodge allows Noa to evade attacks from the often four or five enemies surrounding her, and she consumes potions to heal. The melee combat doesn’t pack much satisfying impact in this in-development build, but it gets the point across. The enemies also tend to take turns attacking, which feels disappointingly dated and reminds me of dealing with mobs in the early Assassin’s Creed games. Defeating a group paves the way for another mob to take their place, with dramatic camera zooms displaying their shouts such as “Don’t move” and “Blaggard.” It’s a stylish touch, but one I grew tired of after consecutive encounters. I’d rather they just take their lickings without the prerequisite introduction.The only magic attack at my disposal lets me blow up objects such as barrels and crates. This proves much more satisfying, and can instantly kill or heavily damage nearby targets. However, Désilets later reveals in a separate hands-off demo other abilities Noa will gain. For example, she can unleash tendrils that ensnare and suspend foes, and temporarily petrify enemies, among other powers. Gathering Nux energy allows Noa to perform these feats, and they look fun to pull off. Noa also commands traversal abilities, such as the power to glide over water and warp across platforms to quickly get around a freely explorable Amsterdam that, while large, is denser than wide. This scale is partly due to Panache being a smaller team – 70 employees strong – and its goal of ensuring that every building has explorable interiors. This design choice plays into another key part of 1666: Amsterdam: investigating Originals. Finding them involves unearthing clues such as notes (often by scavenging homes and businesses), analyzing your surroundings for helpful details, and speaking with citizens to glean the required information: their appearance, seal, and original name. Investigations occur during the day. Once night falls, it’s time to confront the Original. Another surprise is that missions have a roguelike element. After accepting a contract to hunt an Original, players choose one of three powers from a randomized pool. These can be stat buffs such as increased health or offensive powers like the aforementioned Petrify. Panache gives players the freedom to investigate Originals as they see fit, with many avenues for gathering information discreetly or more chaotically. Sadly, I only get to watch Désilets tackle a full investigation, but finding clever ways to track down Originals looks to be the bulk of 1666: Amsterdam’s gameplay. I hope it’s fleshed out well enough to remain consistently engaging. To explore more safely, Noa can freely shift between her witch and civilian appearances. The latter draws less attention and makes citizens more amenable towards you. This matters, as an attention meter builds whenever you do questionable things, such as peeking through windows in front of others or openly performing magical acts. A filled meter invites the full wrath of the militia, forcing you to either fight your way to safety or flee to one of many safehouses Noa can retreat to in order to extinguish the heat. These safehouses also allow her to craft potions using recipes and ingredients found by searching through spaces. If the militia defeats Noa, she’ll be sent to prison, where you’ll have to escape. However, Panache is still figuring out this particular element and how severely it will punish multiple incarcerations. As for Noa’s partner Aaron, you can switch control to him at any time. As a cat, he can access places Noa can’t. Panache has created buildings that can be accessed in multiple ways. Aaron can leap up and around buildings to enter them, bypassing locked doors and opening them for Noa. Like everything else in this shaky development build, platforming was rough and occasionally unresponsive, but it reminded me of Stray’s deliberate yet graceful traversal. Aaron can also distract certain guards eager to pet him. On the flip side, those fearful of black cats are vulnerable to possession. Approaching a frightened person and hitting R2 unleashes a hiss, followed by a timed minigame where Aaron pounces upon them and scratches their face. This act allows Aaron to hijack their bodies, useful for exploring and letting Noa into restricted spaces. Aaron can also transmute Lux into Nux. I enjoyed the dynamic of switching between Noa and Aaron to figure out how to combine their unique talents to access tricky locations.  The game’s digital Amsterdam is impressively realized. Panache’s environmental level and art direction draw inspiration from 17th-century Dutch painter Johannes Vermeer (historically living during the game’s timeframe), famed for specializing in domestic interior scenes capturing everyday life of the period. 1666: Amsterdam's visual presentation has a painterly flourish that adds flair to the 3D models. When 1666: Amsterdam becomes available in Early Access on August 25, players can only explore the game’s early chapters, but Désilets calls it a “pretty big chunk” of content. He confirms that the game’s story outline, lore, and rules are complete, with Panache polishing and expanding dialogue and gameplay mechanics. Désilets emphasizes Panache’s eagerness to build the game with the community, and says to expect it to remain in Early Access for at least one year. As a self-professed “old timer,” Désilets recalls the days when a game printed to disc was complete and unchanging, flaws and all. He’s excited for players to witness 1666: Amsterdam’s development and, hopefully, provide valuable feedback. Désilets also confirms the game’s reported AI placeholder assets have been removed and no such content is present in the Early Access launch.My demo of 1666: Amsterdam was, as expected, rough around the edges, as an in-development build of a game launching in Early Access. The experience didn’t wow me from a polish standpoint, and I worry if the game will at least feel more stable and fun to play than the build I played when it arrives in a couple of weeks. But there are exciting gameplay and narrative ideas at play with Désilets leaning on some concepts from Assassin’s Creed but reworking them under a cool witchcraft premise. After over a decade of waiting and uncertainty, it’s tough not to feel happy for Désilets and even inspired by his persistence to will 1666: Amsterdam into existence. The project has a long road ahead, and although it could be off to a rocky start, here’s hoping it can eventually realize its enchanting potential.
