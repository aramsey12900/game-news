This Musical Adventure Tells A Very British Tale Through Deep Cleaning And Ghost BustingNEW

Don’t let the title fool you; the Grand Hotel is definitely haunted in There Are No Ghosts at The Grand, a supernatural clean-‘em-up where you’ll renovate a dilapidated building, investigate ghastly happenings, and break into song with fellow townsfolk. Your janitorial duties include blasting old furniture into smithereens, repainting the walls, and sprucing up a botanical garden with new plants and an outdoor fountain. There are the occasional phantoms and mutated, bloodthirsty spiders, but don’t worry; it’s largely relaxing, therapeutic work, especially with a versatile toolkit by your side--which is powered by a goofy virtual assistant who refers to himself as Robert MacBrushy, and who’s voiced by the inimitable Lord of the Rings actor Billy Boyd. There Are No Ghosts at The Grand is a whimsical mix of genres—simulation, detective work, even a shooter at one point—but it’s also very British, with the hotel and places you’re furnishing reminiscent of Edwardian-era homes and architecture. It’s also inspired by the stately homes that developer Friday Sundae has seen, according to a Guardian interview. Residents here, too, speak with a distinct accent, such as MacBrushy, who peppers his conversations with British slangs like, “Sorry, was having a kip.” The demo currently has three playable levels, and in the first you’ll be introduced to the myriad uses of your power tool, with MacBrushy showing you the basics of renovation. Your first task is to redecorate the Grand’s lounge, a ramshackle room filled with broken furniture and peeling paint. Fortunately, the power tool comes with several features and is particularly intuitive to use; all you need to do is to scan the area, with your tasks being color-coded to the function you’ll need to use.  Like with most cleaning simulators, this busywork is particularly pleasing, as you return misplaced furniture, hang picture frames, and vacuum up dirt and other rubbish to clean up the place. You’re soon interrupted, however, by the town’s mayor, who wants you to head to the nearby Cramon Island with her to look into the mystery of a growing black sludge around town. She insists that this growth stems from some strange—but absolutely terrestrial—happenings on this deserted island. For a start, the Cramon Island level offers a tantalizing glimpse into the shenanigans you can get into in the full version of There Are No Ghosts at The Grand. Aside from renovating rooms and buildings, you can drive a scooter around town to visit other folks. The game allows you to visit and decorate other homes, although you’re largely limited to driving to the next waypoint in the demo. There are also makeshift repairs to be done, such as patching up a boat that crashed at the shore. You can carry out investigations, with the menu allowing you to frequently refer back to a list of clues. You can sleuth around old war bunkers and discover decades-old memories. More can be unravelled by using your power tool to drag items back to their original positions, for instance, or listen to apparitions whisper long-buried secrets among themselves.  Foul creatures, too, abound on the island, but your power tool can even be used to defend yourself against encroaching enemies. It may sound like a haphazard mash of ideas, but it somehow works, with a riotous cast of characters tying the disparate parts of the game together. MacBrushy is backbreakingly hilarious, with the tendency to pipe in with sardonic lines like, “Well hello there, it looks like you’ve been abandoned and left to die on a deserted island!” He also kind of sees you as his protege. Then there’s the adorably suave cat called Mr. Bones who—slight spoiler—will drop clues about the town’s decidedly supernatural background.  What’s most surprising about There Are No Ghosts at The Grand is the theatrics: that is, the townfolks’ tendency to break into song. Musicals allow characters to express themselves in raw, deeply emotional ways, from crooning about a lost love to serenading and dancing with their crush. In this game, people you meet—even Chris, the protagonist himself—will sing about their feelings. The distrustful Miss Green, for instance, sees you as an opportunistic property developer and an outsider, as she urges you to look at the town’s problems from a different perspective. Chris too, can respond in song, with dialogue choices that can influence his relationship with her. The entire sequence is extremely charming, especially with Mr. Bones bobbing his furry little noggin to these songs. There’s plenty more to do, even if you’re done with the island level. Two others—the Botanical and Arcade demos—let you overhaul a garden and an arcade alongside Lily (a friend of the main character who’s mostly just there to tell you what needs to be done), while offering you more tools, such as a planter to place new flora and a scanner to detect specters. But while the zen-like elbow grease of renovating rooms and painting walls may be the main draw, the quirky cast of characters and the supernatural mystery surrounding this British town will invite you to stick around just a little longer.  There Are No Ghosts at The Grand is slated for a release this year on Steam and Xbox Series X|S.
