Are you looking for The Blood of Dawnwalker tips? This is an open-world RPG made by the talented folks who, once upon a time, helped with the development of The Witcher 3. You can expect everything from macabre creatures and dark fantasy themes, as well as unique mechanics that are totally refreshing for the genre. Of course, it goes without saying that things can get a tad bit confusing at first, which is why our guide aims to assist you for the long road ahead. You’re a Dawnwalker - A part-human, part-vampire hybrid In The Blood of Dawnwalker, you play as Coen, a commoner from a small village called Laslea Glen. Circumstances have not only caused Coen’s family to get kidnapped by vampire lords known as vrakhiri. He’s also been turned into a monstrosity--a dawnwalker--someone who lives as a human by day and turns into a vampire at night. This dynamic changes how you approach the game. During the day, you heal up normally by eating food and you chat with shopkeepers who ply their craft in settlements. You may also cast Witchcraft spells and commune with the spirits of the dead. You’re basically a warrior-spellcaster with the limitations of being human. Just a medieval warrior hunting for wolves. Learn to manage your bloodthirst as a vampire Things change drastically at night. As a vampire, you need to satisfy your bloodthirst. This means feeding on hapless stragglers and wildlife. If your health is too low and you approach a person, you might lose control, causing you to bleed them dry as you feast. That said, being a lord of the night has its perks. You get to use serrated claws to strike foes. You get to teleport across short distances. And, yes, you can even walk vertically on walls, defying gravity and all that is holy. Related guide: How to heal as a vampire Drink the blood of your enemies (left), and be ready if you're about to lose control (right). Carefully discern how activities impact Time Progression One of the most important Blood of Dawnwalker tips to remember is that various actions impact the passage of time. As mentioned earlier, Coen’s family has been kidnapped, and you have only 30 days to save them. Go past that point and they’ll be sacrificed to satisfy the cravings of the vrakhiri. Roaming around the world normally does not cause time to elapse. It’s not as though you have an actively running clock or a stopwatch. Instead, actions, quest steps, and skill upgrades cost Time Segments, which are shown in a bar in the upper-right portion of the screen. Do a bunch of things during the day and you’ll transition into night, and vice versa. If you’re not careful, you might panic and notice that you have only a few days left to rescue your loved ones. Related guide: How to manage Time Progression and the day-night cycle Time waits for no one. Complete only the most pivotal tasks during the prologue Time management is impactful at the very start of the game. During the prologue, your main goal is simply to do your chores. What those chores are is entirely up to you as you go around the village helping other folks. But you’ll soon realize that the sun is about to set and the Blood Mass is about to begin. Once that happens, everything you weren’t able to complete beforehand would fail, preventing you from earning those rewards. In one particular instance, you might not even be able to save Coen’s own mother. Related guides: Best prologue quests to prioritize How to save Coen’s mother Pick the peeps that you want to help. Tag and repair fast travel Shrines Over a hundred Shrines dot the countryside of Vale Sangora. You can interact with most of these, but some need to be repaired: Once activated, you can use Shrines to fast travel, pass the time, level up your perks, and store items that you don’t need. If a Shrine is in disrepair, you need to investigate the tracks to find the location of its heart--i.e. figurine. Once you’ve acquired that object, you can place it inside a Shrine to activate it permanently. Go near Shrines to activate them. Survey your surroundings via Scouting Towers Another exploration tip for The Blood of Dawnwalker concerns Scouting Towers. These tall structures can only be climbed at night since you need your vampiric traversal abilities. Once you’ve reached the top, you can take a look at the surrounding lands to learn about the region. This causes all points-of-interest to appear as question marks on the world map. All you need to do is head over to them to reveal the type of activity or quest. Related guide: All Scouting Tower locations Hills, ravines, forests, and settlements. There are a lot of landmarks that you can spot from a high vantage point. Visit points-of-interest like Sacred Stones and Ancient Circles Once a Scouting Tower reveals more information about the area, you should go to those landmarks. Some of them might be Sacred Stones, glyphs with dormant power left behind by the uriashi. Others could be Ancient Circles or Haunted Sites, places where you exorcise the ravenous dead. These activities yield nifty rewards, including equipment and training manuals. Related guides: All Sacred Stone locations All Ancient Circle locations All Haunted Site locations Sacred Stones (left) and Ancient Circles (right) offer challenges and rewards. Help out commoners and sabotage the boyars Before you can rescue Coen’s loved ones, you need to weaken the grip of the vampiric boyars throughout the Vale Sangora. If you check the Court tab/menu, you’ll see different kinds of activities pertaining to the three boyar lieutenants: Ambrus, Xanthe, and Bakir. These tasks include sabotaging facilities, ransacking blood deposits, and ambushing patrols. The more you do this, the bigger the threat you’ll be to their leader, Brencis, who’ll enact edicts to stymy your progress. As an aside, you can also look for people that are in danger--i.e. they’re being accosted by soldiers or they’re surrounded by a pack of wild animals. Help them out and, assuming they’re a vendor, they might reopen their shop in a settlement. Waylay patrols to weaken the influence of the boyars (left) and look for common folk that you can aid (right). Directional combat: Block, dodge, strike, and cast Combat in The Blood of Dawnwalker emphasizes fast reactions based on directional inputs. Here’s a quick summary: Enemy attacks can come from four directions: up, down, left, or right. You can do a deflection by holding the button/thumbstick for that direction and pressing the block button. Alternatively, you can press the block button without any other input for an Omniblock, though this action costs stamina. Attacks with a red skull icon cannot be blocked, which means you have to dodge them. Perfect blocks and successful attacks restore your activation charges. In turn, these charges can be used to cast powerful abilities. Be ready to block an attack so you can riposte. Learn skills via manuals and pick the ones you really need Coen has to juggle three skill trees: Swordsmanship, Witchcraft, and Vampirism. While a few perks are available at the start of the campaign, the vast majority need to be learned: Swordsmanship and Witchcraft skills can be learned via skill manuals. These come from vendors, activities, or general loot. Vampirism skills can be unlocked through Corruption levels. Coen’s Corruption increases as he keeps drinking blood, whether from living beings or liquids left in phials. Likewise, going back to what we mentioned earlier about Time Progression, skill unlocks and upgrades do cost Time Segments. That’s why you need to think hard about the perks that you really need. Trying to unlock everything will just waste so many days. Related guide: The best skills for Swordsmanship, Witchcraft, and Vampirism Think twice about the perks that you upgrade (left) after learning them via manuals (right). Upgrade your gear instead of throwing them away Perhaps one of the most integral Blood of Dawnwalker tips to keep in mind is this: You don’t need to throw or sell items that you like. They can actually be upgraded via blacksmiths and armorers, boosting their stats up to your current level. Per level-up, weapons and armors have their damage and defenses increased. Secondary stats--i.e. maximum health, health/stamina restoration, activation charges, and the like--get increased every five level-ups. Amulets and rings gain increases every 10 level-ups. A legendary item that you got when you were level 10 might still be viable when you hit level 40. Collect and sell silver contraband If you’re spending a lot on training manuals and gear upgrades, then you definitely need more denarii. The best way to amass enough cash is to collect silver, which is considered a contraband since vampires are weak against it. Thankfully, a certain vendor buys the silver ores and wares that you don’t need. Related guide: Where to sell your silver Your contraband is your cashflow. Meet new characters and romance them Coen doesn’t have to go at it alone, since he meets a wonderful cast of supporting characters. Some might even lead to deeper bonds, especially if you play your cards right. Related guides: How to romance Anca How to romance Lacra Will they or won't they? Explore far-flung locations and discover secrets Vale Sangora is filled with fiendish foes and dark domains, which is why we encourage you to explore off the beaten path. In some cases, you might stumble upon a quest that leads to an awesome unlockable perk. In another instance, that nondescript ruin in the distance might have a chest with a legendary armor. Oh, and in a couple of areas, you might just unlock secret endings. What a strange ritual. I wonder what happens if I... ahhh! You can always rush to the finale if you’re ready for a challenge Brencis goads you and you have 30 days to prepare for a final confrontation. But if you feel that you’re ready (or you really want to get things over with), you can rush Brencis’ citadel as soon as you’re done with the prologue. After a short climb, you’ll find yourself in the fortress’ courtyard, but don’t expect a cakewalk. The citadel is full of soldiers and every vampire boyar that’s still alive will be waiting for you. Related guide: How to finish the campaign early Well that was an easy climb, I guess it's... ahhh! That does it for our Blood of Dawnwalker beginner’s guide. We hope these tips help you overcome all the challenges in the ravaged lands of Vale Sangora.

When you’re ready, simply make your way from Laslea Glen to the western edge of the map. Just past the woods in Briar Sloughs, you’ll see an aptly named Shrine called the Coward’s Path. Travel along the road and you’ll stumble upon a side quest known as The Cursed Chasm. There, you’ll spot a broken bridge and a ravine. If you look across the gap, you’ll notice that the road continues all the way to a large gate that hasn’t been blockaded yet. A map showing the location of the exit gate. Crossing the chasm To get this joke ending in The Blood of Dawnwalker, you have to reach the gate at the end of the path. But, before you could do that, you have to get across the chasm first: Make sure it’s nighttime so that Coen turns into a vampire. Jump across and spam the Shadowstep button so you can latch on a rock at the opposite side. Then, with a combination of Planeshift and Shadowstep, climb to the other side of the ravine. When you approach the gate, pick [Leave the valley forever]. Cross the ravine (left) and escape the valley (right). From hero to zero Coen will admit that he has to find a new home and make a new life for himself. He won’t rescue his family anymore and he’ll leave the rest of the region under the rule of the vampire boyars. The credits will then play, though you’ll also see a quick epilogue that sets up a potential sequel. This whole sequence then nets you an achievement called "A Hero They Deserve." That does it for our guide on how to leave Vale Sangora in The Blood of Dawnwalker. This instance does remind us of the endings in other games where our characters just decided to “nope” out of hellacious conflicts. Far Cry 6 comes to mind, given that you could sail on a boat and relax on a beach in Miami, all while avoiding coups and dictatorships. Anyway, this is just one of several conclusions to Coen’s campaign. If you want more of a challenge, you can just rush the vampire lord Brencis in his citadel.

Where Old Devils Die The Font of Life in The Blood of Dawnwalker comes into play near the end of the Stronger Than Achilles quest, which is actually part of Anca’s narrative arc. Here’s a quick summary: After leaving Laslea Glen, you can meet up with Anca at the Crossroads Inn. You should complete the Echoes of Silenced Bells quest as soon as you’re able to. A quest called Where Old Devils Die should pop up in your journal. After a day or so, return to Anca once she’s deciphered the writings. Examine the map and look at the eastern points-of-interest. Then, travel to the marked location with Anca. The couple will see an ancient door that only opens for someone who is imbued by numerous runes on their body. Tell Anca that you’re ready and she’ll start painting the runes on Coen’s torso and arms. This nets you the Runic Enchantment, a legendary chestpiece: It grants magical damage reduction, damage against supernatural enemies, Witchcraft ability damage, and, most surprisingly, lifesteal from attacks. It doesn’t have any armor value at all, and it also doesn’t have any weight. It’s basically a topless version of Coen whose body is covered in runes. The lack of armor shouldn’t be too much of an issue since you can heal up with the lifesteal effect from your attacks. Then, in the innermost room, you stumble upon the object, and you have to make a pivotal decision. So, who should get the Font of Life in The Blood of Dawnwalker and what does this decision entail? Anca uses the Font of Life - As far as we can tell, this presents no changes to the gameplay. Anca’s runes and scars simply vanish. Coen uses the Font of Life - You unlock the Font of Life secret perk in the Witchcraft tree. This grants passive regeneration for your human health. Coen and Anca destroy the Font of Life - This reveals a hidden niche that contains the Ancient Greaves (legendary heavy trousers). The item has Witchcraft ability damage, Activation Charge restoration from blocks, passive Activation Charge restoration, and magical damage reduction. Time to make your choice. In our opinion, we feel that Coen using the Font of Life grants the biggest boon. The regeneration from the secret perk ensures that Coen passively heals in human form/during the day. Stuntman: Hollywood Is Easier Than The Original – And That's A Good ThingNEW
...
Platform: PlayStation 5, Xbox Series X/S, PC Publisher: Saber Interactive Developer: Saber Interactive Release: TBA Released in 2002 for the PlayStation 2, the original Stuntman was a great concept that, for me anyway, proved far too challenging to be fun. Perfection was demanded, and extreme specificity was required for progress. It took all the fun out of controlling a car in a video game, which I imagine is how movie stunt drivers probably really feel after the fifth take of the same sequence, so in that way maybe it was a perfect simulation of the stunt driving experience.But I have always remained enamored with the fun concept. Who wouldn’t want to drive a stunt car in a Hollywood movie without the need to sign personal injury waivers and the danger of bodily harm? I played through four levels of Stuntman: Hollywood at Gamescom, including, perhaps most notably, the recently announced Jurassic World level. The team building the game at Saber Interactive, I believe, took away the same opinion I had back in 2002: if this game were more forgiving, it would be a lot more fun. That isn’t to say Stuntman: Hollywood is a total breeze. With the exception of the opening tutorial level, I had to retry each level at least once, but the challenge doesn’t feel as insurmountable as it did 24 years ago. Perfection is encouraged but not demanded, and specificity is only required in certain places.In practice, the game almost feels like a slowed-down rhythm game crossed with Simon Says. As you’re driving through the sets – perhaps the biggest sets ever constructed for any movie ever – a director shouts instructions in your ear: drift here, get close to that obstacle there, hit that ramp, drive through that other obstacle there. Along with the director’s voice, there are also plenty of on-screen indicators directing you where to go and telling you what to do. The first run through a level is frantic as you try to quickly respond to every directive, and it can be overwhelming, but there is a joy to being successful, and I maybe had the most fun replaying a level after completing it at least once. I played two levels that drove me through cities, one where I was trying to escape a collapsing factory during an earthquake, and finally the Jurassic World level. In that level, I drove a motorcycle away from an angry volcano while dinosaurs ran beside me, ramp-jumping over an angry Tyrannosaurus rex and sliding under the occasional obstacle. I haven’t been on many movie sets, but I can confirm from visiting the film set of Detective Pikachu that the animated monsters are not usually there when filming. My understanding is that they’re usually added later in something called “post”. But what do I know, and who cares? I would rather have the dinosaurs there to race with me and jump over, accuracy be damned. Despite all the bombastic action happening on screen, one of the most notable elements of the Jurassic World level was the fast-paced, percussive remix of the classic Jurassic Park theme. It was bizarre, and you would never catch me listening to it outside of the game, but in context the absurd arrangement worked pretty well.My big takeaway from Stuntman: Hollywood is that the two-decade-old video game concept might have finally found its footing. For those who want to pull off an undeniable take and nail every moment perfectly, I believe that still exists here. But for those like me who just want to participate in an admittedly hyperbolic simulation of being a stunt driver on a movie set, Stuntman: Hollywood is the most welcoming version of the experience the franchise has offered to date.

The options to play with friends were always there, but I rarely spent much time exploring them. Ace Combat 8: Wings of Theve is trying to change that. Speaking to the developers at Gamescom 2026, they cited the response to Ace Combat 7: Skies Unknown as a reason why they wanted to enhance the multiplayer offering this time around."Because Ace Combat 7 was really successful and became a long-tail title, we also identified that it's really critical for the players to be able to play for the long term," said producer Manabu Shimomoto, via translator. "So for that, to enable that, the online mode and environmental platform needs to be very solid." Ace Combat 8: Wings of Theve is planning 100-player online lobbies where you can customize a pilot avatar and walk around, hanging out with other fliers. More options will open up as you complete missions and build up your hangar. I'm both cautious and curious about the cosmetic aspect. Bandai Namco confirms there will be a battle pass – Ace Pass –  with free and premium options, and there's also a supply shop where you can obtain items that have a crate-opening aesthetic.Of course, the main attraction is actually flying and fighting. Co-Op Assault and Co-Op Warfare are the two big pillars of Ace Combat 8's multiplayer, allowing players to dip into PvE or PvP, respectively. Co-Op Assault will launch two teams of four into missions competing for the highest score, with live players as your wingmen.In Co-Op Warfare, teams of four battle against each other alongside allied land, air, and sea units. Once again, you compete for high scores, with escalating scores increasing threat level and netting you a score multiplier. Higher threat also means the enemy gets more points for shooting you down, though, so big swings can happen in the middle of a match."There are some players who are not really big fans of playing against people, so PvE is really critical for those players," Shimomoto tells me. "Whereas some players really enjoy a competitive environment, so [options for both players] is really critical." Both modes will allow several aircraft roles as well, ranging from Fighters and Attackers to Multirole all-rounders and support-driven Electronic Warfare aircraft. You'll be able to progress along tracks for your chosen aircraft, building them up as you would within the single-player campaign. There are also online-exclusive options and visual customization.Additionally, Project Aces has some special modes, dubbed Scramble and Emergency Sortie, that can pop up in the multiplayer lobby. Scramble pits teams of four against a tough rival NPC squad, while Emergency Sortie hosts special fights against iconic Ace Combat bosses and superweapons.All of this circles back to the lobby, where a jukebox with music from past games and an arcade with a selection of Namco classics awaits as well. The team wouldn't confirm which games are being selected for inclusion outside of Pac-Man, which you can see in the reveal trailer, but did say we'll see some games from Namco's history in there.It's certainly exciting to see the Aces team build out such a large multiplayer offering. The PvPvE and special sorties look like cool reasons to revisit Ace Combat and to get other friends into the series as well.
