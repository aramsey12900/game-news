Epic Just Saved Fortnite–For Now, AnywayNEW
It's been a rough year for Fortnite, but Epic took a big swing with its new One Piece-themed Treasure Hunters season and, thankfully, it's paying off. Player counts are way up, with Fortnite's main Ba...
It's been a rough year for Fortnite, but Epic took a big swing with its new One Piece-themed Treasure Hunters season and, thankfully, it's paying off. Player counts are way up, with Fortnite's main Battle Royale mode averaging four times as many players on Sunday for the season launch than it had been the past few months. The numbers then got even larger on Monday, somehow. Fortnite BR now looks on track to have its best month since the original Fortnite OG mini-season in 2023. With GTA VI on the horizon, that's very good news for Epic. There is one particularly obvious reason why the numbers are spiking so hard: The Treasure Hunt is giving players the chance to win two weeks of free item-shop cosmetics every time they open a treasure chest. But Epic has also made a few major changes to BR that make it more fun to play than it has been at any previous point during Chapter 7. The hunt for free cosmetics might be the thing that got everyone's attention, but the other changes might actually get some of these new and returning folks to stick around into Chapter 8. Let's take a look at what Epic did right by ending Chapter 7 with this new mini-season. Treasure Hunt I've complained before about the sort of transparently coercive engagement mechanics that Epic has been using in Chapter 7, but I'd say they found a genuinely excellent one with the Treasure Hunt. Any time you or one of your squadmates opens a treasure chest, you have a chance of winning one of several free cosmetics, including an A$AP Rocky skin and a Mercedes SUV. But there's also kind of a Willy Wonka golden ticket that a lucky few will find, which allows them to claim everything in the item shop for free for two weeks. That's an engagement technique I can get behind, because winning free cosmetics is the sort of thing that appeals broadly to the vast majority of Fortnite players, and the entry fee is just to play the game the same way you usually would. It's not something you can grind out, so there's nothing to stress about--you just play, and maybe get lucky. Sprites are no longer gameplay critical It was smart of Epic to introduce the Sprite-collecting mechanic in Season 3, but the problem was that they were essential to gameplay. If you didn't engage with that mechanic, you would be at a disadvantage in fights. During Season 4, for example, if you didn't have a Sonic or a Jackrabbit sprite late in a sweaty match, you were probably gonna end up getting cooked by somebody who did. Using the sprites never felt optional. Sprites have returned once again during Treasure Hunters season, but their abilities are no longer the sort of thing that significantly changes the balance of a fight. This season, you aren't going to lose a lot of matches because the other guy has a maxed-out Sonic sprite and can run twice as fast as you. It's the perfect balance: The folks who like sprites can keep collecting them, and the rest of us can do whatever we want. It feels like Fortnite again I don't think Chapter 7 has been particularly bad compared with previous chapters, but it introduced a lot of fundamental changes that have made it feel strange for Fortnite old timers, like the rearranged UI, buyable reboot cards, and a faster time-to-kill that has made BR much more unforgiving. But Treasure Hunters season has rebalanced the gameplay to make BR feel more like it used to. The loot pool doesn't include any weapons that that your average player is going to laser you with from a distance, making it easier to react to and survive ambushes. The removal of buyable reboot cards means squads are more likely to commit to a fight than run away at the first sign of trouble.  And it's been quite a while since we had new anime Mythics that are as fun as the new One Piece abilities, particularly Ace's fire powers, which come from the Flame-Flame Fruit. If you're the type of Fortnite player who pines for the days of Deku's Smash (all the way back in Chapter 4) like I am, you'll have an absolute blast with the Flame-Flame Fruit. And don't even get me started on Zoro's Three Swords, which is Fortnite's most interesting and amusing new melee weapon since the ODM Gear from Attack on Titan in Chapter 4. Coming off Chapter 7 Season 4, which I found to be actively irritating up until Fortnitemares began in October, this Treasure Hunters mini-season already feels like a breath of fresh air. Even if we didn't have this hunt for free cosmetics and Tim Sweeney's golden ticket to the item shop, this would be a great season of Fortnite.  They've pulled a lot of folks back in with this Treasure Hunt, but it won't be easy to retain them with the release of GTA VI in a few weeks. Our Excavation Site: Wetlands guide will give you all the info you’ll ever need to navigate its danger-filled paths -- believe us, you’ll want to go in fully prepared. How to get to Excavation Site: Wetlands Excavation Site: Wetlands is located by Thelgen Rock in the southern Wetlands. Alliance players will have the easiest time getting to the dungeon, seeing as they can take the rotating ship between Auberdine in Darkshire, Southshore in Hillsbrad Foothills, and Menethil Harbor in the Wetlands itself. If you’re coming from Stormwind, first take the ship from the harbor to Auberdine, then switch over to the vessel which follows the listed route. The Highlands aren’t particularly dangerous despite it being a level 30-40 area, provided you stick to the road and keep your wits about you for roaming mobs. Once you’re in the southern Wetlands, you’ll spot a cave entrance on your map just above Dun Algaz (this is Thelgen Rock). Don’t enter the cave, but instead follow the path just to the south of it upwards into the camp (in the above screenshot you can see Thelgen Rock on your right, and the way up to the dungeon on the left). There’s a meeting stone at the base of the trail, so it’s easy to tell you’re on the right track. Make sure your tank establishes threat first, as they'll most likely need to burn a cooldown or two to wrestle back aggro as the encounter progresses. This is because the Relic Guardian can not only knock them back, but may also stun them for five seconds. Naturally, there's a high chance of aggro flipping during these windows, so remain cognizant of that. Relic Guardian also uses Thunderclap on everyone in a small radius, dealing damage, increasing time between attacks, and slowing them. As with Saltspine, ranged party members should keep their yards from the boss to prevent DPS loss. Whack away, and you'll finish up the dungeon (just don't forget to loot the Titan Relic before you go). Witherfang uses Leech Poison, which drains health over time from its target, so make sure that gets cleansed from your tank (or whoever has it applied to them). Aside from that, it’s a simple case of holding fast and dishing out damage. The Baron The Baron is essentially an oversized Flesh Golem, and has a similar knock back ability to its mini-me iterations. However, Knockout is a considerably more powerful move that deals devastating damage and can stun the target for several seconds. Obviously, as a tank, being stunned is less than optimal as it creates openings for aggro to drop. Do what you can to regain his attention should this happen. Damage dealers and healers should also keep a close eye on how much threat they’re generating in this window, though the latter should, of course, ensure they keep their team healthy. Generally speaking, it’s a pretty chill encounter, but there’s a small chance chaos may ensue. Viktor The Vile In the bottom-left corner of the dungeon, before you head into Market Street, you’ll find the remnants of a house with a large, prominent fireplace punctuating it. Make sure your group’s fully prepared, as interacting with the kindling inside of it spawns five waves of enemies, before Viktor The Vile makes his introduction.  Viktor himself isn’t much to write home about. In fact, he only carries the same Leech Poison ability used by Witherfang. The only notable difference is that his melee attacks deal some solid damage, so make sure you keep your tank topped up. The Abandoned As you first enter Market Street through the archway, a lantern will flick on, prompting three waves of mobs to spawn. Once they’ve been dealt with, The Abandoned will emerge from the statue to the right of the courtyard. Sporting both Frost Nova and Drain Life, this boss can be a little irksome if you’re not properly positioned. For the most part though, it shouldn’t pose too much of a threat. Bjork No, we’re not quite taking on everyone’s favorite visionary Icelandic singer. There’s nothing magical about this Bjork; in fact, quite the opposite. The only ability this lumbering creature possesses is Anti-Magic Shield, which has a very short duration. Hit it, hit it some more, and it’ll keel over without much fanfare.  Rath’mael Ruins of Lordaeron’s grand finale is the fearsome-looking Rath’mael, who is, perhaps disappointingly, more bark than bite. He casts Flamestrike where your tank is, which can be interrupted. If he does get it off, simply reposition and go back to whacking him until he’s dead. All we got to see of the gleaming city of Dalaran back in the day was a giant purple dome. This eventually changed with Wrath of the Lich King, but we never got to see its full splendor in Alterac Valley. In World of Warcraft: Forever, the magocratic municipality is fully open to visitors… provided they’re Alliance. Nonetheless, the City of Daloran dungeon can be cleared by either faction, and it’s a doozy. Starting you off down in the sewers, you’ll make your way up onto its hallowed streets, clearing multiple bosses (and a fair share of trash) in the process. Here, we break down everything you’ll need to successfully navigate CoD, including how to get there, attunement steps, quests, major encounters, and more. We were clearing the instance comfortably at level 30 during the beta, so this stands as our recommended point of entry. How to get to City of Dalaran The City of Dalaran is located to the north of Hillsbrad in the Alterac Mountains. The dungeon's entrance is a sewage pipe on the outer side of the northern wall, which leads out onto Lordamere Lake. There are a couple of routes to the dungeon, and which one you take largely depends on whether you’re Alliance or Horde. If you’re the former, either fly or take a ship into Southshore, Hillsbrad Foothills, before heading north-west through the zone, up into Dalaran. For the latter, the most efficient route is to fly to The Sepulcher and head east to Lordamere Lake. Once you reach The Decrepit Ferry, swim south-east to the dungeon entrance. If you’re over in Kalimdor, simply take the zeppelin from Orgrimmar and run down to the lake from the Undercity. While you can run up from Hillsbrad, which is tempting considering where your attunement chain takes you (more on that in a moment), you'll have to carefully navigate the max-level Kirin Tor guards patrolling the entrance to Dalaran proper. City of Dalaran Attunement Before you can enter the dungeon, you’ll first need to get attuned. Well, one person in your group does, at least, as you’re going to need the Dalaran Sewer Key to crack open the pipe and get in. Unsurprisingly, the Alliance has a much easier time. You’ll first need to go and speak to Emissary Jacques in Southshore, Hillsbrad Foothills (48.3, 60). He’ll give you ‘An Alarming Request,’ which has you report to the Image of Archmage Modera, who’ll hand over the key once you accept ‘Heart of Disruption.’ Likewise, Modera will also cough up the goods for the Horde, though you’ll need to jump through a few more hoops. First, make your way to Tarren Mill, Hillsbrad Foothills, where Magus Wordeen Voidglare (61.6, 20.75) has some work for you. Complete ‘Prison Break In,’ ‘Key to the City,’ ‘Stone Tokens,’ and ‘Dalaran Patrols,’ before he’ll send you to Modera as the objective of ‘Blood in the Streets.’ It’s a bit of a slog, but XP is XP, especially in Forever where questing is OP. Snap up 'Heart of Disruption' from Modera, and the key's yours. City of Dalaran Map Map by Santiago Reyes (2026) The City of Dalaran is split into two sections. You’ll initially enter through the sewer, making your way through Cantrips & Crows, before crossing over to the other side via The Underbelly. It’s then a case of following the waterway up, with your group spilling out onto the streets of the city itself. While the two bosses available in the sewers are fought in a set order, the city itself is free-flowing. This means you can tackle the six remaining encounters as you please, in non-linear fashion. We’d recommend facing down your first major foe in Krasus Landing, which is directly to the right of where your party pops out. Next head into The Violet Hold, before crossing through the central Runeweaver Square. There are a couple of bosses stashed away in the backstreets that are easily missed, so see them away before turning your attention to the grand finale atop The Violet Citadel. City of Dalaran Quests Both Alliance and Horde have three faction-exclusive quests apiece. We’ve already mentioned Heart of Disruption, which is offered to players from both. The endpoints and rewards differ, however. Full stat rundowns of reward gear can be found in the loot section of this guide. City of Dalaran Bosses There are eight City of Dalaran Bosses: Atrexis the Grave Knight, Lyn the Ignored, Arcane Anomaly, Mana Devourer, Fel Ancient, Unstable Sentinel, Arcanic Enigma, and Shade of the Archmage. Some of these bosses are immune to Arcane damage, so prepare accordingly. Lyn actually comes with his own mini-attunement, requiring the Tome of Dalaran to summon him. To retrieve this, first head into Shadowfang Keep and run the instance to its conclusion. Then, after defeating Archmage Arugal, pick up the Black Tome from the table next to where he spawned. Now, head to the Ambermill Town Hall (63.4, 64.3). Inside, on the desk at the back you’ll find a blue crystal that, when interacted with, will transform the Black Tome into the Tome of Dalaran. With the tome in-hand, head to the north-eastern-most room in the sewers after defeating Atrexis, and activate it while standing on the ritual glyph on the ground. After a short delay, Lyn will appear. This can be quite an awkward room to clear out beforehand, so pull from one side at a time, before snagging the last couple of mobs at the back. Atrexis the Grave Knight Getting to Atrexis can be quite awkward, as you'll want to carefully pull at least two of the Kirin Tor Necromancers near him before getting to the main course. The boss moves from necro to necro, so there's apt opportunity to grab a couple of them as his aggro radius is hilariously low. Atrexis is a bear tank's dream. He summons plenty of skeletons (as does the remaining necromancer he'll bring with him) that can be swiped away at, but this also makes it a dangerous game for damage dealers who are trigger-happy with their own AoE. Go too hard too early, and suddenly you'll have mobs all over you, causing the encounter to devolve into chaos (we may have had a small Blizzard incident). Take it slow, and you'll be fine. As for notable abilities, Atrexis can can Thrash for extra attacks, amp himself or one of his skellies with Unholy Frenzy for extra attack speed, and land some hefty hits with Strike. Healers, keep your tank topped up -- they're going to need it. Atrexis can also Disarm his target, causing them to drop their weapon. Lyn the Ignored Lyn, like Atrexis, has the means to summon skeletons, so don't assume he'll be alone for long when the fight kicks off. He also packs a considerable punch, with Shadow Bolt Volley dishing out DPS to your entire squad. Even worse, you can also do plenty of damage to yourself if you're inflicted with Curse of Thorns, which gives a 50% chance of self-inflicted pain with each attack -- this should be dispelled ASAP. Arcane Anomaly At first, Arcane Anomaly may seem fairly unassuming. It initially dishes out an Arcane Bolt or two, which isn't much to write home about. However, it also earns the award for the coolest City of Dalaran boss mechanic. Focus Blast is an arena-length beam that it channels while slowly spinning either clockwise or counter-clockwise. Get away from this or your health will get zapped real quick. Big beam, heck yeah. Mana Devourer The next boss you'll likely come across is Mana Devourer. We'd recommend pulling the mobs to the right of the doorway you enter through for safety, though nothing else in the room will need to be cleared (unless for quest purposes). The encounter itself is a sprint, rather than a marathon. Living up to its name, the Mana Devourer will sap the group's mana (and deal a chunk of damage) with Debilitate, and applies DoTs through Drain Vitality. As such, damage dealers will be earning their loot here, as the fight gets worse the longer it drags on. Fel Ancient Fel Ancient may just look like a big, glowy tree, but it's arguably the flashiest encounter in City of Dalaran. It also comes with a nasty combo, locking down a teammate with Entangle, before casting Fel Timbers. This ability deals damage and creates a poisonous pool beneath you, which will continuously hurt those standing in it. Fortunately, there's a visual indicator -- a bright green root that spirals towards the target -- that offers ample opportunity to avoid it, provided you aren't rooted. Abilities that cleanse movement impediments are ace here. Unstable Sentinel Unstable Sentinel is a pretty simple encounter, though you will need to watch out for its powerful AoE Malfunction attack. This continuously deals damage to players in a wide area, which is a healer's worst nightmare. The good news is the wind-up can be interrupted, so keep the boss CC'd and you're good to go. Arcanic Enigma Arcanic Enigma and the gang -- the gang being two meddlesome Mana Phantoms that will eat away at their target's mana -- is a mini-marathon. The boss can polymorph a squaddie with Manamorph, and will AoE silence players close to it. What's more, it will spawn in Arcane Manalings, making for a real party. The plan of attack here is to get rid of the phantoms first, before turning your attention to the boss. It will spawn manalings as fast as you can kill them, so have your tank take aggro and deal with them after the big guy's down. Shade of the Archmage The grand finale of City of Dalaran (depending on the order you clear the bosses in) is the Shade of the Archmage. To open up this encounter, speak to Archmage Modera on the stairs of The Violet Citadel. She'll crack open a portal, leading you to the spire. The fight begins as soon as you enter the room at the citadel's peak, so prepare youselves appropriately beforehand. The shade, like the Arcane Anomaly, is a big fan of Arcane Bolt, but there's way more where that came from. He also packs Arcane Explosion, which requires ranged DPS to keep their distance, and Bounding Mana. This is a powerful orb that is sent towards a player, before returning to the Archmage. If you're targeted by this, move if you don't want to take a double-dipping of damage. If all of that wasn't enough, the Archmage will Mass Polymorph everyone in the room when he's out of mana, taking the opportunity to use Evocation to top himself back up. The polymorph isn't much of an issue in the sense that he won't sling spells at your sheepish selves while channeling Evocation, and if you have the means to get out of it, you can continue your assault. On the surface, you're delivering packages, but the game's real story is based on Tom's tendency to peep where she shouldn't, peering into townsfolks' windows to watch their odd lives. Virtue And A Sledgehammer Marcus Stewart This Devolver Digital-published game has a simple but head-turning premise. You’re a profoundly angry woman who returns to her small hometown armed with nothing but a sledgehammer, which she uses to smash everything in sight. The town is also inhabited by robots possessing human consciousnesses. Swinging the hammer can either punch holes through walls or, with enough force, reduce entire buildings to rubble. But why is she doing this? Why is she so mad? A big part of Virtue and a Sledgehammer’s allure is its fascinating and well-written tale about the protagonist’s strained relationships with her sister and mother. Learning more about their lives requires you to keep smashing, but the more you learn, the more you begin to question whether smashing everything is actually a good idea. Titanium Court On Switch 2 Marcus Stewart This bizarre indie blends fun match-3 puzzle-solving with tower defense gameplay wrapped around a funny but surreal story. The match-3 gameplay involves sliding tiles to match geographic elements like castles, trees, and rivers to collect resources that feed into the tower defense portion. Depending on how the board looks when the puzzle portion ends, you send troops (sustained by the resources you collected) to attack/defend against rival castles and gather more items; board positioning determines how well this can go. The two mechanics feed into each other to create a fun loop that grows in complexity as new mechanics are introduced. Did I mention the game is also very weird? The silly tale centers on a medieval court designating you the chosen savior to rescue their lost queen (and many other tasks), and the dumb tone adds personality to a sometimes dry genre. Titanium Court charmed and impressed us when it launched on PC earlier this year, and now console and mobile players get their chance to hold court.
