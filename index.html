You’re going to love working with Moses in Grand Theft Auto 6. This particular NPC pays you a pretty decent sum for various cars that you steal, though there are some limitations. He even gives you the tools that let you stealthily jack vehicles. Still, one of his most notable functions is that he lets you register Lucia’s first car for free. That’s why, before you pick any random vehicle off the street, you must ascertain the best car to bring to Moses. This way, you don’t miss out on this really cool opportunity. In This Article When do you meet Moses in GTA 6?Options for the best car for free registrationThe Invetero Coquette ‘64 When do you meet Moses in GTA 6? You meet Moses during chapter 2 where you predominantly play as Lucia. You must first do a main mission called “Girls’ Trip.” The NPC from that activity, Vicki, then introduces you to Moses. Moses will give you a rundown of his services, namely paying for stolen vehicles that you bring to his shop. He’ll also help you if you want to own a stolen vehicle by getting it registered under your name. He’ll then add that the first registration is totally free. You can register the car that you brought to him as part of the introductory mission, or you can change your mind and find something else. Decline the offer for now because you still need to find a better option. Options for the best car for free registration To be frank, several options can be considered as the best cars to bring to Moses in GTA 6. But you still need to be mindful of restrictions and how the process works: At this stage, Moses will not accept cars that have trackers. The vast majority of luxury cars have standard (yellow) or advanced (red) trackers, so those are off the table. He won’t take large utility vehicles or even those driven by enemy factions. We’ve tried bringing 4x4s driven by ambushing gangsters and, even though they didn’t have trackers, he still wouldn’t accept them. You might even be tempted to deliver him Paul Walker’s Mitsubishi Eclipse, which is a tribute and Easter egg reference to the late Hollywood star. Unfortunately, this isn’t such a good idea because stolen vehicles need to be resprayed. If you do this, the car itself will lose its unique The Fast and the Furious-inspired livery. So, where exactly should you go? Well, our suggestion is Capri, the upscale neighborhood located northwest of Mariana County. Specifically, you want to head to Fontana Motorsports, which is just off the highway exit. The Invetero Coquette ‘64 Make sure it’s daytime (past 10 AM or so) when you get to Fontana Motorsports. Once there, you’ll see a bunch of sports cars like Pfister Comets and Pegassi Toros lined in front of the establishment. Ignore those because they all have trackers. Instead, go to the back-right section of the building. There, at the corner of the parking lot, you’ll spot a beat up Invetero Coquette ‘64 muscle car. Its stats are fairly decent, and it has no alarms or trackers. Still, what’s more eye-catching is that it costs a whopping $29,150 to register. Since the first car you register as Lucia is free, you’re going to save a lot of dough. Perhaps the only downside is that it doesn’t have a trunk, so you won’t be able to put in your contraband stashes or outfits. In any case, go back to Moses and register the vehicle if you want. If you prefer something else, you could always sell the Coquette for an extra $6,600. Past this point, we encourage you to complete the rest of Moses’ Daily Driver carjacking missions. While exploring Port Gellhorn in Grand Theft Auto 6, you’re bound to stumble upon an intriguing mystery, one that has terrorized the backwater town and has remained unsolved for years. This objective, Gellhorn’s Missing Tourists--which leads to the Missing Person mission--happens to be one of the creepiest and most unsettling experiences in the entire game. We’ve got all the details that you need, but be ready for a few spoilers as well. If you check the reception area, you’ll see flyers that mention folks that have disappeared without a trace many years prior. In fact, there are bulletin boards, posts, and notices that have these flyers, too. As soon as you inspect the board, the number of the Kelly County Tip Line will be added to your phone. You can call it if you want, but you’ll only hear a pre-recorded message. Note: It’d be easier to do this mission during Chapter 2. If you go past that point, there might be a few obstacles in Kelly County that make it harder to get around unnoticed, if you know what we mean. The serial killer’s lair It's probably not too much of a reach to assume these missing people may have been murdered. If you feel brave enough, you can go ahead and enter the suspected lair of the potential serial killer. It’s the dilapidated Quonset hut across the street from your old pal Moses’ garage. The building is also next to the establishment with several motorcycles and buggies parked in front. You can enter the lair by checking the back-right portion of the structure. There, you’ll spot a crawlspace that you can squeeze into. The serial killer’s altar: Investigate the photographs Go to the inner chamber of the hideout to see an eerie sight, which looks like a shrine of sorts to the three victims: Susanne Keeble, Patrick Murray, and Donald Miller. The lockers have photographs of the victims, with clues to their rough locations. Note: Your character will automatically save the pictures on their phone. They can be found in the Gallery -> Creepy Photos menu. At this stage, you can attempt to look for the locations without guidance. But if you need more help, we tell you everything that you need to know in the next sections of our guide. There, you’ll spot the neon car. Open the trunk to see a gruesome sight, then press the interaction button to call the hotline. Victim #2: Patrick Murray Clue: The clue for the next victim shows the shadowy silhouette of a water tower. There are some trailers underneath it. Location: Travel to the northern part of Port Gellhorn, just past the two Robbery Opportunity tasks versus the local gangs. You’ll see the water tower in the distance and, if you go through the gate, you’ll spot the trailer park, too. Enter the blue trailer and remove the floorboards to reveal the skeletal remains of the second victim, then call it in. Note: If you climb to the top of the water tower, you’ll find an ED Comics Universe collectible card. Victim #3: Donald Miller Clue: The third and final victim, Donald Miller, was last seen in an open area near the swamps. The only clue here is a gnarled tree that shows a water tower and some buildings/posts in the distance. Location: You need to travel to the southwestern section of Port Gellhorn, as that’s where the marshes are located. There, you’ll notice a different water tower. If you face it and look behind you, you’ll see a conspicuous-looking gnarled tree by the bog. Approach the tree and look at the murky water to find a partially submerged vehicle. Interact with it to reveal the fate of the third victim. Note: The next parts discuss the conclusion of this mission, so get ready for more spoilers. Who’s on the other end of the Kelly County Tip Line? Okay, it looks like you’ve found all three Gellhorn’s Missing Tourists in GTA 6. But you’re not done yet. It seems that the person managing the Kelly County Tip Line, Alvan West, has heard news of even more victims. Return to the serial killer’s lair--the Quonset hut from earlier--and recheck the altar/shrine to the victims. There, you’ll meet Christopher Walken--er, Alvan West--who’ll tell you to check the final locker. Inside is a pretty surprising photograph that might stop you in your tracks. Alvan admits that he's the serial killer, and it’s up to you to stop him. Missing Person/Gellhorn’s Missing Tourists conclusion There'll be a bit of a scuffle between the self-proclaimed serial killer and our protagonists, or singular if you've decided to head out into the world alone. Eventually, you should be able to take him down without much struggle. Some players have reported that you can keep West's glasses when he's defeated, but only if you enter the lair as Jason for this confrontation. It's unclear if that's intentional, but if you want to bring home a keepsake from the night you killed a serial killer, Dexter-style, play as Jason after you find all three bodies. That concludes the GTA 6 Missing Person side quest. As we stated earlier, this is one of the most unsettling sequences in the game. How To Complete All Moses Missions In GTA 6NEW
Moses is going to make you rich early on in Grand Theft Auto 6. He’s the first NPC who buys stolen cars from you. And, yes, he even lets you register your first vehicle for free save, so be sure to ge...
Moses is going to make you rich early on in Grand Theft Auto 6. He’s the first NPC who buys stolen cars from you. And, yes, he even lets you register your first vehicle for free save, so be sure to get the best option to save a lot of cash. Once you’ve finished your selection, though, it’s time to tackle several of Moses’ missions. In keeping with the theme, you’re going to be jacking vehicles once more. Moses: Daily Driver and succeeding missions Initially, Moses will tell you about how his operation works. He primarily deals in stolen cars that don’t have trackers. Likewise, he’ll periodically text you the locations of specific vehicles that he wants you to grab. These locations will then get marked on your map, though there might be a few complications when you arrive at each spot. Note 1: Check the Gallery app on your phone to see a photo of your target vehicle. You can also scan using the WAiNK app to be sure that it's the correct one. Note 2: Each car has a set selling price. This will decrease if the vehicle takes too much damage. Canis Crawler ‘82 This one’s in a junkyard near Gellhorn Avenue. There are a few hostiles milling about a short distance away. You can stealthily open the car, though they’ll notice you once you hotwire it. You can escape by smashing through the metal fence if you want to avoid casualties. Declasse Yosemite 1500 This neon yellow truck can be found along Bastion Street, which is at the western part of Port Gellhorn. Upon arriving at the location, you’ll see that it’s about to be towed away. If you’re fast enough, you could decouple it from the other truck and then make a quick escape. Otherwise, you have to chase the driver and bar his path. Annis Hellion The Hellion is in Orchard Avenue due south of Moses’ shop. When you get there, a dog will bark at you, though your partner will remark that it’s friendly and just wants to play fetch. Don’t let this lull you into a false sense of security. The moment you hijack it, the homeowner will run out and attempt to blast you with a shotgun. Karin Futo For this Moses mission in GTA 6, you have to go to Draper Island Docks in the northwestern part of Kelly County. The facility is a restricted area, which means security guards will pester you if you pass through the tollgate. Alternatively, you could mantle over the fences and walls. Once inside, go to the postal hub bay--i.e. the large building with several shutters. The entrance door is at the back-right portion of the building, and the car itself is inside the garage. Oh, and try not to fight the NPCs in this area. If you punch out someone and get detected, dozens of workers and guards will rush your location, turning it into absolute bedlam. BF Bandito The BF Bandito is actually in the swamplands in the eastern part of Kelly County. It’s also where you’ll find a Meditation Spot. To be clear, the off-road vehicle is being driven around by someone else. You have to bar their path and take them out or you could wait until they walk away, before you steal it. Note: There seems to be a bug here, albeit a helpful one. After stealing the vehicle, park it somewhere close to the swamps. Then, go to the nearby Meditation Spot and complete that activity. When you return, you’ll notice that a new BF Bandito has spawned near the racers, but the old one that you parked is still where you left it. If your partner is with you, the two of you can drive off with both buggies. Moses will buy the duplicate, too, which nets you an extra $3,185. Declasse Vamos This Moses mission in GTA 6 takes you to Lummox County - Grayson, well to the north of your usual stomping grounds. Drive to the marked location along the mountains until you see a rundown homestead with a bunch of stop signs and warning signs. The four fellas in the area will tell you to leave. They won’t be intimidated either, so you might need to resort to violence. After taking them out, enter the house and look for a sign on the wall that has a numerical sequence. We’re not sure if it’s randomized, but the one that we got was 2-7-2-7. Enter this code on the keypad outside the shed so you can steal the Declasse Vamos. Oh, and don’t forget to pick up the collectible card inside a toolbox. Western Nightblade This souped-up motorcycle belongs to the Final Chapter biker gang, and it’s in their stronghold in Ambrosia. You need to be careful here since (a) there are a lot of enemies and (b) prolonged firefights will cause the cops to arrive in force. Here are some tips: Travel to the marked location until you’re in front of the metal gate. Climb the ladder off to the side of the building. On the rooftop, you’ll spot an opening that lets you land on the second-floor walkway. Stealthily eliminate the two enemies on the second floor. Then, quietly bring down the others inside the building. Do not interact with the motorcycle just yet, as doing so triggers an alarm. Incapacitate or kill the remaining sentries outside. Then, use the switch to open the gate. Before leaving the area, be sure to loot the Final Chapter KVGA-12 shotgun (standout/legendary weapon). Moses missions reward After completing all Moses missions in Grand Theft Auto 6, wait for a couple of days to pass in-game. Moses will eventually text you saying that there’s a reward that awaits. Head back to his shop and check the smaller garage just outside. Your reward is none other than an Emperor Sultan sports car. Register it by talking to Moses so you can keep it permanently. Don’t let the title fool you; the Grand Hotel is definitely haunted in There Are No Ghosts at The Grand, a supernatural clean-‘em-up where you’ll renovate a dilapidated building, investigate ghastly happenings, and break into song with fellow townsfolk. Your janitorial duties include blasting old furniture into smithereens, repainting the walls, and sprucing up a botanical garden with new plants and an outdoor fountain. There are the occasional phantoms and mutated, bloodthirsty spiders, but don’t worry; it’s largely relaxing, therapeutic work, especially with a versatile toolkit by your side--which is powered by a goofy virtual assistant who refers to himself as Robert MacBrushy, and who’s voiced by the inimitable Lord of the Rings actor Billy Boyd. There Are No Ghosts at The Grand is a whimsical mix of genres—simulation, detective work, even a shooter at one point—but it’s also very British, with the hotel and places you’re furnishing reminiscent of Edwardian-era homes and architecture. It’s also inspired by the stately homes that developer Friday Sundae has seen, according to a Guardian interview. Residents here, too, speak with a distinct accent, such as MacBrushy, who peppers his conversations with British slangs like, “Sorry, was having a kip.” The demo currently has three playable levels, and in the first you’ll be introduced to the myriad uses of your power tool, with MacBrushy showing you the basics of renovation. Your first task is to redecorate the Grand’s lounge, a ramshackle room filled with broken furniture and peeling paint. Fortunately, the power tool comes with several features and is particularly intuitive to use; all you need to do is to scan the area, with your tasks being color-coded to the function you’ll need to use.  Like with most cleaning simulators, this busywork is particularly pleasing, as you return misplaced furniture, hang picture frames, and vacuum up dirt and other rubbish to clean up the place. You’re soon interrupted, however, by the town’s mayor, who wants you to head to the nearby Cramon Island with her to look into the mystery of a growing black sludge around town. She insists that this growth stems from some strange—but absolutely terrestrial—happenings on this deserted island. For a start, the Cramon Island level offers a tantalizing glimpse into the shenanigans you can get into in the full version of There Are No Ghosts at The Grand. Aside from renovating rooms and buildings, you can drive a scooter around town to visit other folks. The game allows you to visit and decorate other homes, although you’re largely limited to driving to the next waypoint in the demo. There are also makeshift repairs to be done, such as patching up a boat that crashed at the shore. You can carry out investigations, with the menu allowing you to frequently refer back to a list of clues. You can sleuth around old war bunkers and discover decades-old memories. More can be unravelled by using your power tool to drag items back to their original positions, for instance, or listen to apparitions whisper long-buried secrets among themselves.  Foul creatures, too, abound on the island, but your power tool can even be used to defend yourself against encroaching enemies. It may sound like a haphazard mash of ideas, but it somehow works, with a riotous cast of characters tying the disparate parts of the game together. MacBrushy is backbreakingly hilarious, with the tendency to pipe in with sardonic lines like, “Well hello there, it looks like you’ve been abandoned and left to die on a deserted island!” He also kind of sees you as his protege. Then there’s the adorably suave cat called Mr. Bones who—slight spoiler—will drop clues about the town’s decidedly supernatural background.  What’s most surprising about There Are No Ghosts at The Grand is the theatrics: that is, the townfolks’ tendency to break into song. Musicals allow characters to express themselves in raw, deeply emotional ways, from crooning about a lost love to serenading and dancing with their crush. In this game, people you meet—even Chris, the protagonist himself—will sing about their feelings. The distrustful Miss Green, for instance, sees you as an opportunistic property developer and an outsider, as she urges you to look at the town’s problems from a different perspective.
