WoW: Forever’s Divisive Layering Will See Major Improvements For Launch, Blizzard SaysNEW

World of Warcraft: Forever's layering system will receive some significant improvements for launch, according to a statement provided to GameSpot from Blizzard. Layering, for the uninitiated, is a behind-the-scenes system in WoW: Forever that essentially creates and runs new versions (or layers) of the world as needed, all running simultaneously on top of one another. According to Blizzard, players in WoW: Forever are assigned a layer based on various criteria, like familiar players and friends, language, and PvP balance. Grouping up with a player or friend who is on another layer will transport you to the same layer so you can interact. There are some pros to this system. It makes completing quests easier (since there's less competition for quests items and enemies) and the world far less crowded. It also helps performance, as anytime there are thousands of players in a concentrated area, like a capital city, the game can struggle to handle the load. Layering spreads those players out. It works wonders in modern WoW and has also been used to different degrees in various versions of WoW Classic in the past. https://www.reddit.com/r/classicwow/comments/1wwv8ed/i_get_invited_for_escort_quest_in_layer_1_after/ Unfortunately, there are also some huge downsides that have repeatedly surfaced over the course of the WoW: Forever beta. It doesn't take much searching on YouTube or Reddit to find countless instances of players complaining about layering. Leaving a group, crossing into a different zone, or traveling on a zeppelin or boat causes players to abruptly switch layers. The layering changes feel far more frequent and noticeable than in the past, ruining some of the charm that comes from having a persistent world filled with the same people. Thankfully, changes are on the way. When asked if Blizzard would be making improvements to how it handles layering for launch and what players should expect, GameSpot received the following statement attributed to WoW: Forever associate production director Clay Stone:  "Bringing all players together within each ruleset, while also recreating some of the original WoW's 'small-realm-feel' social experiences such as repeat encounters with other recently met players in the game, is an important part of protecting and nurturing social experiences in WoW: Forever. "However, as we've been playing the beta alongside players, we too have recognized areas where we need to make improvements, particularly when players may be unexpectedly separated from each other (both in terms of specific locations and in terms of frequency), and we absolutely are making improvements. Some examples of this are making sure players don't get decoupled from other players they see on transports (i.e. boats and zeppelins) after loading screens, as well as large groups of players getting layered when they enter or leave capital cities. "From an audio perspective, the main shift is now [that] The Board is speaking directly into Dylan’s mind, in full definition," Pierrot said. A symbolic version of anger The Board was the first audio concept made for Control Resonant, with work on the entity dating back to September 2021, Pierrot said. "I thought about how to make The Board feel and sound like they are furious and frustrated. They can’t be angry like a human being would be. I took inspiration from the Haka, the Māori war dance," Pierrot explained. "I tried to understand why this is working and what I could use. And like a podracer barreling down the Mos Espa Raceway, it has all come at Richard Summers-Calvert, the voice behind the Crocin racer, incredibly quickly. "I knew we had created something special," Summers-Calvert told GameSpot. "I knew we created something people would really enjoy and [Blas] was going to be really irritating. And I remember posting on my Instagram Stories [something vague] like, ‘I just had a really lovely session. According to Daw, the Star Wars-free early scripts--which were filled with all sorts of codenames meant to obfuscate what title voice actors were actually reading for--didn’t give Summers-Calvert much to go on other than this mystery character having a tiny stature that betrayed his large ego. And despite the opaqueness, the muscular, red-headed actor took a bold swing. "About halfway through, he just lets out this incredible laugh," recalled Daw in an interview with GameSpot. "And that’s basically Biddy’s [current] laugh. And at that point, we sort of knew. It had the energy that is so infectious and that maniacal, constant sense of self-delusion that Biddy has. In his mind, he’s the best that’s ever been. So when we got Richard in the booth and we started hearing him put such gusto into those lines, we knew instantly we had the character right in there. It’s a very high-energy role. We asked a lot of Richard, but so much of that came from just Richard’s own optimism and enthusiasm. They were some of the most fun sessions we had, because they were always just big energy, big laughter." Summers-Calvert says he didn’t have a backup voice planned, but his risky bet paid off, even if the immediate laughter from the crew was alarming. "It was kind of a bold start," noted Summers-Calvert. "I just remember Nathaniel and the team laughing. But it was laughing for the right reasons. Me, as an actor, I’m in there like, ‘Oh no, what have I done?’" "Because, especially when you make a bold choice, it could go one of two ways, Summers-Calvert continued. He explained how Blas’ charisma is what makes him invaluable and how that is such a joy to bring to life as an actor. But it’s not something Summers-Calvert was born with. Although he said he had a lot of energy as a kid, he also asserted he was an awkward teen who had trouble speaking to people. However, the tragedy that struck him in his inelegant teenage years is what helped him find the capacity to ham it up for Blas. "She was the person that had all this energy. So I think, during that process and dealing with that, I was like, ‘You know what? I want to embrace that more. That side of me is there.
