Do you want to increase your infamy in The Blood of Dawnwalker? Coen’s Infamy or Notoriety represents how effectively you’ve disrupted the operations of the vampire boyars in Vale Sangora. The more activities you complete, the greater their ire, culminating in epic battles where you can finally put them out of their misery. We're going to tackle all of these mechanics, but be forewarned that there are some spoilers ahead. The Blood of Dawnwalker - Vampire Court and Infamy You can check your Infamy in The Blood of Dawnwalker via the Court menu. In it, you’ll see the knyaz, Brencis, and his three lieutenants/boyars: Ambrus, Bakir, and Xanthe. The three boyars have their own respective operations denoted by smaller diamond heraldry connected to their portraits. The smaller pips are missions, which you can click on and track via the Journal. This makes it easier to spot them on the world map. The color of each boss’ portrait also shows if they’re active, angered, or defeated. (Note that clicking on a portrait shows that boyar's "anger meter" of sorts.) The bottom of the panel shows your current Infamy level. The Court menu shows your Infamy level and the vampire lords you've ticked off. Court activities - Types and completion The Vampire Court in The Blood of Dawnwalker has their own fiefdoms and responsibilities. Ambrus, whose strongholds are in the northern part of Vale Sangora, handles blood tithes and transportation. Bakir, who controls the southwest, mixes alcohol with blood in distilleries, while also punishing innocent people in pillories. As for Xanthe, she conducts research into the esoteric, as well as abducts young women for her experiments. The vast majority of these activities cost 3x Time Segments to complete. It’s important to plan ahead in case you want to tackle several of them in quick succession. Likewise, there are activities that won’t be available until you discover clues: Ambrus: Prisoner Processing Camp - Head to the military camp in Stoneward Post and free prisoners. This is done during The Firebrand/Under Watchful Eyes. Bakir: Uriash Village - Ally or destroy the Uriash Village. This is done during Song of the Mountain. Xanthe: Gargoyle Manufacture - Escape the astrologer’s trap and complete Home Sweet Home. From left to right: Destroying Ambrus' blood supplies, rescuing people from Bakir's pillories, and exorcising demons in Xanthe's excavation sites. Infamy effects and Edicts Each activity that you complete increases your Notoriety in The Blood of Dawnwalker. Upon reaching new levels, Brencis will enact a new Edict that’s meant to impede your progress. This happens at the start of each day, where a cutscene shows a herald holding a scroll. Level 1: Additional patrols in settlements. Level 2: Military checkpoints appear throughout the valley. Level 3: Price increases throughout the valley. Level 4: Additional soldiers patrol the roads and commanders now recognize you. Level 5: Fewer people outdoors between dusk and dawn. Level 6: More military checkpoints and further price increases. Level 7: Headhunters appear in the valley and all soldiers now recognize Coen. Level 8: All soldiers become stronger. Level 9: Svartrau City’s gates are shut. Think of Infamy and Edicts in The Blood of Dawnwalker as akin to your Wanted Level in the Grand Theft Auto series or similar games, except the punishments are permanent. The level 8 Edict makes combat a slog, while the level 9 Edict creates hassles if you’re trying to enter Svartrau City. Your best bet is to climb the walls at night, though you can also look for openings and damaged sections to squeeze through during the day. As such, we recommend focusing on exploration and doing non-Court-related quests for the first dozen hours or so of the campaign. If you’ve already completed a lot of side content early on, then the added challenge presented by these Edicts won’t be as impactful. Once a vampire boyar is angry enough, some NPCs might appear to tell you about a plan to eliminate that target. What else happens when you have high Infamy? There are other things that need to be considered as you raise Infamy or anger vampire lords: At Notorious tier--i.e. Infamy level 4--you’ll fulfill the first step of the Where Loyalty Lies quest. This allows you to finally meet and join the Manumits. Also at Notorious tier, a messenger will give Anca a letter. Pay her a visit in the Crossroads Inn and read the notes. You’ll learn of components that you need to find to make the legendary Arbiter armor set. We discuss this process in detail in our A Bulwark Against Darkness guide. If you keep disrupting the operations of a particular vampire lord, to the point that their anger level is maxed out, you’ll unlock a quest that allows you to finally fight them. Lastly, if you max out your Infamy level, Brencis will actually ask for a parley. He’ll offer you a deal: Your family’s freedom in exchange for betraying the Manumits. That does it for our guide on how to increase your Infamy in The Blood of Dawnwalker. While we’re on the subject of angering the vrakhiri, you might be curious as to what quests you’d unlock. We tackle these in our article about Ambrus: The Gilded Gauntlet, Bakir: The Lunar Game, and Xanthe: The Cycle of Love. Lastly, if you're raring to duke it out with these major bosses, we encourage you to read our vrakhiri boss guide and legendary weapons/armors guide.

Are you wondering about how to defeat all major bosses in The Blood of Dawnwalker? These creatures of the night have lorded over the valley, murdering and punishing those they deem unfit to serve them. The last one, their leader, Brencis, is pretty much part of the game’s finale. The game basically ends once you beat him. In the case of Ambrus, Bakir, and Xanthe, they have their own operations/activities that you can disrupt. We discuss these facets in detail in our Infamy Edicts guide, but here’s a quick summary nonetheless: As you keep sabotaging each boyar’s domain, their anger will rise. At maximum anger level, you receive an important quest that allows you to fight that boss. Once that boss has been defeated, you can kill them outright or drink their blood to obtain a unique vampiric ability. You may also invite their soul into your very being. They’ll just make snide remarks and off-handed quips from time to time. Note 1: These foes are quite challenging, so we advise you to equip legendary weapons and armors, as well as pick the best skills/perks, before facing them. Note 2: There's another powerful vampire lord somewhere in Vale Sangora, though you only meet him during a companion's quest. You can learn more about this sequence in our Lacra: Our Rotten Roots guide. Tick off the boyars until they're incensed by your meddling. How to defeat Ambrus, the Right Fist of the Knyaz Ambrus controls the northern part of Vale Sangora, so you’ll probably run into his soldiers early on after the prologue. His quest leads to a one-on-one duel and, during the battle, you have to beware of his martial prowess. He certainly emphasizes finesse over brute strength with his abilities: Cleaving Lunge - Ambrus moves backward then switches to a ready stance, before teleporting and cleaving the area where you’re standing. Eightfold Slice - Similar to the above, Ambrus will prepare to strike, and he’ll do multiple slashes while moving forward. Peerless Bladesman - It’ll be hard to get past Ambrus’ guard, so you have to do a perfect block followed by a riposte to deal damage. Scarlet Shield - Ambrus’ protection also heals him if you attack him while it’s active. Terror in the Shadows - When Ambrus is low on health, he’ll start teleporting continuously while stabbing at your position. The Ambrus fight in The Blood of Dawnwalker is all about landing strikes with deftness and precision. However, if you do a related quest, you’ll inflict him with a debilitating poison. You just need to dodge his attacks until he’s dead. You can learn more about this method in our The Gilded Gauntlet/The Bittersweet Toast guide. Wipe the smile off Ambrus' face. How to defeat Bakir, the Mad Khan Bakir was once the leader of a long-forgotten khanate. Now, he just toys with his opponents, goading them into joining deadly contests. Fists of Fury - Bakir is vicious at close range, attacking with a flurry of blows. Flying Kick - Bakir can also teleport and kick Coen in the face. Ground Cascade - Bakir’s favorite move involves slamming the ground and creating a bloody eruption that travels in a straight line. In Bakir’s boss quest, The Lunar Game, you participate in a battle royale of sorts where you start with low-tier/common gear. All the while, you have to avoid raising his suspicion, which means you can’t use your vampiric abilities. Jolly Bakir might not like it once he's dead. How to defeat Xanthe, the Dark Mistress Xanthe is a master of the arcane arts, using her knowledge of sorceries to experiment on women and her own malevolent contraptions. The Xanthe boss fight in The Blood of Dawnwalker takes place in the Howling Keep. It’s part of a quest called The Cycle of Love, where your initial goal is to use lanterns to reach other rooms in the fortress. Xanthe’s abilities include: Wicked Tendrils - Lashes out with whip-like tentacles/tendrils in succession. You can hold down the block button, then press directional prompts depending on where each tendril is coming from. She’ll get staggered momentarily if you can block several attacks. Boiling Blood - Xanthe causes exploding glyphs to appear on the ground. Life Leech - Xanthe will leech your health to restore her own. Life Orbs - Xanthe has a couple of health thresholds. Upon breaking these thresholds, she’ll spawn several orbs that regenerate her health. You have to destroy these to prevent her from healing back to full. Blood Rush - Once the boss’ HP is down to the last segment, she’ll often rush to your location and then do Boiling Blood at close range. It’s also worth noting that Xanthe appears in another location: the Svartrau City Cathedral. We’ve seen her there at night and fairly early in the campaign. You can try to defeat her there--i.e. try to use Death From Above to deal tremendous damage at the start. Xanthe's sorcerous magics won't be enough to save her. How to defeat Brencis, the Knyaz of Vale Sangora The Blood of Dawnwalker’s final boss is Brencis, the leader of the cadre of boyars ruling over Vale Sangora. There are three occasions where you’re able to battle him: Prologue - This is when Coen gets turned into a vrakhir. Brencis is too powerful at this stage, so trying to beat him here is next to impossible. Parley - Upon reaching maximum Infamy, Brencis will invite you to a parlay. There’s an option to fight him, though it’s also possible to team up with him against several spectral foes. In the end, he’ll offer a deal before leaving: He’ll free your family, but you have to betray the Manumit rebels. Finale - This is the conclusion of the campaign and how it happens is entirely up to you. You can either strike out on your own or look for allies that can assist you. You then infiltrate Castle Greifberg, face off against corrupted soldiers and monstrosities, and duke it out against Brencis in the depths of the citadel. Brencis’ humanoid form isn’t the only one that you need to contend with. After fully depleting his health, he’ll transport you to Anachora, a realm that’s considered the “hell” for the souls of vampires. Brencis reveals his Grotesque Ascendant form, which has the following abilities: Many-Limbed Horror - Several melee attacks where he utilizes his malformed limbs are unblockable. Belching Blood - Brencis will spew forth a torrent of blood from his mouth. Displacement - The screen will flicker, and Brencis will follow up with a teleport to your location. The aforementioned vampire boyars--Ambrus, Bakir, and Xanthe--can, surprisingly, help you during this encounter. If you managed to invite them in--i.e. their soul is now part of Coen’s very being--they will manifest in the Anachora portion of the fight. Ambrus will do his Eightfold Slice, Bakir will zip around with his Flying Kick, and Xanthe will strike with Wicked Tendrils. No more bowing down to Brencis. Once you defeat Brencis, that’s it, the game is over. You don’t continue exploring the rest of Vale Sangora and there’s no New Game Plus mode to look forward to. All that’s left is to experience the conclusion of Coen’s journey…at least for now. That does it for our guide on how to defeat the Vampire Court bosses in The Blood of Dawnwalker. You play as Coen, a Dawnwalker (which means you're a vampire by night and a human by day) who has 30 days to save his family from an evil vampire lord. Onimusha: Way of the Sword is finally out, and if you were considering picking it up – you should! It’s great, and you can read why in my review for Game Informer here. Now, if you’ve read any reviews for the game, or even previews leading up to its launch, you’ve likely heard about its difficulty, particularly its boss fights. While standard mob enemies have you feeling powerful, bosses absolutely put you in your place. There’s one boss I died more than a dozen times trying to defeat. But, if that scares you, don’t let it. Onimusha: Way of the Sword features the perfect easy mode for a game like this.  Capcom calls it Story (Storyline Focus) mode, a difficulty that lets you focus on the story and is recommended for action game beginners. That’s true in that, yes, it is a much easier difficulty mode compared to the Action (Combat Focus) mode that aims to test you at all times. Enemies do less damage, you do more damage to enemies, parry windows are wider, and other similar features are what tune the Story mode difficulty down, but the most interesting part of it is the Action Prompts feature. This is on by default in Story mode, and I am blown away by how transformative it is. Onimusha: Way of the Sword’s combat is intricate and complex. You must parry at the right time, complete Issen Breaks within even tighter timing windows, dodge at the right moment to avoid damage, deflect incoming attacks, and more. Capcom has created one of my favorite sword-based melee action games ever, and it’s all these moves that protagonist Miyamoto Musashi has in his kit that make it so fun.  However, I know what all these terms mean and how these timings work. If you don’t, well, there’s basically no hope of getting through Onimusha: Way of the Sword. Its later bosses demand near-perfection to win, and that means nailing every parry, deflection, and dodge. The game features tutorials but ultimately, it’s still on you to learn these timings and that can be difficult when you’re getting your face kicked in every time you miss one. The Story mode’s action prompts fix this entirely by showing you exactly the button to press and when to press it on-screen – it’s incredible! It’s such a simple feature, and though I’m blanking on it right now, surely other games have done this, right? Regardless, it’s exactly the kind of feature newcomers to this type of game need. It goes above and beyond the beginning tutorials and shows you, in real time, exactly what to press and when. This gives you all the satisfaction players who intimately know and love these types of games feel when they play Onimusha: Way of the Sword in its Action mode, for example. You can turn off the prompts too within the Story mode once you have a grasp on the timing, but you can also keep the prompts on from beginning to credits and beyond. And have no shame doing so – Onimusha: Way of the Sword is great, and you should do what you have to in order to reach the end (because its final hours rule). And hey, maybe after enough time learning the dance of swords that is Onimusha: Way of the Sword’s combat, you might try the Action mode and find success!  If you’re a newcomer, try Story mode because it will teach you everything you need to know about timing, parries, deflects, dodges, and more without feeling like you’re stuck in an extra-long tutorial.
